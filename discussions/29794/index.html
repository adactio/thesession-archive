Re: Changing a Bridge

Before having a new one fitted, you might try taking a millimetre off the outside edges of your existing bridge. Do it one side at a time: loosen a string and move it across the bridge, and that way the soundpost won’t come out. Mark the string positions on the face or rear of the bridge, shave the top down until the groove has gone, or almost gone, then deepen the groove a touch more (or you could deepen the grooves first, then shave off a bit of wood). A tiny bit makes a difference, and it will cost you nothing if you aren’t satisfied.

Re: Changing a Bridge

The top of the bridge should have a 41mm radius. Don’t worry about the shape of the bridge itself, but if you cut a cardboard template of that radius, all four strings should touch the template.

And if you measure the height of the strings at the end of the fingerboard, the E should be 2.5mm, G should be 5mm (with gut or synthetic strings, they can be slightly lower than that with steel).

The cost of the work varies a lot from luthier to luthier, and also with the quality of the fiddle - a good quality fiddle deserves a good quality bridge blank, student fiddles get a cheaper blank (and possibly less care taken over fitting.)
Personally I charge £35 for a ‘grade AA’ bridge, but everything is more expensive in the cities and big dealers.

Re: Changing a Bridge

With regard to bridge height, if it was as simple as “higher bridge makes the sound better” then all fiddles would be fitted with a higher bridge.

The height of the bridge does affect the sound (the volume more than the tone), but it affects other things too, particularly the playability. To keep the instrument playable the string heights over the fingerboard need to be at (or very close to ) the measurements I gave in my last post, and the bridge height is set to give those clearances.

Bridge height only becomes an issue on old fiddles where the neck angle has sunk, so that a very low bridge is needed to give the required string heights - in that case it is worth considering getting the neck re-set, to restore the bridge height.

Re: Changing a Bridge

Ok, I’ll re-phrase for you.

You can have any radius you like on your bridge. But hundreds of years of accumulated experience have shown that the optimum radius is 41mm, and even a small deviation from that will make the instrument harder to play. Added to which, violin fingerboards are made to a standard radius, so if you alter the bridge radius it will no longer match the fingerboard and you won’t be able to get even action heights on all strings. It would be like putting a flat bridge saddle on a guitar with a curved fingerboard.

Re: Changing a Bridge

“Lie an 8mm diameter pencil on the fingerboard and slide it over the radius with the point touching the bridge. The line should be about the right height at the E side, and about 2mm low on the G side. ”

Better still, split a pencil lengthways down the middle. sand the leaded side flat so you have a pencil with a flat edge and the lead exposed. run the flat edge across to mark the bridge. mark the string height above that line - the measurements are (Skreech ignored): steel strings - from e to g - 2.5, 3, 4, 5mm. Dominant, 3, 3.5, 4.5, 5.5mm - gut, 3.5, 4, 5, 6mm. That’s assuming you have the neck at the correct elevation.
As for bridge quality: “grade AA” is hype. If you want excellent quality, search for a genuine French Aubert “deluxe” bridge. It will not be cheap.

The 42mm radius is approximate. I use a template made from the Beare curvature.

>>“@skreech - you know of an accurate way of checking that a bridge has been cut to the correct radius?”

Yes, you cut a 41mm radius template, slide it in under the strings next to the bridge, and check that it touches all four strings at the same time.

The only thing that matters is the position of the strings, not the wood in between. Some luthiers are cutting bridges with a ‘parabolic profile’ (like the top of an Early English church window), claiming this is ‘better’. Weejie, I think the real difference between us is that one of us is a trained luthier and the other thinks he can fix fiddles because he knows a bit of woodwork. When you talk about setting the action height of each individual string it is fairly obvious which of us started out on guitars. I was taught violin repair by Juliet Barker.

Maybe there are some fiddles about with non-standard fingerboard radii - (there are a lot of untrained) repairmen in this world) but when you come across one it is something that needs to be corrected if you are ever going to achieve a decent set-up. The fingerboard radius isn’t an arbitary thing, chosen by personal preference or following tradition, it is dictated by geometry - if you wanted to uses a different radius, and still achieve a sensible set-up, then you would need to change the string spacing too: unlike most guitars, violin fingerboards are conical not spherical. The strings have to lie over half lines of the cone (ie the string lines have to converge at the vertex of the cone) if they don’t, if the string lines lie at an angle on the surface of the cone, then the string line is no longer flat, it will have a hump in the middle, requiring a higher than normal action, or excessive scoop. If you plane a fingerboard so that it is dead flat under each string, it will inevitably either be a flat plane (which isn’t much use!) or a cone of the correct radius. Those are the only situations where the geometry works.

My reference to ‘AA’ grade bridges had absolutely nothing to do with catalogues, it was simply a short hand way of referring to a top quality bridge blank. Aubert don’t actually make anything called a ‘deluxe’ bridge. Their products are graded by number - a No:5 is student quality, , No:8 is top of the range (although No: 7 is commonly referred to as a ‘Mircourt’ and No:8 as a ‘luxe’. Within each grade blanks are available either in raw timber of pre-hardened; with a selection of foot widths to allow for different bass bar positions; with a standard or low kidney to allow for low neck angles; and with or without an ebony insert under the E. If you are just picking bridges out of a box marked "Aubert Deluxe’ you have a long way to go before you should be let loose on a quality instrument.
