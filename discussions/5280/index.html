… and the night was ruined

You know the score, you turn up to your favourite session and already ensconced are a few newcomers you haven’t yet played with and your heart sinks …

They’re decked out in T-shirts sporting the name of their favourite Irish bar and in front of one of them, inside her tambourine(!) is a motley collection of sh*k*y *ggs, castanets, maraccas, gourds and God knows what other fiendish instruments of concussion … I mean percussion.

I know, I know … the only reasonable thing to have done at that point was to have turned on my heel and hit the high road. But, like an eejit, I stayed and cringed my through the evening, knowing that inevitably the shaky eggs would be produced at some point.

Boy did yer woman pick the wrong moment, when Wannabe Wakeman (threads passim) launched into his signature barndance. It was both funny and disturbing to watch/listen to the tune crash to an ignominious halt and observe theartistformerlyknownaslordofhteflies wag a disapproving finger at the perpetrator and deliver the gentle - but final - admonition “No shaky eggs!”.

(Her husband/partner remonstrated with me. “What have you got against shaky eggs?” “They’ve got nothing to do with the music we’re playing here.” “Says who?” “Says me and theartistformerlyknownetc.” “Let me tell you, bhoy, I’ve been in this game for forty years (me thinks …oh yeah? … well, when are you going to get around to learning a 4th chord then … do you learn one per decade or something?) and I’ve never heard anyone give out about shaky eggs before.” “Save your breath … discussion’s over. House rule. My way or the highway. Etc.”

Yawn!

What with thon pair of blow-ins, the lunatic whistle-torturer and trying to compete with the jukebox in the bar next-door, it was not the finest hour.

Such a pity when you’ve got a nice wee session going, with a good friendly vibe and some cracking playing; and then one or two amadans can wreck the evening … It’s come to something when I spend time at the bar, agreeing with the barman how the session’s really gone downhill lately and asking if he could slip something into the detratctors’ drinks!

I’m still fuming! My lesiure time’s fairly precious and when some eejits waste my time it makes me seethe.

Just thought I’d vent … we’ve all been there. But what IS it about Irish music sessions that causes the musically inept to believe that they ahve a God-given right to play along with (or at cross purposes to) musicians who’ve put a bit of time, effort and care into learning and playing the tunes?

I know I’m just a grumpy old man, but bah and boo!!!
