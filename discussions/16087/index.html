Re: should bodhran players be required to learn melody instruments before they’re unleashed on the public?

On past discussions my good friend Mr Llig and myself have been in agreement on this matter.

Playing another instrument can be a help, not necessarily playing Irish tunes, just playing an instrument.

Now recognising the tunes after a time, just as everyone else at the session does, is a must. You “know” that tune, and know how to play it.

When a solo flute player asks you to accompany him, and launches into a tune neither you nor anyone else has ever heard of, the secret is to LISTEN to the tune, the whole way through first time, and then join in. On occasions like that the bodhran player can join in, unlike the other melody players as they do not know the notes. A certain amount of self satisfied “that was easy to learn” tongue in cheek goes down well at such times.

Being able to play the tunes on a melody instrument may or may not be a help, depends on the individual. Many melody players have little sense of timing as they are used to listening to themselves, rather than the rest of the players at the session.

And at the end of the day, as I have said many times, why would you want to play the bodhran if you could play a melody instrument? This last piece, is merely my take on things.

Re: should bodhran players be required to learn melody instruments before they’re unleashed on the public?

Sorry for entering the fray so late, Eejit, but it seems like the heart of the argument is that some folks think:

1. in order to play the bodhran well one must know the tunes
2. in order to know the tunes the bodhran play must learn a melody instrument.

And then people go back and forth naming famous or not famous players who do or do not play a melody instrument or who do or do not recommend the above.

In my not so humble opinion, I think the idea is mostly gobsh*te.

Do we really think that learning your first ten tunes on a new instrument give anyone deep enough insight into the instrument to help them become a better bodhran player? Really? You learn something unique about the Rakes of Mallow or the Kesh jig that will make you a better rhythm player?

I think a novice bodhran player is better served by listening to as many tunes as possible, and by practising to a few tunes at a time until they learn them inside and out.

That’s how I’ve taught, and I think my students appreciated having to play to the Bucks of Oranmore for hours at time, because they had to find new things in the tune or in backing the tune to keep it interesting.

↳ Posted by dirtyheel on December 14th, 2007.

Should bodran players KNOW a tune before playing?

On a different note, I disagree with the notion of the experienced bodhran player having to know a tune before playing it. Yes, knowing the tune helps, but a good player can know what kind of tune it is in the first bar or two, and pick up the rhythmic skeleton of the tune by the second go round.

I hate it when a bodhran player stops at every tune changes and sits and listens to the tune before entering in again. Are you kidding? You’ve just killed any momentum or dynamics you were building with the melody players so you can sit and look like a studious musician for a few bars. it’s bullsh!t, I tell you.

as many people here point out, including my pal Llig, the joy of this music is the dynamics of the tunes, of how melody players articulate the tunes, and how they vary them as they play. it creates an overall dynamic that bodhran players need to attune to and contribute to. tune changes are wonderful opportunities for dramatic rhythmic changes and shifts that add so much depth to the music.

why would you lose that? are there really that many tunes that do something so complicated that your basic rhythm will totally crap it?
