Re: How many instruments?

“This seems to support my observation that people who play guitars (and to some extent related instruments) seem to be more prone to acquiring large collections of instruments than other musicians. Anyone else noticed this, and any - if true - theories as to why?”

There could be several reasons.
One is that, because of mass production, instruments of quality are much more readily available and are affordable.
Secondly there is possibly a greater variety of guitars available than for other instruments. So for example, within steel string acoustics you have a variety of body shapes (dreadnought, jumbo, 000, parlour, etc), a variety of tone woods including spruce, cedar and mahogany for tops and rosewood, mahogany, walnut and maple for backs and sides. I don’t think you get the same variety in body shapes and tone woods of fiddles, for example.
Thirdly, unless they are historic, high end or rare instruments, there’s not much value in second hand guitars, perhaps because new ones are so affordable. All the instruments I have passed on have been given to friends or charity shops.

But I would contend that, at any one time, most fretted instrument players will have one instrument that they play far more than any of the others in their possession. There has to be “the one”.

Just a bit off topic but applicable if you live in certain climates with these vast collections of instruments. I live in Arizona where the humidity is very low. Keeping the wood happy with higher humidity has involved installing a heavy plastic strip door like you see in grocery stores and loading docks here in the USA. That, and a large humidifier that pumps out about 5 gallons every two days keep the splitting (once) and collapsing (twice) of instruments at bay. I can’t say that it’s comfortable playing in that room but it’s a necessity to protect the investment in the instruments.

Outstanding deals if you know what you’re looking at.

Re: How many instruments?

I just remembered one more instrument I own and don’t play. It’s a “rescue mandolin,” something I found at the local county trash dump, half buried under garbage. It’s a cheap bowl back mandolin from the 1920’s. The headstock was snapped off the neck but still present, held on with just the strings. The slats on the back had separated in a few places. Amazingly though, the soundboard was intact with no cracks and the neck looked to be in good shape.

I glued the headstock back on, clamped and glued the back separations. The bridge was missing so I bought a new one, but haven’t done the last step of sanding it down for action height. I think it might be playable with an extra-light gauge string set when I’m done, but it’s destined to be a wall hanger decoration. It’s too beat-up to be worth anything, and it’s not the kind of mandolin I’d want for Irish trad. I already have a very nice mandolin for that. On a wall hanger, this little bowl back mandolin will be a nice addition to the vibe of our music room, even if it’s not played.

I also don’t really have room to store it in my small apartment that is already filled with a lot of stuff (including other musical instruments). I have also a small 22-string Pakistani-made harp that is not as nice of a harp but easier to store, and probably more suited to my playing style anyway. I kept telling myself I’d sell it back to the harp store where I bought it, but haven’t been able to bring myself to do it.

2. A flute (M&E in polymer). I’d like to learn, but my left hand just can’t manage the stretch (yes, that’s left, not right), especially since even though it’s an RR copy, my narrow fingers juuust cover the largest holes, so I have little leeway for shifting hand/finger position to reach. So I end up with a wrist that is aching within a minute or two. I have smaller flute-like objects that are comfortable to play, but I have never figured out how to get that overly-breathy or windy sound out of my playing, no matter how much I mess with my embouchure. (And I got to try one of Casey Burns’ small-handed Folk Flutes once and found it much more comfortable, but I can’t justify the cost of one of those, even secondhand, even if I offer my M&E in partial trade, especially if I might never have a good playing quality to actually be able to play the thing.)

Then there are the ones I don’t play nearly as often as I should. Whistle gets played the most because it’s the easiest to just pull out, and the easiest to take with me to play outside/in my car (apartment + thin walls = neighbors probably don’t want to hear me), and the easiest to sound the best on so I don’t torture anyone too much since I have pretty much no opportunities to woodshed without being heard by someone else (I used to be able to go play at church by myself as I have a key, but obviously not right now; no one is allowed in the building while it’s closed for quarantine). So, I should play violin more… and the viola I got a few months ago… and my guitar… and recorder… and my keyboard that I bought yeeeeeears ago for composing but usually eschew in favor of the computer these days, and my bodhran which is currently stuck at church because we were supposed to play a St. Patrick’s Day concert so I was keeping it there between rehearsals and then of course we stopped rehearsing suddenly…

And then there’s the mandola my mom bought, never ended up playing, and now I get to figure out how to get rid of it… A couple music stores have told me they’d be willing to put it on consignment, but it’s not an instrument a lot of people have even heard of, so I doubt anyone would be lining up to buy it, but no way I want to deal with shipping it.

And of course, right now it doesn’t matter how much all the things I need to get rid of annoy me by staring me in the face, because there’s nothing I can do about it right now…
