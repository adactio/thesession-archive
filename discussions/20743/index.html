Some have ears ….. but cannot hear!

Picture the scene last night, if you will. Myself & a very fine Fiddle player sitting in the corner of a lovely old, dimly lit bar. Two Fiddlers, sharing a few quiet tunes & swopping stories of wonderful musicians & great sessions. The atmosphere is perfect, with the light & heat coming from the old gas lighting.
In a relaxed manner, we gently slide into a couple of beautiful old Junior Crehan tunes. Moments after we start, the harp player next to us joins in with a rather sympathetic tinkling accompaniment, to be joined moments later by some subtle fingerpicking from the Guitar player next to her.
By now, our thoughts are drifting gently back to Miltown & many, many happy memories of seeing & hearing Junior himself play these same tunes, plus thoughts of other old friends & wonderful musicians who are sadly no longer with us, but are still very much in our hearts. Plus more general thoughts of the best of tunes & sessions shared & the gentle rolling scenery of Co Clare …………………….

THEN THE SPELL IS BROKEN!!!!!!!!!!!!!!

We are joined by {in this case} the Bodhran basher & by the percussionist next to him, who starts bashing a pair of these:

http://www.hendersonmusicireland.com/images/prod/Conga.jpg

while working one of these:

http://www.jhs.co.uk/rhythmtech/dst.jpg

with a foot peddle at the same time! http://www.econ.upf.edu/~fleiss/Welcome1_files/homer-doh.jpg

For some musicians, common sense seems to come quite naturally to them, they are well able to listen & then to compliment the music around them, with taste & to the best of their abilities. They know their own limitations …. so they know when NOT to play, and when it is right to just stop playing, & to sit back & just LISTEN!

Sadly, it is not uncommon for Bodhran players to even be heard playing along to SLOW AIRS! http://bestsmileys.com/angry1/5.gif

Musicians! …………… SOME HAVE EARS, …… BUT CANNOT HEAR!
http://bestsmileys.com/crying/14.gif

So, do you have any horror stories of ‘EARLESS’ musicians, you would like to share with the group?

Re: Some have ears ….. but cannot hear!

This mustn’t be solely a bodhranista-bashing discussion – I know some excellent players of that instrument who listen to the music and play accordingly, and are assets to any session. Sadly, there are other instrumentalists around who really should know better with their playing. Obviously, I’m not getting at beginners, who all deserve to be cut some slack, but a (thankfully) small number of fiddle players who have been around for a long time but have manifestly made no effort whatsoever to improve their technique over the years, despite, at least in a couple of cases, being quite good players on other instruments. Consequently, their poor intonation and tone, lack of coordination and bow control, makes playing along with them a painful uphill battle. You can’t help but get the impression that they just don’t care, which I think is insulting not only to all the other players there who do make the effort, but to the listeners in the pub as well.

…funny you should mention it…

…a recent email to one of my local session leaders after a particularly bad night (this particular sesh is about an hour’s drive each way for me):
………
"I’ve been fretting about this all day…without naming names, I’m probably going to post a question on thesession about this, and thought that I’d give you guys a heads-up, lest anyone get needlessly offended. Please know that I generally have a fantastic time when I show up, and I’m *very* grateful for the new friends that I’ve made, and appreciative of everyone’s talents.
And for the effort that you guys are putting in to keep things going. But…
Personally, I’d rather just stay home than to hear stuff get butchered like it did last night. I was a newbie myself once, but got straightened out real fast. Part of the trouble is, you get some idiot backer playing wrong chords and in the wrong modes *way* too loud on a big boomy dreadnought (when, oh when are these clowns finally going to get proper guitars?), and the rest of the relative newbies get the idea that that’s OK. It’s not.
I understand that you guys are trying to run an open, “anything goes” kind of session, and I really appreciate that. I think that amateur collective music-making is about the best, and I participate whenever and wherever I can. I also care enough about music in general that I hate to hear it get ruined by hacks. And trust me, I’ve worked with and heard plenty of ’em. I suspect that you know what I’m talking about."
……….
At one point there was a lone whistler on melody and four guitars, plus me NOT playing on most of ’em , as I felt bad for the poor whistler and didn’t want to contribute to the din (I drank too much instead, but that’s another matter). Things *did* improve a bit when a few more melody players showed up.
The kicker was when “Alpha Guitarist”, after bashing through a set in the wrong keys/modes, loud, proud, and strong, turns to the whistler and asks “…what key was that in?”
I’d thought about having a friend call me on the cell with some BS about a sick kid in the ER so I’d have an excuse to sneak out early (I consider most of these folks to be my friends, and don’t want to offend).

So the question is, has anyone else gotten to a point where it seems like it’s just not worth the hassle?

I must add that dear session leader and I had a bit of a heart to heart a few days later, and we’re both of the same mind, but he encouraged me to bring extra blood pressure meds the next time. I suggested that defibrillation equipment (for me) might be in order, as well…
…rant over

Re: Some have ears ….. but cannot hear!

A sad tale indeed Al. :-(

I’m always amazed to watch the reaction of other musicians, when a really top class musician turns up at a session. If you watch, you will usually notice that the more experienced players will actually play a little less than they usually do, & spend much more time actually listening & learning.

On the other hand, the less experienced players & of course ALL the buck eejits, will try & play along with every tune, as if maybe trying to impress the star attraction.

I saw one Whistle learner one night, watch in total amazement as a brilliant visiting Whistle player tastefully jumped octaves & added beautiful little harmony runs during the 2nd part of a tune.
The beginners answer to this modest demonstration of excellence, was to almost drown out the visitor during the next tune, by constantly honking away at octave notes at complete random, most of which were not even in tune & certainly bore no relation to the tune & certainly not to any harmony!
It was one of the most bizarre sights I have ever seen in a session. If I hadn’t seen it myself, I wouldn’t have believed it had actually happened!
Talk about the dark side! :-(
