The state of the session scene….?

Hello all,

I’ve never been to a session.
Well maybe I have because there was always people playing traditional music around my grandparent’s house when I was little, although it obviously wasn’t called “traditional music” then, it was just music.
And then my uncles used to take me to the Irish club or the Catholic club and there was always music going on in there too.

So I guess what I mean is I’ve not been to anything like a session for many years or I’ve never been to a one where I took any notice of the music.

So I was just wondering, what is the session scene like these days?
In England, pubs have been dying a death these last few years so have sessions sort of been “disbanding” too or is there still a thriving community?

Another thing, are there still any real genuine places in Ireland or Scotland? You know where old fellas just sit around and play music in the pub and have been doing for years/generations?…… not an organised thing as such like the sessions advertised here.
Not that I’m suggesting that there’s anything wrong with anything here, I think it’s a brilliant resource.
I was just wondering if there is any of the old tradition left or has it all been replaced by theme pubs and fake diddly diddly ceilidh bands?

Thanks.

Re: The state of the session scene….?

Depends where on the planet you are. There are undoubtedly more sessions at the present moment in Scotland than there were in the 1970s and any decade since. That’s in terms of quantity, quality is another matter.
I’m not sure why you think a session “where old fellas just sit around …etc” is any more “genuine” than a session “where young fellas - and ladies - just sit around…etc”. The only difference would be that your old fellas would have been doing it for longer, although there would inevitably be differences in repertoire.

Any session has to be “organised” to a certain extent.

I’ve been to 3 sessions in and near Aberdeen in the last 5 days. The ages ranged from 18 to over 60 - age just doesn’t come into it, and that’s one of the reasons I like this music so much. 2 of those sessions are less than 1 year old [ one only started 4 months ago ]. The other has been going for maybe 15 years or so. There were no regular established “sessions” - as the term is used today - of traditional music played in pubs in Aberdeen before 1972 that I’ve ever heard of. I believe there were one or two out in the nearby countryside frequented by Scottish country dance musicians, but I’m fairly sure they don’t occur any more.
I have no experience of England, so others will have to answer that.
I was in Cork just over a year ago and managed to find 4 sessions on a Sunday. A few years ago, Ennis in Co.Clare was hosting 8 sessions on a Saturday night, and you could find a traditional music session any night of the week. I don’t think that’s in any way typical of the whole of the island of Ireland, but it seems to be in a pretty healthy state all in all.

Re: The state of the session scene….?

England seems to consist of ‘happening’ areas and deserts as far as trad sessions are concerned, whether one is talking about Irish trad or English. If musicians live and play a session for some time in a given place, spin-off sessions are likely to appear in due course as more musos join the scene and/or the first session splits for any reason. Sessions - good ones, anyway - seem to generate more sessions and more players, and those running other local pubs might be more inclined to host music sessions when they’ve actually been to one and realise that they generate reliable bar takings, usually go down OK with most of the other customers, and are not overmuch theatres of gross misconduct. (N.B. The grizzling of *some* customers at diddly diddly is I think a given…) If sufficiently footloose and free, trad afficcionados have often upped stumps and moved to places where there was a good session scene, and many naturally have got into a network of contacts at festivals, which would help towards this.

This is the way scenes work across the field of culture: some live wires start something, and like-minded people feel drawn to it and increase it. This is how Hay-On-Wye and Wigtown (in Galloway) became ‘book towns’, for instance. Nothing to do with ‘culture’ quangos - these merely try to patronise and co-opt what’s been done.

↳ Posted by Wes P on June 19th, 2013.

Re: The state of the session scene….?

Hi Wes

I think you have a rather rose-tinted view of the “real, genuine” session - no-one’s been doing this for “generations”.

I’ve heard from a number of knowledgeable folk that what we think of as a session (with a group of musicians playing tunes together in a pub) just goes back to the early 70s. I go to a session with a fair claim to be one of the longest-running in the land, and it’s no older than that. Sessions are a product of the revival.

Talking to collectors and older players in the 70s I got the impression that the older type of musical get-together was more sing arounds and play arounds in private homes rather than ensemble playing in public.

As for the current situation in England, my experience is that the session scene is very patchy. In Brum a few years ago there was almost nothing going on that I could find - quite surprising. But moving to South Devon there’s a thriving scene, with a good standard of musicianship and some sensational young musicians who’ve gone on to make national names for themselves.

As for your idea that it’s only “old fellas” can play authentic music, we’ll, you’ve probably taken enough teasing on that one. Just get out there and listen to what the youngsters are up to these days - the scene’s never been more healthy…
