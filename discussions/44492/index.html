Re: Burns supper last night?

I wasn’t (wasnae??) nearly as busy as Calum, but I piped at one Friday night and one Saturday night.

Generally Burns Suppers here in the USA are grim affairs, with people who don’t know Lallans stumbling through Burns’ dialect poems, the audience getting neither the sound, nor the sense, of Burns.

Refreshing was Friday night’s event! It was held at a small private High School. After I piped in the Haggis and it was suitably addressed, the evening became an Open Mic poetry reading, with a number of students reading their original poems. I’m sure Burns would think this a fine way to celebrate his memory.

Saturday’s was more the usual thing of Americans unfamiliar with the Auld Scots Tongue doing their best to read poems in what in effect is a foreign language. (Burns wrote poems in Standard English too; one wonders why these aren’t read.)

However one young man had obviously done a great amount of work for his presentation; he made Tam O Shanter come alive with what amounted to a dramatic performance, in a passable Scottish accent.

This is my annual dual-duty gig, where my job is to entertain the Burns party in one half of the restaurant and also to entertain the other patrons in the other half, while the Burns poetry is being read. I use Scottish Smallpipes during these times because the big pipes would blast through the whole place.

Re: Burns supper last night?

From Wikipedia:

“The first supper was held in memoriam at Burns Cottage by Burns’s friends, on 21 July 1801, the fifth anniversary of his death; it has been a regular occurrence ever since. The first still extant Burns Club was founded in Greenock in 1801 by merchants who were born in Ayrshire, some of whom had known Burns. They held the first Burns supper on what they thought was his birthday, 29 January 1802, but in 1803, they discovered the Ayr parish records that noted his date of birth was actually 25 January 1759. Since then, suppers have been held on or about 25 January.”

The standard form is:
Welcome from Chair
Haggis piped in, addressed and toasted
Meal
Immortal Memory
Toast to the Lasses
Reply for the Lasses
Recitations and Songs (often interspersed with speeches)
Vote of Thanks

One nice bit is if you address the haggis you get a nice dram of whisky (which you usually down in one).

Re: Burns supper last night?

“Hey Tuttie Tatie” - the tune for “Scots Wha hae” and, also, much slowed down, for Lady Nairne’s “Land O’ the Leal”: very old indeed! As it says on Wikipedia: “Hey Tuttie Tatie” (also “Hey Tutie Teti” or “Hey Tuttie Taiti”) is a traditional Scots air. Its age is unknown, though it is reputed to have been played by the army of Robert the Bruce before the Battle of Bannockburn in 1314 and during the Siege of Orleans in 1429 (when France and Scotland allied). We finish our local Burns Folk Club night with singing that and a couple of other songs.

In fact there are a lot of tunes used by Burns which don’t end on the tonic: also starting and finishing on the dominant, “A Man’s a Man for A’ That”, some that end on the third note of the scale, some on the second. (And I have a friend who just has to round everything up to finish on the tonic, which does my head in!)

As for the range, Burns played the fiddle, and lot of the tunes were composed for fiddle: the range taxes some singers too in quite a few of the songs!

As for the “all male” thing, it’s tradition I suppose, as many of these clubs were formed even before women got the vote. Just checked with my husband, and one of the clubs he goes to was established in 1889: the other one is a comparative juvenile having started in 1959. While I’m all for equal opportunities, I take mine by going off with a female friend to a very nice concert at Celtic Connections while himself is at said all male Burns supper!

Re: Burns supper last night?

A few thoughts about Burns Suppers and the like….

When I left Inverness, I noticed that there was a much greater interest in all things Burns down South as opposed to in The Highlands. There is actually quite a difference in language and culture up there and it’s not just Gaelic although that does have a big influence.

However, we did have Burns Suppers too and I went to my first one when I was twelve at my local village hall. It was quite a dry affair (In more ways than one for me, considering my age) although I certainly made sure that I had more than my fair share of food. So, much so, that I had an upset stomach in my French class the following day and was sent home. I remember having to get off the bus to be sick somewhere near Culloden Battlefield but a very lovely “clippie” (who we all fancied) took great care of me.

Anyway, I’ve always had mixed feelings about Robert Burns and his “Suppers”. Although I enjoy many of his songs and poems, I can’t really be bothered with all the pontificating, boring speeches, and some of the other traditions associated with such occasions.

As regards the man himself, it seems that he can be “all things to all people” or whatever suits the occasion whether the supper is held at Holyrood, a university club, a church social, or even one’s local folk club. However, by all accounts, he was probably no better or worse than many men of his time. Certainly, a “flawed” individual and a “Ladies’ Man” who would likely have fallen foul with the “Me Too Movement” if he was here today.

As for Malt Whisky, I have become a much more moderate drinker in my advancing years but I still enjoy a couple of drams. :-)
