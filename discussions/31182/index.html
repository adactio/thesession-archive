The yoops and whelps in the Ciaran Tourish clip are a part of the tradition, as far as I am aware - a combined expression of music-evoked ecstasy and encouragement to the player. You’d have to be a prize grouch to disapprove of such things. Somehow, I don’t think that this audience needed any prompting.

Frankie Gavin’s invitation to his audience to make noise sounds a bit like a subtle mockery of (or, perhaps, an expression of his frustration at) their restraint. If he succeeded in encouraging audience members to release their hitherto suppressed ejaculations, then good for him. But the results of trying to elicit artificially enthusiastic responses from, say, a middle-class English audience (a kind I am quite familiar with) can sometimes be embarrassing; to quote one or other of The Two Ronnies, best let sleeping logs die.

Anyway, getting away from that torrid topic, if I’m playing in a pub I don’t regard anyone else in the place as an audience. Mostly they haven’t come to listen to me (and, to be honest, I haven’t come to play for them). They have come to spend ludicrously-huge sums of money on beer in a place where they can shout, swear, relax, be politically-incorrect and tell lies to their mates, and good for them. I’m here with my mates because we love it and the landlord thinks we add a certain ambiance. If they listen to us and they like it, jolly good. A bonus all round. If they go home thinking that the pub had a good atmosphere but don’t quite put their fingers on why, great. They’ll come back next Friday and keep the landlord’s coffers full and the landlord will continue to ply us with beer. If they ignore us and shout all night, well they’ve paid handsomely for the privilege and good luck to ‘em. If they clap along or get us to do Whiskey In The Jar, groovy (as long as they don’t expect it all night). Generally, we get a reasonable amount of respect for what we’re trying to do, and that’s good, but it’s a by-product only and we can’t expect it or demand it. If the pub don’t suit you, there’s always your kitchen. Gettin’ all precious about it sounds a bit, well…English…

Re: Audience participation

I read an article last year (sorry I can’t for the life of me remember what it was called) about the unspoken rules of an Irish trad session. For example, if there is a member of the audience that has an instrument with them that doesn’t usually play with the group- they must stand aside and listen until a musician that is part of the session asks them to join. Joining the group without an invite is subconsciously considered rude.

I for one, do not appreciate randomers coming in and out of the pub singing solos or asking to borrow instruments to play solos without being invited by one of the musicians.

I am aware that this is slightly off-topic, but I think the same rules apply to people that aren’t part of the session shouting and yelling. If they are invited or are part of the group- of course they can. Otherwise, a sidewards glare is in order.

I was a participant in a concert (at least that’s what it turned out to be) on Monday and the “act” before our dance side was a newly put-together group of individuals who were each fine musicians, but one of the fiddlers thought it would be a good idea to get the audiance to clap heavily along with the band. This is why most bands don’t do this, not because of any sense of style or “what is right”, but for sheer practicality.
