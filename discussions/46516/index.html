There were times when I thought I should have cringed at some of my previous posts - AND - I’m a good deal older than you, but I don’t really care now. Too old to worry. As they say: It is what it is…..or rather: They are what they are.
Yes, interestingly, session tune repertoires do change over time and I’ve noticed that, although each session obviously has its own repertoire, some new tunes make their way into the repertoire of many sessions. Maybe that’s just in one area, say, London, but I’ve wandered off to the occasional session outside London and they seem to be playing some of the same ones (that weren’t previously in the common session repertoire.) Hundredth Monkey effect? Who knows. Probably just tunes off popular albums at that time, which are good and/or interesting tunes.
At the BHT in SE London we, or rather the fiddle players, seem to play some Paddy Fahey tunes. A few from Gerry Harrington/Eoghan O’Sullivan, maybe also things like the Cat’s Miaow, Con Cassidy’s, etc but then you get ancient neanderthals like me still perennially battering away at The Dublin Reel, Trim the Velvet, Over the Moor to Maggie, Junior Crehan’s, The Boy in the Gap, Spórt, Connaghtman’s Rambles, etc.
TBH, I wouldn’t worry about changing fashions regarding tunes. Just play what you know. In fact, it’s always nice when someone pulls out an old tune that hasn’t been aired for a while - as long as it’s a good one.

Re: What’s being played at sessions these days?

Jeff, in our case, “isolated playing” meant playing on JamKazam. JK works reasonably well if everyone has the gear, isn’t too far away, has at least some modicum of technical savvy, and is a die-hard enough player to put up with it not being quite as good as being in person. Well, you can imagine that finding people that meet all of those criteria wasn’t the easiest thing, and we only had 8 players in our group of 30+ regular session players that were willing to do it regularly. So we had 18+ months of playing with some subset of the same 8 players, twice a week… Just to keep from going insane, we worked on keeping our repertoire fresh, be both revisiting old tunes that we hadn’t played in a dog’s age, and by bringing new tunes into the fold. I would say I learned more new tunes in the last 2 years than I did in the 5 years prior to that combined! (And even though one of my sessions is happening in person again, we’re still playing on JK weekly too!)

I think what Gobby was maybe saying was that, often times, people’s individual repertoires are driven primarily by their local session repertoire, and that being isolated at home would drive people to find more diverse sources for the tunes they’ve been learning. (Although, isolation isn’t a vacuum these days, and we certainly still have plenty of communication with our session mates).

Nice to see you resurface, Kellie! As I mentioned, people’s repertoires are often driven by their local sessions. Do you have players around you? They would be good people to talk to about what the latest and greatest tunes might be, because they’re the ones you’re most likely to be playing with, and their answers would likely be pretty different than anything I might be able to tell you…

Re: What’s being played at sessions these days?

I think it’s often a case of “The more you learn, the less you know” as far as session tunes are concerned.
We can all become very comfortable in our local sessions but find that our repertoire may be quite limited when we move or visit elsewhere.

Personally, I’ve given up on lists or worrying too much. There are some sessions which I just wouldn’t join especially those with very new tunes which are unique to the players themselves. They may be part of a band and the material is either quite esoteric or even self compiosed.

Mostly, however, it’s usually a “half and half” situation. So, if you are reasonably confident and competent(for the situation) with your playing in general, there’s no harm in just “sitting out” those tunes you don’t know. If it’s busy, of course, you shouldn’t take a seat at the expense of the more regular players.

In time, you will learn the tunes which interest you. Either by ear, osmosis, or the dots if you can identify their names. There’s no real rush really and I think it’s a more natural process to take your time.
