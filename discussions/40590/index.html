Re: Delrin Plastic Border Pipes

Hi, go for it is my advice. Delrin is the material of choice for pipe band chanters world wide. It’s pretty much identical in density to African Blackwood and is easy to turn accurately making it ideal for wind instruments.

I will open this can of worms here and now for the fun of it and you can watch it run for the illumination it may or may not bring you.

It is a fact that the material a bagpipe is made out of makes absolutely no difference to the sound of that instrument. The only parts of a bagpipe that need and indeed ought to move with the pressure of the air passing through it are the reeds and the bag. The bag as long as it is airtight and doesn’t deform under pressure is neither here nor there. The reeds are critical, no matter how good, a pipe with bad reeds it will sound like you’ve just stood on the cat.

What matters after that and equally to it is how well formed the pipe is, i.e. what the dimensions, bores, sound holes, etc are doing and how well the maker has placed them. If these are out even a good reed will sound bad.

People, and I am mentally naming them already, will most likely jump in here and now to deride the idea that plastic can sound as good as the magic stick from the sacred tree that once held the washing line of Pipe Major MacWhoknowswhat. It can and it does, along as it is well and accurately made and the reeds are decent and properly set.

Take my advice or leave it, your choice. If these pipes are well made and have proper reeds you might as go for it. If you want to “upgrade” to a set in wood when you’ve got some cash you’ll be in that position some time or another and you’ll have gained a few years of valuable learning meantime.

For what it’s worth I’ve played pipes for 41 years now. I’ve also spent a couple of years working for one of the UK’s largest pipe makers. I started playing mouthblown smallpipes 15+ years ago and I’ve played bellows blown borderpipes for the last 6.

Re: Delrin Plastic Border Pipes

The material-effect-on-tone debate has gone on for years, and nothing much has been resolved.

From personal experience, I observed that the sort of wood a flute headjoint is made from has a clear effect on performance. Perhaps not “tone” as perceived by a 3rd party, but the way the flute played as experienced by the player, things like resistance, response, and so forth.

With Highland pipes, I owned 2 African Blackwood, 1 Cocobolo, and 1 polypenco/delrin sets from the same maker (Dunbar). I happened to own one of the ABW sets and the poly set concurrently, and when tested the bass drones were found to be indistinguishable in any way. The ABW tenors had slightly less volume than the poly tenors, but when measured were found to have slightly different specs. People who have owned identical-spec poly and ABW pipes from the same maker have found do discernible difference in sound or performance.

I owned ABW and poly Scottish Smallpipe chanters from the same maker (John Walsh) and I couldn’t tell any difference.

All my GHB chanters are poly- I wouldn’t play a wood one due to the fragility.

About makers, yes a so-called border pipe chanter has much more complex functioning than a GHB or SSP chanter, and it follows that it’s more difficult to make, reed, and play.

My chanters from Garvie, Moore, and Swayne all functioned the same, giving a full chromatic octave. Two of the three chanters had High B keys which I feel are great to have, allowing you to play a vast number of tunes you otherwise could not.

It’s a difficult enough instrument with a great chanter! I certainly wouldn’t want to start my border-pipe career with a chanter that didn’t function perfectly.

Re: Delrin Plastic Border Pipes

Sorry, “celticrunesinger”, but I think you’re making a rather fundamental mistake in your understanding of sound.

Guitars have bodies carefully made to resonate, the same with violins and other string instruments. Most of the sound that you hear from a guitar or violin is from the sympathetic vibration of the body of the instrument. Sound is a mechanical energy; touch one of these instruments and you can feel the vibration of the wood that imparts the energy to the air which we hear as sound.

Sound is not like, say, light. There is no sound particle that could pass through another material. For the sound to “get through” the wood the mechanical energy of the sound wave has to be passed to the wood, which then vibrates under that energy, and which then passes the mechanical energy to the air on the other side. Guitars vibrate, fiddles vibrate … a lot, and they’re nowhere near the volume of the GHB. This vibration is what becomes sound as the energy is imparted to the air. I’m sorry for belabouring this point, but the idea that a bagpipe is in any meaningful way making its sound by the vibration of the chanter is preposterous.

The chanters density, and material is, as you acknowledge an essential part of the sound, to agree that a chanter made from oak, or balsa or box or ebony or cocus Or delrin or snakewood will sound different (Or suitable) yet to deny the same is somewhat contradictory.
Either the material makes a difference, or it doesn’t.
These materials and their thickness , shape etc etc are all parts of the end product
Delrin has a totally different molecular structure to a naturally grown organic material such as ABW may well have a similar density but that is but one facet of the instrument.
A delrin chanter and an ABW chanter by the same maker, same design,exact same reed swapped over sound differently. A recording of the same through a set of speakers may well sound similar. To compare ,as one of the supposedly scientific tests does, the reproduced sounds and say the actual sounds are therefore indistinguishable is not science at all.
The fine detail of the reflections between a gold or silver flute may indeed be so minuscule as to be indistinguishable to most people, does that mean they sound the same? No ,physics says they will respond differently . Yes it’s all shiny metal……. so it will sound the same?
A wooden chanter is just a wooden chanter….. and sounds the same as a delrin chanter? This is verifiable nonsense. The differences might indeed be too small for most to notice , but they are fundamentally there despite that.
Makers know, by experience, that different woods respond and sound differently and physics of musical acoustics supports that .
delrins tone is much harder than boxwood, harder than cocus, ebony and even the now standard commercial timber ABW, a dalbergia, a rosewood ,which is now by the way a restricted wood and needs cites.
I foresee a lot more delrin pipes becoming common, but nothing compares to old growth cocus wood IMO. Though I’m very fond of box and other native fruit woods fortheir tone.
To suggest that a chanter made from box would, or should sound the same as an exact copy in delrin is frankly untenable. The difference does matter.
