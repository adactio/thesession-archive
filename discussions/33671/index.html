Re: The tune that gets your blood up?

Through random behaviours of mine over time, I have tumbled to a way of telling which tunes I like most (or more), and which I like least (or less).

These behaviours involve leaving the session for shorter or longer periods of time to visit the gents, bar or smoking area and sometimes to linger in the last two areas to chew the fat with the pub’s intellectual codger proletariat.

The session is audible in these places, not all that loudly or clearly. And every so often I hear a tune that makes me think, “Oh * , I wanted to be in there playing that one…”, and every so often I hear a tune that makes me prefer to avoid eye contact with those who delight in telling me that Irish music is dirgey/all the same/sounds like evil spirits on a gridiron/other, because it gives me insufficient wherewithal to contradict them.

It would seem straightforward to conclude that tunes in the first category are ones I like and ones in the second category are ones I don’t, and this is true to an extent. However, the acoustics of session sounds round corners can deceive. In my experience, they tend to do so - forsooth - by making a tune sound better or at any rate more interesting than it actually is. More than once I’ve headed hastily back to a session, drawn by what sounded like snatches of an amazing beautiful melody hitherto unknown to me played with piquant sensitivity and a subtle backbeat, to find some familiar tune going through the mixer that never made me stiffen the sinews, summon up the blood and imitate the action of a tiger :-(:-(

Re: The tune that gets your blood up?

Of course it’s hard to give you a definite answer that is consistent over time. As for listening, my temporary favourites are usually tunes that drift between the modes, like Chief O’Neill’s, Red Tom Of The Hills, The Collier’s Reel etc. etc.

As for playing, the tunes I enjoy most are usually those rather simple but effective tunes in Gmaj that kind of play themselves on the flute -- without ever starting to bore me while doing so. Currently that’d be Dominic’s Farewell To Cashel, for example.

But if you really want me to name the one and only Übertune (both to play and to listen to), I think I’d decide for The Job Of The Journeywork.
