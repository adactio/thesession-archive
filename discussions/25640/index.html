Re: Turn recorder into whistle?

The more upmarket whistles get, the closer they get to being recorders. The only difference between a reverse-conical-bore whistle and a recorder is the fingerholes. The tone is identical.

So if you drill new fingerholes, you can turn that Dream into a very good whistle, and if you drilled new holes in a Copeland whistle you could make it into a comparably good recorder. But in both cases it would only work if you filled the old holes and made a completely new set. You would also need to know exactly what you were doing, and expect to throw several instruments away before you got the spacing, sizing and undercutting right.

I wouldn’t do either.

“I think plastic recorders should be banned”

Only the very worst are problematic (the sort of thing the Early Learning Centre sells). The ordinary entry-level Yamaha and Aulos recorders are a far better quality product than a Generation for about the same price.

Recorders are better value than whistles at every price point - to get a whistle that works as well as a Dream you would have to spend twice as much. So there may well be a business opportunity there in customizing undrilled Dreams to be whistles. But not as a one-off project.

As pointed out, the physics of the recorder is a bit different to the physics of a whistle.

In this context, the point of difference is that a recorder always plays in the first register. Reason? To avoid the timbral differences. So to do this, the angle of the cone is critical and the fingering becomes very complex after the first octave. The recorder system is a fairly recent development as far as woodwinds are concerned.

The downside of this is that if you drive a recorder into the second register, it will not be in tune - it is not intended to do this.

Re: Turn recorder into whistle?

“In this context, the point of difference is that a recorder always plays in the first register. […] if you drive a recorder into the second register, it will not be in tune - it is not intended to do this.”

That’s complete nonsense. Recorders all overblow, and sometimes go up into the third register. The fingering system is designed to play accurately in tune all the way to the top, which is why recorders are usually far *better* in tune for the high notes than whistles are.

The difference is very audible on the high B, which is an octave overtone on both instruments. On the whistle the tuning is done by closing one hole, period. The pitch is determined only by the length of tube from the fipple to the second fingerhole, with some end effects. This only works right if you design the bore profile very carefully. On a recorder you use an odd-looking fingering which is designed to tune each vibrating half of the instrument separately - the two halves couple and bring the whole thing into tune, which allows greater accuracy and a wider dynamic range without the pitch shifting.

Try playing “The Old Grey Cat” on both. It takes an expensive whistle to get that high B in tune, as it will be on a basic Aulos or Yamaha descant with very little effort.

“[recorders] seem to me a kind of dead-end exhibit in a museum”

There have been far more innovations in recorder design over the last 20 years than there have been for whistles. Paetzold square basses, Coolsma’s hand-made delrin instruments, the Tarasov-inspired “Modern” recorders, the Helder “Harmonic” recorders, Craig Carmichael’s odd keyed things, reconstructions of historical fingering systems that we’ve only just come to understand. Breukink’s “Eagles” are the latest in this line. Summary at the end of this article:

http://www.recorderhomepage.net/torture2.html

Re: Turn recorder into whistle?

Just joined, so I don’t know if this discussion is still going. But since I make recorders and whistles, I though I might share some information.
Classical (conical) recorders do not sound like whistles since the harmonics are different, with strong harmonics in the second and third h. register. Cylindrical whistles and recorders both favor a strong basic and thus sound somewhat similar. But the window and air flow over and under the labium are different. A properly built recorder cannot be overblown into the upper register. Etc.
If anyone wants a relatively inexpensive alternative to one of Adris, then I would recommend picking up a Hohner Modern Line recorder (with German fingering), designed by Herbert Paetzold who taught me recorder making. (Ebay in Germany.) This is what to do: drill the fifth hole to 8 mm and close the fourth to about 6,5 with a few layers of nail polish. This will give you a clean F# and G. Keep the thumb hole since this allows easy crossing to the upper register and provides several alternative fingerings. If you find this too complicated, drop me a line. Just remember: wood sound betters, why the Sweet whistles are so good (and expensive).
