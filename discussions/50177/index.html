Re: Clareen open-back banjo (I’m considering an Oyster model)

Hiya, Brian! Welcome to thesession forums!

I would say that in general, trying to play a resonator banjo as an open back will be significantly less comfortable simply because of the flange, which can be a sharp edge cutting into your lap. That is, unless you remove the flange as well. That’s not possible on some banjos, because the brackets sometimes use the flange in place of the bracket shoes. It looks like the new Oyster models do that. (I have an old Oyster model which has regular bracket shoes, but the flange is individual “petals”, so removing the flange is more work).

There are exceptions, as you’ve mentioned, and the Weymann “Megaphonic” rims are a good example. Those banjos are very comfortable played with or without a resonator, since the brackets run through the rim, not on the outer edge of it, providing a smooth surface on your lap. Although, some people like the outside brackets because they can stabilize the banjo on your lap. My New Paragon Artist banjo sits reasonably stably on my lap with the resonator on, at least until I’m wearing dress pants, and then it will twist in my lap as the neck pulls the left side down…

I would consider possibly talking to Fintan about specifically making you an open back banjo, which I would think would knock some of the price off as well. They could potentially make you a custom Oyster, with regular brackets and shoes, and no resonator…

Best of luck in your search for the right banjo!

Re: Clareen open-back banjo (I’m considering an Oyster model)

Well, a resonator is going to focus the sound more, and it will give it a bit more complexity in tone. This, of course, is potentially at the cost of your ability to hear yourself.

That’s one of the things I really like about playing open back banjos, because you often can hear yourself much better than with a resonator. My “New Paragon Artist” (where ‘new’ means a restructuring of the original Paragon tone system, so 1930’s as opposed to 1910’s-1920’s, so new-er, but not exactly ‘new’) has holes in the side of the rim that lead into the vertical holes in the rim that reside right under the tone ring. This actually acts like little sound ports, so that you can hear more of what the instrument is doing. I had an older Paragon before, and I thought it sounded great, but didn’t like how it sounded to me when I was playing it.

A couple of things to keep in mind. First off, the trend over the last 20 years or so has been toward brighter, crisper sounding banjos. Here’s a recent thread that might be of interest: https://thesession.org/discussions/50133. An open back banjo is more prone to sounding “plunky”. But the other thing to keep in mind is that setup has a lot to do with how a banjo actually sounds. So something like an open back Oyster model can likely be tweaked to get you exactly the sound you are looking for. You might make the head a little tighter without the resonator, for instance. And the Clareen folks tend to lean toward that clean, bright tone, so it’s likely that it would come set up in a way that follows that trend.

And after all, one of the joys of banjos is that if you don’t like the sound, you pull out a wrench and screwdriver and start tweaking things until you like it better!

Re: Clareen open-back banjo (I’m considering an Oyster model)

I have a 20-year-old Oyster and have used it both with resonator on and off in the past. To me it’s louder and brighter with the res on, and quieter and more mellow with it off. As mentioned earlier, the older Oysters with individual “petals” for the flange do make it awkward to go back and forth readily for both modes, but do-able. A custom-made one would be cool!
Gold Tone make good banjos for the price -- a TB-250 was my first one, but I’ve never played their open-back. But my go-to banjo now is a JP, made by Jim Patton in the UK. These are great banjos, based on the old Weymann Megaphonic design, which provides the benefits of an open-back but also the volume one might like for a session. Unfortunately, thanks to Brexit, Jim stopped making them and I’ve only seen one used one listed in the past five years -- nobody I know wants to give them up!
