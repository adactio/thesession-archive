The Kesh at a Sesh?

Someone at your session pitches up with the Kesh Jig, but not all the musicians in the circle join in.

Of those who don’t, there may be someone who actually doesn’t know it, (albeit that’s unlikely). More likely however, there will be one or two who do know it well, but disdain to play it, on the basis that it’s worn out and daggy. We’ve all seen the signs – rolling the eyes, setting instrument down on the table, checking for messages on smart phone etc. I don’t hold with this attitude. A good axiom at sessions is to always join in if you know it, but sit out if you don’t.

But what about the rest of the musicians that do join in – (albeit with varying degrees of enthusiasm). Do they listen all the way through the first iteration before joining in? They do not - they join in as soon as they recognise it, usually after one or two bars have been played. They are able to do this, because there really is only one version of the tune. Sure, they won’t all be playing it precisely in the same way, but it will be close enough not to cause any issues.

So if as I say , there is really only one basic version of this tune, why then do we need 33 versions of it (at the last count) on this site?

This is unhelpful as you can end up losing sight of what the basic tune is. Better to post the basic notes and let others experiment with their own ornaments. If the basic tune is already on the site, we don’t need to know how every individual chooses to play it

I’d still recommend listening for how the tune is being played before jumping in - unless the person starting it is so new that they need help to be able to carry it through the first AABB.

I remember once early on when someone started Out on the Ocean, and as it was one of the few tunes I knew then, I eagerly jumped in, only to realize most of the way through that they were playing it in a rather different setting, very beautifully, and I was not improving it with my poor playing of my understanding of the usual setting. Yes, I’m sure that is the case but there’s no need for most of them to be posted, in my opinion.
Many musicians can be inventive and have their own styles of playing but that is often unique to themselves. They may not even play a tune exactly the same way themselves on different occasions.

Personally, I prefer to learn from the basic tune…..not necessarily the “bare bones” though which is something a little different. Usually, I end up playing it in my “own style” anyway such as it is.
If I do come across a slightly different setting I like or am particularly inspired by a certain player, I may try to emulate this but I still “mostly” end up sounding like myself. :-)

Sometimes, I am presented with slightly different settings containing ornaments and variations for a “performance” and I’m obliged to follow this. However, in a session situation, I usually just like to adapt “on the hoof” if I hear somebody playing a tune slightly differently. Normally, I wouldn’t be seeking out a copy of/or atempting to learn a slightly different setting in these circumstances.
