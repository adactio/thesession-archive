Re: The session leader

This thread seems to have a deceptive premise. I thought the question was whether or not there were any unpaid sessions in Ireland, but saint seems to really want the thread to be a ‘paid session’ vs. ‘unpaid session’ debate.

I can’t speak for paid session hosts in Ireland, but as someone who gets paid for hosting sessions here in SF I’ll add this: I coordinate the rotating session hosts at our local, and I attend all the sessions whether I’m one of the paid session hosts or not. On the nights that I’m not being paid I come for the exact same reason I show up on the nights I am being paid -- a love for the music. Because the amount being paid is so nominal I doubt any of the other hosts would agree to do it if they didn’t have a love for the music as well. But if you were to remove that nominal payment for session hosts, the session would probably die.

I think this is the realization the publican had when he decided to pay session hosts. It doesn’t bring in throngs of people, so he isn’t doing it because he thinks he’ll make money hand over fist. As a matter of fact I think he actually loses money on many nights. The reason he continues to offer the money is because he too loves the music and wants it in his pub.

So if paid sessions contribute to the longevity of the session -- what harm is there in that? By my experience it doesn’t change the reason musicians are playing it, and most of the paid sessions I’ve been to in Ireland, (and in our local pub,) are much better for it. I’m not saying unpaid sessions are bad, and I’m not saying paid sessions are better than unpaid ones -- I’m just saying there’s nothing wrong with either.

Re: The session leader

In case any of you get me wrong, I’m not against the principle of paid anchor persons/core musicians for “regular” pub sessions or when musicians have been “invited” to play on a particular occasion. In general, as long as the those in charge are flexible (within reason) and “facilitate” some variety while welcoming(again within reason) good visiting musicians, these paid sessions are a good thing and ensures that music is always guaranteed.

Sneetch says “a lot of publicans prefer to book bands/session leaders and may not want random people off the street”
This is true but in Scotland and Ireland, many publicans do not mind and even many so called “session pubs” will welcome extra sessions outwith the normal times. Many of these impromptu sessions turn out to be the best.
I do agree that these may becoming less common as publicans now “expect to pay” and everything is compartmentalised, e.g Thursday is session night, Friday has a live band, Saturday..football and Sundays dominoes (All random examples). So, you’ll get something like “It’s a Thursday night the fiddlers come in” and so on.

I’ve also seen situations at recent Scottish folk festivals when three or four musicians, quite often students from a folk music course or similar, are paid to have a session in one the town /village pubs to “make sure something happens”. However, the frequently just do their own thing so all the other players, singers or whatever end up starting off their own sessions either in another corner of the pub on in different premises althogether.

So, while “paid sessions” can be a good thing…even necessary in some circumstances, I’d argue that you still need to allow and even encourage other types of arrangement. Also, musicians shouldn’t feel unable to participate under these circumstances. In Sandy Bells and many other Edinburgh pubs, professional players would (and still do) frequently sit down and join the regulars for a tune or start something off themselves if nothing was happening. Of course, they shouldn’t be expected to do this but, if they feel like it, why not?

Re: The session leader

Same in NJ Al, for most. The pub owner will often hire two people who are dominant good players, so the music stays on track… and also, since they advertise music, that in case no one else shows up, there will indeed be music. Generally they are not paid a whole lot, and are expected to keep the music going non-stop…. no talking breaks, the rest of the crew can, but if there is too much of a lapse, and that means a few minutes, they either get an ugly look or the jukebox is put on as a not so subtle hint. In addition, the hosts are as a rule expected to advertise the session, bring in both musicians and listeners, who in turn will theoretically buy enough food and drink to cover the hosts’ pay at very least, and hopefully to make a profit for the bar. It’s business from the owner’s end in most cases. If it is not profitable or doesn’t support itself at least it usually will end.

Some sessions, there is no host, but at least around NJ the better ones have two main players, there are a few exceptions though. The fact that these people do turn up at others’ sessions shows they are not just in it for the small amount of pay.

There is sometimes resentment though from other players. Some feel the turn to host should maybe be passed around, but then it gets sticky where you have to let some who can’t really run a tight ship or play well be the host, the music can fall apart a lot, the owner gets mad, people complain, and it ends. On the session I run, this idea has come up from a few people…. I would gladly leave it to someone else so I can relax and have fun sometimes, but I am expected to support the session, advertise it, hire a guest host, generate enough to get them paid, and record the sessions and produce a CD …which in turn supports the session with its sale. It is not a pub and the food and non alcoholic drinks are free (it’s BYOB, nothing is sold), so that gets covered out of what money we generate, we never want to charge for anything. They are not commercial CDs just in house for tune learning and a memory of a nice session. Besides, to do all this I have spent so much of my own $$ it would take over two years to break even. Not complaining, I love our session, but whoever wants to say it’s unfair that one or two get paid should look at the work the hosts often have to put into keeping it running. They do this, and the house gets all profits, even if it’s a great night, but they don’t really mind that, they don’t even think about it. People, even paid hosts, as a rule are there because they love the music.

I really feel the owner of a place needs to somehow compensate the musicians, whether in actual pay or in food or drink. They use the music to draw in customers, and should give back a little something. If it is in the form of two paid hosts who can put on a great show by themselves if need be, or free drinks and food….just something. Usually the unpaid players will get a drink free or all drinks free, or beer is put out in pitchers, just something. And if they don’t pay two to commit to be there no matter what, they can’t complain if one day no music is there at all if everyone had something else to do.
