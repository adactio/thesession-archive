Sounds like modern pop, which im not a fan of. *i still love her fiddle playing though* So now im thinking, “Maybe it’s just the Irish Fiddle, and the rest of Irish Traditional Music.?” I think i got it right this time. I’ve listened to a lot and a many, and i LOVE this stuff. But now i’m shopping in these American stores and can’t find anything by the Irish Fiddlers mentioned here. So now i have to shop online, which will take hours of searching, research, and listening, to make sure i’m getting the real thing. Hopefully i wont end up wasting too much money :/ -sigh- the things i do for music.

Re: A “Celtic” music disappointment.

The things you “learn” here. Amazing.

I’ll certainly buy that the dots are not the way to go. I’ll be happy to rail against midi files any time. But now we are “told” that recordings of fine players are not a useful resource. Oh come now! Check the wig glue; something is coming unglued.

No, recordings aren’t going to measure up to sitting in a session with fine players. No question. But for someone with a passion to learn this music living in an area where session playing not be the greatest or sessions may be hard to get to the recorded examples will certainly be useful. No question sitting beside fine players is the best experience short of tutorials with those same fine players. But to surround yourself with this music is not nearly as easy in Round Rock, Texas (a fine Baroque/Early Music festival there by the way) as it is across the pond or in New York, Chicago, Baltimore, the D.C. area and some other spots.

To get back to the OPs comments: Llig has it right (welcome back!!) we learn from our mistakes. And, we change in our perceptions as we become more sensitive to any particular style of music. So, those of us who buy recordings all end up with lots of things we would not have bought had we been further along. My solution to this is/was the public libraries which, at least where I live, have large and eclectic collections of recordings from which one can develop a sense of taste. The library is not likely to have much of a collection of Irish “pure drop” but it will probably have enough of the Celtic Women sort of thing to help you get past that interest.

Take a look at what recordings are recommended here. Check out on-line places to buy. Set aside some money for “trying things out,” and try to spend it wisely. CD Baby and lots of other sites allow you to purchase a single cut from a CD. That can make the money go much further. And do get to live situations as much as possible. Nothing will match it.

Good Luck

Re: A “Celtic” music disappointment.

Why is it such a hot potato that the music played in the vast majority of sessions is rubbish? It’s just a mere fact. And that the vast majority of players who self style themselves as players of this music are rubbish too. And by inference, the vast majority of people who post here will be rubbish. Some know, but a heck of a lot of ’em will be deluded. Even the vast majority of touring bands are rubbish, as illustrated by the fact that the vast majority commercially available recordings of this music are rubbish.

The only consolation is that because this music is so popular and widespread, there is still a lot of the good stuff kicking about.

So how does a relatively isolated beginner filter out the detritus? The simple answer is that they can’t. It takes an age of patience and learning about it. An age of making mistakes and false assumptions. an age of correcting mistakes and reassessing hard won conclusions. An age of growth. All things that have become harder for youths in this digital age of immediacy.
