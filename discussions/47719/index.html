What is an “average” musician?

I thought this might make an interesting discussion.

A little political aside, firstly(Forgive me, folks)..
The argument often used by the anti trade union media and Tory politicians etc is that the average wage of nurses, railway workers, and so on is actually quite high. However, “Average pay” is a misleading term as it implies that the majority or workers in a particular occupation (or in general) earn this. Most earn less than the “average”. The figure is always “skewed” by those who earn more than the average.

Now, in the context of this site…

Many members here might also have their own idea of what an “average” musician is.

I would consider myself “below average” in any case but I’d like to stick my neck out a little and suggest that most of us will surely fall into that category. Not because we are necessarily “bad” or not good enough players but because the number of exceptional musicians will always “skew” things and raise the average.

So, next time somebody tells us that your playing is “distinctly average”, we should maybe accept this as a compliment.
:-)

Re: What is an “average” musician?

“Average” can also mean (ha!) different things in different places/contexts. For example, in Ireland there is a strong network of Comhaltas branches and teachers, and your “average” trad musician in Ireland would likely have availed themself of at least some of that, or possibly even been dragged to lessons as a kid by parents before growing to actually enjoying the music. While trad is still a minority interest in Ireland itself, you would not be able to find many people who hadn’t been exposed to it at some point in their lives. Comhaltas also often gets criticized for homogenizing sound and technique, so “average” could be thought of as more of a stylistic concept rather than simply based on skill.

Outside Ireland, and especially outside the key diaspora areas (London, New York, Boston, Chicago, etc.), I’ve noticed a lot of people who “discovered” trad as an adult, having previously not really known much about it. Sometimes they’re musicians already but in other musics, sometimes not. An “average” musician in, say, Omaha, Nebraska (to pick someplace randomly) may be more likely to be one of these adult learners than someone who grew up surrounded by trad. This can mean a fairly different approach to, knowledge of, and feel for trad music and playing. So, someone could be quite “average” in their area but quite below or above average for another.

Depending on how you define “musician,” though, I do think the long tail of people who know a few tunes or dabble would actually drag the overall average down. And of course, defining “musician” upwards or with a minimum level of play to “count” would end up being a sort of “No True Scotsman” fallacy, where you could essentially say whatever you want to make an “average” musician that appeals to you.

Re: What is an “average” musician?

“if you just play a lot without much thought”

Good point, HappyCamper.
To me, what elevates someone’s music is that they have something to say with each tune. That is, they’ve taken time to feel whatever the tune is about, given it a good deal of thought, internalized that feeling or idea, and know what they want to express when they play the tune themselves.

Mind, it’s all too easy for us session players to fall into the trap of just collecting and regurgitating tunes for the sake of being able to join in. But we can each contribute much more when we put the time and thought into understanding each tune. The old boys and girls used to call this living inside the tune.

The other key aspect is being a good listener, as you’re playing, so you can synch with everyone else in the circle. Ideally, everyone else reciprocates.

Maybe I came of age in an era when that was the point of making your own music. It was certainly what was emphasized by the musicians who were kind enough to mentor me. So even when I was just starting out, I thought of interpretation as the fundamental reason for playing music, not an advanced, aspirational, and perhaps unattainable goal. Still do, even as my physical abilities decline with age.

Re: What is an “average” musician?

When it comes to receiving compliments about your playing from members of the public, I learned a long time ago, to take them all with a pinch of salt.
Many years ago, while rattling my Banjo at a remote country pub session, high in the Antrim hills ( last century 😉 ) a fairly tipsy bloke came up to me at the end of a set & said he wanted to buy me a drink because I was .. & I quote - “The best Banjo player he’d ever heard”.
Naturally I puffed my chest out with pride & accepted his offer of a pint.
However, when he came back with said pint, I asked him if he’d actually heard many Banjo players & he said - “No, you’re the first!” 😀
