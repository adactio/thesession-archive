If I had known that Pomme de Terre (and some other people) would be so offended by my misuse of German, I would have said: “Yes, Sir! Mr. Potatohead!” to Pomme de Terre instead.
I first heard about the Nazis and how evil they were in history class when I was a teenager and I could not believe what the teacher was telling us. When I got home, I asked my father about the Nazis and he confirmed what my history teacher told us in class as well as adding some details and information which the history teacher did not tell us.
Ever since then, it has always bothered me how (supposedly) human beings could be so inhumane and do such terrible things to other people. I can understand being angry and upset with other people but not to the point where I would want to do such terrible things to them.
My late father-in-law (who died last year) had an experience similar to Pomme de Terre’s aunt when he was an enlisted man in the Army in World War Two. He was in his late teens (he lied about his age when he enlisted in the Army) when he was ordered to help liberate one of the concentration camps. It was such a terrible experience for him that it haunted him for the rest of his life and gave him horrible nightmares.
My wife told me that after her father was discharged from the Army, there were many nights when he couldn’t fall asleep until he was completely and totally drunk. Sometimes he would disappear for days on end and they didn’t know where he was. This happened when she was a little girl and it is still having an effect on her. If I am a few minutes late getting home from work, she will call me on my cell phone to make sure that I am okay and am on my way home instead of trying to get drunk somewhere.
When my late father-in-law would get drunk, he was one of these mean and angry drunks who became loud and obnoxious. When we first got married, my wife was afraid to be around me when I was drinking beer because she was afraid I would start acting like her father did when he got drunk. Now that she has learned that drinking beer makes me mellow and relaxed, she doesn’t object to me drinking beer just so long as I do it at home instead of drinking somewhere else and then trying to drive myself home.
By the time I met my wife and started dating her, her father had been clean and sober and dry for several years and he managed to remain that way until he died last year.
My mother’s father didn’t go to Europe. Instead, he spent most of World War Two guarding one of the Aleutian Islands off the coast of Alaska from a Japanese attack which never occurred. When the Japanese bombed Pearl Harbor, my grandfather was the commanding officer of his local Army Reserve unit. When war was declared, my grandfather and his unit were promptly mobilized and sent to Alaska. This happened just before my mother’s sixth birthday. There never was a birthday party or celebration for my mother because her parents forgot about it. I don’t know how the rest of you feel about celebrating birthdays but when you are six years old, celebrating birthdays is very important. During the next four years, my mother and her family rarely got to see her father and they didn’t know where he was most of the time, either.
I am not telling these family stories because I want or need anyone’s pity or sympathy. I just want you to understand that I have family members whose lives were affected quite a bit by World War Two.
Approximately thirty years ago, when some neo-Nazis attempted to cause trouble by marching through Skokie, Illinois, I was hoping they would be denied a permit for their parade. I was disappointed when the ACLU (American Civil Liberties Union) intervened on the side of the neo-Nazis. I do know that if I was Jewish and.or a survior of the infamous concentration camps, I would object to letting these neo-Nazis march through Skokie.
Also, going by the crazy ideas of the Nazis on racial superiority and racial purity, if the Nazis knew the truth about just how mixed up my ancestry actually is, they wouldn’t hesitate to shoot me on the spot right here in my house and leave my body sitting here in front of my computer.
