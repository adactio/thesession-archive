↳ Posted by 5stringfool on December 30th, 2016.

Re: Name this tun please and is D whistle whata hes playing?

“I just found this fingering chart http://www.thewhistleshop.com/misc/fingering.htm and it says that D is the highest note. … whoever said high B is the highest note … ?”

You *can* play 3rd-octave D - and notes higher than that, if you really want - but it gets pretty shrill and the whistle is less forgiving than in the lower registers. Furthermore, the fact is that trad tunes with notes above 2nd-octave B are few and far between; most whistle players either steer clear of them or modifiy them so that the top notes are shifted into a lower octave*.

*2nd-octave Cnat and C# are not so difficult to play cleanly (although the former needs its own special cross-fingering), and there are a few tunes in whch they are played at their proper pitch:
https://thesession.org/tunes/3199
https://thesession.org/tunes/177
https://thesession.org/tunes/692#setting20955
https://thesession.org/tunes/21#setting12400

Tunes like https://thesession.org/tunes/74#setting12549 and https://thesession.org/tunes/196#setting196 played on the whistle, complete with all their acrobatics and no jumping down of octaves, are best left to those with complete mastery of the whistle.

↳ Posted by Arthur Gondolor on January 5th, 2017.

Re: Name this tun please and is D whistle whata hes playing?

It “sounds” Eb on the recording because the man is using another whistle. Use a D whistle and you’re fine, in which case the tune will sound G. Unless of course, you choose to play the third setting (which is notated in D), but you don’t have to, since your chart shows how to play the c natural (and by they way, if anyone wonders, that’s a note you MUST learn).

Summary:
On the recording, a Bb whistle is used, and therefore tune is played in Eb. By the same logic, if you use a D whistle, the tune will be played in G. This tune happens to have a setting that can be played with D fingerings (but as you’ll see, the melody won’t match 100% for range reasons).

Good luck (and have fun!)

↳ Posted by Arthur Gondolor on January 5th, 2017.

Re: Name this tun please and is D whistle whata hes playing?

It’s an Bb whistle (not an Eb whistle) but what he does matches the first G setting pretty much, something like this:

K: Gmaj
DG BG | dG B/A/G | FG AB | cF G/F/E |
DG BG | dG B/A/G | FG AB | cF G2 :|
|: ga/g/ fe | dB B2 | cA A2 | ed de/f/ |
g2 f/g/f/e/ |dB B2 | cA FG | AG G2 :|

Compare this with Setting #1:
K: Gmaj
DG BG | dG BG | FG AB | cE FE |
DG BG | dG BG | FG AB | cF G2 :|
|: g2 fe | dB B2 | cA A2 | ed de/f/ |
g2 fe |dB B2 | cA FG | AG G2 :|

Not too big a difference. As for the actual NOTES he’s playing in Eb, they are a third lower. So for instance every D is a Bb - which makes sense since he’s using a Bb whistle. Typing it out in Bb doesn’t make a lot of sense, since he’s effectively playing IN G, but if you’re desperate, here it is:
X:1
T:Mrs Ryan’s
R:polka
M:2/4
L:1/8
K:Ebmaj
B,E GE | BE G/2F/2E | DE FG | AD E/2D/2C |
B,E GE | BE G/2F/2E | DE FG | AD E2 :|
|: ef/2e/2 dc | BG G2 | AF F2 | cB Bc/2d/2 |
e2 d/2e/2d/2c/2 |BG G2 | AF DE | FE E2 :|

What IS different between this G (or Eb) version and the D setting you’ve chosen is that the B part starts in the higher octave (not the G, but the g), and that works best with the G fingering. In short, it’s a typical G tune for the whistle (or flute, or pipes) - it doesn’t go a lot higher than the high g, and it also makes use of the low D. Perfect. No wonder G is a common key for Irish tunes.

Arthur-you’re welcome, but I would add that that particular piece of fancy trickery is easy-peasy since you are already fingering the A note. The only slight difficulty is getting the 3rd finger to cover the rest of the hole to play the following F#. The trick I use, for what it’s worth, is to slide the finger across the hole as I add the 1st finger of the right hand. Keep in mind that the intonation doesn’t have to be spot-on; in fact it can sound kind of cool if it’s a little “blunt” as our friend Jim Dorans would say.
Learning to half-hole will expand what you can do on the whistle exponentially , so it’s worth a try.
