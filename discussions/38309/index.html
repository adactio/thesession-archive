Taking Music out of Schools.

I know this is another long post but I ask that you read it in it’s entirety if you intend to respond. Also, if you want to say something but not publicly, cause this is very controversial, feel free to personally message me. This is important to me so I would appreciate all the views and opinions I can get.

Back in 2011 when I was coming to terms with having dropped out of school for changing my major, I met a beautiful young woman on a flight to visit home. She noticed my instrument case and we immediately hit it off, as she was a music teacher. But she wasn’t teaching here, she was teaching in South America. She told me her story about how so many music programs were being cut close to home and other places she could go, and that it was difficult to find work in the States. She exclaimed that though it was difficult and times have been hard, that she had no regrets, and that she’s happy with her decision, because she can share the love of music with the next generation. This conversation was pivotal in my deciding to teach, so I took it for what it was and that was the end of it.

Now, I’m not into politics and government, and I’m surely not into conspiracy theories, but something bothers me that I can’t shake, and that is the fact of the cutting of music programs, everywhere. Being on an instrumental intermission, I’ve been getting a bit into Physics and Music History. As of late, I’ve come to the conclusion that music is vital to our existence, even though it’s not evident on the surface. I believe this because I’ve learned that Music connects us, while everything else separates us(the main idea of the discussion).

I realized this after having a heated debate with a coworker/friend of mine. The other night at work we were talking about racism, extremism, and terrorism. I was explaining to him what the “BlackLivesMatter” movement stood for and that it’s antagonistic and instigative to say that it might turn into a terrorist organization(as him and so many others have been saying). His belief is that any group, any clique, any faction, any formation of people that is exclusive, has the potential to become extreme and radical.

Now my friend, he is a conspiracy theorist. The poor guy is so confused about everything, and everything scares him, and this he admits. He’s always telling me crazy stories and all this anarchist stuff, so I have to take the things he says with a grain of salt. Arguing back with him I say, “I’m a musician, but I’m not extremist. I love music with all my heart, but if my kids wanted to be athletes, or something else non-musical, I would love them anyway. I wouldn’t force music on them, I would let them choose what they wanted, and I’m the most ‘extreme’ and ‘radical’ musician I know. So, your point is disproven and invalid.”

I turn up my nose and fold my arms, thinking I’ve won the debate, and he says, “But wait, music has no enemies, does it? No one is trying to take it away from you, no one is provoking you, no one is trying to change you, no one is antagonizing you. So you being a musician and not being extremist is an invalid argument.”(his eloquence is lacking, so everything is paraphrased. We argued for a couple more minutes until he made himself clear)

After realizing what he was trying to say, I had to admit, that he was right, because “If someone tried to take music away from me, I would lose my mind and destroy them”. I said it in good humor, but I know it’s true. Now, my friend may be as crazy as a cat with the devil in his mouth, but he was right about this. But what does all of this have to do with taking music out of schools?

During the debate, my friend said, and I quote, “Music has no enemies.” As I’m here thinking about this, I realize what exactly he’s been trying to tell me. Everything *does* separate us. Faith and Religion separate us. Government and Politics separate us. National boundaries separate us. Language separates us. Our societies, cultures, and races separate us. But Music, Music brings us together.

Even when we add religion, government, nationality, language and culture to music, it creates a barrier. But if we look past that barrier, and leave the music to itself, we can be reconnected. How is it that we can enjoy music of different languages, or different cultures and societies? Music that we don’t understand on the surface? Because music is the universal language, and we do understand it. And in it’s purest form, it can bring everyone together in Peace, Love, Unity, and Respect. We’ve all seen it.

So to bring it back around to the post’s title: Why, why, why in the world are our governments taking music out of school? All of this inspired by the Big question, “Why is there always enough money for war, but never enough money for education?”. Why attack the youth, the future generations? Do they not know that it could be the only thing to bring the world to peace? Or do they know, but don’t want peace? Or do they know but don’t want progression and evolution? Or am I just losing my mind and should lay off the Physics, Music History, and conversations with conspiracy theorists?
