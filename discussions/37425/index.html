Re: Seriously how difficult is this? - Matt Molloy The Masons Apron

I don’t think it’s the speed alone that’s impressive, as much as that honking tone he gets on the octave jumps, which is brilliant. It’s like he’s accompanying himself on another instrument in the middle of the tune.

Compared to other instruments I’ve played (mostly the fretted variety), working tunes up to session speed seems to come easier on the flute. Probably because the two hands don’t have to coordinate to play a note, as with fiddles and plectrum instruments. Of course getting anywhere near a level like Molloy’s involves a lot more technique than just how fast you can flap your fingers. He’s on top of the mountain, compared to someone like me, down here in the lowlands.

Re: Seriously how difficult is this? - Matt Molloy The Masons Apron

I am a flute player, and one who, when I first took up the flute in the late 1970s, tried to emulate Matt Molloy’s playing as best I could. I transcribed a number of his solos.

About how difficult it is to play like Matt Molloy, let’s face it, there’s only one Matt Molloy. There are so many things that go into sounding like that: tone, breath control, embouchure flexibility, intonation, rhythm, sense of style/idiom, precision of fingering, command of ornamentation, and he’s top-shelf at all of it. Then he goes beyond all of this to create great music.

There’s a great multiple-Grammy sax player that I listened to who, though technically impressive, left me cold. Another sax guy told me “that guy’s playing has the emotional depth of a mathematician doing an equation”. Someone can be a great player without being a great musician, in other words. Matt Molloy is both.

Or as somebody famously said “the musician must make the impossible, habit; the habit, art.”

Or as an old Irish fluteplayer told me “Dat Matt Molloy: dere’s no flies on him.”

Beyond playing great, Matt Molloy is often credited with coming up with a way of doing certain piping ornaments on the flute. As is usual with such things there’s probably somebody somewhere who did something like it before Molloy, yet no doubt it was Molloy to brought this piping-style fluteplaying to the forefront.

Re: Seriously how difficult is this? - Matt Molloy The Masons Apron

Actually as a flute player myself , ( the op ’s link is no longer available ) , for Matt to play the McGuire version of the Apron in G ( if that is what was linked ) would be a walk in the park. He’s done stuff more difficult, eg The Contradiction in A , and maybe CM Mulhaires and maybe the Moving Cloud.

I agree he is a genius, and for Matt, playing the Apron in front of a large crowd, solo, at full speed, while maintaining impeccable tone and breath control in G is easy.
McGolderick, Crawford, and others could do it too, as we see with the tremendous Orlaith McAuliffe, much more difficult in A, but she’s all over it.

From the man himself ( http://www.mattmolloy.com/interview-with-matt/ )
“But just to try those things, to see if it can be done, I messed around with those. I am still messing. I just worked away at it. Suddenly it’s not a problem any more. You have a whole line of flute players now that see this can be done, they’re doing that and some more.”

End of the day, Molloy is the one a lot of us see when we look up to the top of the mountain.
