How to categorize this music?

I’m a beginning fiddler and am discovering new music for inspiration. I’m a huge fan of a group called the Gothard Sisters -- in fact, I’d really like to model my sound after theirs.

However I’m having a hard time classifying them. They market themselves as “Celtic,” and my friend told me that their music sounded Scottish. I’m a complete novice at this, but my personal opinion is that although they play Irish-style tunes, they have a rather classical sound to them.

EXAMPLE 1 (trad song): https://soundcloud.com/the-gothard-sisters/the-road-to-lisdoonvarna

Do ya’ll agree with me that there is a classical style to this playing, or am I mistaken?

Also if you have time, I’d be interested in how you guys would categorize their original compositions (e.g. Irish, Scottish, Celtic, “modern,” etc.)
EXAMPLE 2: http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XjFNdGyegDA “Flying Sails”
EXAMPLE 3: http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eTUNeY5q8CQ “Three Coins”
EXAMPLE 4: http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pM3J7dLO5-4 “Willow’s Waltz”

Thanks for anyone who takes the time to do this. I absolutely love their music and would love to put a name to what I’m hearing.

Re: How to categorize this music?

Traditional music is, in my opinion, part of a living anthropologic body, shared and accepted by a community and obeying to some perpetuated rhythmic/harmonic values established by the same community, most of the time functional for the music´s purpose, like dancing. This music is Celtic inspired, it´s well performed rhythmically and in tune, but it´s not trad in my view.
What I think is that Celtic music has so much richness and deepness, with hundreds of amazing tunes, including modern compositions, and composing music make´s it evolve. Therefore new compositions must have a strong connection to these values, like ornamentation, interpretation, that in the end must be identified as part of that wider common ground. They have the aptitude to go further in trad, with some good workshops for technique and some sessions. It´s easy to criticize and, the final purposes of a critic are to gain more people to the cause of music, not to lose them!

Re: How to categorize this music?

Good to see lots of help along with the rejections.. :)

There is one other issue not mentioned: Many folks use tunes from various traditions (Scottish, Irish, whatever,) as a basis for their performances without ever intending to be traditional in the way they play those tunes. And since they draw tunes from the Celtic countries their music gets that name from where record stores store things, or from the fact that “Celtic” music is popular right now. They may make the decision that what they create speaks for them, or they (and those who support them) may be mostly interested in developing a large following that will bring in money. Liberace, who was quite a fine pianist often laughed at himself saying that his bad music made good money. And he was right. So are “Celtic Women” and in many respect the “River Dance” phenomena. Everyone reacts to this kind of thing differently. I kind of like the original River Dance, but never could manage Liberace or Celtic Women.

Folks who play in the tradition are often not going to like things that, in their view, are bending the tradition unacceptably. At least some here will reject the Transatlantic Sessions (recommend you give a listen Emma if you don’t know those recordings) as being too far outside the pale. Others will like those same recordings. All of those folks will check in here and almost any opinion can and will meet with objections from someone. But the good thing about all of that grumbling is that, once you (or perhaps they) get past the grumbling they are a gold mine of information; as indicated by the later posts above.

So Emma, don’t be put off by the grumbling and do listen to the suggestions. Even when I personally disagree with the comments I almost always find the suggestions of interest.
