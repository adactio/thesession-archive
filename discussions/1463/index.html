Handling disrespect of The Music with grace (or not)

This weekend a very good friend of mine (and bandmate) had a birthday party. A few of us brought instruments, including a couple classical and jazz musicians. When our band was playing a few tunes, my good friend invited his classical friend to play along on violin. She looked at the sheet music (my friend is still in the process of memorizing, as he is classically trained himself which is why there was sheet music present) and proceeded to say “This music never ends, it just keeps going around and around” in a very disdainful sort of way. It may actually be true, but it was the disdain that offended me. And she repeated the comment a few times, and then proceeded to play from the sheet music. It sounded terrible, not like a reel at all but like some sort of robotic music. She obviously was not familiar with or even remotely enamored by the music at all. I had to get up from my harp and go get a cup of coffee--I was so angry.
Of course I said nothing, because it was my friend’s party and I didn’t want to spoil the festivities and also this woman is older and more experienced musically than me, so any remark from me would have seemed disrespectful.
Maybe I am over-sensitive, but I care so much about the music and hate to see is trivialized.
Anyone else have a similar experience? How did you handle it? With grace? Or with fire?

Andee doesn’t say how competent the lady (?) in question was - just that she was older and more experienced. But it might have provided an insight as to whether she was as superior as she herself thought. However, most classical musicians that I’ve met are continually amazed at the memory retention of the average traditional musician. The usual comment runs, ‘If you take away the music score, I can’t play a thing.’ And, by and large, they are as respectful of our traditional discipline as we are of theirs.

Of course there are differences, both in style and approach, and I think these were demonstrated very well in a TV programme of a few years ago when Frankie Gavin met Yehudi Menuhin (Was it ‘River of Sound’?). Menuhin was slightly disparaging of Gavin and the trad style when he said that the playing was mainly confined to the first position. And then they played a hornpipe together, with YM reading from the music. Well, you would have thought that Mr Menuhin was playing with the bow up his ass, his interpretation was that stilted, jerky and totally without flow or feeling of any kind. It was wonderful to behold!

Anyway, to the point in question, the lady *was* totally insensitive and ignorant and clearly did not know her bass from her oboe (ouch!). I think you did right, in the circumstances, by ignoring her tasteless comments. I can only hope she might one day subscribe to this message board and be re-educated!

The funny thing, of course, is that this sort of thing happens in any group of people, in one way or another.

Ironically, we’ve lost the ability to take the music and run with it -- previous to about the so-called Romantic period, sheet music was considered the starting point and had little to nothing to do with what the musician actually played (that sounds familiar, doesn’t it?). In fact, many of the composers (especially those virtuosi who composed for themselves and other virtuosi) didn’t even bother writing down much of what they intended would be played, just getting down the basic chord structure and the basic melodic idea.

One might even say that the aural traditions such as ITM are one of the few places left where the great classical tradition of allowing the musician to freely interpret the music around a basic framework survives. :)
