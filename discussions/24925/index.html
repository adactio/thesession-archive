Rook - I don’t think that makers of instruments should be given a free ride, but I do think that one should have some patience with someone who’s in this sort of business. They’re generally a one person operation, perhaps employing a part-time assistant who’s learning the trade, they generally don’t have any administrative help or the resources to employ any, and as such they’re pretty vulnerable to sudden shocks.
Consider:
If making pipes of decent quality were an economically viable proposition, there would be an analogue to the Martin Company cranking them out, or at least there would be a maker with a proper business model and some sort of administrative help. Since we don’t really see that, we should assume that most pipe makers will be more or less hand-to-mouth operations, and thus they will not have the resources to adequately handle emergencies (health, personal, financial, or other sorts). Since those emergencies come to everyone sooner or later, we should assume that every pipe maker will at some point in their career be in the position that Bayley is apparently in now - behind on orders due to some unforeseen circumstance and overwhelmed by the business.
This being the case, it seems to me that the OP’s post was unwarranted, especially since it’s clear that no queries had been made in knowledgeable circles before the posting was made. That seems to me quite irresponsible, and more likely to disrupt Bayley’s business than to facilitate the delivery of the pipes in question, so on the whole, to me, the original poster looks like an ass.

On and on it goes.

I am an instrument maker.

You know, when you do this kind uf ultru-niche specialist stuff, there’s essentially just yourself. You do it because you have a passion to do it. One cannot expect any help because there’s no money in it. Life? Yes.

So when you get sick or problems with sources of woods, metals etc .. if your kid breaks a leg .. or any one of the infinite little surprises life brings .. all of this can keep you from your passion.

What it boils down to is: if the player wants the instrument made (there are no customers in this game) THEN the customer wants it or not. From teh instrument maker’s perspective - it will get done as the player needs, but the player has not bought the maker’s time - only gotten a lend of his passion. The maker applies his passion and the thing gets done when it gets done. One makes one’s best estimates and they are always wrong.

It’s not like a factory where one has an organisation dedicated to reducing time-risk. An instrument maker must put the quality of the instrument above ALL things including time, otherwise the instrument is probably not worth having .. we’re talking about Uilleann pipes here .. I can’t think of any other instrument that demands such a broad range of pinacle skills, materials and lifetime dedcation. I don’t make them - simply not enough years left in my life to do it justice.

Perspective - there are some violin makers who are taking orders for people’s unborn children so that the instrument will be done sometime when the kid is in his/her teens or 20’s. They take deposits.

SO I don’t take deposits .. and I don’t obligate the player to buy when it’s done. Then again, I am not using Mamoth horn .. gold sometimes - which the player pays for and owns and can claim at any time.

I am currently delivering somewhere between 3 and 6 months later than estimate .. I am acutely aware of how this frustrates some players .. most tend to understand and will cancel or wait depending on their own circumstance. If it was possible to simply throw more hands on the job I would, but it’s impossible - who could I trust to produce MY work? And if I could? How could I claim it as my work?

One thing is for sure - I get every second I possibly can in my workshop practicing my passsion. The work might be slow sometimes, but it never actually stops.
I feel a bit regretful that there will be unfinished orders on my book on the day I die.

And, Yes, I have a couple of “players” who are on a list of “never to be dealt with again”. I’ve learned to recognise the type and discourage their orders when I “smell a rat” .. they are often re-sellers and “entrpaneurs” .. I sugest they look into financial derivative products and leave music/life to those who are willing to create value instead of blood-suck teh good people round them.

Chris will have his own reasons .. But if you’re reading Chris - no matter how hard things get, you will never need the money from this low-grade kind of human - it always comes with a price higher than the cash. You just can’t afford it.

Are instrument makers insane? Could be that’s part of the geography ;) as a player, you’re either in or out .. if your out .. well you have nothing to say.

And will anyone read this?
Probably not .. thje thread’s too long and old. But my passion demanded this got said. Now I can get back to work :)

Re: Something you should know about Christopher Bayley…

. I ordered a set of mouth blown border pipes from him, back around 2002. It took about 9 months and almost $1,400, not including shipping, for me to get the pipes. I was never so excited in my life, when the Fed Ex truck rolled up. I took the pipes out and assembled them, and they were very obviously poorly made, even to the eyes of a novice. The three drones were roughly made, almost as if they had been carved, rather than lathed and they were rough inside and had little burrs and splinters in them. The bore was rough and uneven and the drones were not in tune with the chanter, no matter how I tuned them. The chanter was just a slightly smaller version of a highland pipe chanter, made of inferior looking wood and the reed was just a very easy cane reed. The drone reeds were standard cane reeds, made for highland pipes, most likely Pakistani cane reeds, not matching or even made of good cane. The pipes sounded like pure crap and they were never tunable. I was furious and I called him internationally at about $2.00 a minute and demanded an explanation. His response was just about as scandalous as his work. He said the reason they were so poorly made, was that I had put too much pressure on him, to make them quickly ( remember, 9 months to make one set of pipes, by a supposed master maker) and that he had been forced to buy inferior “bits”, to speed up production. I demanded a refund, to which he replied “I will just send you a proper set of reeds and better drones and a chanter, if you will just give me more time”. I knew he had my nads to the wall, so I agreed. He did finally send me some sawed off E Z Drone reeds that kind of worked and sent me a cheap border pipe chanter, but the drones remained as they had been, when I received the pipes and they were never in tune with the chanter. After about two more years of demanding a refund and being ignored, I finally put them up for sale, with full disclosure, for about 1/4 of what I paid for them, hoping a real pipe make might want them as a fixer upper project. A year later, I ended up selling them to a German woman, who wanted to try to fix them and use them in her band. Long story short, do not do business with Christopher Bayley, assuming he is not in prison.
