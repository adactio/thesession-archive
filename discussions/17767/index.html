Dave Freshwater: Let’s set the record straight…

I’d just like to correct some of the blatant rubbish i’ve been reading on some of the boards here.

when i was looking for a quality instrument, i discovered dave living about 30 miles away from me. so i paid him a visit.

dave is one bloke working out of a (fairly large) garden shed, set up as a workshop. instruments can be seen in various stages of completion, along with repairs and special projects. to say freshwater instruments are “factory made” or mass produced is frankly bulls***.

piles of wood can be seen maturing in dave’s garage and you only need to spend a few minutes chatting to realise you’re dealing with a true craftsman.

i now have two freshwater instruments: a standard mandolin and an octave mandola. dave kept me informed about progress throughout the construction of both. i only took delivery of the mandola about a month ago and it was subject to a couple of weeks delay. however, and it’s a big however, you have to appreciate that we live in the far north of scotland and we had winter conditions up until a couple of weeks ago. the temperatures have been WILDLY fluctuating and this played havoc with glues etc. once again, dave kept me informed throughout.

sure, i could have pushed him harder. but why? he’s a decent man doing an excellent job on an instrument i want to cherish for a long time.

as for quality: i live in a community surrounded by fantastic musicians (they’re all much better than me), some of whom are recording artists. everyone i’ve shown the instruments to has commented on their obvious quality and great tone (improving all the time)

it may be worth pointing out that Ritchie Blackmore, a renowned perfectionist and guitar god, uses dave’s mandolas. dave doesn’t advertise this, but it’s there on the Blackmore’s Night web-site.

i have no connection with dave at all, other than being a VERY satisfied customer. i could add a great deal more about the fantastic service i’ve received from dave freshwater, but feel free to respond with any questions you may have.

Corsican Dave

Re: Dave Freshwater: Let’s set the record straight…

I’ve posted before about my Freshwater octave mandolin. Dave’s instruments are at the budget end of the hand-made instrument market and this is certainly reflected in the quality of the workmanship.

If it takes 6 months to make one of these instruments then it would be better to say so rather than create false expectations with an estimate of two months at the time of sale.

The biggest problem in my opinion is the lack of a truss-rod on Dave’s instruments. To compensate for this the necks seem to be very heavy. If you get a problem with the neck on one of these things there doesn’t seem to be any way of correcting it.

I agree with you that the timber seems to be pretty good and this results in a nice hand-built sound.

I’m selling my octave mando on e-bay shortly, I’ll be very surprised if I get back anything like what I paid for it.

I’ve got nothing personal against Dave, I’m sure that he’s a top-class luthier. I think that he’s aiming at the wrong end of the market and cutting corners that shouldn’t really be cut.

Re: Dave Freshwater: Let’s set the record straight…

I have two Freshwater instruments and another on the way. I am very happy with both. The action on the tenor mandola was a little high, but easily fixed by a local luthier. They are on the heavy side and the necks are thick but this makes for an instrument that is very stable in all temperatures and humidities. We play a lot of outdoor gigs and I rarely have to retune. Instruments that are hand made in a home workshop are subject to a variety of delays and this is part of what you can expect. If you don’t want that, you can buy an instrument made by a far eastern factory and get what you pay for. A cittern I bought from another luthier was held up when his wife had a baby just as it was due to be shipped. David Freshwater has had to deal with parts supply unreliability and extreme weather lately. I don’t mind the wait because I know he will not ship to me until the instrument is ready and I don’t want him to rush it.
