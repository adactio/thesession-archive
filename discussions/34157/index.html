I’m not suggesting that you should use a metronome (I don’t, and have never done that), but you have to find a way to (get enough hang of the tunes you know in order to) play full bars. Re: Another upload for you guys to trash

The pointy thing ^ in front of a letter (not a number!) makes it sharp.
The flat thing _ makes it flat.
the equals sign = puts it back where it was before the pointy of the flat thing showed up.
Big letters (not numbers!) are heavier than little letters, therefore are an octave lower, with the change occurring at ‘c’, thus
CDEFGABcdefgab
you can make the big letters even heavier by putting little weights on them like this
C, D, E, etc, and they go an octave lower still.
Or tying little balloons on like this
c‘ d’ e‘ f’ etc, and they go an octave higher still.

each time you add a weight or a balloon, it increases it by one octave, thus --

A,,, A,, A, A a a‘ a’‘ a’‘’ etc.

Re: Another upload for you guys to trash

You’re not alone - people (who aren’t musicians) rarely have a sense for rhythm. I’ve been to a number of birthday parties, gatherings etc. where there has been an element of singing. Everyone sings together, and whenever there’s a long note, you get to hear all variations of (for example) a dotted crotchet.

Try playing four notes (1-2-3-4) with even bow strokes. Same note, phrase, whatever.
Slur two notes: (1-2)-3-4. If you’re playing open strings, the slurred note should be exactly as long as the next two together. Long, short, short
Slur two other notes : 1 (2-3) 4 -short, long, short.
Slur two other notes: 1-2-(3-4) - short, short, long.
Slur three notes: (1-2-3)-4 - looong, short
Slur three other notes: 1-(2-3-4) - short, looong.

You can even make up phrases to get a feel for how long the notes should be. Hope this helps.

Arthur, I meant to say earlier, what’s meant by ‘turning the beat upside down’ (that’s not happening any more). If you’re playing a 1-2-3-4 rhythm with the accent on the ‘1’, and you slip out of time, you can end up with the accent on the ‘3’.

Another example would be a drummer laying out the same rhythm of 1-2-3-4, using [bass drum] / [rest] / [rimshot] / [rest], with the accent on the bass drum (because of its volume), then changing the patten to accent the third beat, you’d get [rimshot] / [rest] / [bass drum] / [rest] , which would be ‘turning the beat upside down’ and could be off-putting. The Stones did that in ‘Start Me Up’, right at the beginning, drummer coming in on a mis-cue. They decided to keep it in there instead of re-taking, and now it’s a little piece of rock history :)

Hi Arthur

When you’re doing something that doesn’t work, you have to change things up. I would agree. Find something you haven’t tried, such as lilting along to tunes, playing along with slowed down music, learning to play other kinds of tunes, going out contra dancing or set dancing, ditching sheet music, picking up a non stringed instrument or whatever else you haven’t tried yet. Stick with it for a month at least and see what it does for you.

I know Jim has put on his teachers cap and given you thumbs up to go ahead and pursue your Donegal style obsession (I say obsession in the best possible sense if the word - it’s good to be obsessed by something, to have a goal in mind and be motivated). I take issue with that. I think it’s what’s holding you back in getting musical phrasing - the flow Stiamh is talking about above.

Because Donegal music has mostly single bow strokes and because donegal music has mostly similar note lengths, that’s what you’re going for. But the result, it seems to me, is that you are connecting the idea of an 8th note duration with that of a bow stroke. And all your notes in your mind have the same value (except when you “accent the 1 and the 5”, which is a terrible idea). This is creating a hurdle because your bowing arm has gotten super good at this and so any attempt to figure out how not to be list at sea is hampered by your bowing arm’s habit of playing single notes.

One of the thing you need is to get a feeling for how notes add together to form phrases - and I think your playing amply proves that single bow strokes on equal length notes is not what is working for you.

Put the Donegal obsession on the back burner for 6 months - it’ll still be there when you get back. Add swing and slurs to your playing - not as an end goal, but as a learning tool to connect notes together into phrases.
