Re: Examples of Good Session Websites

I don’t know of any sessions with websites, so I can’t give you any examples, but this seems like a pretty straightforward question. You want the schedule and location and contact information for the venue, and a paragraph or two about how your session is organized and who is welcome to play.

The best advice I can offer in terms of information design for the web is the classic and often ignored cliche: keep it as simple as possible, which means cutting out anything that can possibly be cut out. In this case, you’re building a page for one particular repeating event, so give people the information they need to find their way to this event, and maybe a few links to more general sites like this one where they can learn all about sessions, what tunes to learn, when sessions first started happening, how often to oil their flute, and so forth. The less you add, the easier and better life is for your visitors and for you.

Re: Examples of Good Session Websites

I’d agree with Johnny Jay that Facebook is definitely the way to go, rather than websites, which people take less and less notice of these days. And backed up by email for those who don’t do Facebook. This is the way several folk clubs and sessions that I am involved with do it. For me, as admin of two of them, it just means sending a monthly email and updating the Facebook page. It’s easy enough to add photos, posters (jpg format best) and music files.
A couple of tips if you do go down the Facebook route:
1. Don’t go for the Business model of a Facebook group: it is extraordinarily cumbersome, unnecessary and difficult to navigate.
2. Go for the classic, basic Facebook group model, and make it “private but visible” - this will keep scammers and spammers at bay, while still allowing people to find your group. You then decide if you want to have any screening questions for people wanting to join the group, who may post new posts and whether or not you want to vet all new posts.
The combination of Facebook and email work well for communicating any last minute changes, such as bad weather cancellations, or as recently for us, a change to the usual timetable.
And since this is an Irish music website, it’s worth mentioning that the recently formed Edinburgh Comhaltas group also advertises its activities via Facebook and email.
Good luck!

Re: Examples of Good Session Websites

It’s actually a sad fact of life that a lot of this modern technology has made communication and the distribution of information more difficult in many areas. At least, that’s what I’ve found.

For instance, I was involved with my local folk club for two extended periods in the eighties and also the noughties/tens.
In the 80s, we relied upon “word of mouth”, flyers, posters(where we could get away with it), and a postal address list. We usually placed an advertisement in the local newspaper and got a mention on the folk programme on local radio(No such thing now, really).

During my second stint, we had created a web site and had an e-mail list. We still produced paper flyers but seemed to have given up on posters. Local radio and newspapers seemed less co-operative but we still got the occasional plug. “Word of Mouth” still helped although people seemed to be talking to each other less. There was a growing assumption that everybody knew what was going on or should. We also started posting on forums which was useful too but each one had its own followers.

Although I’m not involved in any such matters now, I get the impression that it is becoming even harder to cover all the bases. Many clubs, organisations, groups tend to focus on just one or two aspects of publicity and getting the word out and miss many of their potential audience, followers etc.

Increasingly, I find that if I need to know what’s going on at a club, festival, concert hall and within the local session scene then I have to actively seek out the information myself whereas, in the past, I’d have been made aware of it in a variety of ways.
Yes, if I’m on a mailing list or signed up to a FB or similar, this helps. However, not everybody wants to do this and even I don’t want to be “signed up” for absolutely everything. I’d much rather keep things simple.

If anyone wants to use WhatsApp, it is possible to set up a group where only the admins are allowed to post messages.
