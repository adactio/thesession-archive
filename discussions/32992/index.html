Re: Recommended Listening

BB-I would recommend listening to other instruments than the mandolin,which is relatively new to Irish trad. Listening to other instruments will strengthen your understanding of the style,help you to better understand issues like ornamentation, and bring you ten-fold joy. When I started music school many decades ago as a classical guitar major I quickly learned that piano, piano,violin,cello flute and vocalists all had better repertoire than my chosen instrument. Listening to and learning this music made me a better musician, and as I was close to NYC I was able to hear many of the greats like Horowitz,Rostropovitch Rudolph Serkin Franco Corelli,etc live in person.Many of the best concerts I attended didn’t have a single plink or plunk.(Of course I also saw/heard John Williams,Julian Bream ((Andy Irvine’s teacher)) Segovia et. al. as well)
Listening to flute and whistle players like John McKenna, Mike Rafferty, Harry Bradley, Matt Malloy and many others will help you learn about phrasing. The great fiddle players-Micheal Coleman, Paddy Killoran, Johnny Doherty,Paddy Canny,Bobby Casey,Lad O‘Bierne, etc.,etc, will teach you much about articulation,double-stops and other aspects of the style, some of which can translate directly to the mandolin. For ornamentation you can’t beat pipers,like the great Seamus Ennis, Patsy Touhey, Willy Clancy, Liam Og O’Flynn Richard O’Mealy,etc.
Many,but not all, of the artists I have listed are players from previous generations,some going back to the 19th Century. There’s a reason for that. This is traditional music, and the further back you go,the better the sense of the tradition you get. Of course there are many,many wonderful modern players who play “pure drop” as well If you are as fortunate as I, you will get great joy as well as a great education from these wonderful artists Happy Listening!

Re: Recommended Listening

There’s so much good music out there. If you use a service like Pandora or Spotify, which will suggest music to you, be discerning in your listening, because you will be fed stuff that isn’t particularly traditional, and stuff that isn’t particularly good. They don’t have everything either, so you’ll find some lack of variety, or find that the quality goes down as they try to automatically figure out other stuff you might like. If you’re using a service, try to pay attention to the things that you really like. And then maybe come back here and ask for things that are similar…

There are a ton of great suggestions above. But what I’ve found over the years is that what I really like to listen to is not commercial recordings. They can be good and interesting and fun to listen to. But I really like listening to more organic things, like scratchy, noisy session recordings and tunes that people send to me specifically to learn.

You’ve already had suggestions (in this thread, and others) of good mandolin albums. But it’s also good to listen to other instruments. I find recordings of solo piping and fiddling to be useful in giving me a “flavor” of style and ornamentation. And then finding ways to give the music the same flavor with a plucked instrument can be an interesting challenge. You’re never going to make a mandolin sound like pipes. But you can find things that complement what a piper is doing, and provide a similar emotional response.

But also be aware that learning something specific, like a particular tune, from a solo recording is a dangerous thing. In a solo commercial recording, they’re doing things to make it stand out. They don’t have to make it fit in a group environment, so they may be shifting keys or modes, and doing outlandish melodic variation that sounds great in a recording and will get you murdered in a session. So use commercial recordings of Irish music as a guide for the intangibles - the rhythms, lift, drive, and “flavor” of the music, while minding the fact that these people are showing off in ways that don’t necessarily fit within a spontaneous group environment like a session.

Re: Recommended Listening

When listening to Ceol Alainn collection, please note that it follows the taste of its original creator, Dragut Reis. Dragut Reis has a good taste for music, no bullshit there, but what you find there is mostly played in style of southern counties - Kerry, Cork, Clare. This style is distinct and specific, but it is not the only one. Sligo or Donegal fiddling is great too (honestly, I prefer these styles), and accordion is played in a different, less bouncy and more “rich” manner by pretty much everyone except these southern guys.

So do not limit your listening to this collection, it’s not a gospel of true Irish music or something, just a good collection. A really good one, I admit.
