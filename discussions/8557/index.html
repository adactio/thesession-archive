Re: BODRHAN ACCEPTANCE

I think it’s helpful to consider why the bodhran is as popular as it is. Keep in mind that these comments come from someone who appreciates and plays the notorious instrument.

At gigs and sessions I’ll play it on the odd tune here and there and I’m amazed how there will always be somebody who will come up to me and tell me how much they enjoy the thing. Perhaps what they like about it is that when I’m playing it I’m not playing my flute and concertina, but I’m going to assume they just like the sound of a drum in the music. So why do so many people like it? Well, maybe it’s easier for a listener to grab onto if they aren’t as familiar with ITM as the musicians.

Just about every kind of music has a drum of some sort, and maybe it serves that sort of purpose -- providing a backbone for the rest of the music to take shape with. I don’t mean to give it more importance than the tune, but rather just illustrate a role it might play. But just like with the body, if the backbone is weak or off -- it doesn’t feel very good and even has crippling effects. For this reason when it’s played in a way that upsets the other musicians it’s met with such contempt. (Picture someone with a horrible back-ache.)

That said, when the bodhran is well played and the style matches the other musicians – it’s really great and has a wide appeal. But the fact that it can be met with such extreme disapproval only underlines it’s power and importance. If it can’t be played well – it shouldn’t be played at all. (Except at home along with CDs or sympathetic and compassionate friends.)

Relax a bit there bodhranman. I’ve been playing percussion in ITM for almost thirty years. Where is Bodhran Bliss? Odd to see the bodhran topic being discussed yet again without Bliss contributing his opinion. The topic does feel like a marathon, and it is more fun to play a bodhran than talk about it. In the right hands, it adds alot to the music. But this is typically percussion played TO the music with some type of synchronization with the group and with an appropriate range of expression. There are times I play the bodhran like a bass instrument. There are times I emphasize triplets and triplet chains. There are times when simplicity works. There are times when a light touch works. There are times when putting the bodhran aside to play another instrument works. There are times to quit playing and shamelessly take advantage of a free drinks offer.

Sessions are sometimes difficult when there is a large percentage of new people each time or few regular core players that have worked out their compatibility with each other. Compatibility with a bodhran player and vice versa is probably more critical than most compatibilities in a group.
