New composition

Now I am proud and I mean proud of this one. I think it’s my best composition yet. I just wrote it so I haven’t learned it yet. (sadly that means no recording yet) I would love to know what you guys and gals think. So please do share your thoughts. The inspiration came from a Cardinal that I saw in my backyard a while ago. I tried to imitate the Cardinals signature song. If you listen you can hear that in the B part. (BTW when I compose I usually do it when I can’t play my whistle so it doesn’t really disturb my practice time that much.)

X: 1
T: The Cardinal on the fence
R: reel
M: 4/4
L: 1/8
K: Amaj
|:c~A3 AFEF|Acec fcec|fafa e~c2B |ABcA B2fe|
c~A3 AFEF| Acec afab| bafa e~c2A| BABc A3 f:|
|:(a.c‘)(a.c’) fa2e|(3aba (3aba (f.a) (f.a)|~f2~e2 cecB | ABcA B2fe |
| (a.c‘) (e.a) fa2e| af/af/af/af/ (a.c’) (e.a) | bafa e~c2A| BABc A3 f:|

↳ Posted by Nigel Gatherer on January 23rd, 2017.

Re: New composition

I do find the concept of posting up newly-written tunes in abc format (or whatever) a little bizarre. As opposed to recording them, I mean. It is abstracting a tune from its performance, which seems doubly odd given the nature of Irish traditional music. Sure, we might learn hundreds of tunes from notes in a tune-book, but those tunes usually possess the almighty distinction of having already been played by thousands of players for at least 100 years.

While a good tune is a good tune because of the notes in it, we only come to acknowledge it as a good tune in performance; and ultimately only when it is performed by lots of people and enters the canon; or when it is performed by a big-name performer on one of their albums. (The latter generally leads to the former) We would not all be playing The Butterfly if it had been written by me and stuck up on a site in abc format, as opposed to being played and recorded by the brilliantly inventive fiddle player that was Tommy Potts. (Cue old chestnut discussion of how The Butterfly is not really Potts’ tune but two trad tunes squished together etc etc)
