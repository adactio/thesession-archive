Meta in the Mandolin Cafe

Yesterday, having an interest in a thread about a Trinity College bouzouki upgrade, I followed the link to another site (i.e., the Mandolin Cafe). The first thing I noticed about their discussion pages is that everybody seemed very civil, totally caring and helpful, and very focussed and objective about the question at hand. There appeared to be no supercilious innuendo’s, no bait casting, no put-downs, no sarcastic wit (or wit of any kind), no battles of ego, and not even the slightest hint of disagreement. So my question is this;- What the hell is wrong with these people??. I couldn’t get home fast enough! I bet they don’t even have any meta-discussions.

I first came across the Session a year ago, when I was trying to find the answer to various fiddle problems that were besetting me as a new returner. The posts I was coming across (cos you don’t have to be a member to see them) dated from 2006 or earlier. They were helpful, chatty, jokey, friendly. What a charming site, I thought. But just before I joined I saw another thread where people were being incredibly nasty to each other, going for each other by name, insulting their playing etc. I went ahead & joined anyway, but was surprised to find that five years later these *s-h-e-n-a-n-i-g-a-n-s* were par for the course and many of the original helpful posters had apparently left the site. However, I have to say that on most of the first few threads I posted, the repliers were patient, polite, lovely in fact - perhaps in deference to my auntly status. :)

But of course I stayed out of the really nasty ding-dongs. Until recently. But it’s taking a toll on my nervous system. Read my lips. There is absolutely no need to attack people or call them names or use pseudo-expletives when you are disagreeing with them. A site can still be lively & mischievous & jokey. It doesn’t have to be boring. I know this because last month I joined Classical Music Forum. As it says above, they have lots of moderators, quote facilities, like facilities et al. But I think really it’s down to a different clientele. There are so many different styles of classical music that you can’t just get cliques in a corner. There’s a baroque group, which I belong to, and we make little jokes about the atonal mob, but it’s funny, witty, chaffing.

It’s not really a question of ‘why can’t the Session be like that’? The Session actually used to BE like that; and it still is a fabulous place to be, when the posturers don’t set out to wreck it.

Now - have a nice day, everyone, or else! :) I have to practise as it’s my lesson today. I just hope it isn’t like last week when I parked my car outside Fiddle Guru’s house and found him in a nearby carpark learning to ride a unicycle. Yes, I mean that literally … sigh!

Before going on, I anticipate that I should apologise (once more) to Jeremy for (a) furthering a discussion of which I admit has no DIRECT relevance to Irish music, and for (b) now being drawn into mentioning a bit of personal information that would be more appropriate in my profile (where I don’t want to put it).

I have only actively participated in this site since last Christmas, and I must confess that in my comments I have contributed very little, or perhaps even nothing whatever to do directly with Irish traditional music. On the other hand, I’ve been an avid follower of this site for about 10 years, and I have a total passion for ITM and have eagerly sponged up as much education as I could, both from the tunes section and the discussion pages. I have always seen these two sections as almost separate domains. To me, the discussions have always felt analogous to when I used to sit in the university refectory with fellow impoverished students. In between classes it was a place of socialisation where we would argue even the most ridiculous things, mostly in good spirit and occasionally not. But I’ve always felt that I learned just as much in the uni refect as I did in the lecture rooms. That’s kind of how I see the discussion pages. And the freedom of discussion is very valuable to me in the following way (this is the personal bit):-

For near 10 years now I’ve ‘suffered’ from a condition of social anxiety and as I live in an isolated spot and leave home as little as possible. I’m quite happy in this condition and enjoy my solitude. But here’s my relevance:- I can’t physically attend a session (so am I a fraud on this site?), and it took me ten years before I could muster up the courage to socialise even on the internet. I did so at first with great trepidation, and so many times since Christmas I’ve had to fight the urge to withdraw. But why was I drawn out of my cyberspace solitude ONLY by this site?

Part A. of my answer is that I have a passion for ITM (the culture, the history, the geography and the people). That’s why I’ve been a follower for 10 years. Part B. of my answer is to do with my desire to participate, and that is that there is an overriding good character within the Session community that I love. Sure there are the idiots and annoying individuals (and perhaps to some I’m one), but isn’t it still like that back in real life? (??). But most of all there are contributors to the discussion pages who I really respect either/and for their opinions, good humour, good nature, and (regardless of personality) great musicianship. I would never name names, but this is a community that I enjoy, and appreciate. Yes, much of what we discuss (not just my pointless topic) is not DIRECTLY relevant to Irish music, but surely the Irish music is a shared passion that binds us together as a community, and as a community we surely benefit from our ‘OPEN’ discussion time in the refect (so to speak). Okay, Jeremy, there’s the occasional food fight, but it’s absolutely true that I would not want to be contributing member of this site if it was as decorous as sitting round tables of The Mandolin Cafe.
