Well Rounded Music

I am beginning to think that some people’s idea of a good session must be pretty boring. All these ‘purists’ trying to find the Holy Grail of ITM can hardly be a bundle of laughs to be around. Imagine a session with 5 fiddle players (or whatever the holy grailstrument is) all scratching out the same notes – no rhythmic section, no backing – after 20 minutes it would be the holy grate. And God forbid that somebody should actually…oh no…sing.
For me every session should have tonal & musical depth. Song is as much part of ITM heritage as any tune and the voice brings a welcome change of sound as much as another instrument.
Due to other life commitments I hadn’t played a session for a long time, until last Monday at the Black Boy in Winchester. What a great bunch of unpretentious guys all there for the pleasure of playing (various instruments) together….and a couple of songs thrown in for variety. Altogether a well rounded evening – and being rather short for my weight, I like to think I am a well rounded musician. Down with THIN sessions.

Re: Well Rounded Music

I’m into musical depth and being relaxed. It is really all about having fun, after all. But there’s a fine line between singing Led Zeppelin for a bit of craic and having a hippy crew come in and turn the session into a djembe circle…

Like I’ve said before, the two ends of the spectrum between the purists and the progressives are good to have, pulling us in both directions. That’s what keeps the middle of the spectrum alive and thriving. By “middle of the spectrum”, I am referring to those of us who like to go out and have a good time playing tunes in a style that tries to stay faithful to the Irish tradition. Which is part of what makes it fun…

I also think that it may be more important for sessions on the American side of the pond to try to be a bit more purist about it, because we’re so far removed from the surrounding culture. It’s more difficult for us to hold that link to what makes it an Irish session because of that. We’re more likely to get bluegrass, old time, or appalachian players wandering in and muddying the waters, when there are those of us that would prefer to keep the music sounding Irish.

Pete

# Posted on October 10th 2007 by Reverend

The crux of the matter, and the post above by Nutter explains things in a way.

To me, a “purist” session, given the origins and I do not mean London pubs, has tunes, songs, craic, party pieces, recitation, dancing. Nowadays most would have about three of those elements.

To me a “progressive” session is one where a group of “elitists” get together with rules, and play the true spirit of the soul moving music, a mystical experience reserved for melody players only. This is a new thing, certainly in Ireland.

I must admit I have often said to people who tell us “you could make a fortune in New York”, my reply is normally, “Yes, but who wants to sing Danny Boy and The fields of Athenry 3 times each every night, seven nights a week?” Both good songs by the way, the first especially, although I prefer it played as an air.

I appreciate that the USA is also full of people who want to join in and batter bodhrans badly (alliteration) and play blue grass, so maybe things are different.

Put it this way, in 41 years of going to sessions, I have never, ever seen a djembe drum.

Re: Well Rounded Music

I am utterly uninterested in how anyone defines what well rounded music is. How anyone defines what a session could or should be. In fact, I’m going to stop calling what me and my mates like to do a session, lest it give people the wrong idea.

Me and my mates have a peculiar hobby. We like to play music from a very tightly defined sub-genre. We go out to the pub and indulge our hobby. And we are not interested in people who are not familiar with this tightly defined sub-genre. And if anyone tries to invade our evening with something out with our self-penned remit, well, after a few civil and polite warnings, we may well be rude to them.

If what you experience from our indulgence of our hobby bores you, we a fortunate enough to live in a city where there exists ample opportunity for you to take your expectations and preconceptions elsewhere.

Though more importantly, those passing through who share our dedication to the sub-genre find a find an excellent place to communicate and express themselves - without the clutter.
