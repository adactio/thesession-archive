This idea that has been repeatedly trotted out in this thread - that only experts in ITM should be giving advice, is absolute rubbish. If you only listen to Martin Hayes, the best you can hope for is that one day you might know as much as Martin Hayes. If you listen to everyone, you’ll land up knowing an awful lot.

ITM experts may be the best source on issues of style, but players from other genres can still give good advice on instrument technique, absolute beginners can empathise with other beginners and share their experience of the learning process. I doubt if there is anyone here, writing or just lurking, who doesn’t have something positive they could contribute.

And remember that not everyone who asks questions here is interested in becoming a pure-drop Irish session musician. Many (possibly most) just want to play the generic ‘celtic’ music that has more appeal to the general public. For them, advice from an all rounder may be more useful than that from a pure dropper.

I think one angle to Bernie’s comment is that a while ago Irish music was mostly played by farmers coming together and played whatever instrument they could get their hands on, in whatever way they could play. No one would get angry about bad tuning, bad rhythm, etc. At one point Irish music started to get popular outside Ireland, with emigrants spreading the music around and non Irish people getting into the music.

Interview with Brendan Taaffe, Fiddler Magazine, Summer 2001.

Q: Your triplets are very distinctive. How did you develop them?

A: I probably consciously worked on them in the sense that they never seemed to work properly. So they developed from trying to bow them properly but not succeeding. There’s a slight difference from what might be known as a Sligo style of playing in that it’s a different bow direction.
