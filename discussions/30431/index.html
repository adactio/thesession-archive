Are you happy with your Fleadh Cheoil na hÉireann 2012 competition results?: Questioning the process.

For those who were in Cavan this year competing in the Fleadh Cheoil, or indeed, in previous years, are you / have you been content and satisfied with your adjudication / results? I am intrigued to know how people feel towards the decisions made by these Fleadh adjudicators, particularly in the marching band competitions. Also I am not sure how the individuals / groups results are distributed following competitions, but I do know that the marching band adjudicators do NOT allow any feedback following the reveal of results - NO points what so ever. They mail out the results some days after having competed. And worst of all, you DO NOT receive your fellow competitors results from the section you are competing in.Therefore you cannot compare results. Plus the feedback tends to quite general. I really question the process. the fleadh exudes unethical behaviour in my opinion. Any thoughts?

Re: Are you happy with your Fleadh Cheoil na hÉireann 2012 competition results?: Questioning the process.

Partiality in the judging process was something we picked up on at the very first Leinster Fleadh that our kids went to. It just seems to go with the territory - certain people from particular families are expected to do well in the results and they usually do. You take it or leave it.

With a bit more experience now, I see the whole Comhaltas competition thing as part of the ‘music industry’ in Ireland that extracts money from parents for lessons, instruments and exams/ competitions. Sure that’s a bit unfair on people who run local Comhaltas branches on a largely volunteer basis but you often find when you look deeper that the same volunteers are also involved in commercial lessons etc. There’s nothing wrong with earning a few bob from teaching and it’s great to see the music being passed on but as I note above, it’s all part of a system which parents and families fund.

Maybe it’s always been thus to an extent, we might have a vision of children learning from parents and neighbours but there were always travelling teachers as well. The system is just a lot more commercialised now.

Re: Are you happy with your Fleadh Cheoil na hÉireann 2012 competition results?: Questioning the process.

@zippydw I am not sure what you mean by the description “…being something vanilla…”?

I could write a book on what I have witnessed at fleadhs. The influencing of results by competitors, their followers / teachers is absolutely obvious. I would share some evidence but I am sure it is of little to those who are not involved with the bands. It is so political that it is nauseating!!

@Ben, the marching bands are composed of a variety of sorts. There are accordion bands (accordions, percussion), miscellaneous bands (flute, whistle, accordion, percussion), fife and drum bands (flute, percussion). The bands vary in size. A lot of the bands, with the exception of the fife and drum bands, include majorettes, wherein altogether, about 40 to 50 members are a part. The flute bands have around 30 members nowadays. Each band has to compete using a marching routine that is between 10 and 12 mins long, containing marches, a hornpipe, jigs, reels, polkas etc. It takes a full year of blood, sweat, and tears to bring a band of that many people to the adequate standard for the all ireland fleadh.. Especially in the senior sections. The fleadh has broken bands up because of their decisions in the past, with some aborting the fleadh altogether. Many times there has been obvious, deliberate, decisions made to put an inferior band in 1st place. In fact, some winning bands have said that in the past, they were awarded first place knowing that they didnt deserve it. These decisions actually mess with the minds of the people who work so hard all year to be unjustly placed as runner up or lower. What makes it worse is that you are lumbered with adjudicators who are unwilling to reveal where it was that a band has missed out on their win.
