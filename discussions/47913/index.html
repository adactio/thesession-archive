Question for the guitar players

Hey guys, I have a question here for the fellow guitar players. I’m looking to buy my first good acoustic and I’m wondering what other Irish trad players find to suit sessions. I’m a aware that it’s the player and not the guitar that makes a good accompanist, but I don’t want to buy a guitar only to find it lacking in a session.

I mostly play folk/trad ballady stuff (think ye vagabonds or planxty), but also 70s singer songwriter stuff. I’m currently thinking an all mahogany smaller bodied guitar like the Martin 00015sm. My only concern is that it won’t have the volume or projection to properly support fiddles and pipes. Does anyone play this style of guitar, and if so how have you find it in a session? My thought are that it would work great in a small session (3-5 players) but would probably be drowned out in a larger one?

Re: Question for the guitar players

Smaller-bodied guitars do seem to blend in better at small to medium sessions. I’m not a big fan of dreadnaughts at sessions. But then I’m also not a fan of large, loud sessions. (I’ve lost track of how many times I’ve been bothered by loud guitar backing at sessions, but I’ve never encountered a guitar that was “too quiet.”)

As lynnhayes suggests above, some backers have more than one guitar in their quiver so they can choose which will best fit the circumstances.

Another option—whichever type of guitar you go with—is to have a range of picks on hand. A lighter gauge (thinner) pick will sound less bassy and may be easier to still strum energetically (when needed) while producing less volume. A heavier gauge (thicker) pick can produce more oomph (when needed) with less effort.

It’s easier to carry six different picks than a six-pack of guitars.

I’d also go along with trying out lots of guitars and finding the one that speaks to you. You’ve got to like the instrument and it’s got to like you, in the sense of allowing you to express yourself in the way you want.

The size of the guitar, in terms of suitability for sessions is a comparatively trivial issue. ↳ Posted by RobertJohnston on February 21st, 2023.

Re: Question for the guitar players

The problem with bringing an amplifier to reinforce an acoustic guitar is that it’s easy to result in creeping volume wars in my experience. Especially if other players in the session follow the lead and start amplifying their instruments.

You can’t quite hear yourself as much as you’d like so you turn up the volume on your amp just a little bit. Then the other guy with an amp, or just the fiddlers start playing a little louder to match or override your volume as a backer. So you turn up a little more. Now everyone is blasting away as loud as they can play and the quality of the playing goes to hell. Doesn’t always happen that way but I’ve seen it a few times.

Acoustic instruments in an Irish session may not always blend together perfectly in a group; some may be quieter or louder than others but it usually works out okay. Better to be a little too quiet as long as you can hear yourself, than to offend others with volume. It’s the “do no harm” principle that often applies in sessions.
