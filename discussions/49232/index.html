Re: “Drive” in melody playing, what is it and how do I practice it?

I think of drive as the ability to propel the rhythm forward without speeding up. Gentle syncopation through intermittent accents on 2 or 4 tends to be sufficient, a little bounce or swing, and steady downbeats without excessive triplets.

Basically, listen to Kevin Griffin.

Re: “Drive” in melody playing, what is it and how do I practice it?

Drive happens when you consistently land just slightly ahead of the beat, even jumping occasional downbeats well ahead of the beat. Triplets that move through 2 or 3 pitches that lead to a downbeat also help. (E.g., the B/B/c triplet here: … B/B/c|dA A/A/A dAFA| ) The result is a sense of insistent momentum, pushing the tune forward. The trick is to play slightly ahead of the beat while holding the beat steady, that is, without speeding up.

Good example of drive here, on jigs and a reel. Note that when Cathal goes from Donegal Lass to Cuil Aodha, he lands more on the beat, letting the drive relax just a hair, then he builds it up again.

Re: “Drive” in melody playing, what is it and how do I practice it?

Gimpy’s examples are great. I was actually going to post a video of Cathal Hayden playing, but that video is even better. If you look at the spots in the video where the camera shows both Cathal and Seamie’s hands, you can see how much ahead of the beat Cathal is compared to the guitar. (Look at the sequence in that video starting at 2:02, and slow it down to 50% using the settings icon).

So the question is how do they agree on where the beat is? And the answer is that *most* of the beats are together, but as Gimpy points out, a good number of the strong beats on banjo are played a bit early. What this does is gives the music a sense that it is speeding up when it isn’t. The problem is that some players will latch onto the early notes, and actually end up playing faster to “catch up”, resulting in the tempo increasing, so it should be something that you pay attention to. Banjos have probably the sharpest attack of any instrument we play in this music (including the bodhran), and it cuts well in a session. And sometimes people latch onto it. So if you’re finding that happening, you also have the power to keep it from speeding up.

I tend to play with a fair amount of drive like that. It is something that developed organically in my playing over years, and not anything that that I sat and consciously practiced… But I would say that if you listen to enough music that is played with drive, it will start appearing in your music. And simply understanding what it is and how it happens can help the process along. If you practice along with recordings of people playing with drive, that can help too.

It’s one of those ephemeral aspects of playing that in some ways has to be felt.

I’d suggest taking say a reel that you know well and listening to different version on e.g Spotify or YouTube.

Re: “Drive” in melody playing, what is it and how do I practice it?

Backing can be mellow—think Dennis Cahill: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KGSBYzqBc5E

or driving—https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d0YkxvXOuys

But the core role of the backer is to provide a steady beat. The melody player(s) can then play behind that beat for a more relaxed feel or ahead of the beat for more drive.

Of course, the backing typically matches the basic feel. So a driven tune usually gets more aggressive strumming and big downbeats. In the driving clip I’ve linked in this post, Steve Cooney really punches the strings and also adds triplets to build momentum in certain phrases, sometimes matching Martin’s flurry of triplets. But he mostly keeps the downbeats steady so Martin can choose whether to play behind, on, or ahead of those pulses.

Re: “Drive” in melody playing, what is it and how do I practice it?

On the beat, off the beat, before the beat, behind the beat … to me, ”drive” means that the player (of any instrument, really) has as beat that’s steady enough, some emphasis on the backbeat (but not so much that it’s predictable or monotonous). Speed has something to do with drive, but it isn’t everything.

The ever-elusive Jig rhythm - https://thesession.org/discussions/43981

Re: “Drive” in melody playing, what is it and how do I practice it?

I’m always thinking of ‘drive’ & ‘groove’ for the fiddle - to me, it’s all about ‘tension & release’.
The 1st beat in a measure is usually my main target, so I want to build up some tension to get to it. Music Theory can help push me toward that target - like I can substitute different subdivisions for the last beat (usually a weak beat) in a measure (more, smaller notes give the illusion of speed & visa versa) & I can try out different types of accents (metrical, dynamic, duration or pitch change) where there wasn’t any. I feel a little variety is important (I’m still learning just where & when to apply this), because having every measure equally driving feels (to me) just as boring as having no drive.
I think slowing down & studying Cathal Haden’s playing for how he adds drive is a great idea - even gives me some ideas for on the fiddle! When folks start learning at a young age they do pick up what’s important by just listening & playing. As an older adult learner I also learn that way, but I definitely appreciate the analysis to let me know what I have to work with & what to focus on - to save me time.

↳ Posted by Kellie on April 27th, 2024.

Re: “Drive” in melody playing, what is it and how do I practice it?

From a fiddler’s point of view (me), ‘drive’ is something we can do ‘to’ any melody. It’s manipulation of the phrasing, changing the space between notes (microtiming deviation) - like adding a little swing or syncopation, along with choosing ways to accent important beats, or off-beats. Signature Trad Irish ornaments can be used to percussively accent OR help sustain a note (depending on articulation), which can help with drive - but I think it’s more about ‘expressive timing’, poorly timed ornaments do much more harm than good.
…any “scratch of the bow on the fiddle” has absolutely NOTHING to do with rhythm, or drive.

↳ Posted by mandocello8 on May 4th, 2024.

Re: “Drive” in melody playing, what is it and how do I practice it?

@kellie - While putting drive in the music may or may not involve ornamentation, it certainly is possible to have drive with zero ornamentation.

You aren’t going to find “how to add drive” in a book. It’s all in technique and timing.

If you want to understand drive, find a recording that you feel has drive and slow it down with a tool like Transcribe or ASD and listen for the microtiming of the notes, silences, articulation, pulse, and volume, all factors in creating “drive”.
