Re: Eadgbe - dadgad

Right. I’m not sure if this point has been made here before on a previous thread, but I feel it’s well worth making. Here goes…

You play an instrument, not a tuning.

The harmonic function of a chord is dependant on it’s constituent notes and on the melodic context into which it is placed. Different voicings and harmonic effects should be achievable in any particular key depending on the time and effort the guitarist (or bouzoukist…) is prepared to put in to learning the instrument.

As an example or two. Chuck Berry v Segovia. Mainly standard tuning. Different effect - same tuning. Mick Taylor (Slipknot) v Arty McGlynn (Slipknot-not). Mainly dropped d. Different effect… Pierre Bensusan, first album v Pierre Bensusan, latest album. Mainly dadgad. Different effect… etc.

Conversely, take a player such as Clive Carroll. Different tunings, but they all sound like Clive.

By all means try different tunings. Standard is only standard because it’s the one most people use most of the time. But be wary of finding they become a crutch, as the over-reliance on a capo can become. Tunings are tools. They are not the job itself.

Find a group of friends. Find the sound that fits with the sound they produce. If a particular tuning facilitates this use it. Just don’t depend on it.

My crutch of choice, by the way, would be dropped d. Just not all the time. ;-)

Oh, and rhythm’s important to, as is standing your round… ;-)

For backing I use standard exclusively now. The reason is I don’t have a regular gig these days and when I sit down I don’t know what is going to be played so for me standard covers all the bases.
