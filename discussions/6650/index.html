guitar picks and string gauges

I’ve not long been playing ITM, having migrated from classical guitar. I like picking tunes, throwing in the occasional chord to give it a lift or for emphasis, but mostly sticking with the melody.
I am most comfortable with a flat pick of .88mm. I have tried thinner picks but they don’t feel right. But I wonder whether I am sacrificing anything (speed, accuracy) by using such a heavy pick. Is it worth persevering with thinner picks? Am I hampering myself? Also I asume that string gauge is related to this. I don’t know what gauge strings I have - they’re just what came with a fairly inexpensive guitar. What thoughts do others have?

Should I be picky about string gauge? Should I just string along with any old pick?

Re: guitar picks and string gauges

Which guage you should use can depend on the instrument. It’s also a matter of preference as is your choice of pick. I use a .73mm for the mandolin usually but sometimes will change to a 1mm pick for more volume when I’m really confident with a tune.
I use different guages of strings on various instruments.
You’ll probably experiment with several possibilities before you’re happy. We all do. Generally, though, I wouldn’t recommend a pick that is too thin and be careful not to use strings with a too thick guage that might damage your instrument. Other than that, it’s just what you feel comfortable doing.

Re: guitar picks and string gauges

There’s no right and wrong here - you just need to find what’s best for you. I actually don’t use a pick at all, because I find the sound from strumming chords with a pick to be a bit “clunky” and cumbersome. I fingerpick a lot of chords, and so my nails on my picking hand are long(ish) and when I’m strumming chords I hold my hand like I’m holding a pen, and do the down stroke with the nail of my first finger, and the upstroke with my thumbnail - I find that that produces a lighter and somehow “nimbler” sound.

As for string gauges, I like to use really quite heavy gauges for finger picking, but lighter ones for strumming. Obviously that involves two guitars, which I can’t afford, so at the moment I’m using some Elixir medium-lights (i think they start at 0.11 on the top E). They’re very good strings and have a lot of tone compared to other strings of a similair gauge.

Anyway, the point of all this ramble is that I don’t think there is a definitive guitar method for this music - listen to other players, you’ll find that nearly every good player has developed their own style - so should you!

Good luck and happy playing.

Re: guitar picks and string gauges

I think the balance between string gauge and pick thickness is important. Furthermore, the stiffness of the type of plastic from which the pick is constructed is also an issue as well as the thickness. Jazz guitarists playing in the Django Reinhard (spelling?) style, may use picks made out of sections of bone or antler. Others players may use extremely thin picks. I have personally found for myself (l only accompany on the guitar, and don’t pick tunes) that a Jim Dunlop pick of 0.60mm is ideal (the grey ones, with “Jim Dunlop JD” on one side and “USA NYLON .60mm” on the other). I use light gauge strings (0.012 - 0.053 inches). However, I’ve noticed other misicians using the identical pick (and thickness) for mandolin and banjo.

Having said all that, It’s what you find works best for what you’re doing - trial & error is the best approach.

Re: guitar picks and string gauges

Of course, what works for you is what works for you. Part of the problem, though, is that it can take a long time and a lot of patience to figure out what works best. It can take months to adjust to a new approach and give it a fair trial. For that reason I think it’s a good idea to at least try the most widely recommended approaches.

If you collect enough data from pickers, there is a statistical pattern that emerges. There seems to be a plurality, if not majority, of flatpickers who feel that medium gauge strings (.013…) and a heavy-ish pick (neighborhood of 1 mm) give them something close to an optimal picking situation. I fall into this group, but I don’t think it’s optimal for everyone.

There are some fairly sound physical reasons for the choice. Everything else being equal, medium gauge strings can produce a clearer, fatter tone than lights and they don’t deflect as much when plucked, so there’s less movement in the process. They feel harder to the fingers, but they also allow you to lower the action so they can be almost as comfortable as lights. A thicker pick also can produce a clearer, fatter tone and, again, doesn’t deflect as much as a thinner one. So, if a strong, clear tone and economy of motion are good things for you, this is a good choice to at least try.

I found that I could play faster and with less fatigue when I converted to medium strings and a thick pick. I think I remember that it took a couple of months to realize the difference.

But, … Your hand skills may not be oiented to the minimum movement approach. Or you may prefer a less tightly focused tone. I’ve known a few people who played light strings with a thin pick and did pretty well by choking up on the pick to minimize the deflection. Some others have cultivated a wide swing of the wrist or arm that seems to work well with a thinner pick. I think this compromises the speed, but that might not be a problem. Norman Blake plays a very cool style with this approach.

There are lots of variables that complicate a discussion of the matter. For example, picking light strings close to the bridge feels a lot like picking medium strings over the sound hole, because there’s less deflection, but the tone is very different. Also, the strings are spaced a little farther apart. Then there’s the whole business of how to angle the pick.

I think it’s good to get a lot of ideas on the table and give ‘em a try. But be patient. If an idea appeals to you, give it at least a few weeks before you give up on it.

I use a Santa Cruz OM PW with D’Addario Flat-Tops in light gauge sets (EFT16 sets). I like the half-round finish on the Flat-Tops. It polishes out in just a few hours of playing and minimizes the shriek of sliding along the strings. For my hand chemistry they last a long time, too.

I find the orange TorTex picks to be just the right size and resistance for the Santa Cruz (and the Crump bouzouki, too). They are smaller than .88mm, but all mine have the size rubbed off, so I can’t tell what they say on them.
