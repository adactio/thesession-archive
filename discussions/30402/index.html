I’m not exasperated, indignant nor astonished (at least not here, this time…) - merely slightly saddened that our highly powerful media seem to promote cultural tunnel-vision more often than challenge it, especially in what is supposed to be a pluralistic age.

Why do I care? Re: Off the radar

To my mind, there’s a continuum that starts with any one of us playing to the wall at home, moves through sessions (kitchen, then pub) and eventually ends up with the pro bands. It’s all more or less the same music. Somewhere along the line, ‘professionalism’ creeps in, with all the paraphernalia, until you end up singing for Disney. I think the unifying factor is the character of the music, so long as it isn’t perverted somewhere along the way, which to her credit, I don’t think JF has done. (I did note on her website a couple of calls for her to sing in English more, which I think would be a mistake).

It’s the basic down-to--earthness that cuts through most things you can do to it, and people like that because they know it isn’t written-for-profit celebrity-pop music. I think they are also curious about that continuum. Most importantly, I think they just get with the rhythms and moods, which are pretty universal. Again, I go back to the comments at the end of the R5 interview about the growth in interest.

DrSS, I don’t feel any ‘obligation’ to share it, and certainly not to force it on people, but I do like to see/share people having a good time. People overheard our rehearsals, and from that spontaneously came a series of bookings, sooner than we hoped, all of which sold out. Admittedly, they are small, and it’s not that we are particularly great musicians or anything, but that’s not the point – quite a few people want to hear this stuff, even in Essex, and they’re by no means all traddies! I’m sure some of it’s novelty, but does that matter? They still enjoy it and come back for more. And we’re not having any difficulty finding other places who want us either.

It may just be where I live – I guess the reaction would be pretty different 40 miles down the road amongst the rude-boys of East London, but who knows?

Entirely fair enough that most people here aren’t that bothered – I’m not on a crusade, and I quite like yaalhouse’s niche too. but I think it’s a shame if people talk themselves into a corner by persuading themselves that the interest isn’t out there when it actually is. If people like it, why pretend they don’t? Unless, of course it’s actually a form of elitism by denial… ;-)

Yes, that bit is simply personal opinion, though there are plenty of others saying the same thing right now.

I’m delighted when the music gets a good reaction, yes, because it reinforces my view, but because I enjoy seeing people happy. But you have to let people come to it of their own accord; if they don’t, fair enough - no music speaks to everyone…

My beef with the media is when they act in ways that might actually *prevent* people ever discovering stuff outside the commercial mainstream, or when they act to dilute that stuff by trying to *make* it mainstream. Hence my O.P.

I’m pleased that JF is doing well - there are plenty of comments on her website from people who have only just ‘discovered’ her, and I think their reactions are significant - but I also share people’s concerns that it could ruin not only her music, but a lot more besides if the mass market ever really came piling in. I guess I’m really making a marginal case, at most.
