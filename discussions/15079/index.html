Re: You were right all along…..

I’m sorry, but I simply can’t see where I’m supposed to have been “nasty”.

I’ve heard *a* well-nigh sacreligious version of Take Five in the wrong rhythm … ugh … don’t know if it was Grover Washington, Jr’s or not …

I agree with you, Bob, that you can find dictionary definitions of “melody” that include or exclude the use of the term “rhythm”. I also agree that “melody” is a near synonym of “tune”. But that doesn’t help us, because it doesn’t tell us whether we require the element of rhythm, musically, for us to be able to interpret something as a “melody” or a “tune”.

In my own musical studies over many years, it has been generally accepted that both the terms “melody” and “tune” require an arrangement of notes that are not only arranged by pitch but also in their spacing from one another and their length - their rhythm. You can have variations on a “theme”, like the great Paganini variations by Rachmaninov, but just about all of those variations on the “theme” have different “melodies”.

You can’t make a jig out of a reel and call it the same “melody” … or “tune” … or whatever. And before anyone else jumps on me for this, that is irrespective of the number of different “versions” there may be of that tune. (Of course, if the versions become *too* diverse, they may be deemed separate, but cognate, melodies.)

But, frankly, I don’t know why we’re getting hung up on that. The main issue here is the idea that it’s OK to bang away on a drum whenever you *don’t* know the tune, which seems to me to be the opposite of what should happen. You should *only* play percussion in this essentially melodic music when you can add to it, or at least not detract, and that requires a great deal of subtlety and a very good knowledge of the tune being played.

I’m perfectly entitled to have that opinion. Surely you’re not going to call me “insulting” or “nasty” for believing that ‘keeping the rhythm’ is nowhere near enough?
