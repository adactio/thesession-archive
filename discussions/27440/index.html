The title of this thread is misleading…..

If you go to the site, it clearly states

"TradConnect

Connecting Traditional *Irish* Musicians Across The World"

and further down

“The sites goal is to get every traditional *Irish* music player in the world onto it”

“the real is aim is to get people that are learning a traditional *Irish* instrument on it”
(Emphasis mine thoughout)

Although there is a brief reference on The Session.org to Irish music on the home page, i.e. “The exchange of tunes is what keeps traditional Irish music alive”, it doesn’t seem to be as restrictive. While it’s true that the majority of members here are mainly involved in Irish music(many aren’t necessarily), this site is mainly a “de facto” Irish site.

Not everybody who learns a traditional instrument is an Irish(as in musical style) player nor do they all want to be. Many, of course, will play Irish music or tunes(there is arguably a distinction here) as part of their repertoire while a large number of mainly Irish players will include music from other tradition and even genres in their own personal repertoire.
There is also, as we know, a large “cross over” between different traditions these days.

So, if the opening poster does have some influence with this new site, I suggest that they clarify whether it is exclusively for Irish music or trad music more generally. I’d be more inclined to be interested if it was the latter although I’ll obviously still check it out.

↳ Posted by DaveL335 on May 3rd, 2011.

Re: Great Website for people learning to play a traditional instrument

Had a look around it and agree with other people’s assessment that it needs to develop a clearer sense of purpose. What can it offer that this site and Chiff and Fipple don’t, other than the capability of embedding videos on the site, which is pretty cool. Keep in mind that the Chiffboard has sections where people can post tunes, people can advertise their services as a teacher or ask if there are any teachers in their area, and also the “city guide” section where you can advertise your session or event or ask if there is anything happening wherever you’re going. These sections of the Chiffboard aren’t heavily used, suggesting that there isn’t a huge vacuum in the online trad music world for those kinds of services. Or it suggests that existing methods, posting here or posting in the "instrument’ sections of Chiff and Fipple, are more efficacious and get more responses.


I looked at the registration form and don’t like any of the “instrument levels” it offers. An “advanced” player can play at “near session speed?” What does that mean? “Session speed” is one of those meaningless phrases, since you can have awesome, quite tight, high level sessions at a laid back pace and totally crap sessions at a frenetic pace. If you decide that you can play at “session speed,” rather than “near” it, your remaining option is “professional.” According to the description, even I could be “professional” as I’ve been paid for the odd gig. Not often, but it happens on occasion.

Trying to categorize people by playing ability is always a bit dodgy (like “intermediate” piping classes at tionols and festivals, where you have a huge range -- from people who are pretty good players but too self-effacing to call themselves “advanced,” to people who figure that now they have three tunes, sort of, they must be “intermediate”). That said, the categories offered in tradconnect.com are particularly useless.
