Re: Opinions were too mixed on a composer search filter on the session… how about a filter for non-trad?

I think the problem with trying to build a filter like that is deciding where and how to draw the line. Who makes that decision? Jeremy? That seems like a lot of work for a thankless job… Or is the decision crowd-sourced? Seems like a good way to get some fun arguments started here!

So when Paddy Fahey was in his prime of writing tunes, it seems like they were pretty much immediately accepted into the tradition by folks around him, and would spread through the world of trad like wildfire. But his tunes are definitely pretty unique, and “modern”. So are they ‘trad’ or ‘modern’? Composers in recent years have had quite an impact on the tradition, and that’s probably true at just about any time during the history of this music. So if you look at it that way, composing tunes is *part of the tradition*!

In my mind, there are three things that contribute to a tune being considered ‘trad’. 1. It was composed by someone who is an accomplished player in the tradition. 2. It fits within the general constraints of our traditional music as far as time signature, key, etc. 3. It is played by people in the tradition, and continues to spread.

I personally consider tunes by the likes of Ed Reavy, Vincent Broderick, Charlie Lennon, Sean Ryan, Maurice Lennon, Josephine Keegan, Paddy Fahey, Paddy O‘Brien, et al. to be part of the tradition. So I would consider them ’trad’.

Re: Opinions were too mixed on a composer search filter on the session… how about a filter for non-trad?

“Can I ask when a ”new“ tune becomes trad?”

In the context of my understanding of this site’s purpose, when it starts getting commonly played in traditional Irish sessions, independent of it’s date of origin and to some extent country of origin.

This is the issue with “original” tunes published by the composers themselves, or with some “pushing the boundaries” genre-crossover tunes, which may be lovely on some CD, or played in someone’s basement for their cat, haven’t made their way into the actual session repertoire.

Of course, there are many tunes which aren’t “traditional” or even technically Irish that have made it into the common session repertoire and absolutely deserve to be represented here and do represent the evolving state of the music. I think the key factor is that the tunes are actually played in sessions.

Now, I guess one can argue that this site can be a way for people to promote tunes they want to see played in sessions vs. tunes that are actually played in sessions. Not sure where that line is intended to be drawn.
