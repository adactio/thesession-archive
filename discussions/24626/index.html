Re: How do you play with somebody with a set of B flat bagpipes???

Nate, easiest way is for you to tune up a semi-tone, that’s what Scottish fiddlers usually do. Scottish pipes have to be tuned to concert Bb as they usually sit naturally slightly sharp of that but it’s relatively straight forward if he’s any good. Get him to get an Eb whistle too so that you are both always up a semi-tone and you don’t have to keep tuning up and down. There’s a pile of session tunes suitable for pipes on my profile page if you want to have a look.

Re: How do you play with somebody with a set of B flat bagpipes???

Although the Highland pipes are referred to as being in B flat, they are nominally in A, which is in fact what they used to be before the pitch drifted to A=440. All the music for them is written in A, and pipers refer to high and low A, and never B flat. If you tune your fiddle so that your A matches his, your only problem then would be finding common tunes. Oh, and being heard.
Actually B flat is not a bad key to play in, and a lot of Scottish fiddlers use it.

↳ Posted by gam on May 17th, 2010.

Re: How do you play with somebody with a set of B flat bagpipes???

Gam, most pipers who play with others tune to Bb nowadays. Most pie bands and soloists plat sharp of Bb. You’re right that there is evidence of some sets being closer to A in the past though that was probably more by chance than design. And yes, pipe music is written in A though no sharps are shown and the 7th is natural. But for Nate he should get the piper to make sure he is in Bb so that others can join in if it comes to that.

Also Nate you wouldn’t transpose from D to Bb. You don’t need to Transpose if you tune up a semi-tone. If the pipes are playing in Bb you play in A, if pipes play Eb you play D, if the pipes play Cm you play Bm, etc. It’s a lot simpler than you seem to be imagining.

Re: How do you play with somebody with a set of B flat bagpipes???

As mentioned above - tune the strings on your fiddle a semi-tone sharp. There are only 9 notes in the bagpipe scale. The highest would be “a” on your top string, descending “g”, “f#”, “e”, “d”, “c#”, “B”, “A”, and “G” on your 3rd string.
There are any amount of bands in Scotland these days using pipes and fiddle. Check out the recordings or any “Youtube” videos of “Battlefield Band”, “Tannahill Weavers”, or “Ossian” , or more recently “Breabach”, “Daimh” or “Bodega”

↳ Posted by Lucy Rietmann on May 19th, 2010.

Re: How do you play with somebody with a set of B flat bagpipes???

I don’t know if the original poster’s piper friend knows about them, or already has them, but Highland bagpipe chanters are made nowadays in several different pitches.

The standard modern chanters have their so-called “A” pitched at around 480 cycles. This is halfway between the keys of B flat and B.

Back in the 1960’s bagpipe chanters had their “A” tuned around 466 cycles, in other words Concert B flat.

In the 1970’s and into the 1980’s the pitch of bagpipe chanters was rapidly rising and you’ll find old chanters pitches at 469, 472, 474, etc etc etc.

Nowadays nearly all leading Highland bagpipe makers offer chanters in 480 and 466. The 466 chanters might be called “B flat chanters” or “orchestral chanters” etc depending on the maker. The biggest current maker, McCallum, offers chanters in a range of pitches, 466, 474, 477, 480, 482 if I remember right.

Also a number of current makers offer chanters in A 440. If your piper friend got one of these you wouldn’t have to do anything because his tunes would come out in A Mixolydian, D major, etc.

So anyhow if he got a chanter in 466 yes you could tune up a halfstep.

But if he has a normal modern chanter pitching in around 478 to 480 you’ll need to be a quartertone between B flat and B.
