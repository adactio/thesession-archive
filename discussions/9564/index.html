What is your ‘Favourite Session Pub’ - & why?

We all spend hours slabbering on & on & on here about how to play at sessions, & how to behave but I just wondered what, in your opinion, makes not just a good Session Pub but a really excellent, 1st class Session Pub?

No doubt this has been covered before, but as we all start thinking about Summer holidays, perhaps it might be an appropriate time for each of us to sing the praises of our own favourite session pubs to give nomad musicians some good ideas.

Top of my list has to be ‘SANDY BELLS’, Edinburgh:
https://thesession.org/links/index/search?name=Sandy+Bells+
Firstly because that’s where it all started for me, but mainly because it is such a cracking session pub & has been for nearly forty years, not a bad record.
It was the only real session pub in Edinburgh, back in the early 70s, & every touring musician would call in, & I’m sure they still do, so just about anybody you care to mention on the trad or folk scene has been in there & there have been many wonderful sessions.
Well known regulars in those early days for me included all the Boys of the Lough, Phil & Johnny Cunningham, Dougie McLean & all of Jock Tamson’s Bairns, to name but a few, plus of course a whole host of less well known names who have been the mainstay of the place down through the years, & names like Peter McClements, Adam Jack, Harry Lawson, Freddy Thompson & Willie Beaton spring to mind.
There is so much musical history to that place that it has to rank as one of ‘THE’ best session pubs anywhere!

Second, would have to be my Friday night haunt for the past 14 years, ‘The HOUSE of MCDONNELL’, Ballycastle:
http://www.causewaymusic.co.uk/ballysess.html
It is more or less the same kinda shape as ‘Bells’, & stepping into this bar really is like stepping back in time.
It still has the old wooden room divider which kept the front licensed grocers area separate from the pub proper.
Like Bells there isn’t much room for the musicians but this leads to a close relationship between the musicians & punters which can lead to some great fun for all concerned.
Over the many years this has been a session pub, loads of great musicians have passed through, so the very walls have loads of stories to tell.
The owners, Tom & Eileen O’Neill just love the music & really enjoy meeting musicians so you are sure of a warm welcome when you dander in here, especially if you have a musical yolk under your arm!

So, to answer my own question, looking at my description of the above pubs, I would have to say the natural decor of the place comes into it as both have an olde worlde, unkempt charm about them.
They are small & pokey which leads to a cosey, intimate atmosphere.
Both have a long, long, long tradition of the music, & in both the punters really do enjoy not only listening to the musicians but also having the craic with them.
I’m not sure what’s happening in Bell’s these days but in Tom’s the owners really enjoy the music & thoroughly enjoy the craic with everyone & take great delight in welcoming new musicians to the pub - e.g. when Slainte walked in last week, he was very quickly handed the visitors book!

Re: What is your ‘Favourite Session Pub’ - & why?

My favourite session pub is alas no more.

The Liverpool bar had a great and worthy reputation as a music bar. The proprietor Tom Kelly was a huge fan of irish music; there were two sessions in the place on a Wednesday and a Sunday night. There was a long table for all the musicians but since the pub was quite small there was difficulty sometimes in fitting in the 20-30 musiacians who might show up!
There was a low ceiling and a great buzz about the place when you walked in; you felt like you were part of some secret society. Tom used to be very generous with the pints and sandwiches. At the end of the night when he was calling last orders, someone would shout “Tom for a song”. He would give us a couple of great songs (he’s a cracking singer) and we’d launch into the tunes again. Tom would finally clear us out at least an hour or two later than last orders. if he was in the mood he would signal the end of the night with a round of whiskeys.

The music in that place could sometimes be storming. Everyone there was totally dedicated to the making of music when the sets started up, and the craic in between was hilarious. Tom wasn’t the only singer either, and there were many memorable songs over the years.

It wasn’t the most comfortable place to sit; that was part of the crack - getting a good seat and moaning about the “scalphs” in your arse if you didn’t.

We’d as often as not get a crowd of French or Spanish tourists in who would get excited about hearing the music. I have these visions of beautiful tanned exotic women in the ugliest and most garish windcheaters you can imagine. they always got involved in the craic; they didn’t have much choice really.

If you wanted to take a break from playing, there were always some great characters propping up the bar and the staff had their own quirky humour too. There was a bit of a seafaring theme to the place as it was in the docks area - a pillar in the centre of the bar bound tightly with ships rope, a ships bell behind the bar, pictures and postcards of far-flung shores.

The land around the Liverpool was bought up bit by bit until Tom’s was the only premises standing - this lone three storey building resolutely ignoring the “progress” around it. You could even see where the floors of the adjacent buildings had been.

Finally the place got knocked down. I believe Tom is now managing Kelly’s Cellars, another fine music bar in the city centre, after a few interim ventures. The musicians have moved the “Liverpool sessions” to the John Hewitt. It isn’t the same. I still can’t believe that place is gone!

I’m sure there were some awful nights there but for the life of me I can’t remember them.

Pass the tissues *sniff* :)

Re: What is your ‘Favourite Session Pub’ - & why?

The sessions in Ireland have been the best I’ve experienced hands down, but to determine my favorite would be very difficult. My favorite in Dublin would have to be the Cobblestone. Every night there’s a great session, and every weekend is like a mini-festival. Some of the best players are regulars, and just about every other player of note has passed through. It also has the added benefit of being owned by a man who himself loves traditional music, as well as a patronage of serious ITM fans.

I have to say the pub in Sligo that dervish members own is a nice one as well. The owners obviously love the music and have set up a nearly perfect space for tunes. It even has bar-front access to the drinks. Brilliant musicians are the regulars and many of Ireland’s best players pass through there as well. And the punters there are well versed in the finer points of the music.

Ennis has a great session going in several pubs, but my favorite there would have to be Brogan’s. Eoin and Desmond are holding it down and provide a great welcoming and easy-going atmosphere. The owner there is also a huge fan of the music.

The common elements between all of these sessions are, brilliant musicians, educated and attentive punters, and a publican that truly understands and loves the music. That’s what my favorite session is.

Re: What is your ‘Favourite Session Pub’ - & why?

No no no Conán - Tom’s old pub at 96 Short Strand was ‘redeveloped’ which was where he got the money for the Liverpool Bar.

There were some fantastic times there - tunes every Wednesday night [downstairs] and Saturday afternoon [upstairs].

The downstairs bar was a madhouse with very strange accoutrements all over the place. The tables were old Singer Sewing Machines, there were road signs from all over the world and even one of the ships lights from the ship that was used to run guns to the UVF in the early twenties [The Clyde Valley?].

One night a couple of guys even arrived with a 16 ft canoe which Tom asked them to take a hike with. It was later reported on police six on UTV that night as having been nicked from a house on the Malone Rd!

At the time of it’s hey day it was the worst of the troubles [mid 70’s] with sectarian killings going on all over the city but Tom’s 96 was a haven. Musicians and singers from both traditions were made very welcome even though it was in the republican heartland.

I remember many’s a day when there were maybe 15 or more musicians and not a paying punter about the place but Tom continued to lash the free drink at us - mighty days!!

I remember the McSherry’s being trooped in by their parents and then ‘instructed’ on what to play. Other characters included oul Tommy Maguire, Artie Fox, Frank Kelly. Musicians included Davy Maguire, Mickey Finnegan, Andy Dickson, Gerry Callaghan, Marcas O Murchu, Tommy Smith, Leslie Bingham, Brendan O’Hare, Brian Montague, Maurice McHugh, Mickey Horgan, Phil Boomer, Tom Hartley, Spooley Kelly, Ciaran Carson, Kieran Fitzpatrick, Synchopated Sid, Robbie Hannon, Terrie Brown, Dermy Diamond, Aidan Short, Ciaran Mackel, Brian Harte, …………………..

And of course Tom himself always gave us a song or two - those were the days sighhhhh :-)
