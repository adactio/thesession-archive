Added by Weejie on March 23rd, 2014

Are composers worth mentioning if you knowingly submit their tunes?

This is both an offshoot of a recent thread on compositions and the result of a member submitting tunes, knowing full well who composed them, but not even thinking it is worth giving the composer credit.

Does anyone think that composers of tunes aren’t worth mentioning if you submit their tunes?
I would be surprised if anyone does - apart from one person, at any rate.
I’m asking this question purely to find out if my high horse should be in its stable, and I’m overreacting.
IMO, it is taking a big enough liberty to post someone’s tune without prior permission. Not having the decency to acknowledge the composer is taking that liberty a stretch too far.

Re: Are composers worth mentioning if you knowingly submit their tunes?

Not only worth mentioning—absolutely must be mentioned. Stripping a composer’s name from a composition is unethical.

I totally agree that composers have to be mentioned and get credit from their work. I’m a composer myself.

In my honest opinion I think that stripping the composer’s name is unethical when there’s no reference to the procedence of the tune. In most of the cases of modern tunes, there’s only one recording available (usually made by the composer itself). Also, tunes use to be copyrighted so it’s unlikely they’ll appear in other recordings (unless they pay the composer or ask for permission). In this case, TheSession.org is well constructed because it does not only mention the composer, but promotes his recording (by just clicking in “There is/are X recording(s) of a tune by this name”).

The real problem comes when the tune gets popular or recorded many many times. In that case, it would be necessary to state who’s the original composer, just as a way to recognize his work —it’s not like he’s going to get royalties, at the end this is TheSession.org. In that case, a “Composer” field would be nice when submitting a tune (you can do it in abc notation). Anyway, the average folk musician cares enough to do some research himself and google the original composer as well as other data.

Re: Are composers worth mentioning if you knowingly submit their tunes?

Since we aren’t taking sides, then, I’ll say that in my opinion it is a matter not only of morals or etiquette, but also of respect -- respect for the composer, but also respect for the music. Rather than tunes being shovelled around like so many lumps of something to be sorted into piles, they should be treated as individuals, accorded the care and regard they deserve.
It is a bit discourteous, to say the least, to take someone’s artwork, photocopy it on a cheap machine, and stick it with all the others in a filing cabinet marked ‘art wot I have collected’. Also it is unreasonable to substitute an alternative name because you don’t know the original, or prefer a different one. It just makes life harder for the rest of us.
Apart from doing your utmost to ensure that a tune is submitted in ABC as close as you can get it to the way you heard it played -- or saw it written elsewhere -- you should (again, in my opinion) provide whatever background and/or information you are aware of as a way of maintaining the integrity of the music, and the individuality of the tune.
The composer’s name would of course, be included should it be known.

Re: Are composers worth mentioning if you knowingly submit their tunes?

I think the broader point here is that it’s always good to leave some kind of comment with a tune submission.

I don’t want this discussion to scare anyone away: don’t be put off submitting a tune for fear that it might have a composer that you don’t know about.

The important thing is that when you submit a tune, you provide some kind of context. That could be “I learned it from this recording….”, or “I learned it at this session…”, or “It was written by this composer…”, or even “I can’t remember where I first heard this.”

I know that some people are more reluctant to post comments than ABCs because English isn’t their first language, but honestly, any kind of comment that adds some context to a tune submission is always appreciated: don’t be shy.

JoseACerro, I take your point about newly-composed tunes that tend to only appear on one album (the composer’s), but even in that situation, I think it’s better to explicitly include that information in a comment rather than relying on the “Recordings of a tune by this name…” list.

Re: Are composers worth mentioning if you knowingly submit their tunes?

It is not a trivial topic.

Good manners requires a poster to give attribution. It is usually not a legal requirement. Leaving out the composer’s name is not automatically unethical; the ethics depends on the motives of the poster. Although it is unethical (and illegal in some cases) to take credit for work that is written by someone else.

These are is not just issues for ‘living’ composers. There are copyrights that survive the death of the composer. Respect for a person’s work does not end at the moment they take their last breath -- nor does it depend on whether you know they are alive or not.

Be careful to avoid spreading misattributions. This happens all the time, and just because your ‘heard’ that so-and-so composed something, does not mean it is correct. There are plenty of examples of things getting into print, and it becoming the general knowledge based on misattribution.

This thread ended three years ago, but I can’t resist a personal anecdote related to that of Jim Dorans, Fiddleologist: He had posted his composition “Mister Twister” on thesession but without a composer credit; then later: “today one of my students said ”I fancy learning this one“, and roughly played a few bars of the tune. I was surprised to hear it , and asked him where he had heard it, and he said he only knew it from the playing of another fiddler, nothing more.”
My anecdote from 1973: Jean Redpath, Scottish folk singer then teaching singing for Wesleyan University at her home once a week in Middletown, Connecticut, went off to Folk Legacy Records to record an album. She came back raving over two songs she’d heard from Folk Legacy bosses Sandy & Caroline Paton: “You left your footprints on my stomach when you walked out of my heart” and “The river of my tears washed out the bridge of my nose”. I told her that I composed those and had taught them to Sandy & Caroline. They surely mentioned that David Hughes composed them, but I was only one of about 8 people who’d been at Jean’s home workshops for about 6 weeks, so she didn’t make the connection with this David Hughes. BUT: I taught them to her, and some time later she sang “Footprints” at her Carnegie Hall concert and credited it to “my friend David Hughes” (as a friend of mine in the audience told me). Fine by me. I was less happy when American Telephone and Telegraph used my chorus in a TV ad in California around 1980 (I was in Japan, but friends told me), but I couldn’t be bothered to chase them on copyright matters. Surely many other composers have had similar experiences. Anyhow: Indeed, please ALWAYS credit a composer if known.
