Ok, thanks you very much to everyone, all this critiques would help me to improve. I’m going to sort all of this critiques for do some comments:

- Odd chord structures: yeah, I’m spanish and I know this tunes have some influencies of other folk musics and jazz (e.g. the B part of the first tune, which is a tipical “spanish cadence”: Im VII VI V), I like that! I do not want to be rude but, does anyone else feel injured for me to post that tunes? I remember others posts like mine, furthermore I consider my music quite influenced by irish music.

It takes guts to submit your own comps in this way, and to welcome criticism. It’s also good that you did it here instead of as some do, dumping them into the tune database. ‘New’ is also welcome there, but with consideration for the rest of us and the webmaster’s reasonable request for penance, see the FAQs under ‘Help’.

As to my opening comment, the tunes did nothing for me and the playing is what drew my attention.

“Need of flute-technic improvement (specially rhythm): Although I do not consider my flute technic as bad as ceolachan does” - - - there is no scale given as to ‘how bad’, but I stand by my original comment. Your playing is sloppy. You need to work on crispness not only in your ornamentation by your fingering too. It wouldn’t hurt for you to work through some tunes sans any ornaments and to just focus on fingering crisply the melody line, and defining the beat, bars and phrases… I stand by my original comment, my ‘opinion’. At least you can play a recognizeable melody, the ones you’ve given transcriptions for. But music, and this ‘dance’ music, is more than just a sequence of notes, and rhythm is more than just a basic beat, which you also did not define well. I wouldn’t have bothered to comment at all if I didn’t think that it might at least direct you to more important things about this music than your attempts at composition.

As finished previously, and with honest good will ~ for you, and always for the music ~ best of luck….

Re: 3 of my tunes

I “third” ceolachan’s comments. Everyone esteems their own “music” to a higher degree than others- our ego, especially when they’re starting out, and in the heart of the learning curve. Not everyone is qualified to offer good criticism, but to those who are qualified (i.e. ceolachan, et. al.) we should reserve a unique openness. The extent to which you improve your playing is directly proportional to extant to which you are chagrined by those in a position to chagrin. Such are the “growing pains” of learning. If we listen to our ego in the face of good criticism, the tunes will not improve.
“I do not consider my flute technic as bad as ceolachan does, I absolutely agree with this: I need to improve my style a lot!”
-I think you should consider what ceolachan is suggesting to improve your tunes. The player Carlos Nunez comes to mind. Even though he is from Galicia, and not of Irish stock, his tunes are sharp, crisp, and incorporate techniques we use all the time in ITM. Yet his style and his music are unique and “other-worldly.” Think of that as you improve upon your music. To reiterate, you’ve shown courage to post your compositions here. Keep working the tunes and stay open to good criticism. It will either make you better or bitter (worse).
