Best PA system for Ceilidh band?

My ceilidh band is looking to buy a PA system for gigs (we’ve just hired them until now) and I wondered if there’s any particular brand/features we should hunt for? Has anyone had any joy with personal mics? We have accordion, guitar, whistle, 2-3 fiddles and bodhran :)

↳ Posted by Twhistle on August 14th, 2014.

Re: Best PA system for Ceilidh band?

First add up how many channels you need on the mixing desk, then add at least two “spares” that’ll determine the minimum size of the desk - allow for any foreseeable additions to the band. Buy powered speakers to suit the maximum venue size & numbers you need to cover for the majority of your gigs. Marquees demand the most power … an example may be 2 x 400W 12“ + 2” Cabs (RCF/db/HK/QSC to name but a few) good for 200 people in a 25m Marquee. You shouldn’t need subs unless you want loads of Bodhran or Box Bass.

Decide who pays/owns it and what happens if someone leaves/joins the band.

Understand how to use it.

Decide where you’ll store it and who has to lug it around.

Re: Best PA system for Ceilidh band?

Excellent advice from Titch above. In particular:

“Decide who pays/owns it and what happens if someone leaves/joins the band.”

It sounds so simple, but it isn’t. OK, you buy the PA and divide the cost in equal proportions amongst the band members. A couple of years later the band is still going but someone decides to leave. Clearly they shouldn’t get all their money back as the gear will have depreciated in value during this period. But how much should they get back? This needs to be decided BEFORE you buy the PA. If there is no pre-agreed arrangement, there will be endless arguments and hassle! And what happens if a member of your band is temporarily unavailable and you use a deputy. Does the deputy have to pay to use the band’s PA?

Yes, marquees need the most power but unless you’re in marquees for most of your gigs, I think that you should be able to get away with less than 400w front-of-house speakers than Titch suggests. You’re not a heavy metal rock band!

Titch suggests powered (active) speakers. These do give a marginally better sound than passive speakers but they are a lot heavier and thus quite hard to lift onto the speaker stands. Extra cables are needed, too.

Don’t forget that you’ll need foldback speakers as well as the front-of-house ones.

Whereas mics are best for a “performance” band or for recordings, for a ceilidh band I would use instrument pickups.

Finally, don’t forget the “extras” e.g. an extension cable (in case the power socket is a long way from the stage) and a small toolkit containing screwdrivers, pliers and spare fuses for emergency repairs.

Re: Best PA system for Ceilidh band?

The “long pole” PA’s are small line arrays, the have very wide dispersion characteristics. These are becoming more and more “popular” as one advertised advantage is that they can be set up behind you and work as Main and Monitor. Bose, HK, Adam Hall, Fohn all spring to mind. I personally found that with Bose the sound required a lot of processing (for fiddle) compared to the “flat” characteristic of more traditional boxes. Also for a Ceilidh band having the caller blaring out behind you will be a nightmare - we mix our caller twice as loud as any musician and do not have him on monitors. Line arrays are generally more expensive.

Regarding weight of powered speakers - with the now common digital power supplies and Neodymium magnets this is no longer a consideration. 400W is about typical for a good quality 12“ powered cabinet. No saving in cost going much smaller unless you can get away with 10”. It’s much much better to run a system at 50% than flat out, these one box systems will struggle in the mid range when pushed hard.

I agree (obviously) with pickups on the fiddles. Any mics should be close mics mounted on the instruments.

All your gear will need PAT certification.

Not a legal requirement in the UK - although may venue managers (e.g. local authorities and hotels) are now demanding it.

Public liability insurance is also advisable (and many venue managers are making this a requirement, too).

If your gigs are small ones (say, up to 150 dancers) I still maintain that 200W front-of-house speakers should be adequate. That’s all we use for such gigs and we rarely need to turn up the gain beyond 50%. For the occasional large gig, you could hire bigger cabs. The larger fee should cover the cost of the hire. A smaller PA is easier to transport and store, if space is a consideration.

Re: Best PA system for Ceilidh band?

Linear arrays have advantages and disadvantages. For singer/guitarists they have a big advantage in that their polar pattern is very shallow, which means most of the sound is directed at the audience, not bounced off the ceiling, which reduces reverb and also means you need less overall power. On the downside they have poor bass response due to the small cone sizes, and whilst that doesn’t really affect singer/guitarists or many traditional bands, But if your band includes any bass instruments or drums you will probably need to use them in conjunction with a sub.

As a ceilidh band you are probably going to find yourselves working in extreme acoustic conditions - church halls and gyms whith the acoustics of public toilets, where sound keeps bouncing around for three days after you’ve stopped playing, and outdoors/marquees, where there are no acoustics at all. For most outdoor events 400Wrms will be more than enough (when comparing specs make sure you understand the difference between watts RMS and ‘Japanese watts’ or peak music power - an amp described as 400W peak music power actually produces the same maximum power as one rated at 100Wrms). I use a 500Wrms system, and even for outdoor events with 400 people I’ve never had to turn it up to full wick. But outdoors/in a tent you will have to use a bit of reverb to stop it sounding dead - if the mixer you choose doesn’t have built in FX you may need to buy an extra unit.

At the other extreme, in church halls and gymnasiums you are going to have to do everything you can to minimize the natural reverb. One thing that helps a lot here is if you can get speakers with adjustable pole mounts so that they can be angled down - pointing the speakers down a few degrees so that most of the sound is absorbed by the audience instead of bouncing off the end wall makes an enormous difference, I’ve never understood why all manufacturers don’t make their speakers adjustable.

The other thing that is essential is an equalizer on the output. It doesn’t matter where it is, it might be built into the mixer or the power amp/monitor, but if you are mixing and matching you may well land up with a mixer and amp neither of which has an equalizer built in, in which case you will need to add one as a separate unit.

It depends what instruments you’ll be using, but in my experience your main preoccupation, once you’ve got the kit, ownership and management sorted out, will be dealing with problems like feedback. If you’re using acoustic instruments with pickups, expect a low feedback threshold. Conventional monitors can make the problem worse. We never use them.

Also, remember that, unless you can afford a full time sound man, whoever owns the kit will probably want to set up the sound mix. If you don’t stay on good terms with him/her, your playing may never be heard again.

Re: Best PA system for Ceilidh band?

The number of watts doesn’t actually mean much, unless you also know the efficiency of the speaker system. A smaller amplifier with efficient speakers can be louder than a huge amp with inefficient speakers.

And also bear in mind that it’s not a linear relationship - even with the same speakers, a 400W system is not “twice as loud” as a 200W system.

It’s just like car engines - my car has an engine that’s nearly twice as big as a formula 1 race car. But guess which one’s faster….

Go to a good dealer, that isn’t tied to any particular manufacturer. Explain your requirements, and ask them to let you try some systems out. Once you find one you like, ask them if you can hire something similar for a gig to test it, with the hire price being refundable against purchase.

Speak to other bands in your area and try out their kit.

Make sure you have some spare mixer channels - even if it’s only to plug in an iPod for half-time music, or accommodating an extra singer.

Buy something that’s sized for 80% of your “regular gigs” - don’t get huge amps and speakers just because you have one big gig that comes along every 2 years. For that one, you hire extra.

Remember to budget for microphones, stands, cables and odds and ends. The costs mount up. Also, buy some good cases and covers for everything, to protect your investment.

Label it all clearly with your name and phone number - especially “accidentally liftable” bits like cables, mics, DIs.

Think carefully about transport and storage, and weight. Bringing a PA into a venue before a gig generally isn’t a hassle. Taking it out afterwards, when the punters are drunk, everyone is tired, and you all want to get home, is a pain - so minimise your work.

And don’t buy cheap disco rubbish. Go to a musicians shop rather than Maplins or a DJ shop.

Re: Best PA system for Ceilidh band?

I run a Yamaha EMX500 12 channel ($1500) which puts out 500 watts a side, enough to run two FOH and two fold back speakers for most gigs and for bigger gigs add extra small powered mixer to run fold back while Yamaha runs 4 FOH. I’ve used this with various unpowered speakers and lately with my JBL 15" powered speakers. Plenty of channels for vocals and instruments, can add sub mixer anyway to increase this. My first desk was Yamaha EM300 which was great for 20yrs and the latest is lighter, has more features and power but just as straightforward and flexible for my needs.

My band is usually 3 piece but sometimes 4 or 5, with each musician playing more than one instrument. My guitar goes straight into the desk, as do fiddle, banjo, mandolin etc. I bankrolled the initial purchase of the mixer with the band paying $50 gig to use it with communally owned speakers. When people moved on I bought speaker shares out and ended up as sole owner. We split our fee equally after $50 comes out for using my PA. I can use the PA whenever I need (I use it for side projects and school concerts etc). After 35yrs of doing it this way it has simplified things and been a good financial investment (and tax deduction).

Shure SM58 microphones are an industry standard for vocals - cheaper mics can be less robust and less resistant to feedback. Speakers are best on stands for FOH and 15" speakers give a fuller sound.

Hope this helps.

I would suggest that no matter what you decide upon for speakers, wattage etc, get the most channels in the mixer that you can afford. ….however, if your aspirations only go as far as village hall kind of gigs and you’re not going to have a sound man, keep the number of instruments/channels to a reasonable minimum, otherwise you’ll still be doing (or arguing over) the sound checks when the audience is going home.

Re: Best PA system for Ceilidh band?

There is a lot of good stuff in this thread, however its probably good to look at the original question, for a band with accordion, guitar, whistle, 2-3 fiddles and bodhran & has anyone had any joy with personal mics?

For any band of this size fold-back will be essential if your all going to keep in time with each other in all but the smallest room. And with all your bands instruments, feedback in this is going to be your biggest problem - don’t skimp on the flold-back system as if you do it will soon come back to bite you. Regarding the use of cheap personnel mics, whilst it seems like a good idea can soon be not be such a good idea with the fiddlers waving the instrument around after each set has completed, and the inevitable feedback howl. Better to have a fixed mic if your new to the game or better still get a decent pickup/microphone and know how to control the things, fiddles are designed to be loud acoustically and can easily lead to feedback problems in my experience. Our fiddler uses a ‘Bridge’ violin and we have very few problems with it but you pays your money and you take your choice. The same feedback issues can be said of all the other instruments to a lesser degree. If your still going down the personnel mic route my only other comment is avoid the ‘Microvox’ brand - overpriced IMHO, we used one on the Flute for a while and had nothing but problems.

Our band set up has just updated the fold-back and now use 2 x 10inch active monitors (Studiospares) and we have a dual Graphic Equalizer to control feedback - you need to know how to use it (we’ve a sound man on the case so the tweeks can soon be made mid tune).

Microphones - don’t bother with the cheap stuff - you get what you pay for, our band uses Sennheiser mics, but any good brand will do but expect to pay £100+ each not £30! We also use an even more expensive Sennheiser radio mic for the caller, but as she likes to go up front and in the audience a good mic is essential to avoid feedback in the main PA (£3-400 ish from memory).

With regards to the main PA a mixer with enough channels (with a few spares) is a must and any good mixer will do (we use an Allen & Heath unit), but I would advise to get one with Phantom power to power DI boxes and not use battery powered pre-amps - avoid these like the plague. In our band the battery power in the two Fylde instruments (Octave Mandola & Bouzouki) has both caught us out mid gig - the batteries now get changed annually even if they don’t need it! We use Behrinnger DI’s on the advice of our (professional) sound man as the ‘best for the job’ so not everything from this particular company is rubbish if you believed some of the posts on here!

Finally we have a 500W per channel main amp and two 12" speakers for the front of house, we’ve not had the need for anything bigger (yet) and would hire if needed.

Now if you go through the above there is a tidy sum of loot for the above, and we have not got the above overnight, and the system has grown as we have gone on (with a few mistakes on the way) and some better buying decisions would of saved us some money but we’re all learning. As a band we typically put by 10% of our takings to pay for the gear and other incidentals (web site, PI cover etc etc.) with loans from band members to purchase the bigger items until the kitty catches up. Good luck on your journey. . . . .
