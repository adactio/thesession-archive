Different people have different reasons for becoming multi-instrumentalists, and I’m interested in knowing why some of you have chosen to pick up a second, third, or more instruments.

Re: Multi-instrumentalists

I’m just barely a “multi-instrumentalist.” Fiddle is my main instrument for Irish music, but I’ve played banjo, mandolin, and guitar longer than fiddle, so it wasn’t much of a reach to add whistle and flute. I wish I had more time to play them.

Besides being fun, it’s nice to hear the same tunes come from a different voice. Whistle is just a no-brainer if you play this music--you can take it everywhere and it’s not hard to learn (just not simple to master). And flute is just dead sexy, how can you resist it? :-)

I also enjoy the big differences in ergonomics (or lack thereof) among the various instruments. And knowing something of how wind instruments work after so many years of playing strings makes me a better session player--better able to appreciate the confines my flute, whistle, and pipe friends deal with when thinking about range and those pesky F and Gdorian tunes…..

Re: Multi-instrumentalists

My main reason for taking up the instruments I play is that I heard them played at some point, liked the sound they made and wanted to make the same sound myself - and, as Earl says, it’s fun.

There were other specific reasons why I play the particular instruments I play. I began playing piano when I was small, because there was one in the house, because my big sister was learning and I wanted to be in on it. I began playing guitar, I suppose, because it was ‘cool’. I began playing mandolin and whistle because they happened to be in the house, and I was getting interested in ‘folk’ music (which, in my mind, at the time, included ‘traditional’ music of various kinds and more ‘contemporary’ music). I never really ‘took up’ the tenor banjo - I just used to have a go on other people’s banjos in sessions until, eventually, I decided to buy one of my own. I began playing the fiddle because I felt restricted by the limitations of the mandolin. Likewise for the piano accordion (although I play klezmer on it, not Irish trad).

In retrospect, I find playing more than one instrument useful in that it gives me a broader perspective on the music and offers different ways of interpreting a tune, which can be carried across from one instrument to another. In particular, playing the whistle (which I have been playing as a ‘second’ instrument concurrently with the mandolin for about 18 years) has, I think, given me a fuller understanding of traditional phrasing and articulation than I would have got through playing the mandolin alone.

Re: Multi-instrumentalists

I picked up whistle because it was cheap and easy, I liked “irish” music, and I felt that at 21, it was my last opportunity to not be completely a-musical my whole life through. I then fell in love with irish music and decided I liked the sound of the flute.

A few years later, I wondered what it was like playing an instrument that could make chords and my favorite sound of those was concertina… of course I still have an accordeon reeded concertina - but at some point I will “upgrade” to the sound I love so much.

I really like the intuitiveness of concertina (and of a second instrument after some musical experience) - some of the time, I know, like on flute, exactly what note I’m playing - other times, I just sing in my head and it comes out on concertina, with no clue what keys I’m pressing.
