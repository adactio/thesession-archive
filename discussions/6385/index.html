But it’s only music, after all…!

Last night, a couple of us were talking about the fact that this stuff is only music, and while that’s in some ways quite a lot, it’s also just a little. I’ve heard stories of people divorcing spouses and breaking up other happy relationships for it, suffering great mental anguish over their perceived inabilities at it, jumping through hoops to be accepted at their local session, what have you.

Okay, so it makes people momentarily happy. Can it save a life? Maybe, I’ve heard some rather touching stories about that. Can it “make a difference”? Depending on what you think that means, it might. Is it worth divorcing a spouse or breaking up with a significant other over? If the relationship wasn’t right in some other way and the music thing is more a symptom of a bigger problem, maybe. is it worth agonizing over your abilities at playing it? Guess it depends on your priorities.

Anybody got any stories or other thoughts on whether it’s ‘just music’, or whether it’s ‘The Music’?

I don’t know the answer, but I often wonder about these things. So I’m going to continue playing under the delusion that it will matter, and that someday I will be teaching aspiring young pipers and making a difference in their lives.

We told stories about and on each other, other friends, and other sessions.

I got to put a new team dress on one of our little dancers and watch her eyes light up at the sight of herself in her new dress. She got 4 seconds and two thirds at the feis, and excitedly told our TCRG that she was convinced it was because of her new dress. She told her mother that the team dress was extra special because I made it. (Never mind that I make all of those team dresses. :) )

I got to chat for an altogether too short time with concertina maker Bob Tedrow, a member here, and his wife Klari, and his adorable daughter Alex, who took a first in the music competition and did very well indeed at the feis. Hopefully we’ll get to have some tunes when they come back for the Estes Park feis in September.

I had a chance to have quite a long chat with another new acquaintance, a lovely woman named Julie. Both of our fathers have passed away in the last couple of months, and we spoke about our common experiences at length. Julie brought up a couple of things about our situation that I hadn’t thought of, too, which was brilliant. (And in fact it was Julie who inspired this thread in the first place.)

We attended the Last Night’s Fun gang’s usual session on Sunday night at The irish Snug. It was a lovely night, not too loud in the bar, great tunes and good crack. An Irish friend, not a muso, whose father passed away within hours of mine was there at the bar. She’s going back to Ireland to cope with family matters for two months, so I’m glad that I got to sit with her and chat about all sorts of stuff before she went.

I got to read this thread, too, and your guys’s thoughts on the matter. Can’t discount that.

The weekend was crowded with things like these, and most of them happened because of this stuff. I’m sure that if I didn’t play music, there would have been other things just as memorable to take up my time. But I can’t imagine a better grounding for things like these.

It’s all about people and how you live your life, right enough. It’s only music, yes, and so of little importance in the grand scheme of things, but at the same time it’s of supreme importance because of the events it supports.

Anyway, that’s my story, and I’m sticking by it. :) Thanks for sharing, guys.
