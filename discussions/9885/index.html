Sessions that die

Seems to be an inordinate number lately when you look at the “sessions” comments. Or are people just being unusually conscientious about letting us know?
Why do sessions die?

I think I can guess at some common reasons.
1. Started in a pub that’s not all that enthusiastic in the first place.
2. Too many attendees (players or listeners) who don’t spend enough money in the pub but fill the place up, taking seats and space from regulars. Or worst of all, “shushing” them
3. Started where the musicians and those who would like trad music are not really pub goers in the first place. Inevitable clash of cultures follows when folkies turn up on appointed night. Especially if they take up the most prominent or favourable positions in the pub
4. Free drink. This might upset people, but I think this is one of the worst things you can do to a session. Yes, a couple of drinks on the house to stalwart players now and then, or when it’s a particularly good night, at management’s discretion. But as a right, and all the time - no. Attracts all the wankers and pisses off the staff.
5. Strict rules - can make some players feel unwelcome, and usually fails to transmit any feeling of bonhomie or good crack to the general punters who then start to agitate against the session
6. No rules - let’s idiots take over.
7. Not enough players committed to turn up every week so it dwindles and dies. This can sometimes be because the pub is too far from where any of the sessioneers live - mor eimportant than you might think. You only have to have a situation where someone turns one night up expecting a session and finds the pub empty for them never to come back.
8. Held at a time when not enough can make it regularly - tricky cos most city pubs will only have a sesh on a Monday or Tuesday when a lot of folk are tired and have work the next day

Most of these points are aimed at players’ behaviour rather than management or pub patrons - but if we want a session to continue, it’s in our hands and there’s no point in blaming others. It’s just a bunch of guesses. Thinking about the pubs I know of with long-lasting sessions, it seems to have grown organically perhaps because musicians frequented the pub anyway so the atmosphere was already there.

Re: Sessions that die

Out of interest, where are all the people who say they get drinks playing (in what country)? Round the sessions I’ve been to (and I haven’t yet gone on an Ireland session tour - Shetland’s first on my list) the sessions that have tended to live on the longest are the ones where it’s a simple matter of some musicians, where the number of listeners (outside of those with instruments who just aren’t playing) is grossly outweighed by the number of players and players are in a public lounge type room which is reasonably quiet and people playing can the music (if not directly themselves should it have lots of people). In an environment where the musicians compete with those who aren’t playing for volume, space etc then the session becomes more likely to fail.

The idea of “punters” has a couple of problems, firstly if it’s not a sit-down-gig they will tend to talk amongst themselves, and deliberately or otherwise create a battle for the loudest volume, the second being if the management is principally interested in keeping the paying drinkers happy, if the session passes enters a quieter phase halfway through (for example), the punters, only “hearing” the music when it’s loud and fast start to talk, and the it’s not easy to get things going again from there, or worse the management demand that the session doesn’t hit that phase as more people leave the pub during it.

The only exception to this I’ve found, is where the punters are musicians, in large numbers (and without instruments, so they can be classed as punters) and are principally there to be sociable.
