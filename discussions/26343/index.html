Well there may be no “land untouched by human hands” left apart from a little flow country and dods n sods of various bogs etc but old mother nature has a startling way of reclaiming what was once tethered.

I defy anyone to go to the middle of the rannoch moor and tell me the landscape is not natural. People have shaped and hewn dwellings folds etc but these are a but a carbuncle in what is still a desolate wilderness by any standards. Just try walking across it!

It was once thought that the expanse of rannoch was a huge forest of bonny cally pine felled and burnt by nasty man over millennia to create this bog. Recent research now shows that after the initial spread of birch wood the bog took over and there is no evidence that it was ever widely forested. So apart from the black water dam & the west highland line, the ground remains largely as it was when ranged over by bear beaver & wolf.

There are some interesting spots on hills near me where burns have cut through bogs showing the complete cross sections of the peat down to the bedrock. In a layer around 6" thick above the bedrock and below the peat, in some places, you’ll find the 10,000 year old birch wood squashed almost flat and perfectly preserved, the first growth after the glaciers of the last ice age receded, still there. It still burns like the fresh stuff when dry.

Buzzards & Eagles, actually look quite similar in flight if there is nothing to provide a sense of scale. So the easy way if looking up at a soaring buzzard/eagle is to look at the wing tips;

Eagle has a jagged (splayed) wing tip. If you stretch your arms out and spread your fingers, that’s your eagle. If it’s a long way up the wing tip can look rather square.

Buzzards have a rounded wing tip with it’s jaggedness confined to the back edge of the wing.

Of course if there is something to provide scale, as ethical blend points out, above an eagle is: “surprisingly huge, when seen in the flesh” and therefore identification is easy.
