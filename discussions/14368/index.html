Re: How to Box Clever?

Tuning:

A box has a number of reeds - usually 2 or 3? In PAs you can get more though. When you talk about ‘wet’ or ‘dry’ tuning you are talking about how closely tuned the reeds are to each other. They are usually tuned slightly differently from each other and this gives that accordian sound. If they are wet tuned they are far apart. If they are dry tuned they are close together. Wet tuned boxes tend to sound louder than dry ones because you are getting that broader sound. If it is too wet though then it will sound crap!

Re: How to Box Clever?

There are people who make very nice music on the D/G but it’s not nearly as common in Irish music as the B/C and C#/D tunings, dare I say “for a reason”…?

As for wet or dry, BC or C#D, it’s really a matter of opinion. Listen around and see what kind of style you like, then work from there. Even on a single box, there is a massive spectrum of different styles that could be adopted while playing it; something that’s far more about the player than the instrument. There have been heaps of discussions about b/c vs c#d that might be worth a look especially for learning which recording artists use which system.

Re: How to Box Clever?

>why does nearly everyone play B/C? Is it because it’s the done thing or is it better for getting runs of notes weithout changing from push to pull too much?

I think that’s it. B/C has, from the bottom, D-E on the draw, E-F#-G on the push, A-B-c#-d-e on the draw, and so forth up, with some other ways to be clever as well. I’m finding the main drawback of my particular instrument is the awkward layout of the basses, but I’m learning to do some things with them.

You’ll do a lot more push-pull on a C#/D instrument, but the layout is more transparent. In fact, as I noted in another discussion, it’s almost exactly the same (on the D row) as a D harmonica, which means you can learn tunes on a $20 Special 20, and they’ll sound pretty good. Same limitation as the melodeon, though.

One thing that might not be immediately obvious as a factor would be the action of the keys. Are you looking for a more Joe Burke or Johnny Box (aka Johnny B. Connolly) sound? A lighter setup will allow you to be more fluid, a stiffer action will encourage that rhythmic sound Joe Burke sound, or at least that’s how it works out in my case.

Again, trying a bunch of instruments, if possible, will be your best guide.

It’s possible to get runs of notes on a DG box , because so many notes are duplicated on the two rows, but in opposite directions. For instance, the 7th bar of Trip to Sligo I play all on the pull,alternating from the D to the G row.it’s also possible to get rolls that go all in one direction by crossing rows in this way. That typical Morris musician sound tends to be because they are sticking mainly on one row or the other, (and to be fair, a lot of English tunes work fine like that.) To play Irish music on a DG you need to use a lot of row crossing.

I decided on a B/C box, because even though it didn’t lay out quite like the D harmonica I like to play, it seemed a sensible system, with one row being the equivalent of “white keys” on a piano and the other row the “black keys.” (that analogy doesn’t hold up extremely well, but it kind of gives you an idea what I was thinking) And if you play music other than Irish, it is a bit easier to play the BC box in non-sharp keys like F.

But I find it very easy to play, reliable, and well constructed. There are a lot of other good brands out there, and I am sure people will chime in with other examples.
Good luck, and enjoy the world of free reeds!!!!!

Re: How to Box Clever?

Things I wish I’d been told before I started doing box:

listen to other’s boxes to decide wet/dry. I’d recommend dry.

stick to 2 voice - as less greedy for air than 3 and lighter

8 basses are plenty for the left hand side and not much used by many players anyway. Check that the air key is in a position comfortable for you to use readily during tunes.

get the best box you can afford - that is to say, reeds that speak readiest, bellows that are least stiff, and keys that are lightest/lowest/smoothest.

Flat closed keyboard work best for me. Rotella-adjustable l/h straps are really useful as straps stretch over time and fit is important.

I use two shoulder straps and a backstrap - it keps the right hand side very stable at speed and works and feels the same sat down or stood up.

Get B/C tuning. You get slightly more than the whole fiddle range up to top C, fully chromatic, on a 21-key box. Playing in every key is difficult for a start, but there’s no better system IMHO.

Good luck

Re: How to Box Clever?

Runs of notes are one thing but don’t forget that Irish tunes are full of arpeggios. Yes you can play a D scale with 3 bellows movements on a BC as against 5 on a C#D.

But look at this phrase from The Cameronian:

A2 FA DAFA | GFEF GAdB | A2 FA DAFA | GBAG FD D2 |

By my (paper) count this comes out at minimum 9 bellows movements on C#D and 15 on BC.

Both systems of course have much to commend them, but there is the swing of the fashion pendulum and I think this is going against BC these days. The enthusiasm with which they defend their system would seem to bear this out, as would the number of BC players going over to C#D or playing tunes in press-draw style on their records. :-)

My advice would be to try to borrow a box of either stripe for a few days, and then experiment with the fingering systems of each on it and see which makes most sense to you.

Nick Spencer - You are correct that the key of A on a B/C consists of nearly all draw notes and if you haven’t got proper control of the bellow your left hand can end up out in the street. When | started playing and nobody knew much about music, playing a tune in ‘Key of A’ was known as ‘playing on the draw’. A good example of that was ‘The Athol Highlanders’ we all tried to play it in ‘Key of A’, because that was how we heard it played by the Scottish Bands. There are two notes repeated on the B/C box. Note B is a ‘draw note’ on the C row of buttons and a ‘press note’ on the B row of buttons. Note E is a ‘press note’ on the C row and a ‘draw note’ on the B row. Both notes are part of the ‘A’ Scale and by using them accordingly you can have good control over the bellows. I find that the reel ‘The Boys of Malin’ which is usually played in ‘Key of A’ is a good testing ground for any two row player,

Re: How to Box Clever?

Don’t get too hung up on whether all the notes of a scale are in the same direction. It really doesn’t make much difference - and it’s the in-out nature of the instrument that gives it an interesting signature. All notes in one direction tends to produce an overly legato sound, which is the thing most people don’t like about the piano accordion.
Believe me, the BC can be played pretty fast… ;-)
