What do we take for granted? What are you thankful for?

Added by Jerone on November 19th, 2015

What do we take for granted? What are you thankful for?

I was scrolling through my Facebook newsfeed and found a meme that states, “It took me 18 years to realise the alphabet is Twinkle Twinkle Little Stars”. Learning that there’s no poetic connection, I realize the quoter is talking about the melody. I’m thinking, “Wait, the melody? I’ve always known this. I figured this out years ago.”. I then notice my friend mentioning that she’s just now figuring it out herself, and she’s older than I am.

As the pattern of my mind so routinely goes, I start asking myself, “As a musician, what all have I taken for granted?”. But this isn’t the first time I’ve asked myself this. When meeting people while busking, or teaching my younger students or kids I’m mentoring, I often think about just how uncommon musical knowledge can be.

Even as the topic of race and equality here in America has been the subject of many discussions amongst friends and peers, talking with them about white privilege and male privilege has caused me to wonder if I have any special privileges as a musician.

Personally, being a musician is a major part of my identity, just as much as me being an English speaker, or being American is. It’s not only how I see myself, but how others see me as well. Just as well as I could be judged for being from one of the worst cities in the state of Ky, I could be judged for being a fiddle player rather than a violinist. Or for choosing to be a mentor rather than being in a rock band. You can’t even talk about me without mentioning music in some way, or you won’t have much to say.

So besides the obvious free beers at the pub, what privileges do you think we have as musicians? What could we be taking for granted? Or maybe an easier question, in the spirit of Thanksgiving season, as a musician, what are you thankful for?

I guess for me, I took for granted the subtle freedom of being able to go out and play music for strangers and make new friends. And I’m thankful that throughout my life, I’ve always received support from all sides and that very thing is what kept me playing.

Re: What do we take for granted? What are you thankful for?

I’m certainly thankful for the musical community and friendships that I’ve developed over the years of playing. The community is what drew me to the music in the first place, and a vast portion of my social interaction with other human beings is based around the music.

But the community, and especially the musical connection that I feel with other individuals, is often what I take for granted, too. Several times, I have felt the pain of true loss when a player I took for granted suddenly wasn’t there anymore. It has happened to me because the person moved away, it has happened because of physical injury and the player can’t play anymore, and it has happened because of death.

So I do try to take the time to reflect on the good times with other players, and ignore all the quirkiness and social awkwardness that invariably comes with the odd little social scene that surrounds Irish traditional music.

Dr Silver Spear -“ On the unhappiness-to-pleasure scale, for the ten years I have been playing Irish music, the former wins. I wish I felt otherwise, but it is what it is.”
I felt so sad when I read that. Because I’ve appreciated most of your posts to this site, and it seems crazy to me that ITM could possibly mean unhappiness…. (or have I misunderstood?) I’m one of those people for whom playing is an unrivalled source of joy, always, every time, satisfaction guaranteed as it were…
But it did make me think back to a time when I’d reached a bit of a flat plateau with my whistle playing which seemed adequate but not much more, new tunes didn’t inspire me etc. What got me out of that impasse was the discovery almost by chance of another instrument - the harp! It just gave a whole new perspective on the music - harmonic rather than just melodic, and actually gave me lots of insights into the structure (chords and all) of these tunes I’d been playing. That was 15 years ago. I haven’t really looked back - I still play loads of whistle, whenever I can - at home, at gigs, and at sessions - and harp too when it seems appropriate. I don’t play harp particularly well!! But in terms of pleasure that doesn’t seem to matter too much, I can still spend hours playing and emerge with a smile on my face.
