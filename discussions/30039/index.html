As if the expression of the four notes doesn’t count for anything. OR better yet, those that think we need to be in tune when we arrive.

I believe the harpers had a said “expression” in tuning up?

Re: d e g a

G is actually a better note to tune to on the flute if tuning to a box or tuner. If tuning to a fiddle then tune to an A since that is the convenient open string. (A on the flute is a note that is too easy to bend out of tune unintentionally since the relevant hole is usually quite small. And D is often an exceptional note not always directly in tune with the otehrrs without special blowing.)

However, I know approximately where 440Hz is on my flutes and my playing in typical weather conditions so I usually know roughly where to start. And I know how it develops over the first couple of sets. If I find myself having to lip consistently in one direction or another then the general note of the session is obviously not A=440Hz and I adjust accordingly.

I actually quite like tuning to a fiddle if there is a reliable one available.

Outside the session, the best way I know to tune is to plonk a device which can generate a loud sustained A=440Hz note on the table and get everyone to play the notes of a D Major chord along with it - you can soon tell who is out of tune… And get whistle players to tune their upper octave too!

↳ Posted by Richard D Cook on June 14th, 2012.

Re: d e g a

In my session I like to tune to the general sound, because there are always at least two boxes (D/Gs), so they are the ones that control the pitch.

The great violinist, Ruggiero Ricci said when he was off-stage in the green room waiting to go on to play a concerto he would warm up and tune to the general pitch of the orchestra in the hall while they were playing an overture. He would then re-tune very slightly sharp to allow for the possibility of his gut strings going flat in the humidity of the hall. If he was performing out of doors, like at the Hollywood Bowl, he would tune very slightly flat because the gut strings would tend to go sharp in the dry open air.
