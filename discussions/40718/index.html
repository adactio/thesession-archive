Instruments for Busking

Hi all,

I’m a music teacher in Ireland looking to get into busking. Been playing pop/classical music on a variety of instruments over the years. I’d like to find a balance between my own enjoyment, pleasing others through music and making a few bob.

Recently been learning trad music. I was just wondering if ye could advise on the following for trad busking;

1. What is, in your opinion, the most profitable musical instrument to busk with?
2. Do you think it’s better to busk solo or in a group?
3. What instrument do you think people would like to hear on the street?

Thanks

Re: Instruments for Busking

(Responding to OP and all posts.)

1. Don’t play the guitar, unless you’re really really good. Like, classically trained, finger-picking good. The most profitable instrument is the instrument you are best at. No joke. People love music, and they’ll love you if you play good music.

2. I would say it’s better to busk in a duo. Three members or more and the money has to be split too many different ways. As a solo act, I don’t get a lot of attention, unless I’m the only one in the general area, and duos attract a lot more attention. Simply because they are louder and have more opportunity for variety in their sets. Duos make a lot more money than I do, and I think the “divide by 2” is worth it.

3. People respond very well to the Fiddle. I street perform playing piano and fiddle, and I do well with both. People complain about guitars. One of my personal top compliments received is, “I’m glad you’re not playing a guitar”. Play something easy on the ears. People tip on sound quality much more than they tip on instrument novelty. If they don’t like how it sounds, they aren’t going to tip you because you’re playing a concertina instead of a fiddle. Also consider endurance, and how long you can comfortably play your chosen instrument.

4. You don’t have to initiate pedestrians. That is a myth. I don’t talk to pedestrians at all unless I’m “responding”. If you have a tip recepticle, and you can be seen and heard, and people like you, they will tip you. It would be good to wave, and respond to people who speak or compliment you. And of course, if someone sits in your space to listen, you may want to greet them, because it’s polite. I ask them if they have a request. Oh, and always give your “thank you”s when tipped. (Note, initiating pedestrians may help, but it doesn’t sit right with me because that’s what the beggars do. I do well enough with engaging.)

5. I’m investing in a constume(for fiddle) but street clothes have worked fine for me on piano. I do try to dress professionally though, just to express a seriousness about my act.

6. You can make money anywhere busking where there are people. To my experience, a place that is quiet where there are a lot of people is ideal. If the crowd is too big, and the ambience is too noisy, your passerbys can’t hear you. This, for me, is one of the most important factors.

7. Definitely stand if you can. I sit because I play piano. I do not sit when I play fiddle. You take up less space when you sit, it’s easier for people to look over and past you, and you look like a piece of furniture, and you lose aural space.

8. You don’t have to play familiar music. Sometimes I get tips on popular songs. Sometimes I don’t. Sometimes I get tips on obscure songs. Sometimes I don’t. I play my entire repertoire of all genres. I’ve received tips for everything that I play. And sometimes the most popular songs don’t get any tips at all. Depends on who’s listening. I got a huge tip just the day before yesterday for playing some less than popular New Age music, and that set has gotten some of my best responses, even though it’s one of my most obscure sets. With all that being said, the biggest tip I’ve received on fiddle was for a request of Cotton-Eyed Joe. So my advice, play it all, all the time. You never know who’s listening.

9. Busking is an excellent remedy for performance anxiety. Because people aren’t focused on you or directing their energy towards you. If you want to face your fear of being shy, busking would be a very helpful and profitable way to go about it. Especially if you know that you’re good on your instrument. And most people aren’t rude enough to insult you, unless you’re really really bad. But you’ll be great if you’re good. (Practicing in public cured ever ounce of performance anxiety in my body, and I had performance anxiety for nearly 15 years.)

10. Have fun and enjoy yourself. Own your instrument, and own your space. People respond to your energy, if they see you happy and enjoying yourself, and emotionally invested in the music, you will attract a lot more attention. Be seen, be heard, and own it.

Good luck!

Re: Instruments for Busking

Veteran Irish busker here! I don’t have time to read the thread but I will try to briefly answer

1. That’s subjective and often comes down to your quality of playing as opposed to the instrument. That said, “niche” instruments are almost a guarantee - hurdy gurdies, pipes, dulcimers, harps, etc. The fiddle is also a fan favourite. Things like the banjo, accordion, flute, whistle, etc, are only really good when playing with other people.

2. It’s pretty much almost always better to busk in a group. Bands, or groups, make way more money than solo buskers… Trust me, there’s been many a time I’ve squeezed my box on the streets, good vibes in the air, coins coming my way, only for a band to rock up around the corner and suddenly people stop listening, the money stops coming, crowds gather around the band, you become less relevant and the motivation fades… Plus playing with people, IMO at least, is funner. That’s not to say you CAN’T busk solo but you’d want to be either really really really good at your instrument or play a niche instrument like the ones mentioned above.

3. That’s too hard to qualify because everyone is different and everybody likes different music. I would say if you’re playing guitar and you’re not an amazing singer, then most people couldn’t give a feck. Honestly just try it out, try different instruments, and see what works. Again it really comes down to the quality of your playing, not necessarily the type of music or instrument… Though that said, playing Irish music for tourists in the summer is a definite cash cow!

Re: Instruments for Busking

“… the almost universal use of amplification by buskers …”

It depends where you go - round these parts (Mid Wales, Shropshire) it’s more the exception than the rule, perhaps because there’s less ambient noise to contend with than in busier places.

I actually have strong feelings about the (superfluous*) use of amplification. One of my primary considerations for choosing a pitch is whether or not I can hear another busker (or any other music). I might well not notice it once I start playing, but the point is, if I can hear other music from where I am standing, then anybody walking between where I am and the other busker will never hear either busker without cross-contamination from the other. Bring amplification into the equation and the necessary separation to avoid cross-contamination is increased severalfold. So, whether wittingly or unwittingly, the busker that uses amplification may be monopolising the street or town they are playing in. Some buskers might simply respond by getting their own amplification - or turning it up, if they already have it - but this only increases the problem of cross-contamination.

*I have no objection to instruments that rely on amplification, played at respectful volume.

With regards to “seed money”, the coin you put down will often reflect what others will tip. For example if I put in a €2 and €1 coin then I will likely be tipped with €1/2 coins. However if you put in 20c/50c coins (even if the total sum adds up to a few euros) that’s what people will have the tendency to donate… I’m not sure on the psychology but it definitely holds true, I can tell you from experience! I think what people see, people think is the appropriate amount to tip. So if they see €2 coins they think that’s what the standard is, or whatever. Of course it doesn’t mean that you will strictly get €2 coins but you will notice a higher proportion!

Not that busking is all about the money but it sure does help :)

Re: Instruments for Busking

I believe that children are your best friends when busking. If I can engage the kids, their parents not only contribute more, but (just as importantly, since it’s not all about the cash) have a lot more fun.

Accordingly, instruments that appeal to kids are good. I’ve found very large and very small instruments appeal to kids. I’ve played a lot of instruments in public, and the ones that have gotten the most kid response are my upright bass, ukulele, and tin whistle. Be prepared to let the kids “play” them. For tin whistle that could get a little gross for some people, but if you’re a parent you are used to a spit-filled relationship with kids anyway. Another option is to have a second cheapo whistle with you to let the kids play. They probably only want to blow into it and make a noise.

As far as how to receive tips, I use one of these containers:

https://www.quill.com/folgers-classic-roast-ground-coffee-48-oz-canister/cbs/54698152.html

with a big “TIPS - THANK YOU!” on the side. I don’t really like all the money in my case. At first people were confused as to whether to put money in my guitar case or the tip can (and “a confused mind says no while a clear mind says go” so it should be very clear to people how to contribute). So now I put the can inside the guitar case and people seem clear on that concept.

Absolutely play to the people. And really know your songs well. The better you know the songs you’re playing, the better you can interact with people, the more fun all of you have, and (a nice bonus!) the more money you make. I wrote this quora answer a little while back that might be useful:

http://qr.ae/TUpa20
