The poetry of tune names …

A while back - being confined to the house on account of the arrival of wee baby boy - I developed an interest in creating (or “locating” in books, catalogues, etc.) tune titles for non-existent tunes. Sad, I know … But some people who came across these lists of hundreds upon hundreds of titles with no tunes (a sort of catalogue of “gan ainms” in reverse) found the concept vaguely amusing. Others, doubtless thought I was off my rocker … ho hum!

Anyway the exercise was spurred by the way in which I’m affected not just by tunes but their very titles. As far as I’m concerned there are hordes of tunes whose titles are, if not “poetic” then eloquent, apt, image-forming.

I’m not talking about the “Boys Of…”, “… Lassies”, “Humours Of…” titles. Nor indeed the tunes named after players.

Rather these sorts of title:-

I’d Rather Be Married Than Left; The Lark On The Strand; Pay The Reckoning; The Day I Met Tom Moylan; The Hunter’s House; Touch Me If You Dare!; Walk Out Of It, Hogan!; The Fisherman’s Lilt; Old Hag, You Have Killed Me!; The Cat’s Rambles To The Child’s Saucepan; Currants For Cake, Raisins For Everything Else; I Buried My Wife And Danced On Her Grave; The Spirits Of Wine; The Drops Of Brandy; Jazzing With Mag Leary; The Cat That Kittled In Jamie’s Wig

I know that tune titles are “generally” mere arbitrary labels used by players to “file” the tunes in their memory banks and that while some titles have a degree of significance, or a story at least, most don’t.

And yet there are titles which epitomise aspects of the music in much the same way as certain tunes epitomise the music itself.

As a wordy and musical lot, do any of yez ever get a buzz out of tune titles? D’yez ever read the sleeve notes of a CD, cassette whatever and find the very tune titles having an effect on you?

Or am I such a sad case that I should immediately sign myself up for a life of quiet contemplation in the Brotherhood Of The Perpetual Anorak?
