In the UK such recordings are known as library discs or records.

The Chieftains, with singer Mary-Clare Breathnach and The Castle Céilí Band, made one called ‘Ireland’ for Chappell Music Publishing in 1986.

Side one consists of The Chieftains strutting their stuff through nine ‘typical’ tracks. The second side is far more interesting since it features each of The Chieftains playing solo except for harp accompaniment on a couple of tracks, so you get Paddy Moloney on whistle and pipes, Matt Molloy demonstrating all his flute versatility on The Butterfly, harp from Derek Bell, fiddles from Seán Keane and Martin Fay, plus a bones solo from Fay and a bodhrán one from Kevin Conneff. Kevin then lilts one and sings two solo tracks, followed by two also from Mary-Clare Breathnach (once of Macalla). Lastly, there’s a couple of tracks from the Castle Céilí Band.

Re: Suggest a mix of 11 tunes for a CD?

Now that we are clearer what you are doing, and what your knowledge level is, ecooke, we can be a bit more helpful.
Like Johnny Jay says, you don’t need 11 tunes, you need 11 tracks, and the tracks that include jigs and reels will have multiple tunes. A normal tune, played three times through AABB, which is customary, takes about 90 seconds, so if you want your tracks to take at least three minutes, you are looking at two or even three tunes per track. Except for tunes that have more than two parts, which are usually repeated less times. I would suggest that, for your slow tunes, you have a couple of slow airs, and a waltz. For the slow airs, don’t try to have all of the musicians play at once. The slow air, played as it should, is a solo affair. The waltz everyone can play together.
Not sure if you want unique tunes, or a collection of old favorites that many might recognize. If you want to stick to familiar tunes, the old tried and true that most everyone knows, you might come up with something like this (and I believe all of these are old enough to be in the public domain):
1. Reels: Red Haired Boy/Merry Blacksmith
2. Jigs: Morrisons/Out on the Ocean
3. Air: Ned of the Hill
4. Polkas: Egans/Maids of Ardagh
5. Jigs: Connaughtman’s Rambles/My Darling Asleep
6. Waltz: Inisheer
7. Jigs: Blarney Pilgrim/Kesh
8. Reels: Far From Home/Silver Spear
9. Jigs: Haste To The Wedding/Tripping Up the Stairs
10. Air: Lament for Limerick
11. Reel: The Bucks of Oranmore

Re: Suggest a mix of 11 tunes for a CD?

I’d be interested to know if you’re planning on providing dialogue underscore arrangements for the tracks, as well. Or will you just split out the channels and leave it to the engineers? I think the challenge will lie in taking a very melodic, rhythmic genre and taming it so that it can work under dialogue without clashing. I suppose you could do it with uilleann drones and the low notes on flute or fiddle, or use a cello or bass (non-trad but what the hell) to carry a simplified version of the melody.

Also, Irish music might seem really repetitive to some people, as it’s usually just eight bars repeated, then another eight bars repeated for once through a tune, then you go through the tune three times or so and you’re done. You might want to consider the suggestion above, to have sets of two or three reels each etc. to add variety and texture, essentially movements, to a longer piece. Or at least make sure that the different tracks of the same tune type (i.e. reels) are played at the same tempo so they can be stitched together if necessary.

Sounds like an interesting project. Good luck with it. And if you are going to copyright these tracks, so that no one can play any particular tune without your expressed and written permission, could you include The Kesh on your playlist?
