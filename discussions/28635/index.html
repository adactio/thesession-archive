Hands off our BBC ~

Details of planned BBC cuts:
http://www.bbc.co.uk/news/entertainment-arts-15186116

The government making the squeeze, the BBC have plans to make profound cuts to local content of ALL their regional radio programmes. For our community here in the Northwest of England this includes BBC Cumbria, BBC Radio Lancashire, BBC Manchester and BBC Radio Merseyside. They plan to standardize all programme content across England from 7pm to 10pm every night. This could mean the loss of local folk programming, such as the Northwest’s weekly ‘Drift’ and ‘Blue Notes’ programmes. This would be a great loss. These programmes support our local talent and venues by spreading the good news: gigs, events, as well as a good listen and valued education for the ears, the mind and the heart.

The BBC are conducting a consultation on this subject, via the BBC Trust website, http://www.bbc.co.uk/bbctrust/index.shtml - consultations:

http://www.bbc.co.uk/bbctrust/consult/local_radio.shtml
http://consultations.external.bbc.co.uk/bbc/localradio/consultation/intro/view

You’re invited to fill in a questionaire asking you to give your views on how BBC local radio fulfills its mandate to your local community. Please take the time, and with vested interest, to support your local folk and blues programmes, as well as local content in general. It’s hoped that if we give these programmes our support in significant numbers the BBC will conclude that they are indispensible, and that they won’t be able to quietly kill them off.

“Don’t it always seem to go that you don’t know what you’ve got till its gone ” - Big Yellow Taxi, Joni Mitchell

Help us to work against the BBC’s plans to guillotine local folk and blues programmes.


More options -
What can we do to defend our local, folk & traditional radio programming? ~ get angry and take action!!! ;-)

1. Visit the BBC’s complaints page and make your objections heard, don’t spare the powder ~

http://www.bbc.co.uk/complaints/

& as a local action, Merseyside ~ Write to Mick Ord, the Editor of Radio Merseyside:
• By e-mail: radio.merseyside.news@bbc.co.uk
• By post: BBC Radio Merseyside, 31 College Lane, Liverpool, L1 3DS.
• By fax: 0151 794 0909
• Make sure you mark your e-mail, letter or fax: “For the attention of Mick Ord”.

And the same action can be made with respect to your own local BBC radio. Don’t be quiet, be vocal, and spread the news and wake up your friends to take action.

Feel free to include this information in any newsletters…

FYI - my last complaint to the BBC concerned a couple of traditional tunes!

My complaint:

---------------------------------------------------------------------
The Tudors – BBC 2 – Friday 19th October 2007

This episode included a scene with musicians playing. The first tune played was “Liliburlero”, which was written by Purcell in 1687. The second tune played was “Royal Oak” - also written in the 17th Century.

Why then were Tudor (i.e. 16th century) musicians portrayed playing these tunes?

In particular, I would have thought that the BBC would known the date of “Liliburlero”, as this has long been used as an introduction to the BBC World Service.

---------------------------------------------------------------------

The response I received from the BBC:

--------------------------------------------------------------------

Thank you for your e-mail regarding ‘Tudors’ broadcast October 19.

I understand you have concerns about the music played during the programme.

‘The Tudors’ has been billed as a drama, rather than a documentary, a spokesperson at programme acquisitions has described the series as follows:

"‘The Tudors’ is a modern and exciting take on the story of Henry VIII and his reign, dealing with the monarch’s younger and wilder years. The series deliberately set out to take a different approach to previous conventional historical dramas and is in a similar in vein to BBC TWO’s hit drama series ‘Rome’.

The BBC was careful, from the outset, to forewarn viewers of the general tone - and the adult content - of this lavish series. In the weeks running up to transmission we initiated a trailer campaign which highlighted the nature of the series and most of the press and listings magazines also spoke of the heightened modern ‘soap opera’ feel."

I would like to assure you that we have registered your comments on our audience log. This is the internal report of audience feedback which we compile daily for all programme makers and commissioning executives within the BBC, and also their senior management. It ensures that your points, and all other comments we receive, are circulated and considered across the BBC.

Thank you for taking the time to contact BBC Information.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
