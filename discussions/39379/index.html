Sheet music collections

When was the last time (if ever) you bought a sheet music collection of ITM? For me it was probably the late nineties. The reason I stopped is partly due to the rise of the internet, but not entirely: I’d been collecting for nearly 20 years, and I found that most new publications had a lot of duplication - in a book of 100 tunes, there might be less than half a dozen that I didn’t already have in another collection.
When I started playing in sessions, some 80% of the repertoire could be found in O’Neills. Times have changed and the repertoire is much more varied now. If I’m looking for the notes of a new tune, my first port of call will now be sites like this, but I still use my books. I like browsing through collections, and trying to sight-read unfamiliar tunes, a good way of practicing. It’s not so practical to do this using online resources. The most useful and well-thumbed collections I have are Breathnach. I like the look of O Am go hAm by Tommy Peoples, one of my very favourite musicians. It looks like a beautiful book, and I was very tempted, but the cost - €57 - is a bit prohibitive. The collections I’d most like to have are the complete volumes of Bulmer and Sharpley. I only have volumes 1 and 4. I presume they’re long out of print, but if anyone knows where I can get hold of the missing volumes I’d like to hear from you.

Re: Sheet music collections

I bought quite a few recently. The best, which has been occupying me quite a bit, was: Johnny O’Leary of Sliabh Luachra: Dance Music from the Cork-Kerry Border, edited by Terry Moylan.

About 360 or so tunes. Loads and loads of polkas and slides that I was unfamiliar with. And quite a lot of tunes I already knew well from O’Neill and elsewhere, but just in slightly different versions that I tend to prefer - the differences being, to generalise, that they’re perhaps a little simpler and perhaps a little more modal.

Sometimes, learning a tune from paper rather than by ear can make all the difference. Sometimes. One of the tunes from the Johnny O‘Leary book that I now keep playing is “Johnny When You Die”. Turns out I’ve heard Padraig O’Keeffe & Dennis Murphy play that tune on the album ‘Kerry Fiddles’ - an album I’ve listened to thousands of times - and never thought much of it as a tune (unlike most everything else on that wonderful album!)

Re: Sheet music collections

I buy them all the time.
In one of them by chance I hit on one more very good reason for being able to sight read (and write).
It was a balalaika book, either Dorozhkin or Ekkel, I can’t recall.
The tip was to free yourself from dependency on the notes - but to revisit frequently, as repeated performances from memory can go a bit stale, with little errors creeping in. So they may need refreshing by reference to the source. The source could be your own transcription of course, not necessarily external.

In fact that is what you find on this site - many of the tunes have been put in from memory and are not quite the same as, say, O’Neill. Sometimes better or more interesting, but very often a bit lacking in comparison, with little omissions and short cuts.
A marvellous resource nevertheless for which I’m very grateful, but always worth looking at other sources as a double check.
The more books the better!

Re: Sheet music collections

I still use sheet music a lot and buy books even although I am an “ear player”. I prefer to learn in my own way though and, to me, both methods are complementary. It’s not one or the other as many people would like to argue.

Aside from the fact that some sheet music publications just aren’t very good, the main issue is that even the simplest of tunes will have any number of different settings or versions depending on which book you buy. The same goes for recordings or performances in live or session settings too, of course. Whether it’s right, wrong, or just different doesn’t really matter too much and I think, in the end, it’s finding a setting or arrangement which suits yourself. of course, if you are playing in company you have to be able to adapt…especially if the remainder of the company don’t… ;-)

More recently, I’ve purchased quite a few music books written with harp and clarsach players in mind. These will offer different arrangement and also, of course, the Bass clef line in addition to the main melody. Many of the tunes I know already but it’s good to get new ideas. I don’t always agree with or enjoy the arrangements but I can adapt them to suit. Also, I can use those I do like with melodies I already know.

To sum up, basically I think books and sheet music are fine as long as you are not “ruled by them”. You should have enough discipline to “play as written” when it is absolutely necessary though.

Re: Sheet music collections

I’ve bought quite a few and will consider buying more. I figure, for the money I pay, if I learn only two or three tunes from that book I’ve gotten my money’s worth out of it. But my instrument is hammered dulcimer, and there just isn’t any original stuff out there written for that instrument. Sure I can download from here to my heart’s content and I’ve gotten some great stuff that way. But sometimes it pays to get tunes arranged by a master of that instrument, whatever it is that you play. But hammered dulcimer repertoire is not just ITM and C**tic music ;-) but also pulled heavily from American and Canadian fiddle and bluegrass music, as well as dance tunes and songs from a variety of world cultures. So buying sheet music books here in the States can open up a whole new world of stuff, literally. A good example of this are the numerous instrumental books written and published by American country music singer Roy Clark. So I will buy books because:
A) It has tune(s) I want to learn arranged by a professional
B) To get tunes arranged specifically for the instrument I play
C) To add to my collection- some books are “collectable,” that is, not neccessarily worth a lot of money, but are collecable to me because of the author/publisher, content, or (especially) rareity.
D) To learn more about the music, many books have some good historical and factual info about the music or composers
