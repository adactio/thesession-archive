Re: CSI ~ body parts

All right. I’ll bite. At the risk of a slagging, I’ll give you my favourite metaphor. :)

I think music is like learning how to ride dressage, where you sometimes have to break it down into its elements for pedagogical purposes. At the same, such a description is totally artificial and the music, or the way the horse moves, must be understood in a holistic fashion. You can’t disconnect the ornaments from the tune any more than you can disconnect the front end from the hind end of a horse. But when training beginner musicians and riders, or green horses for that matter, you sometimes have to talk about just one thing.

The arched neck of a dressage horse (http://www.dressagedaily.com/photos/historical/Rembrandt-94weg-21.jpg) comes from the horse pushing off its hind legs and using its back muscles to balance the weight of the rider. It shouldn’t come from the rider pulling the horse’s head in with the reins. That whole picture of lightness, suppleness, and balance -- Olympic gold medalists in the photo I linked to -- comes from complete, holistic connection between horse and rider. But when I train a baby, I teach him to lower his head and soften his jaw in response to light pressure from the bit, without moving anywhere whatsoever. I also teach him to push with his hindquarters and move in balance without worrying too much about what he’s doing with his head at first. Those things are inseparable when it comes to doing correct dressage, but it makes the process clearer to the green horse when it is broken down into its elements.

A well played tune, in my head, has the lightness, rhythm, and cadence and balance of a Grand Prix dressage horse, but no one starts off that way. No good dressage trainer will say that the way the horse holds his head is a separate thing from what he’s doing with the rest of his body. And believe me, many many people make the mistake thinking that it is; of *only* using the reins to pull the horse’s head down and not bothering with the rest of their, or the horse’s body. It’s a contradictory thing -- you may have to separate them briefly to teach them but it is imperative that how and why they are connected it is also taught. So yeah, I see why Michael doesn’t like separating ornaments from the tune. I still think you have to sometimes do it.

Re: CSI ~ body parts

Ornaments are like adjectives, or better yet, profanities. And a tune is a story. It can be told many ways - by many different people. Some folks, try as they might, tell a rather uninspiring tale. Others can take a simple thing and make it sound incredibly exciting. I don’t mind swearing. In fact used appropriately, it can really spice up a good tale at the pub. But I don’t want to hear and entire story of F-bombs either. A good story teller never dips into the same well of descriptive words too often. It dulls the ears. Same thing goes with a tune. You have the basic idea, and then the profanities and descriptions, (i.e. ornaments and phrasing) to make it interesting are up to YOU - the player of the tune. Don’t use the same ornament in the same spot of the same section over and over again. It dulls the ears to the story. Be creative. Be interesting. Tell a good tale with your tune.

From my own humble perspective that I get the tune “simple”, as alluded to with the Christmas Tree example (I take it that you mean a bare tree which is then decorated?) to some wonderful expositions on how another perceives the tune with its dressings as an entity.

There’s a lot to be said for both schools of thought, and I am fascinated personally by these different approaches to tunes, and whether to separate the component parts contained therein.

Getting away from, but in keeping with the whole process of bodies, guts etc., which one finds in programmes like CSI, I look at tunes like I look at words. But with tunes, instead of letters use notes. When you put the letters together you get words and when you put the words together you get sentences, maybe even literature!

So with notes, they might become phrases, motifs and when they go together they can become tunes as opposed to sentences. And for me, that’s where the punctuation comes in.

Also, when it comes to speech, everyone has different accents, and different areas might lend different nuances to words, phrases and expressions. I believe I mentioned that with reference to the different fiddle styles I found in my travels.

I’ve done a little search here and found the notation for a tune I play, “The Peacock’s Feather”, which is close to the source I got way back in the late 70s in Lancashire.

https://thesession.org/tunes/665

Now when I got this tune I got the “bones” of it, near enough the notation above. This lovely old player just went through the notes nice and gentle one Saturday night and I had the tune.

The example I now quote is a few years ago and is how I played it when a friend came to visit and we just had a little play

http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xUjM8woSAQk

Being specific, some of it, though not too much, is articulated with tongue as discussed in this, and the previous, thread, and some of it shaped solely with fingerwork. The same goes for the breathing. But this is what I describe as the genes, the skin, eye colour, blonde, red hair etc.

Llig, you could have the same tune essentially but give a totally different rendition, but we would all know it as the same tune.

Maybe I git my metaphors wrong, but hopefully this helps explain a bit more.

The first little drop(second A) opens the excitement door a bit wider, not that we go faster, but beginning to gain energy. Breathing smoothly. Then comes a different drop (first B part) a different kind of drop but still the same river. Test some moves, breathing, surf this little wave, ferry to an eddy, peel back into the current. Then comes another interesting drop(second B part) The river is gaining more energy. Breathing deeper. Waves are a little bigger, the eddys are gaining energy, the paddle strokes are in fluid unison with the water. The boat slips through the water with graceful glide. Now we approach the first big drop(second time through the tune, first A part) Over the edge we go, nice gain of energy. More dynamics of water energy, the river(tune) is now showing its personality. Re: CSI ~ body parts

I definately don’t find the rhythm of a river to be the same as a tune in a structured sort of way. i.e.4/4,6/8 etc. If I do that, then I’m trying to force the river to do what I think it should. Doing that is what gets you killed. It’s more a case of being open to the rhythm that the river or the tune has to offer. I feel a tune or a river with the same open myself and let it come in approach. The rhythms of tunes and rivers are significantly different. No two rivers have the same exact rhythm and every time I run one I do things a bit differently each time. That’s much the same sort of thing I feel in the music. I’d never try to play a tune with the same rhythm as a river. Same as I’d never(well hopefully never) try to force a slipjig into the rhythm of a hornpipe. Maybe there’s a slipjig out there that can do it, but I’d probably drown trying that. I try to feel the rhythm that the tune has to offer. Then experiment with the tune’s offering within its individual personality. Brian’s approach to the Peacock’s Feather comes to mind. It’s the open myself to the flow and let it come in approach that starts to make the similarities between running rivers and playing tunes have an interchangeable process for me. I identify with the way water works so when I approach a tune with the same open myself and let it come in idea. I find myself able to feel what the tune wants rather that make the tune do what I think it should. If I try to make a tune do what I think it should, I generally fail. However if I allow the tune to tell me what it wants, I have much better results.

I have had those two questions I asked previously nigglin’ away in the back of my head, so here goes…

What is the river bed without the water?

~ a river bed, and still with a certain beauty, as I’ve wandered dry river beds with my grandfather, who had a knowledge and love of rocks. But there was still the awareness of something missing.

What is dance music without the dance?

~ Music can and does survived without the dance, and has persisted long after that inspiration has grown distant. I can enjoy the classical bent on a mazurka, but there too it is obvious of the severed tie to dance, and the lack of that deeper understanding. I love slow airs too, but when they are disconnected from the song that too can be obvious, and somewhat clumsy, or even silly. I have great appreciation for a beginners efforts, if not much for those with great technical skill that butcher a tune just because they can, cramming it full of their ‘cute’ and irritating pyrotechnics, and completely losing the plot as to rhythm and lift and ‘dance’ or ‘song’…

Amongst a selection of classical music we have Artur Rubinstein perfroming Chopin mazurkas on piano, 1938-39. It’s shight as ‘mazurkas’ and useless as regards dance, other than modern free form improv, so no appreciation from me on either of those counts. But, we enjoy the listen, appreciate the talent and their interpretation, though ovbiously disconnected from at least some of its roots. While aware of this and of what’s missing we can still enjoy and appreciate it for what it is.

‘Connection’ is important, that ‘whole’. I have never thought of ‘ornaments’ as being part of the living and heart beating whole of music, nor do I even think of it as the breath and articulation of music, but more the clothing on the body, the decoration, the choices and additions or subtractions we make in personalizing it. Some have all the right in the world to take it basic and functional, while others can choose to gussy it up in frills and lace and the bling of sparkling jewelry. As long as the body of life that is music, the heart beat of it, as long as that life remains, well, it is quite able to carry a wide variety of clothing on its back, from birthday suit to rediculously outrageous and even Tim Burtonesque… Though there is a point, I think, when the clothing distracts from the life it adorns, where the extremes can smother the true character and spirit, drawing more attnetion to the outrageous, or a ‘label’ than to the personality and history beneath the bling. Again, it is a matter of choice. We have that right and freedom to decide what we like or dislike, what we want or not for ourselves. Those decisions can be more or less in keeping with a given tradition, or completely disconnected and flittering about in its own fiction…

Before the building of reservoirs, seasonal dry creekbeds were a very normal thing. Also, some rivers and creeks really are dead and will never run again because of reservoirs. People have altered the natural cycle of many rivers and creeks. Only the biggest of rivers and the highest mountain creeks had water year round before reservoirs were built. They follow the cycle of the seasons.
