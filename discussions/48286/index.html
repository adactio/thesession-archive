Antisocial media…

Ok, for background purposes I was once happy to perform in public (usually compensated with cash or my own weight in alcohol - whichever was more expansive). But for the last few decades, I have regressed from the limelight, moved up to the top of the mountain, and now play mostly on my own or with a few select friends who I love, and for the fact they love and respect the music and love playing. But a few months ago, I attended a private house party, and a select few of us played from dusk until dawn. I can attest that drink was taken, not all of the notes were hung in the right place at the right time, but there was a lot of love and smiles. The next day somebody tagged me on feckbook, and there were some rather unflattering pictures of ourselves, weary and worn just before the dawn. Not impressed. I didn’t sign any consent form, nor had I realised that by being at a party my visage and whereabouts were now available for the world to see.
My problem is that this weekend I am once again coming down from the Mountain and have been asked to meet up with some friends at a bar where there is a regular session. I have seen regular updates from the previous nights, where someone is ‘capturing the moment’ on their phones and posting 20 seconds of poorly recorded out of context shite to the web. So, any advice? Should I just stop playing once the cameras are out? Should I ask them to be respectful of the ‘moment’ and the music and put the cameras away, get out their instruments and join in, or just listen? I feel like this may have been discussed previously, but am ready to have a tshirt printed which says I don’t condone or consent to social media posts, so feck off.

Re: Antisocial media…

I think it is important to make the distinction between customers taking videos of a pub session and players taking and posting videos of each other in either a public or private session.

In the first case, about all you can do is ask they please turn off the blinding video light if it’s on. They absolutely can take a video, and often it’s good publicity for the session. I’ve never said no to a punter asking if it was OK to take some video of our session, and I let them know I appreciate their asking first.

In the second case, you can certainly request that the other players not post video of your playing on social media, and if they are reasonable people, they will respect your request.

You said much of what I wanted to say.

Sessions, of course, can take place in all manner of venues these days but many still do take place in what we here “in these islands” refer as a “public house”.

I think both words are important here. Although it’s main purpose is to sell alcohol, the premises are open to all members of the public(within reason…unless it’s full or they are known to be undesirable for some reason) and it’s also like a “house” where people will come to visit friends, socialise, and maybe be introduced or meet new friends. It’s obviously more convenient at times to borrow space in the public house as opposed to arranging “meet ups”, events etc in a private home (your own or someone else’s).

That doesn’t mean, however, that you shouldn’t be entitled to a certain amount of private space when you are there. More so, arguably, as many of the people there will strangers. At home, presumably, everybody will have been invited and greater familiarity is more likely and less of an issue.

Of course, all sessions and premises are different and if musicians want to be left alone they should try and arrange a room/lounge which is separate from the main bar. Many do work that way and some are more successful than others. We usually don’t really want sessions to be completely private though and, in these situations, respectful listeners and punters are usually still welcome to come in and enjoy the experience too.

Furthermore, there are all sorts of “spectacles” to be observed such venues.
I wouldn’t dream of videoing women on a “Hen night” or men on a “stag do”, nor football/rugby supporters having a sing song, couples on the dance floor, and much more besides. DrSilverSpear

Sometimes I think we deserve our desserts, because it seems mostly of “western” origin amusing over or getting some kind of thrill out of bloopers of others, making a spectacle of others. Just about everyone is guilty of watching some scene or another, candid camera or what have you, and similarly of filming without regard at some point. So even if we reject that eventually, we still are “complicit” or are expected to have an understanding. In some other cultures it is still frowned upon, just bad taste.

The trouble is that previously there were only still cameras and each picture was costly enough. Holiday sightseers would tend to photo the most important, i.e. themselves. Holiday snapshots of a bar would not be seen again either. Professional photographers understood discretion. Hence the Japanese becoming the butt of the joke, turning up loaded with cameras photographing everything (but usually architecture and so on all the same).

Video equipment until the 90’s was bulky (and costly), VHS or Betamax, separate power supply etc. So someone taking video was a scene in itself, and you knew. The even earlier amateur filming equipment by reel was expensive (and rare), and so with forethought.

Then it all went digital and everyone was carrying a camera at all times via their phones, and that cost nothing to take pictures from. If you think of the few times you might have filmed without consideration, and multiply that by the number of people around then there will always be someone doing so, and that is not even counting those who get a kick out of it. Add the web to the mix and that is where we are.

The funniest experience I had was with a border dispute, neighbours had clearly fenced in a quarter of our property for themselves and would not consider otherwise. So that is court eventually, but in the meantime they did not like it being explained to them and became hostile afterwards. I’m not confrontational, but I will speak my say and outline a point. Well they just took to insulting and trying to disturb us in reply, for nothing. So one day when they are acting that way I make it obvious that their words are being recorded by jingling a phone in the air briefly…might make them think and recording sound on your own property is allowed. So a bit later one of the neighbours asks more reasonably something about the paperwork on my side, and being naive I go and fetch it and go up to the fence to talk over it. However there I am met by another couple standing shoulder to shoulder looking at their phone which is pointed at and presumably filming me. I stand there in disbelief, one hand full of papers still sort thinking of and offering them. Then I just feel like a complete plonker because they are obviously trying to wind me up and trying to get their own back … the neighbour who comes up the fence hassling them or something. I just walk away. I try to imagine they weren’t actually filming me but were only pretending to. Just too odd though.

The point is that you just don’t know what anyone’s intent is nowadays when they film or photograph you, so you are left to imagine that they like horses or something, but really you just don’t know, and that makes it at the very least intrusive or disruptive for that uncertainty it brings.
