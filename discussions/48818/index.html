Ideas… Rating tune settings

I know that discussing infrastructure on the session.org can be a sensitive topic and I’d like to start with genuine appreciation for Jeremey and all that he and this site have done. There’s little question the site has huge impact in facilitating others to learn Irish music and learn some of the history and tidbits behind the tunes.

As I’ve done before, occasionally I get a decent idea and I thought this one was good enough to share and collect feedback on.

The tune settings part of the session.org is one of the best elements of the site -- we all know that there are various settings of a tune depending on from whom it was learned, and that these settings are often guarded fiercely to emphasize correctness.

My thought is why not have a way for users to thumbs up or thumbs down a setting so that the first setting that shows up at the top of the page is the most popular version, and the second most popular, and etc.

Ranking these by age as is currently done is interesting but doesn’t have nearly as much value as ranking by popularity. Obviously any kind of popularity contest brings challenges, but there’s no need for any user to be able to actually see how many up or down votes might exist.

Would be curious on thoughts. Perhaps Jeremey already thought of this one and it’s in the woodwork or won’t be because of reasons beyond my comprehension.

Thanks

Thady, I don’t have a horse in this race because I don’t learn tunes from the database here. But you asked for feedback, so here’s my tuppence. It’s likely worth what you paid for it. ;-)

For playing in sessions (as opposed to gigs), tune settings (interpretations) are ever subject to a tension between (a) richly capturing the nature and scope—the unique character and idiosyncrasies—of the tune vs. (b) presenting a basic, baseline (perhaps bland) version that’s as broadly acceptable (at sessions anywhere) as possible.

Would ranking them here by “popularity” favor the latter (b) over the former (a)? Would that in some small way contribute to what some old codgers decry as the erosive dumbing down of the tunes?

Ultimately, for any one person, the “best” setting is the one that most closely mirrors what your mates play at your local session. That setting may vary substantively from the version played at the pub down the street or in the next town over. General “popularity,” assigned by the global hive mind on an internet forum, may be far wide of the mark.

Re: Ideas… Rating tune settings

Thady, no doubt you mean well, and perhaps your aim is less about showcasing the “best” settings and more about minimizing long scrolls through settings that fall short due to what you perceive as “wrong” keys/modes, “wrong” notes, missing beats, etc. I can understand that temptation. But it’s a doomed quest.

I got a chuckle out of the notion that the active membership of this forum somehow represents the “trad community.” Or that there is a “most correct” setting of any tune.

Context is king. Consider:

- For tunes that go below D, whistle, flute, and pipes will want a different setting than fiddle, banjo, mandolin, etc. (as Conical Bore points out above). For other tunes, they’ll favor different ornamentation, melodic variations, and so on, suited to their particular instrument. Does the “most correct” setting of even a simple tune like Miss McLeod’s fall in G or A? You want Paddy Fahey’s Jig (https://thesession.org/tunes/532), but are you looking for the Gdor setting, or the Gmajor setting (as Mr. Fahey himself played it), or some blend of the two? Is one of these “more correct” than the other? Only if it matches how the tune is played at your local session. Fiddlers love the Foxhunter’s Reel in A, but that gives fluter’s fits and they prefer it in G. Calliope House gets played in E, D, G, and probably a few other keys, depending on local preference.
- A session that favors Flook, McGoldrick, At the Racket, or maybe Four Men and a Dog will want a different setting than one that prefers the old ways.
- What if a person prefers a Sligo setting over an East Clare setting? Killarney vs. Belfast? Is one “more correct” than another?
- A beginner may want a bare bones setting, while a more advanced player may be looking for nuances of ornamentation, variations, bowing, etc. transcribed from a recording of some master musician.
- If more people in one country participate here than those from anywhere else, should their tastes set the standard for what’s “most correct”?

Thady, I’m puzzled by your notion of a “a rating system that uses chronology.”

It’s not a rating, just a simple chronological sequence based on the date of posting. 1 pm isn’t any “more correct” than 5 pm just because it comes first. You keep missing two critical points:

1. There is no single “most correct” setting. Many variables influence the “worth” of a setting—instrumentation, ability of the player, regional style, specific session style, key, mode, etc.
2. Anyone who wants to play this music will have to adapt. The tunes aren’t static. To fit in at almost any session anywhere, you’ll have to tweak how you’ve learned a tune, no matter which setting you started from.

Together, these two factors mean that, even if settings were ranked here by popularity (or any other metric), every person would still have to search for the one that most closely suits their specific purpose and circumstances. And in all likelihood, they’d still end up making substantive and significant changes to the setting to make it work at their local session. Or simply to suit their personal taste.

Not sure what’s so difficult to understand about this. Yes, this means that newcomers to this music have some work to do. Best get on with it rather than wishing for shortcuts.

I’ll add a third critical point to my two above. This is absolutely central to playing at Irish sessions:

3. More than likely, the best setting won’t be in the database here. It’ll be the one you learn from your session mates. Or the one you learn from some master player passing through who walks you through *her* sense of the bare bones of the tune and then shows you a dozen other ways it is and can be played. One: we go to one or more sessions where the tunes are played and we want to join in. This will involve asking what the tune is called then finding versions of it. There is a good chance that no one setting on sites such as this will be an exact version of how it is played in one session. It is quite likely that there will not even be one exact setting played in the session. So it would be a mistake to try to choose any one version and learn it, and then recite it exactly note for note. Better to get a mental structure for the tune and adapt to how it plays out in in the sessions

Two: we want to play for our own entertainment. No rules here. I will play about with tunes in all sorts of ways. Nobody else in going to express a view (other than ‘isn’t a bit late to be making a noise’)

Three: we are involved in playing for dancing in some way (I play for clog dancing sometimes). Here the melody is secondary to the speed and pulse. It can cope with quite a lot of variation in what notes are played. It cannot cope with speeding up etc. There is little room for the subtlety in ornamentation etc - well nobody would really hear it.

Four: we are performers - maybe solo maybe group. Chances are we will create our own arrangements according the styles we have developed. So tune settings from sites such as this may be a starting point But they will only be a shadow of what we end up doing
