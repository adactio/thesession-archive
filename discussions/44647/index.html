“… isn’t the UK in Europe …”

Great Britain (which makes up the greater part of the UK) has, by virtue of physical geography, been separate from mainland Europe since prehistoric times (sorry to state the obvious). Physical insularity brings with it an element of cultural insularity. In my memory (being British born, raised and resident), there has always been a notion of ‘The Continent’ as a monolithic entity, separate from ‘The United Kingdom’, ‘Britain’, ‘The British Isles’ or ‘Great Britain’. Recent political developments aside, a reference to ‘Europe and The UK’ is not anomalous.

Incidentally, when I was in Latvia in the late 90s (not quite 10 years post-Communism), it was commonplace for people to refer to all things to do with Western Europe (i.e. west of the former ‘Iron Curtain’ - including the UK, of course) as ‘European’; they regarded ‘Europe’ as something separate from themselves, their country and their culture, irrespective of contiguity and tectonics.

Re: How will the spread of the corona virus affect your session going, if at all? Esp for those of you in Europe and the U.K.

Seriously, Three numbers: Transmission Rate, Death Rate, Time Period.

(1) If the transmission rate is below 1, then the infection dies off. If each sick person infects two others, then the infection grows exponentially, doubling every time period. In 8 time periods, you infect 2^16 = 65,000 people. Coronavirus, meet Italy.

(2) The death rate for younger people is fairly low. But it becomes very high for older people with pre-existing health issues like smoking, heart, lung, problems. Divide these numbers in half if they make you scared, and you will still be scared:
18% 80+
10% 70-80
5% 60 - 70

(3) Time period. Do you infect two other people each week or each day? The hospital system has X number of beds. If the disease surges quickly, then you quickly run out of beds. Witness Italy.

(4) One more number: The coronavirus is now ubiquitous and pandemic. If 1% of the people in your city have it, then, a group of 10 people gives you a smallish chance of infection. In a group of 1000, then 10 people probably have it. Thus, concerts and sporting events are being shut down.

Personally, I’m on the hermit-ish end of the scale: I work from home, but also teach dance, which I am cancelling. In the past week, I encountered about 20 people, not counting store clerks, door knobs, faucets, shopping carts.
