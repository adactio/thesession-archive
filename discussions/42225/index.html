With little respect for Yoda I think llig would appreciate a fellow player who puts in her personal best effort.

I try. Personally I think Luke Skywalker’s mentor was attempting to be motivational rather than literal when he said, “Just do it, already! No TRY…only DO.”

O.K. I’m paraphrasing. But I think learning, whether it’s trad music or following the Jedi’s lessons, it requires time, effort, trying and personal improvement. And that’s enough, because to appreciate the experiences of art & of a life well spent one only needs to know (& only compete with) oneself. In music, in session I don’t want to compete; I don’t “need” the additional pressure of being better than anyone other than myself. All I need is to make an effort to be my own personal best and appreciate all the other players who I choose to make music with.

Ben

Re: With little respect for Yoda I think llig would appreciate a fellow player who puts in her personal best effort.

I measure myself by how well I can join in, how many tunes I manage to play, whether I can keep up with the pace, whether I seem to be improving in comparison with last time, and whether, on slower airs, the tone of my fiddle sounds nice.

Obviously I notice the ability of other players, and since I’m a pensioner-returner to the fiddle, there are a few people who are better than me. :)
That’s good when I’m playing along and improving my playing while having the odd mistake covered by their excellence. And it forces me to ‘up my game’.

However, it annoys me when some of the good players set out to play solos, to show how good they are by playing Eastern European tunes at a Celtic session, or recently-composed ‘jazzy’ tunes to show how cool they are, or else they’re playing as fast as they can go in order to boast and exclude just about everyone else in the session and not just me.

But I don’t envy players like that - because it isn’t how I want to play.
My goal for my playing is to play the music that I love (Scottish, Irish, English & American traditional/folk music) the best that I can.
I love playing my fiddle, and I feel so blessed that by a fortunate coincidence I took it up again in my third age.

And my goal for my session experience is to have a good feeling among players and audience and that nobody was excluded for long - everyone had their chance.

My fiddle my joy.

I’ve never thought of sessions as competitive spaces, but then again, I’m not a competitively minded person. The closest to competitive I get is in a conversational context. I like to have my ideas challenged so that I can hopefully come out of the dialogue with a more accurate understanding of the topic, even if it means being proven wrong. I actually get a thrill out of it, because it’s exciting to think I could learn something new.

There is only one main standard that I measure myself against when playing this music. “Does it sound Irish?”. Unfortunately, that’s a bit… difficult for me to measure from my perspective. So I reduce it to other standards. “Is it danceable?”, “Is it lilting?”, and “Can others play along?”. And… those are yet still pretty difficult to measure from my perspective. So I just hope that my peers are open and honest with me about the quality of my playing, good or bad. That way I will always know how to continue to work towards the goal. The more that I feel that I have an authentic Irish sound, the more I enjoy myself. Because that Irishness is why I appreciate and adore this music.

Re: With little respect for Yoda I think llig would appreciate a fellow player who puts in her personal best effort.

The idea behind the quote is not unique to Yoda and deserves respect. The concept is that “trying” contains an implicit statement of not being able to do it. Which is usually self fulfilling. Conal said something interesting in a flute workshop about tone - he said that if you ever catch yourself playing half heartedly then stop. Just put the flute away. Better that than learn to fail.

Every time you play a piece for “practice”, it must be an honest performance of the piece. (phrase, ornament, whatever is currently in focus for you…) And should be intended to be correct. It may fail, but your expectation for yourself should be that it will work. And if it fails then work out why and go back and do it right. (slower, more deliberately, whatever is necessary…)

Avoid ever just running through a piece to get to the end or to count repetitions. (Note that this does not mean you shouldn’t do things like closing your eyes and twiddling your fingers to remind yourself how a particular piece goes… Or humming to yourself while picking up a new tune by ear. But do aim to hum it right by the time you’re finished.)

And as in all things Zen, thinking about it with the analytical part of our brain is not really going to help that much. You just have to do it!
