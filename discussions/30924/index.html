The Caller Leads or Leaves or Discombobulates? Wireless & Clueless?

From a previous discussion where comments were said toward the end about ‘wireless’ being the way of the present and future, so the caller can get down and move with the dancers, and intimating that anyone still tied to a wired mic on stage with the musicians is a bit of a dinosaur ~ I thought it was here, but couldn’t find it:

Getting Along With the Caller? (or not) :-/
# Posted on November 11th 2012 by ceolachan
https://thesession.org/discussions/30837

Which is the most considerate option over all? Is it the wireless option or sticking with the band and being in a position where you can see the whole floor, as up on a raised platform like a stage? Where is the best place to manage a dance, musicians and dancers? Maybe jumping on the band wagon of new kit and flash isn’t really the best way to go? Personally our first preference, wherever possible, is for things to be un-plugged, non-electric‘’.

But, I’m really looking for stories again with regards to such things, stories about the highs and lows and pitfalls of callers ~ from callers, musicians and dancers…

I’ll start with one, being well aware of the attraction of wireless and having seen it in many situations, but, as will be the usual, there will be some of my bias in this. This tale is from the two of us and from the dance floor. Here’s my little offering to start it off:

We were at a ceili which was part of a celebration of a life, a dear friend. There was a meal. There was a dedication and there were speaches. And, after all that, there was the ceili(dh). They’d hired in a band and caller, no one we knew. Most of our little community of musicians and dancers were there for it. The caller was wireless, had a headset and a pack. This caller was, it seemed to us, on a guess, just over 5 feet high, if that. All during the ceili(dh) they were mostly out of sight, that is unless they were right next to you, as they wandered around the floor. While he did walkthroughs and once we got started, wandering all over the floor during this, folks seemed confused, and you’d see some of them looking around for the source of that disembodied voice. Where were they? They’d be describing moves and calling prompts but there seemed to be a need amongst at least some of the dancers to be able to connect this floating voice to a source. They were additionally losing connection with the dancers in not being able themselves to to keep track of the overall dance floor and to see where problems were happening. Things fell apart as a result of this. I mean, there was a stage, where the band was left, and from there they would have easily been able to see all needs and hopefully address them, including where there might be a need for another couple to fill out a set.

Making it worse, they had made some so-called adjustments to standard dances, supposedly to make them ‘easier’ for the un-initiated, instead of trusting those on the floor that already were familiar with all these dances, the majority of those present. The result of all this, and in part the blame of the wireless kit, was that everyone suffered some confusion. It was still a fun night, but the caller had little to do with that. There also seemed, to several of us, to be some weakness in the link between caller and the musicians too.

So the caller gets high on breaking loose from the musicians and getting down in amongst the dancers, but who is all his really for? I don’t see a clear advantage to either the musicians, the band, or the dancers. It seems to be, to me at least, something that feeds the ego of the caller, as if they really needed that extra rush of being down in the scrum of it all…

Any tales of callers - good and bad and all shades of gray inbetween? - especially if it is about going wireless or not…

Re: The Caller Leads or Leaves or Discombobulates? Wireless & Clueless?

I’ve called occasional dances at weddings and St. Patrick’s Day parties and a wireless mic is invaluable. I only call in situations where almost nobody knows the dance. I use the wireless to demonstrate and get everyone lined up. There’s no “high” about it. It simply saves me from shouting over the party noise background and losing my voice.

I agree that some people find the disembodied voice aspect of wireless confusing, so I try to stay on stage if possible. I’ll tell a counterstory about wireless being useful though. I did a St. Patrick’s gig at a nursing home with a trio. I was informed that everyone wanted to try a dance and NOBODY knew how. I was calling alone, as the other two musicians needed to play the music. As careful as my explanation was, the dance started to fall apart. I had to spend the whole dance calling from the floor, nudging people into place and making sure the progression at the ends worked. There was no way it would have worked without the wireless mic because I had nobody on the dance floor to help out.

I’m old fashioned - A wireless is great for teaching in the middle of a circle; but home base is better for the dancers to refer if questions arise in a longways set. The caller can perceive possible problems better from up above than down on the floor. Re: The Caller Leads or Leaves or Discombobulates? Wireless & Clueless?

I find a wireless mike handy for the walkthrough, when you can be down on the floor gently guiding people in the direction they hadn’t realised they needed to move in ( aka pushed ). Once the dance starts it’s better to be on the stage, if there is one, where you might be able to see more of what’s going on down on the floor. And you’re next to the rest of the band.
I don’t carry an umbrella with a duck’s head handle or whatever, but I do wear a distinctive hat ( think Terry Pratchett with a colourful bandana ).
I tried using the headpiece system, as my guitarist was very keen on the high-tech approach, but you try wearing a) a headpiece mike b) a hat c) a ponytail, and d) putting on a bass-guitar over all that !
Calling for another band but not playing was easier, I like to be at the front of the stage, where you can mime/mimic the actions you want to get the dancers to do.

As to pushing people on the floor…….no matter how carefully and concisely you describe a manoeuvre there will be some people who either don’t understand what you have asked them to do, or will persist in doing something else because they think that’s what you meant.
Part of this is from there no longer being a large enough number of opportunities for people to do barn/country/ceilidh dancing, so you are starting from scratch much of the time with an audience on the floor.
So, occasionally, you DO have to push; ever so gently, of course.
On the other hand sometimes the audiences aren’t bothered they’re not doing this very well, but are still having a whale of a time, in which case it’s best to let chaos rule, so long as it’s not actually dangerous. I draw the line at baskets where the ladies‘ feet leave the floor, I always make the point that the “gentlemen’” causing this to happen are responsible for the medical bills and damages to the people behind them.

↳ Posted by Guernsey Pete on November 26th, 2012.

The general cliche ~ don’t blame the tool!?

I can only speak from my experience, but, in general, and there are always exceptions, I find that those that need to be down on the floor are often those that shouldn’t really be calling at all, that have misjudged their crowd, the dancers, and have at some point lost communication, or haven’t had it in the first place. I know more dancers that have that problem though, who resort to ‘pushing’, virtual or real. A ‘push’ doesn’t necessarily mean a physical shove or tug, but that happens too.

The loss of understanding should never be ‘blamed’ on those who have come for a good time. It should always be taken on hand by the caller, that just maybe they’ve screwed up, either causing confusion through vague or misleading descriptions, or having not prepared properly, and prepared for all possibilities, or that the caller has just chosen the wrong dance for those present. There is that responsibility about calling that a lot of folk who chose to do it, or are accidentally thrown into it, can often be clueless about. Too often a caller comes in with a set intention and little or no flexibility to adapt to a given situation.

As you’ve so rightly commented, the opportunities to develop as a dancer, to gain experience, are less and less and more irregular and inconsistent, unless you’ve taken on that mantle as ‘dancer’ and chase up every opportunity going. But, and I’ve plenty of experience here, that doesn’t necessarily make you a ‘good’ or ‘considerate’ dancer. The same goes for the tag ‘musician’. Obsession doesn’t equal quality. So, you’re right, less experience out there, and even where there is ‘experience’, that doesn’t necessarily mean skill. This is also true for calling and callers. Just because you do, or want to, does not mean there’s any skill involved, it does not guarantee you’ll be a good, aware, courteous and adaptable caller. Of this, I’m sure, there are no shortage of examples. A great caller can zero in on a problem and carefully untangle a mess without having to jump off the stage and chase down the problem and take command. A good caller give confidence and doesn’t take it away. Resorting to the physical, and even directing too much attention to a problem, in general, robs those under focus, being pushed, of confidence.

You’re right thee too GP, when a little nudge is needed, and I do not mean physical, you do have to be gentle, kind, reinforcing the right action, not really shoving. And yes, sometimes you do need to let go of the reigns from time to time, when folks are having a great time despite things not being 100% perfect, but you have to guage that for the whole floor, not just the few having a whale of a time whippin‘ it up. The craic is a collective one, and sometimes you need to put a ’gentle’ cap on the kind of exhuberance that can spoil it for the usually less expressive majority. It’s a balancing act, always.

Yes, the basket swing, WHEW! There is an art, and several cultures have different ways to do this, where there can be a launch, WELL SUPPORTED, but not at a community ceili(dh). I’ve seen the over exhuberant leg lifting basket swing end up with that basket breaking up and someone flying across the floor and against and under a table, and in another situation one poor girl dislocated her shoulder, and in another situation someone in a set nearby was violently and painfully kicked. And then there have been shoes flying off too. But even a simple swing, I have suffered from a torn shoulder blade for years, caused by a very large and exhuberant friend whose weight grew exponentially with her forcing the swing. She had all the weight, she had control, and while she may have had fun, I’m stuck with a pain in my shoulder blade. I did mention ‘courtesy and consideration’ earlier, that applies to all concerned, and that mood is also part of the responsibility of the caller. You are always doing more than just teaching and calling dances, more than just helping make the connection between music and dance, musicians and dancers. The caller sets the mood, helps or hinders the craic depending on how they manage that too. If you’re patient, considerate, courteous, the dancers will generally pick up on that and respond in kind. If you’re miserable and impatient, that too will multiply. All that can be subtle, but it can also be blatant.

A good caller observes and adapts, and that might require all kinds of things, including being on the floor from time to time. ;-) It’s like any kind of teaching, the best is where you find the strengths of the person being taught and help them to teach themselves, so not like shoving grain down a funnel in order to make Fois Gras, it should, ideally, be more ‘free range’… You provide the feed in an obvious and clear way and they’ll find it and nourish themselves. ;-)
