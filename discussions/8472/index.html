Apologies to newer people here but feel free to comment anyway, but to those of us who have hung on for X amount of years….(I’ve been here since Fergus was about a couple of months old. I’m just curious. I don’t think I’d be insulted with most answers.

Re: How have we changed…

This site has definitely made the world a bit smaller for me. It’s kinda comforting knowing that wherever I go, there’s bound to be somebody who knows of this site, is a frequent poster, or I’ve had correspondence with. I don’t think there are many social areas in life where people can say they have access to or know people in countless dozens of cities, states, countries, and continents. This site has allowed me to make that claim, which I think is a pretty cool claim.

In terms of this site as a resource, this has become my one-stop shop. When I first started coming here (earlier than I realized--thought I came later in terms of the site’s existence, but apparently not), the tune database in particular was pretty sparse, so I would have to go elsewhere for tunes, but now I can pretty much always find the tune I’m looking for, check what CD I have the tune on, and then go and learn the thing.

Really nothing but praise for the development of this site over the 4 years or so I’ve been around it. Only difference is that when I first came here, I would be surprised if I went to a session and met somebody who knew about the site. Now I’m almost surprised if I don’t meet somebody who knows about the site. Takes away from the novelty a bit, but really can’t complain otherwise.

Re: How have we changed…

I haven’t been here all that long, but the site has evolved in many ways since I’ve been here. Jeremy keeps adding new bells and whistles in response to queries he puts out to the membership. His changes have improved many aspects of the board.

There are still problems, and I have no idea how they can possibly be resolved. The way the tunes from recordings are linked to the tune database is the biggest one. But there are so many ways that the board and tune/recording database are useful that it really is minor by comparison.

Whenever a new tune I like comes into my awareness either from a recording or hearing it at a session, the session.org is my first stop for sorting it out. I try to find the tune first, and then see if I have it on any recordings. If I don’t I might get the recording or just begin looking for different transcriptions. When I find it in the database I immediately go to comments hoping to find different settings to explore. I’ll be delighted to sometimes find out who composed the tune and other stories behind it to add to my intrigue. It’s hard to imagine not having this access now.

And of course one of the best things is meeting in person people you’ve gotten to know in here. Being able to ring Jimmy in Dublin and meet up with him at the Cobblestone, running into Ottery on the street in Ennis, meeting Helen at a session there, and all the folks who come to the Plough and Stars. Not to mention either donuts (if she has her way) or pizza (if I get my way) after the session with Zina.

My experience is that the tone was friendly and conversational from the start. We’ve gone through cycles of hair splitting, but these usually resolve back to a more generous spirit.

Before Jeremy opened the site, his comments about the tunes he was posting set the tone--they were informative, friendly, and unassuming. So the first open threads took off in that same tenor.

Also, a lot of the earliest threads tended to focus on questions about how to play the music or a particular technique on a particular instrument. Naturally, there was less editorializing and speechifying on such threads. If they ran long, it was because it can take a lot of words to describe the details of how to do a bowed triplet, for example.

Yet the crack was good--I miss the quips from Glauber, and Mad Baloney’s wind ups. But today’s comics are fun to read too.
