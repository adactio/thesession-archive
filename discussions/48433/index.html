Re: Harry Bradley says….

Harry’s description of sharing the tradition is accurate to a tee!

But have to make one point here, as there appears not to be any other interested. Accent; Getting Irish music in Ireland is utterly and completely different to getting it anyplace else. Because it is not just the music; but, also the society around it, the local accent, the events where it happens and the way, by ear over there, it is shared among native players. IOW if you needed the dots, they can be got, but for the most part not necessary.

Whereas, over here the major source is media, concerts, music schools, for example the O’Flaherty Music Retreat here in Texas, and, OC, sessions. The rest of that topic is an adventure in new ways of playing old tunes.

Yet today with the trend in Irish venues in the USA going back to ballads and away from tunes, -my local The Cottage Irish Pub on Broadway San Antonio is a great example- , the source of your schooling will become irrelevant, just so long as you can blast a couple of fast tunes in the appropriate place/s. Think Pogues, Dubliners, Andy Irvine, as opposed to The Bothy Band, etc. Here, I recall at events in Ireland there would be a sing song band with an Accordion player just for a couple of tunes, reels ‘The Bucks Of Oranmore’, ‘The Sally Gardens’ and so on, if a jig you’d often hear ‘Up Sligo’, or something similar. In those times ‘trad’ was not pure drop, or perceived to be solely from anyplace, it was just local, nothing more. Folks would never ever say that a player wasn’t ‘Clare’ enough for their taste, like I heard over here about a musician. They would not say such a thing because they could not even imagine it; what was on ‘Ceilidh House’ on Radio Eireann being their source of Irish music education, not the internet which had not been invented.

So, yeah! get it by ear and enjoy your local flavor of Irish music, that’s how popular music grows.
