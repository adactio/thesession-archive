I’m with Cheeky Elf on this one. I play bouzouki, guitar, mandolin, tenor banjo (albeit badly) and hammered dulcimer. Bouzouki’s tend to be accompaniment instruments though there are those of us that use them primarily for melody. Mandolins are almost strictly melody instruments because chords on them sort of sound sucky. Same with a banjo. Hammered dulcimers can do either but are generally called upon to play melodies. The guitar can do anything in the hands of a good player, in the hands of a less-than-accomplished player, it can be a detriment to a session. Concerning portability, wind instruments take the least amount of space at a crowded session with mandolins coming in second. Then guitars, bouzouki’s and hammered dulcimers.

As an after thought. The Joe Foley though, is a very quiet instrument and perhaps that’s why those style of instruments seem to be getting a bit less play. All of this can be taken with a bit of salt though as there is no way one individual can discern what’s trending among so many very accomplished musicians. I only wish I were one. Sigh…

Hi Katie,
Re your question to me, I’m going to hedge and say “yes and no”. Some Scottish tunes will sound unmistakably Scottish, especially strathspeys, by virtue of all those “snaps”, and others could only be Irish, but there is an overlap, as I hinted before, with some tunes being claimed as originating both from Scotland and Ireland, with different names in each place.
Terminology can be a double-edged sword as you’ve already found on this thread.
In Glasgow, every January, we have an amazing music festival called “Celtic Connections ”- it started out as less than a week long, but now lasts nearly 3 weeks, and has achieved a global reputation : in the early days it mainly featured artists from those countries/areas considered to be Celtic, i.e. Scotland, Ireland, Wales, Cornwall, Brittany, Isle of Man, North of Spain(Galicia and Asturias especially) and places where Celts had emigrated to, such as Cape Breton, and with an emphasis on the folk and traditional music of those Over the years, it has encompassed pretty well all genres of music from just about every continent, with increasingly tenuous “Celtic Connections” - to the extent that some people have asked if a name change for the festival would be warranted.
