Re: There are “too many tunes already”!

Every time this discussion comes up, I wonder if it wouldn’t be more productive to look at Tune Families, as opposed to most popular, most hated, most overplayed etc. I’m not the best person to discuss the theory, but I’ve read several comments from Philippe Varlet (for example) talking about tune families and the relation between various groups of tunes. This seems like it would be a more useful approach to understanding Irish music than saying, “Well, I’ve learned my first 100, only 901 left to go…”

After you’ve learned the common tunes in your session, wouldn’t you be better off becoming familiar with the tune families around The Bucks, Drowsy Maggie etc. in each of the modes? (I don’t know enough to know if the families cross modes yet, although I imagine they do.)

Not that there’s anything wrong with learning tunes as they catch your fancy, but after many years of that I’m trying to organize them into some semblance of order, so that I’ll still be able to recognize two or three during my declining years.

I’m not arguing for a rigid taxonomic hierarchy (ok, the biologist in me is…), but a better understanding of the natural groupings of tunes, that would allow us to put new tunes in perspective, and make it clearer to learners where the extraneous and the essential tunes are.

On the other hand, if the idea of tune families is just a construct and there are no natural groupings other than the modes, I’d like to know that too.

Re: There are “too many tunes already”!

“If it’s traditional it ought to be older than your ownself.”

I agree; and have said so.

Most Irish music today, even that played by those who fancy themselves as “traditional,” is played in a style that I myself can remember arising; and I’m just a Baby Boomer. A Neo-Baby Boomer at that.

Standard disclaimer: I have no problem with that. I do it myself. My only problem arises when it is promologated as traditional, never mind when such new styles are promologated as “what The Music is really all about.”

I can write all the the poetry I want in the ancient Greek language and style, but they are not anceint Greek poems, because I am neither ancient nor Greek.

“So it would have to be based on structure alone--shared melodic phrases, mostly”

Oh, absolutely. That’s the whole point of the idea of a family. A mammal isn’t defined by its chronology, but by whether it’s warm blooded, bears live young, is covered with hair, etc.

This gets to the root of some of the issues I’ve had here when discussing instruments. After nearly 50 years of playing every one I can get my hands on, and about 35 years of playing around with making them, often experimentally, I have a very hard time seeing an instrument as an individual “thing.” I see the family and all the possible variations within it. Compared to a pipe organ the guitar and banjo are the same damed instrument, a box of some sort with a stick on it, like a fiddle.

Which last point raises the issue that even the idea of family is variable depending upon the perspective you chose to view things from. What could be thought of as the “standard” taxonomy of instruments is really just one of a few dozen of them, depending on what you’re talking about. In one the guitar, banjo and fiddle are all so closely related they’re virtually indistinguishable, in another they aren’t related at all. It’s a matter of similarities and differences, not absolute identity.

Same with tunes. It’s not a simple issue.

There’s only so much within the bounds of the music that can be tampered with until it comes full circle. Eventually, people go back to the roots. No? I guess I think of the whole thing as a running tap… the drops come out at random but they adhere to the rules of flow and direction… they maintain a bounds. Diddley, I think, is a lot like that.

So, finally getting to the point, I think there can never be enough tunes… that’s what makes the tradition grow… that’s what keeps people interested. The ones that people like, will live the ones that people don’t will die. Water that drips on your forhead in the same spot for a long time can drive you mad.
