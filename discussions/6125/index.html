Post Paddy’s Day Analysis - How was your’s?

Well how was it for ye all?

Brought my two youngest kids over to see the parade in Downpatrick and the atmosphere was great.

New up and coming band Beoga were on stage and despite the worst efforts of the sound man they came across really well. Young Eamonn, Damien and Seán Óg, and Niamh will go far.

Unfortunately, music wise, that was about it. Went to Speedy Mullan’s and despite the notice on the blackboard declaring the session started at 3.00 there wasn’t a musician in sight. Well - there were three bodhrán players and me with my whistle and flute but hardly a quorum for a session!

Thankfully we made up for it later with a great wee session up in Jim English’s pub in the hills up above Castlewellan.

Great tunes, good craic, loadsa Powers, a couple of chilling out breaks - mmmmmmmm

Finally back to a friends house for grub, more chilling, and a few tunes to wrap up the evening before hitting the sack.

All in all? Not bad at all.

I hesitate here to mention anything that skates anywhere near politics - but this next thing is an amazing, indicative, and positive thing, in my view. My bro pointed out that the Sussex Arms on Long Acre (in the middle of London) is one of these pubs, festooned with St Pat’s banners, and had a huge crowd spilling out of the doors, in giant green hats, last night. Back when Anglo-Irish relations were less cordial, the Sussex Arms was actually bombed by the IRA. Could there be a more graphic depiction of how things have changed in the last couple of decades?

Roaring Jelly gets Rave Reviews!

“. . .you weren’t great--you weren’t fantastic--you were PHENOMINAL!!!!!!”

“. . .They talked about the NM motto: It grows as it goes. Apparently, the state wants to get rid of it because nobody knows what it means. I do: it is about Roaring Jelly.”

Of course, these were from occasional members of the band - one from south of Albuquerque, and one from Cambridge, England - who came back to join us for the St. Pat’s gig, but still. . .

We did have a semblance of a sound system this year so the back of the bar could often hear the bass of the piano, sort of like those car systems that come up behind you going “umBOOM umBOOM”, so that, uh, helped. But we also had a good 4 hours worth of music (counting the time between sets as “music” I guess - it might have been appreciated as much as the music :>) ) so we played from 7:30 until after 11 with only one repeat of a set (we did our French Canadian set last - by that time at the bar, who knew?). I must admit we improved on Danny Boy this year - the little high counter melodies from the peanut gallery and the scmaltzy Ab chord before the final drawn out “so - o - o - o” at the end left us all - laughing. Ah, wouldn’t be St. Pat’s without it.

We had 9 of us; 3 fiddles, a banjo, a mandolin/bodhran/spoons, a bodhran/bones/whistle, a pianist/vocalist, a pianist/spoons, and me on piano/hammered dulcimer/wooden-soled shoes. Pretty typical of our “who can show up?” instrumentation. And our new guitar player came as our fan club. We don’t have anywhere *near* enough roadies (I hear that’s common - maybe we don’t feed them enough), just one pianist’s boyfriend and a Russian dancer (yes, to Irish jigs and reels) friend of the Cambridge fiddler who helped us schlep about 3 loads per car. We do offer official band business cards with your name on them to anyone who might be interested. . .

We work for - hm. I think we got $69 in tips last night. Not our record. The next big gig that we know about at the minute is July for the Kitchen Angels Lavendar Faire at the local living history museum. We hope to help them raise more than $69 for their lunch-for-shut-ins programs.

Cathy

Re: Post Paddy’s Day Analysis - How was your’s?

The Morris dancers were actually quite fun. On a minor correction they were the Belchamp Mrris Men from Essex, not Kent. They were also not dressed in white but were “blacked up” with painted faces, black shirts, trousers and jackets and millions and milions of badges. I was chatting away to one of them and he said “Don’t cheer. It will only encourage them. You will never see a bigger pile of pap in all your life.” He explained that it amazed them how they always got such a good reception in Scotland as back home they were considered a bunch of “poofs” - I suspect that is because seeing them is a rarity and has novelty value.

They were not really Paddy’s Day celebrations. It was Lochgoilhead Fiddle Workshop’s annual performance really, primarily a chance for their classes to perform to an audience. It was great entertainment and it continued afterwads with us all packed into the local pub singing away at the oddest session ever, complete with Morris band, various guitars and percussion.

All together now:

“I’m singing in the rain, just singing in the rain, what a glorious feeling I’m ha ha happy again. Thumbs together! A ti ta - A ti ta - A ti ta ta - A ti ta - A ti ta - A ti ta ta!!”
