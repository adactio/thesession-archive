Re: Looking for an Air

I’ve explained this before. You can submit slow airs… as long as they fit into some kind of recognised pattern, e.g. a slow jig, a slow reel, a slow waltz, etc.

The reason for this is that the term “slow air” says absolutely nothing about the the note length or how many beats per bar the tune has - it only tells us that it’s slow. And an air :-)

I would include slow airs in a heartbeat if all slow airs had the same structure (e.g., 4/4 time with 1/8 note length). But just think for a minute about the slow airs you know. Some are in 3/4, some in 4/4, some in 6/8.

So, to repeat, yet again, once more, hopefully for the last time…

You *can* include slow airs. The tune won’t be listed as being a “slow air”, it will be listed according the meter it is in (jig, reel, etc.). If you can live with that, submit to your heart’s content and use the “comments” section to explain how slowly it should be played.

If you insist that there should be a category called “slow airs”, I’m sorry - no can do. It would be as descriptive as having a category called “really cool” or “old chestnuts” but it wouldn’t tell us anything about the meter of the tune.

As for songs, the same applies. You can submit the melody (provided it fits into an existing tune type such as jig, reel, etc.) and use the “comments” section to provide lyrics. This has already been done with some tune submissions.

But again, if there were a category called “songs”, it would be impossible to generate the ABC headers for that category… unless we decided beforehand that songs had to be, for example, in 2/4 time with 1/16 note length.

Everything clear now?

Re: Looking for an Air

Since I’m the eejit who posted Anach Cuain, I guess I’ll take a stab at responding to Fintan’s comments (and those of others who posted in the comments section under the tune itself).

I live in Montana. There are no ITM players here (including me) well grounded in the tradition. I found Anach Cuain in sheet music form, written in 6/8 time with no tempo suggested. But it clearly plays as an air, which is how I play it despite never having heard it performed.

When I typed it into The Session as abcs, I assumed I had no control over tempo…as far as I know, Jeremy sets the playback speed, and the abcs themselves don’t stipulate tempo--there’s no line for it. 6/8 is a time signature, not a tempo setting.

Frankly, I’ve enjoyed learning about the song…a deeper meaning I might never have come across if I had not posted this version here. I would never claim that mine was some sort of authoritative version, and it may not even match the song melody for all I know. I certainly didn’t suggest anyone should play it at “dance time.” I guess I don’t assume that 6/8 always means “jig” any more than I would assume 4/4 always means “reel” played at 90,000 rpm.

I own a copy of Tomas O’Canainn’s book “Traditional Slow Airs of Ireland,” by Ossian Publications, and I notice that the airs are written in “standard” times: 4/4, 2/4, 12/8, 3/4, 9/8, and yes, even 6/8. I can see that it might make more sense to write some 6/8 airs in 3/4, but given that the sheet music is such a poor approximation anyway, I’ve never worried about it when reading airs. The phrasing--and tempo--usually asserts itself as the air unfolds. And I usually try to find an aural source if I happen across a transcription for an air I want to learn (please let’s not rehash the whole argument about the validity of sheet music in an aural tradition).

As for Fintan’s larger question, I’ll take Jeremy’s lead and when I post an air, I’ll mention in the comments section that the tune is to be played slowly and freely, not strictly as notated. Doesn’t that solve it? I mean, don’t we all do that when we learn a tune off The Session anyway? If you’re playing jigs as straight groups of eighth notes, I guarantee you’re not sounding authentic. But the convention is to notate them that way. Aren’t airs subject to the same conventions (and limits) of sheet music?

To me the only shortcoming of this wonderful site is that too few of us use the comment section with the tunes. It’s disappointing when a new tune shows up and there’s no story or anecdote or history or source for that version or playing tips or interpretation from someone who knows what it really sounds like. And it’s almost as disappointing when no one responds to the comments that are posted with a tune--like it’s been ignored or abandoned there. I’m surprised actually, with how talkative we appear to be, that there aren’t long discussion threads in most of the tune comment sections. Whazzup with that?

Re: Looking for an Air

For those who don’t know who Jean Ritchie is, we have her to thank in a large part for the revival of interest in folk/traditional music in Ireland, Britain, and North America, and connecting those traditions with her Kentucky mountain music. Her Fulbright scholarship to go to Ireland to record folk musicians brought a new sense in people’s minds of the importance of traditional music, on both sides of the Atlantic. Tommy Makem describes how this American woman came to their house to record folk music, and all he knew was one tune on the whistle. When he realized how important that music was for someone to come all the way from America to document it, he decided to learn more. The archives of her recordings and her husband’s photographs are the Ritchie/Pickow archive in Galway. Here are her words, “Alice is referring to the Ritchie-Pickow Archive, housed in the Hardiman Library at University College Galway, in Ireland. It consists of George’s and my work during my Fulbright Year, 1952-53. We recorded and photographed musicians in Ireland, England and Scotland, but this archive has only the Irish materials. It is set up as a traveling exhibit, available to any organization or school in Ireland. It’s very appealing to me, because, as one walks around looking at photos of the singers or players, their music is playing in the background.”

Here is a photo from that project of Jean Ritchie recording Seamus Ennis:
http://www.irishmusicweb.ie/texts/ennis.html
