Re: Key signatures

Beethoven called B minor the black key. Quote wiki:

“In Baroque times, B minor was regarded as the key of passive suffering. The theorist Christian Friedrich Daniel Schubart (1739-1791) regarded B minor as a key expressing a quiet acceptance of fate and very gentle complaint, something commentators find to be in line with Bach’s use of the key in the St. John’s Passion. By Beethoven’s time, however, the perception of B minor had changed considerably: Francesco Galeazzi wrote that B minor was not suitable for music in good taste, and Beethoven labelled a B minor melodic idea in one of his sketchbooks as a ‘black key’.”

Re: Key signatures

It’s worth noting that it’s just the key that effects how you feel about the tunes, but the range. The vast majority of diddley music is within the range of the D string on the fiddle and the bottom D on the flute and pipes, to the high B, the pinky on your e string. A little under two octaves. And using only the notes D, E, occasionally Fnat, F#, G, occasionally G#, A, B, C, and C#

So, for example, a tune in B min has the range from the minor third (the bottom D) and finishing with the highest note being the tonic. This restriction plays a very important roll in the shaping of the tune.

Dmaj starts with clear octave (the bottom note being the tonic gives Dmaj and Dmin tunes a very distinct characteristic) and finishes on 6th

G maj goes from the 5th (or fourth below if you count it that way) up the the major third. With one clear octave sat right in the middle. This gives Gmaj tunes (unlike the examples above) a feel of a greater range, because they span parts of three octaves.

Re: Key signatures

Rather than colours, it’s handy to assign names to key signatures. Here’s why. If you’re leading a tune set at a session, and call out the new key just before changing to a different tune/key, your call can be misheard

In a noisy session, “G” can sound like “D”

To overcome this problem, assign names to the key sigs, and call out the associated names instead:

e.g.
“G” - George
“D” - Donald
“A” - Andrew

Re: Key signatures

Coming a bit late into this discussion i.e. it’s already descended from serious to comic or even mildly deranged - the way of many threads. Nevertheless I wanted to add my two penn’orth to Llig’s excellent analysis (lots of posts above) of why different keys feel different. Apologies if someone has already said this (I have only sped through all the posts), but another reason keys feel different, at least on fiddles, banjos and other stringed instruments is because of the different sympathetic vibrations of open strings. For example if you play a G on the D string the open low G (if it’s in tune) will vibrate in sympathy and add colour to the note. Play an F and you get little or no audible symathetic vibration from any open string. So tunes in the key of F will have a different feel to a tune in G.
