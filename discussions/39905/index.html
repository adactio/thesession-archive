Re: Bad selfmade flute headjoint. (Much) help needed

Since nobody seems to have mentioned it yet…

Is your delrin headjoint conical or cylindrical? I suspect cylindrical… Boehm headjoints are conical with a cylindrical body and Irish flutes (simple-system) are the opposite. There is absolutely no way you can get a cylindrical Boehm body to play on pitch with a cylindrical headjoint - it just wasn’t designed to work that way.

Re: Bad selfmade flute headjoint. (Much) help needed

It’s not the total length rather the total volume that bears on tuning. You need to consider the additional volume as added by the chimney. Also the position of the hole has to be in the right place as when a sound wave travels it’s has areas of maximum and minimum movement: antinodes and nodes respectively.
Woodwind are particularly sensitive at this end of the instrument, think sax/ clarinet/ uilleann reeds . There is a balancing act between tone, tuning and playability and that’s why it’s such a fine art and people spend serious money to get seriously good instruments.

Re: Bad selfmade flute headjoint. (Much) help needed

@Koos
“meant to write WESTERN concert flute. The headjoint is cilidrical and the body is cylindrical.”

Nope, no such animal. Well, some cheaply made wood, bamboo or PVC flutes (usually with tuning problems unless addressed as below) or a fife.

Silver/Boehm flutes are cylindrical bodies with a headjoint that APPEARS cylindrical (outside diameter), but actually have a tapered/wedge or parabolic bore inside (to correct tuning - hah!).

Irish flutes have bodies with a tapered bore, paired with a cylindrical headjoint that has a cylindrical bore.
