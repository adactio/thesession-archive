Re: Considering a Tenor Banjo, thoughts?

There are a couple of issues with trying to use vellum (skin) heads on a banjo, especially depending on where you live. Vellum heads are much more reactive to changes in temperature and humidity. You might find yourself in a session, where the banjo plays fine at the beginning, but as the night goes on, it gets warmer and more humid in the pub from all the people, and the head softens and starts sounding different, and then you start to get buzzing and ultimately might not be able to play at all. I have had a few vellum heads that were in the 80-100 year old range, and they were a bit more stable, but still prone to changing depending on the conditions. Having said that, the vellum heads often create a more complex tone that can be interesting.

Another thing to consider is how your right hand position is going to be - whether you’re going to brush your fingers, or the backs of your fingers on the head, whether you’re going to plant a finger (or fingers) on the head, or whether you’re going to not let your hand touch the head at all… Different head materials will sound different depending on whether your hand it touching it at all. Fingers brushing on the head can make a scratching noise, which is by far the worst on a frosted top head, followed by vellum, and then the Renaissance heads (which are a papery plastic, which can sound more like vellum in tone), but smooth plastic heads don’t create much sound if you’re brushing against them.

If you’re looking to expand your banjo listening repertoire a bit, look up Cathal Hayden, Damien O’Kane, John Carty, Angelina Carberry, and Enda Scahill. You’ll find some good variety in there…

I love vintage banjos and collect them but I don’t really recommend them for beginners unless you’re familiar with all of the issues that may accompany them or can pay someone to do a proper setup. I look for vintage banjos that are as close to ‘original’ condition as possible and that means many come with ‘skin’ heads. Beginners should be able to find a modern good sturdy banjo without too many setup issues.. Gold Tone or Deering are good choices.

Avoid skin - get a decent clear plastic head - skin is aesthetically pleasing but a devil with tuning. I have an Emerald Elite (clear head) and it holds its tuning really well. I had skin on a banjo mandolin once and it was a nightmare to tune and keep in tune

Re: Considering a Tenor Banjo, thoughts?

There are a lot of factors that can affect the tone of a tenor banjo. The head material and head tension may have the biggest effect, but tone system, pick material, pick gauge, rim material, and tailpiece can all affect the tone somewhat significantly. This is why a good setup goes a long way, but it’s also possible that the person who sets up the banjo might have different tastes in what it should sound like. So it’s helpful to know how to do some of the tinkering yourself!

Responding to several different posts on this discussion.
Strap or no strap: I played without a strap for many years, but realized my left hand was too anchored on the neck, supporting the weight and keeping the neck still. Since adding a strap (whether playing sitting or standing) , I found more freedom to move my fretting hand.
Vellum/skin head v. plastic: my 1930 Paramount William Lange Model B came with all its original parts, including friction tuners and skin head. I found myself re-tuning every few minutes. Changed out the tuners and head years ago, and never looked back.
My banjo has a heavy resonator, which I leave on almost all the time. I control volume in several ways: I use quite light gauge strings (.09mm E to .28mm G), light picks (.5-.7mm max), and picking “attack” angle and force.
Good luck, OP. Get yourself a modestly priced one and have at it!

Re: Considering a Tenor Banjo, thoughts?

Great thread, lots of good comments. One option to consider (you’re in Florida?) is Craigslist. It’s a weird place with lots of jerks and scammers, but I’ve had very good luck finding tenor banjos there – 2 Bacons, a Slingerland, a Vega, and a Bacon banjo-mandolin.

I have a fetish (for want of a better term) for vintage US made instruments, and all of mine are pre-1930. If you go this route, plan on upgrades (new head, possibly switching to geared tuners, neck reset, and bridge).

With regard to head tension, it does make a difference. Too loose and the tone sucks and the nuts fall off. Too tight and it usually sounds tinny. I’ve been using a drum dial to make sure that I’ve got consistent tension (regardless of what the intended setting is) around the head (usually measure as close to the rim as possible). I’ve seen ads for expensive little torque wrenches, but with older banjos there are so many different friction spots that a torque wrench is a joke.

Regardless of whether you buy new or old, you’re probably better off if you can do some of the tinkering yourself. It’s mostly mechanical stuff and you can undo most things if they aren’t right. The best analogy – google “monty python bicycle repairman”.

You’ve got a pretty good audience here – let us know what you do and how it works out.

Re: Considering a Tenor Banjo, thoughts?

As far as the sustain on a banjo vs. mandolin, I will say that they’re just different in the curve. A mandolin has a smoother dropoff of sustain. A banjo has a sharper attack, and a quicker dropoff, but the note will still ring for a long time if you let it. I think you will find that pretty any of the left hand ornamentation will be easier, physically, on the banjo. The string tensions are lower, and there’s only one string per course. But it’s harder to hammer on a note and have it ring on banjo, because it doesn’t have the sharp attack. Most of the hammer on ornaments that I do come very shortly after a pick, which can sound somewhat like a slide…
