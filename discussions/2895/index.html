Re: Out of Tune! MOI!?!

It can be a constant struggle. I think there are three reasons why it happens:

1) Your body changes in relation to the flute as you play over the course of an evening. You may start to slouch or jut your chin forward - giving you that “tear-drop” shaped embouchure. These things can cause you to go sharp. It’s good to evaluate how you’re interacting with the instrument periodically throughout the sesh.

2) The wood warms up.

3) Other people go out of tune. I have a hard time if I’m in tune and there are other flute players out of tune. I find that I’ll start to match them.

I think that an electronic tuner can help. And definitely tune to the “G” note. Don’t listen to what the other instruments will tell you to do. Do not tune to an “A” or a “D” if you’re playing simple system, especially an older flute. It’ll just throw your intonation further out of tune.

Tuning’s a really important thing to be conscious of. You’ll have more fun in the sesh if other people enjoy having you there. Some people can be a pain in the rear about tuning, spending more time tuning than playing and after a while it’s kind of pointless. But it’s one of the most important things to get right.

Also, if you don’t already, try playing with a fixed-reed instrument player for a while. You can’t fudge with that like you can when playing with fiddlers and other fluters. It’ll also help develop your sense of “rightness” about notes - unless it’s a really wet accordion - “I need a ‘G’ note from you… just ONE ‘G’ please!”

Re: Out of Tune! MOI!?!

Oh, one more thing… if you use an electronic tuner… be careful of how you approach the tuner. If you put in on your lap, crane your neck forward, and slouch over to get a solid note out of it you’re going to be blowing into the flute at an entirely different angle than you’ll be blowing while sitting up - hopefully. If you have to bend over to get to it you should be careful to keep your head erect and bend from your hips, not your waist. You’ll get a more true note that way. Cheers.

Sorry, Melody, but I agree with Michael … playing out of tune is more off-putting to other musicians than simply playing a wrong note here or there. Everyone makes mistakes, but playing consistently sharp or flat wrecks the flow of the music. And an attitude of “Well, I don’t care, and I’m going to keep playing anyway!” is eventually going to get wearing on your fellow players.

When I get lost in a tune, I don’t continue playing wrong notes … I stop and wait until I can pick up and know where I am, again. If I notice that a string is out of tune (perhaps a bumped peg or temp. changed) I stop playing until, between tunes, I can get someone to play me an “A” or break out my electronic tuner, to re-tune.

Re: Out of Tune! MOI!?!

Seriously, the flute is probably the most difficult instrument to play in tune and all flute players have difficulty at times especially if it is a big session.
My advice to you would be:
* Get yourself a good teacher who can help you with your intonation problems and who can let you know if your flute is in tune with itself. If it isn’t, I wouldn’t advise messing about with it unless you know what your’e doing - take it to a reputable flute maker/repairer if necessary.
* Practise at home, not in public.
* At the session sit next to an accordian or fiddle player who is always in tune and listen in to them as you play and adjust accordingly. Ask the accordian or fiddle for a note at intervals between tunes so that you stay in tune.
* The flute tends to go sharp as it warms up so adjust it by pulling the head out accordingly. You can warm up the flute without making a sound by covering all the holes, then cover the embouchure hole completely with your mouth and blow down it.
* You have to learn to use your ears to stay in tune. Automatic tuners are for string players and are of no use to flute players. If I saw a flute player using one in a session I would make sure to sit well away from them.

It is very satisfying when you are spot on in tune and well worth the effort. On occassions when you just can’t get it right I have found it is best to put the flute away and just listen instead.

Re: Out of Tune! MOI!?!

I don’t claim any sort of authority, but… I’ve recently been working with a well respected flute maker and his opinion is that you should tune to “G” with simple system flutes. He said that it’s especially a good idea to use “G” if it’s an older flute. Players of newer flutes should check with their maker, they may have tuned the flute to an “A”. But if the maker modeled the flute after an older flute the “G” rule probably still holds true. I think you should make sure the cork is in the right place and then test out tuning to an “A” a “G” and a “D”. See what works best for your flute.
