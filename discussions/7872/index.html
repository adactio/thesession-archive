↳ Posted by Ptarmigan on September 27th, 2005.

Re: do use all play just trad or classical to ?

I wouldn’t attach any shoulds or oughtas to this topic, but I think most trad players *could* benefit from exposure to the classical technical training and music theory. After playing mostly trad stuff for fifteen years, I got pretty heavily into classical guitar for about five years and it definitely helped me to be a better player in whatever style I tried. I’m sure it helped me in learning the fiddle, too.

A lot of people make the argument that classical training handicaps you for trad playing. I don’t think this is generally true. When someone who was classically trained from an early age takes up something like ITM as an adult, they do have to go through a period of adjustment, but compared with someone else with no training who takes up ITM at the same age, they should still have considerable advantage. What’s really important is that they *both* immerse themselves in the tradition with the right attitude.

Classical crossovers sometimes make the mistake of assuming that trad music is just another application of what they already know how to do. This is the fatal mistake. I have to laugh every time I hear a professional choir try to sing a spiritual or a folk song. Same idea. Ya gotta get down with the folks what knows how to do it and be a beginner.

↳ Posted by wormdiet on September 27th, 2005.

Re: do use all play just trad or classical to ?

I think technique is technique. Yeah, ITM and classical are two totally different beasts, but if you know how to play an instrument for one genre, you already have that background if you want to play that instrument in a different genre. If you can play a scale on a classical flute, then you can basically play a scale on an Irish flute. If you know that the key of G has 1 sharp in classical music, then you already know that the key of G has 1 sharp in ITM. Many of the basics don’t change.

I personally went the immersion route to transition from classical to ITM. I used to say that I wasted all those years playing classical music, but I’ve since come to realize that without classical, I would have progressed much slower in ITM. I think this is true for lots of people making the transition. You just really need to immerse yourself.

↳ Posted by dmarie on September 27th, 2005.

Re: do use all play just trad or classical to ?

I’m a classically trained violinist, but I think you have to let go of classical technique to be able to play trad stuff really well. Learning by ear helps with that, because I personally tend to get more relaxed when I’m not focusing so much on the sheet music. Improvising helps a lot, too (y’know, trills or little grace note runs, swinging 8ths and 16ths, etc.), as does listening to a lot of trad music and getting a feel for trad technique.

I’d say that a classical background CAN be a hindrance to playing trad stuff, but it can also help. Especially with technique and reading music, etc. Being classically trained also makes it easier to learn trad music on that same instrument.

I think you can play both (I do), but you need to have separate and distinct techniques or “feels” (for lack of a better word) to your music.

Hope that helps!

With regard as to whether or not to play classical: As far as technique goes, I think it’s different with each instrument. We could also consider other influences or musical beginnings in this thread. The link may be a little tenous to some I guess, but think about some other beginnings that can shape our approach to ITM. There aren’t that many of us out there but for most harmonica players (& I mean diatonic or blues harp) I’m guessing that the Blues is the place where they started. That applies to me… there are a lot of great blues licks or effects that, when used carefully and with some subtlety, sound fantastic in ITM. It may not be everyone’s cup of tea but all I have to say is listen to Brendan Power. Having said that -there are difficulties doing basic decorations in ITM on a blues harp… rolls are practically impossible for me. Harp players just have to invent new ways of giving the tune a little variation.

It’s not my native (??) music but for me the Blues is like a ‘folk’ music on its own and is a great place to go when I need a break (even just a little one) from ITM. One of the cool cross over things between the Blues and ITM is the importance of the intrinsic rhythm of the tune.

I don’t have any classical training, I didn’t want to go to music lessons as a kid - too busy on saturday mornings playing cricket. I wonder what would have happened had I gone with the music. There were no instruments available that appealed at the time (my fingers have always been too slow - it was only when I was given a blues harp by my brother some years later that I rediscovered my interest in playing music. And then it was with the Blues - in the last couple of years I’ve been playing so little Blues stuff and much more in ITM that my facility in the Blues has declined.

One last thing that is worth mentioning - had I learnt a bit of music as a kid I probably wouldn’t have half the difficulty playing from the dots that I do now. I can pick up stuff from ear, however if I’m learning from any written music it has to be translated into harp tab. A slow process.

↳ Posted by Bob himself on September 28th, 2005.

Re: do use all play just trad or classical to ?

I’ll weigh in on this one. I’m slightly amazed at how often it comes up. I have been playing classical piano full-time for the last fifteen years and I’ve yet to turn 21. I’m a music major and classical music is my foundation for just about everything that I do.

That said I put a lot of it aside when I’m playing folk music. Since I switched from violin to fiddle at the age of eleven I have used the fiddle as a release. I don’t study fiddle from a classical standpoint directly. But I can’t deny the influence of classical music on my playing. It’s funny though, being a fiddler and playing a lot with guitars helped immensely when I hit my collegiate Theory and Musicianship classes. Why? Because I could hear all of the chord progressions.

I’ll always be an advocate of classical training because I’ve had it. People who are fully against it typically didn’t have any classical training themselves.

I know some wonderful traditional musicians who cannot read a note of music and understand absolutely none of the concepts of classical music theory. They still play amazingly well, but when they teach large workshops they struggle because they cannot verbalize what they want and need to say. Like the gentleman who was teaching a waltz to a group of mountain dulcimer players. He gave them tab for it, but the tab was in 4/4. He never noticed the difference but it’s kind of hard to play a waltz in 4/4 timing.

All in all classical training is not neccersarily neccesary, but it sure is useful, at least, to me that is. It’s probably a case of being a personal choice. I agree with what was said above about needing to listen and immerse yourself in the music you are learning. It applies to everything from an Irish folk tune to a Mozart sonata.

dmarie, I stopped in on a vocal music hour and happened to hear one of the guys singing a southern gospel-esque arrangement of This Little Light of Mine. It wasn’t to the standard tune or anything, very soulful piece. And was sung by a white tenor. My only thought coming away was that he sang to white, he needed to sing it like he was black. So I understand the whole it isn’t racism thing, it’s a cultural thing.
