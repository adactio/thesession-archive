Added by showaddydadito on November 22nd, 2004

Too many backers?

Something that’s been on my mind recently, and is now crystallized by something the estimable Mr Gill says in the thread on bashing of chosen instruments.

Now I like a bit of backing from a guitar, or a bodhran, but I sometimes find that I get to a session and there is an eager collection of backers there - with no other melody instruments. Typically there might be 2 or 3 bodhrans, 2 or 3 guitars, a bouzouki, and someone who taps a foot on a table leg. And when I arrive they want a tune to join in with.

Does this ever happen to you?
If so:
Do you play a tune - only to be strummed and thumped out of hearing?
or do you sit and wait?
or do you voice your reservations about the imbalance?
or do you suggest that someone else plays a tune?
or do you deliberately play something very quietly on a mandolin?

Come, people - be not shy. Tell us of your experiences and feelings about melody instruments being overwhelmed by backers. And if it is a problem - give us your thoughts on how to overcome the problem.

↳ Posted by showaddydadito on November 22nd, 2004.

Re: Too many backers?

I don’t think the 4ths tuning of the guitar is all that relevant. It’s almost the same tuning as the lute and viol family, and they could move tunes along a fair old pace when required. There can be some advantages in guitar tuning with some tunes in that you can play the next string up instead of having to finger the note as you would on the fiddle/mandolin.tenor banjo. Bach wrote three fairly difficult sonatas for the viola da gamba (same size as the cello but with frets and 6 strings tuned in 4ths). These works are in fact a little more difficult on the cello than on the instrument they were written for.
I don’t think the fret spacing is a problem either - the guitar string length is quite close to that of the cello. I never noticed any problem when shifting between guitar and cello in my guitar-playing days.
There aren’t all that many melody instruments with a completely logical and easy system of fingering - harp and 1-string Chinese fiddle perhaps (one or two of the box instruments, but perhaps someone here can tell us about those - I’m not knowledgeable enough). The standard keyboard layout (piano, harpsichord, organ etc) is strange, to say the least - different fingering for pretty well every scale, and the left-hand fingering isn’t the same as the right-hand. And if you play the church organ you have to sort out your feet as well!
What it all boils down to is that you have to put in the work to learn the scales and arpeggios for your chosen instrument. There aren’t any short cuts - you have to do the time.
Trevor

We used to have this problem at the pub I frequent. I called it the Wall Of Rhythm, after the old “wall of sound” technique that Phil Spector used when mixing his albums. We sometimes had only one or two melody, backed by four accompanists. When this happened, I would put down my guitar, pick up my melody instrument of choice, and play melody on those tunes I knew, and sit quietly listening to those where I did not know the melody. The others did not seem to fully realize their overwhelming power as a team. Over time, however, more melody people started coming, and some of the backers dropped out, and it has become more balanced.
