Re: Session playlists

I think if there was a set list it would cease to be a session, spontaneity is all part of the game. However, most sessions have a core of tunes that they play fairly regularly. If you are an experienced session player thinking of visiting a different session (on holiday or whatever) it is worth contacting the leader (or the person who listed the session) - they will probably be able to suggest a few tunes that are likely to be played. If you are thinking of joining a local session for the first time it is probably best to go and just listen a few times - make a note of the tunes they play that you know, and someone will probably be happy to provide names for the tunes you don’t know and suggestions for others to learn.

Re: Session playlists

The closest thing I’ve seen is in traditional Swedish music where groups of people tend to play the same kind of repertoire, especially at get-togethers. A group may have their list of “allspelslåtar” (“tunes that everyone plays” - in case somebody would like to join the group) on a website, another group has their list and so on.

As many have said, if we can hold our own in a local session, we should be able to join other sessions. I can understand the original question, though, and have even been asked it when I’ve mentioned that “we had a great session last night”. “Oh, interesting, what tunes/songs do you normally play?”.

As for the shared core repertoire of say 50-100 tunes (look at Dow’s list, or any random “session tunes” book etc.), but everyone just assumes that everyone knows them so there’s no point of publishing that list. Beyond that imagined core, the session could take any direction.

Re: Session playlists

Agree with the comments above - it doesn’t quite feel like a session if there’s a strict playlist to work to. Part of the magic is gone.

However, there’s a a monthly pub session near me which has developed an interesting format which is worth mentioning. The session itself starts quite late, and has the traditional freeform session format. To support beginners, it is then preceded by an hour and a half of guided playing, for which experienced members produce setlists, supported through a dropbox folder, with a different setlist every month. Though there is no guarantee that these will ever come up in the session, many do, and there are quite a few beginners who have made their way through into the main session through this mechanism. There are very few repetitions of tunes or sets across the monthly lists, so it presents quite a significant learning challenge every month.

Thought I’d mention it as an example of an interesting organisational idea, though one that will only appeal to some.

Re: Session playlists

Kind of feels like a bad vibe here. Maybe it’s just me (it usually is) but I feel no need to criticize a session that offers a list of the most common tunes played by several of it’s usual members. I’ll use the terms set list and playlist interchangeably. That just seems like a friendly way of being inclusive and welcoming to visitors and new players. A set list isn’t a contract. No one should seek legal advice to play what they feel like. Wanna list of what we often play? Mail me and I’ll send you one. Just don’t expect all, or any, of the tunes to pop up on any given night. It’ll just a list of tunes that if you start, we’ll gladly play.

There some more annoying things that tend to happen with sessions. Some sessions are little more than performances by a band. Some turn into the “Fiddle and Flute Show” where one or two players insist on playing the most obscure tunes they know to the exclusion of everybody else. (new tunes are always welcome, that’s a different issue). Other sessions are ruined by the guy who wants to play an endless succession of Airs, or plays everything at warp speed, or sings one after another, or hijacks somebody else’s set. Another annoying thing is when the sets become inbred to the point that you just can’t play one tune without putting the same tune you ALWAYS play behind it. Sometimes a session just gets stale…he same tunes every time.

So, hey, a playlist is just a suggestion. It’s purpose is to give you an idea of what gets played some of the time so that you can join us and feel welcome. It should be taken and used as such. Here’s another idea. Bring you own list. There’s gotta be some good stuff on it!

Re: Session playlists

Personally…I go to sessions to play. To play in real time with real players. What I don’t enjoy is listening to, or playing an endless string if solos…even if they are tasty little tunes. That’s what CD’s are for. And in the real world I’ve found that every session group I’ve ever met tends to have favorite tunes that come up often. Just as often new tunes creep in, as they should. Equally often somebody looks up after a set and says“we haven’t played those for a while”. That’s just the way things go. It’s the way tunes get popular and get passed around. It’s part of the social structure of the event. That’s true for every genre I’ve ever followed. I’m getting better at picking up tunes on the fly and even better at learning them from recordings and videos ( I usually avoid the stylized performance versions). I may not be the best player in the bunch but I’m kinda proud of the fact that I’ve never learned a tune unless someone, somewhere, played it live in the same room. I’ll even learn tunes I don’t love just because some of my friends do. Point is that of the thousands of tunes available we can’t play ’em all, all the time. The relatively few we do know we keep alive by playing them together frequently

That’s what a session is, a gathering of old and new friends playing tunes, some familiar and some new, together. Anythings else, seems to me, is a performance, a competition, or worse, a game of “Mine is Better than Yours”. That said…I’d appreciate a heads-up with some of your favorite tunes and just as much, I’d like you to play me some new ones. Hopefully I’ll have one or two you’ve never heard.

Becoming a sessioneer is more of a journey than immediate gratification. It takes years to collect enough tunes you can play off the cuff in order to fully participate. Until then you have to just accept that you aren’t going to be playing every tune, a lot of tunes, or any tunes very soon.
