Re: Bouzouki question

I have a Trinity College and I love it to bits (not literally). Of course there are more prestigious and expensive makes, but for what I play it suits me fine. Trinity College also make two grades of bouzouki. I have the cheaper one. The more expensive one is to my ears quite brilliant, but as I said, I’m very content with the one I’ve got. it’s a lovely instrument by any standard. Just make sure that you buy one that’s professionally set up (Elderly instruments should be fine from what I’ve heard). You can find them much cheaper on places like e-bay and such, but they come as is, from the factory, and you don’t really save cent by the time you get them up to standard. Also, in getting it set up you may need to decide whether you prefer your G and D strings in unison or octave. Mine came in octave (because it was second hand), but I’ve read many comments saying that unison is better. I guess it depends on what you want to play. I haven’t bothered changing mine because I just love the sound as it is. TC also make a decent octave mandolin (I recently bought one for my friend) but the bouzouki is better. Anyhow, in short, I’d endorse them.

Re: Bouzouki question

There are, of course, several decisions to be made re purchasing a ‘zouk; factory v luthier; flat-top v arch top; octave v unison; floating bridge v pin-bridge; ’zouk v octave mando-scale; etc. Not to mention budget.
Trinity represents an economical compromise, remembering also that there are several other brands that are exactly the same; mine says Ozark on the head, and Made in Korea on the back of the head, but it’s about 9 years old now, and in need of a refret. Johnson and Vintage are also from the same mold - sometimes the head design is different, but you can see the actual body-shape is identical.
The Eastern European-made ’zouks are probably best avoided, unless you’re really short of disposable income.
When I hit the big-time I’m going for a Davy Stuart 5-course long-scale myself…….

Re: Bouzouki question

Trinity College instruments are reasonable priced, have an acceptable tone, and reasonably playable if properly set up. Mine is at least twenty years old and has aged surprisingly well. I give Elderly five stars for value added. They will ship you an instrument on approval and allow you to return it, no questions asked, if you don’t like it.

If you plan to pick fast melody lines, as opposed to strumming chords, keep in mind a bouzouki has a long scale length and is tuned in fifths, as opposed to fourths like a guitar. Lead lines demand a lot more stretching of the fingers.

Re: Bouzouki question

Nonononononononononooooo Gobbo !
NEVER tune the top string down to D if you want to play tunes - that way you’ll have to go up to the 9th fret instead of the 7th for a top B ! I thought GDAD tuners were strictly chord-players - OK that includes Pete Coe, who’s pretty good, but I don’t reckon it’s a good idea for tune-players.
As to the difference -it’s scale length; a bouzouki is about 660mm, an octave mando, by comparison, is about 100mm shorter - this is only about 3 frets difference, but it requires a different heavier stringing, even if you tune them the same. Also most ’zouks have the octave stringing on the bottom two pairs of strings, which I like myself.
Most octave mando players tend to be tune-players, many bouzouki players tune to GDAD and chord, a bit like DADGAD guitarists. I don’t think the original Irish ’zouk players chorded much, they were much more interested in playing tunes and bits of single-string accompaniment, but things have developed since then.
