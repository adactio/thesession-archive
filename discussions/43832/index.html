The Session From Heaven

Well, the great thing about sessions is that (in many cases) anyone can join in.

The downside is that in such a scenario, the resulting experience can be far from satisfying, owing to factors like extremes of playing ability and experience, plus external annoyances like crowd noise, over-exuberant pub punters and the like.

In contrast to the “Session from Hell”, I thought I’d post my idea of the “session from heaven”, and I’ve experienced a few of them. The best one I can remember was in McGann’s in Doolin, on a weekday evening and not in the tourist season.

I myself was a tourist there, and I had brought my fiddle along to hopefully join in a session. The music was playing when I walked in, around 9pm, so I got a pint and sat over near the musicians.

There was fiddle, flute, mandola, banjo and bodhran. The sound was just amazing - everyone in perfect tune and time, and the overall sound could be described as being laid-back, tempo relaxed, and no one instrument could really be heard above the others.

I listened intently through about 3 or 4 sets, then at a break, one of the guys spoke to me, and asked me if I wanted to join them. I did, and took my fiddle out, but I didn’t actually play. I just sat and listened.

Everything was just perfect - everyone blending in with each other, mellow-toned, banjo picked well away from the bridge, etc - just spot-on! I could really add anything to it. Oh, and I eventually heard the bodhran - just a pulse all the way through, played with a lot of damping and modulation, unlike the “thump” I was used to hearing in other sessions.

I did get invited to play a few tunes of my own, so I played a few of my own comps solo, and they seemed to go down well.

I’ve been to a few sessions like that one, but only a few. They are few and far between, for me anyway.

We record our session every week. Have done for at least two years. We do it as a means to learn each other’s sets, but the recorder is amazingly good for being so unobtrusive. It’s even in stereo. Of course, not every set is a gem worthy of repeated listening for pleasure, but I have put together a compilation of about two hours of music that really captures the atmosphere and sound of a session from heaven. Good playing, good instrumentation and good tunes. I know having this testament to what Irish session playing is all about will be important to have later. It won’t be as it is now forever and I’ll want to revel in the memory of these dear times with dear friends for as long as I draw breath, whether or not it can still fill my flute.

Re: The Session From Heaven

There are a couple of sessions that stick in my mind. One was in at the Willie Clancy Week in 2000, in the kitchen of Tom Malone’s pub (as it was then - a much smaller pub than the current one and on the opposite side of the road). Pipes/whistle players Dickie Deegan and Peter Phelan were present, trading tunes. It was well on into the week (possibly the second weekend) and I had got to know a number of the musicians there. I was sleep-deprived, under-nourished, propped up by coffee - all of which might have contributed to the somewhat altered state I found myself in. It was my first experience of getting completely lost in the music, not quite sure if I was asleep or awake, fingers seemingly playing the tunes (even ones I didn’t recall ever hearing before) by themselves without any conscious input from me. I still hold this session as a kind of benchmark for musical ecstasy.

The other one was a very different kind of session. I was cycling through Trim, Co. Meath, en route from Dublin to Drumshanbo. It was still early afternoon and I had only covered about 2/3 of the distance I had planned to that day. But Trim was a beautiful place, steeped in history - and with a hostel located in an old stone house. So I decided to stop there and after checking in and exploring the town, I enquired as to whether there was any music that night. I was directed to Brogan’s pub for that night’s session (there were seemingly sessions on several nights of the week). I turned up to find a small group of musicians (perhaps 4 or 5) playing wonderfully warm, relaxed, sympathetic music. I was welcomed into the circle without hesitation and was able to fit in perfectly. I got talking to some of the other players between tunes and was even offered a place to stay on future visits (which I never followed up…). This session exemplifies for me how this music (perhaps music in general, but some kinds more than others) can carry with it a spirit of generosity and kindness and is, in a very real sense, a medium of communication for things that are beyond words.

Re: The Session From Heaven

I travelled to Ireland, for the first time, to play @ a music festival. It was in a Gaelic speaking area and I sat down with everyone to play. I didn’t know how my tunes and rhythm etc would go down.

It was about 3 in the morning and the sun was about to rise. I cracked out with 3 of my favourite irish jigs and the sesh was alive. Variations, that let us enjoy the soul of the tune over and over, were a must as we just delighted in the melodies - repeating them until we all knew the next had to burst through.

I realised the oneness of the Scottish-Irish tradition, language and culture. When a song broke out - its ‘sacred’ nature was respected with reverend silence and when the tunes started again we didn’t hold back. We were Gaels, not whites or blacks, native or foreign, working class nor ‘Westerners’.

It was lovely to see a place where the beauty of the language was preserved in and for itself - not in reaction to its historic oppression.

I had to leave for the plane about 5am - but the link created has remained in my heart and mind to this day.
