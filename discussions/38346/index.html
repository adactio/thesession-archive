ok…the question is phrased funny, its like a riddle. “if I’m doing 4 clicks one for every single notes played rather than 2 per bar how many bpms is that then?”

the literal answer is to look at your metronome and read what speed you have it set. the metronome is beating at whatever rate (beats/min) you set it at

now if you are saying that you have set your metronome to click on every “single” note and you get 4 clicks per bar, then you have the very definition of 4/4 time

but, as we have discussed to great lengths on other recent threads, reels are played in cut time (2/2)

so if somebody says “I play reels at 114 bpm” and Arthur wants to convert his metronome setting the correct answer is………

divide your metronome’s displayed bpm by 2

Re: Question about bpms…

It might help to think of it not as how many notes per click but which notes are on the click.

Play the tune and pay attention to whether those notes are on the click.

You could also practice with hornpipes first. They ‘work’ played slowly and the swing may make it easier to hear which notes should be in time with the clicks. Try starting at 60bpm for two click to a bar, then when comfortable with that switch to 120bpm but play at the same tempo (so 4 clicks to the bar). Then work towards a more normal playing tempo. Then try reels.

I went to a workshop by a well known Irish musician who advised us to ‘learn a reel as a hornpipe to start with’. The reason given was that if one can play them slowly with a strong swing the swing tends not to get ironed out so much when played at a fast reel tempo.

@Arthur: You deserve a Scouts’ badge for the knots you’re tying yourself in here.

It seems to me that the easiest way to clarify things for you would be to refer to one of your own recordings. So, The reel in this thread https://thesession.org/discussions/37718 is played at around 60bpm (counting two beats per bar). So, that is considerably slower than you are likely to hear a reel played in a session (~90+) - but it is no bad thing to play at that sort of tempo or slower whilst you are learning. Even in a session, it is better to play slow and well than fast and badly - good players will be able to make a tune ‘dance’ even at slow tempos.

I usually tap my foot when I play, and the way I would ‘naturally’ do that would be two taps per bar, so that would seem the obvious way to set the metronome. That is not to say that there is not some use for setting the metronome in double time (4 clicks per bar) or even, as millionyears_bc suggests, half time (one click ber bar): you might want to use 4 clicks per bar to check that all your crotchets are consistent within the bar, and you might want to use one click per bar to check whether you have lost or gained time when you reach the end of each bar. Setting the metronome to click on every quaver (8 clicks per bar) would not be of any real use, since the quavers are rarely played evenly in reels and jigs (besides, most metronomes don’t go that fast anyway).

>>“How am I supposed to know what bpm Im playing at without a metronome. If I were asked to start a tune in a session I would have no idea how to play ‘at 90bpm’ ”.

This is where your foot tapping comes back into the equation.
The speed your foot taps at naturally is governed by how high you raise it. To illustrate how it works try slapping your thigh with your hand - start just raising your hand 1/2", you’ll be clapping quite fast, now start raising your hand higher. As you do the tempo will naturally slow down.

The same thing happens with your feet - the higher you raise your toes, the slower the beat will be. You will quite quickly get to know how high you need to go to give the desired tempo. If necessary you can slow down more by tapping your heel instead of your toes.

Tap a couple of bars to set the pace before you start playing, even if you don’t tap right through.
