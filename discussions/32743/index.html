↳ Posted by Danjo on November 7th, 2013.

Re: How has Globalization effected the Irish music scene (1970s-ish to present)

I think the most compelling example of globalization in ITM is the keyless wooden flute. Prior to the 1970s, there were essentially no wooden flutes being made specifically to play Irish trad, keyed or keyless. One either used a 19th-century flute or made do with a Boehm flute. At present there is a surprisingly large number of flute makers who not only do enough business to make a living, but have waiting lists that can be years long. Without the global market and the internet, this situation could never exist.

Re: How has Globalization effected the Irish music scene (1970s-ish to present)

“ I am of the opinion that ”globalization“ began as soon as one group of people traded with another group of people. ”

In which case, that would mean that globalization started when someone discovered that they could walk to the neighbouring village and trade a mammoth skin for the bones of a sabertooth tiger. According to Wikipedia (yes, I know…), some German historian has more or less made that argument (well, kind of) saying that globalization started when the ancient civilizations of Sumer and the Indus Valley started trading with one another. There appears to be more agreement amongst historians that it can conceivably be understood as starting in the 16th century, and definitely the 19th.

I don’t think the opposing argument can even be made -- that Irish music developed in a vacuum and has never had any outside influences. Of course it did. A lot of the common tune types and instruments came from elsewhere (fiddles were what? Italian in origin? Same with accordions. And the uilleann pipes as we know them are a late 18th century invention and the earliest origins of bagpipes in general have been traced to the Middle East). It therefore seems to me that the movement of people and ideas has always had an impact on the music. Is that the paper you want to write? If you are more interested in how technological innovations, i.e., recording technology, the internet, whatever, and subsequent ease of transmitting information has affected Irish music, perhaps narrow your question to that, rather than using fuzzy and debatable terms (academics argue over what it means) like globalization.
