Re: Ruaridh MacMillan

While I may be generalising slightly (one has to when discussing style, style itself is a generalisation of how a collection of individuals play) I don’t think I’m generalising “pretty heavily”. But you accept the truth of it, generally speaking anyway, even if you made the lame excuse that trying to imitate the highland pipes is difficult.

But I wasn’t really talking about imitating, though there is a certain amount of imitation. I was saying about taking on some more of the subtleties of the pipes.

Maybe what you say about the fiddle not being anywhere near as popular as the pipes has something to do with it. If there are really not that many West Highland fiddlers, then the “style” itself is, by definition, based more on the individual styles of less individuals. As the sample rate decreases, the relevance of the individual increases. And there is therefore less input into the creation of and the sustaining of that style.

And I just don’t find that style of fiddle playing to be particularly interesting or inventive. However, of the West Highland fiddlers I’ve known (there’s one in particular who we were talking about earlier, but I’ve seen it happen with a few players) there own styles of fiddle playing have developed immeasurably since being influenced by other styles of fiddle playing and music making in general.

Of course, you could complain that that is diluting a somehow purer and distinct Scottish regional style. And that would be correct. But if this matters to you and you would prefer to still be able to hear that distinct regional style, then the best thing it could do to stop it being pickled in the museum of folk culture would be to look at home for inspiration, and surely the fabulous wealth and subtlety of the indigenous - and more popular - piping tradition would be the place to start.
