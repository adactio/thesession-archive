Multidimensional or a Monoculture?

Aye, I’m still thinking about sessions, & now I’m wondering what your session is like?

I have three in my week & two of those tend to be 90% ITM, & wall to wall tunes.

However the other one is very different.
For example, last night we had:

Northumbrian Pipes - & lots of lovely tunes from that tradition.

Scottish Smallpipes - & loads of hard core Scottish M/S/R sets.

2 Fifes - & a whole bunch of wee marching tunes from the Fife & Lambeg tradition

a Singer - so Irish Ballads & Folk Songs (which always keep the audience entertained!)

a Silver Flute - with a selection of Airs

Traditional Scottish Singer - so we enjoyed some lovely unnacompanied Scottish Trad & Scots Gaelic songs

Plus the regular Fiddle, Harp, Mandolin, Guitar & Bodhran helping to nit it all together.

Inevitably it was a very warm, friendly, relaxed session & I must say, most enjoyable.

However, it has to be said too that I do also love those head down & four solid hours of ITM tunes ……… as long as we get a healthy mix of different rhythms & not the dreaded brain numbing wall to wall REELS ONLY insanity.

So, tell me, what mix of music do you enjoy at your session?

Is your session multidimensional or a monoculture?

Re: Multidimensional or a Monoculture?

This notion of a universal Traditional music mush makes me think of what has already happened in Ireland over the last 50 years where much of the strong regional variations have been lost to us, or at least diminished, due no doubt to ease of travel, CDs, recording contraptions etc etc & of course the handiwork of the mighty Comhaltas machine.

So I then imagine that process complete, with everyone playing all the instruments with no regionality of style & all playing with immaculate technique, then I magnify that idea on a global scale …………………………… & I don’t like what I see.

Call me crazy but I like, for example, the old, simple Concertina style of Clare & I do not think we all have to move with the times & become super technicians & I also like a plain style of Fiddling which uses long simple notes & doesn’t involve every single note having to be ornamented, so no I don’t buy into this great plan to train up a master race of ITM automatons.

So no, I don’t see anything wrong with an East Clare Fiddler cultivating his own areas style & not wishing to speed up & play like a Donegal man, or a Kerry man or a platitudinous Comhaltas style.

By the same token I see nothing wrong in enjoying Northumbrian Music or Scottish Music or Breton Music & nothing wrong in wishing not to hear it all, one day, sounding the same.

Let those guys ’n gals try their “glorious fusions”, by all means, good luck to them, but let’s not loose the glorious diversity which the various traditional musics of the World offer us.

Re: Multidimensional or a Monoculture?

The number of different breweries in the UK owes a huge amount to The Campaign For Real Ale in and after the Seventies. Back then, the overwhelming preponderence of beer came from the “Big Six” breweries, which cynically produced junk beer for pubgoers who had no other choice - and some local breweries were just as bad. CAMRA started by promoting the beer of the breweries that were still producing decent stuff; and as time went on, more and more breweries came into existence as the big ones reeled with the blows and began to sell real ale in their pubs. CAMRA has been said to be the most successful consumer pressure group ever (though I wouldn’t know how they work these things out…).
Mind, the nature of unpasteurised / unchilled real ale does leave it open to the chance of going flat / sharp / whatever, and one’s pint of Rodent’s Winter Widdle may turn out to be deficient: but go back to the Good Old Days of Watney’s Red Barrel, et cetera - no way!

Porter was invented by accident in London, I gather: a brewery worker left the malt (I presume) too long in the oven one day, and returned to find it had gone black. They brewed it up nonetheless. The brewery had a lot of custom among the porters of a nearby market (Billingsgate? Covent Garden?): these took an immediate liking to the new black beer, thus giving it its name.

Re: Multidimensional or a Monoculture?

ITM should certainly be seperated to an extent from commercialism, so that it is not driven up a cul-de-sac by commercial forces alone. The other false extreme, the stylistic, artistic forces could be equally resrictive though if we make the mistake of ‘forcing’ playing styles to conform to some cartoon of ‘regional styles’ that were only ever loose-fitting running threads of *some* localised similarities.

The greater part of any performer of course is not the simple nuts and bolts of regionality or any other quantifiable techniques but how he or she melds them into a coherent, unique personal playing style.

‘Sligo fiddle style’: James Morrison or Michael Coleman? They were just down the road from each other and they played VERY differently from each other. If you look at these things in any detail the beloved (and misunderstood) ‘hard and fast’ classifications and definitions start to break down.

Thankfully we have Arts Councils in Ireland that are sponsoring events/ performers/ researchers along artistic lines so that they don’t necesarily have to be commercially viable at the expense of stylistic/ artistic considerations.
