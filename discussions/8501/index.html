Are House Concerts - the future?

Just had an interesting E-Mail from a couple of guys from Boulder, Colorado.

Apparently they perform, what they call ‘House Concerts’ & are hoping to bring this concept over to Ireland next May, 2006!

For some strange reason, the words Newcastle & Coal immediately spring to my mind!

Anyway, they specialize in Original Progressive American Folk Music, Traditional Celtic Guitar, Voice and Harmonica, are trying to get bookings in Ireland for their ‘House Concert’ tour & have been ‘pioneering’ the House Concert Circuit in Europe since January 2003.

Now, you may ask, what are house concerts?

Well, apparently:
“house concerts are a wonderful grassroots phenomenon where world-class musicians and developing local talent alike perform in the comfortable intimacy of private homes and similar nontraditional spaces.”

"They’re fun to attend, fun to present and fun to play. You can
have perfect sound, a great party, and world-class music right in your own home or the home of a neighbour."

“There’s no experience quite like it for the listener or presenter.”

Now these guys currently book their own house concerts across the USA, Nova Scotia, Ireland, England, Holland and Belgium.

If you want to learn more about House Concerts, check this out: http://www.houseconcerts.org/

Or you can check out the music of ‘Jon & Rosh’ at: http://jracoustics.com/music.html

Anyone wish to comment?

Re: Are House Concerts - the future?

This concept has been around for a long time. I remember going to one many years ago in Ireland - it was a fundraiser. The fashion for house concerts has been up and down for probably centuries.
More recently about 8 years ago I was re-introduced to the concept here in Perth (Oz). The nice thing about the house concert is that there are usually no overheads and all the door money can go to a cause or to a visiting artist. There is no need for great acoustics or a PA system. It is a more sociable evening than a concert hall event (generally).
They provide a niche for a touring artist who is not up there in profile to the point they can fill a 200seat hall with all it’s attendant costs. The ultimate is when you get someone who is tomorrow’s big name, to enjoy in a more intimate surrounding.
Irish Traditional Music seems to work much better in this concept than in the Hall type concert (IMHO).

Re: Are House Concerts - the future?

Hey Dick,

Not a new idea for sure. I remember attending, and performing at, bluegrass concerts in private homes in the 1970’s. Concerts such as these filled in the schedules of traveling musicians as well as offered a more intimate setting for those local. This intimacy was always an attraction for me as either a performer or listener. Back then the number of venues booking bluegrass acts was extremely limited and this music was at its peek, at least in my short existence anyway.

It seems that around here most musical performance venues, especially the pubs, are looking to book more of the Irish rock and Pub song acts rather than the traditional music. For the Pubs these types of acts draw more folks looking to drink massive amounts of the black stuff and/or what ever brew in vogue that day. Fair play to the publicans as they and their employees need a roof, to eat, and to pay bills just like the rest of us. That said there are still many venues in NYC and the surrounding area that will book traditional music but these performing opportunities are very limited compared to the other entertainment choices. House concerts offer yet another opportunity for the musicians.

I think house concerts will always be with us thankfully and their occurrence may even increase in frequency. After all they offer a musician the opportunity to make a little money, play tunes, and enjoy a good bit of craic and they offer the attendees an intimate opportunity to enjoy the music, meet and learn a little about the musician, and perhaps even share a tune or two at the end. Sounds like one of those elusive win, win situations to me.

I’ve approached the little venue I run here in Bayonne this way. The performances are held in the basement of the oldest church (God’s house) in the city. We can seat up to seventy or eighty people in folding chairs. The musicians split the take at the door with the church. It’s not fancy but the craic is great and the church raises a little money towards the restoration of the building. The ladies bake and offer other snacks and soft drinks.

Peace,
Ed

Re: Are House Concerts - the future?

We’ve hosted house concerts in our home for about 4 years. Acoustic music of any kind works well and simplifies the need for extraneous gear. It’s great for the musicians cause they get the entire door take and can count on a friendly and warm room of patrons with no hostility. Our friends and neighbors get to see new (or sometimes world class) music right down the road without traveling long distances and we all meet new music lovers and artists. Everyone brings food and drink to share. As hosts, we get a great show right in the comfort of our home. I don’t know if they are the future -sometimes a loud crashing concert in a big venue is the way to go - but they have an established history in the US and it’s a really nice option for lots of artists and spectators.

Re: Are House Concerts - the future?

Me and the wife convinced our church to host concerts as part of a coffeehouse series. Church likes it because it increases their visibility in the community, brings newcomers in the doors, and provides family-friendly entertainment. We like it because all the overhead is covered by the church, the ticket proceeds can go almost entirely to the artists, the church earns a little from snack sales, and prices stay low so more people can attend. In fact, we often let kids attend free, and sometimes the local elder care facility and half-way houses run vans. It is music as a community, non-profit, social event.

And listen to what Gary Martin says about house concerts--I attended my first at his house this summer, and he knows how to do it right!

I may be tempting fate, but I haven’t worried about insurance. Homeowner’s insurance here should cover liability for injury or damage, I think, though they could argue that a concert constitutes “business use” of the premises. On the other hand, I could argue that it’s not a business, since I don’t take any money from the proceeds. Also, I call the admission money a “donation” and don’t issue tickets. But you’d have to read your insurance policy or ask your agent to see what your situation is.

My understanding is that as long as you consider any money taken in as “donations” and it’s all handed over to the musicians -- it doesn’t fall under what would be considered a “business”and you wouldn’t need insurance beyond what you normally have to cover yourself regarding visitors or parties.
