Irish Dance Schools make me sick

I am a local session player of intermedisate skill level in the Boston area. I have cousins in Ireland, go there to visit, love the trad music and my pride runs deep. So when I took my 6 year old Shannon to get invloved in the Irish dance schools I was shocked. They are discusting.
All about competition. My wife was thrown out for peaking in the door??? WTF? My daughter was ridiculed and yelled at while trying to dance in tears. And the other schools are all about bloated “Fesh life”. Over priced dresses and wigs. A musician partner of mine talks of having shoes thrown at him by dance instructors when he was young. And they never dance to live music. I would love to start a program for dance and musicians to get together. No competitions, live music. Where did Irish dance go wrong?

Re: Irish Dance Schools make me sick

BTW, it has nothing to do with some “American ID” being different from some “Irish ID” kind of thing. As currently run by An Coimisiun, feisianna and standards for Irish dancing are pretty much the same throughout the world. It’s easy to forget that this Southern, Munster style of dancing is not the only kind of Irish dancing out there, but it is NOT American only by any stretch of the imagination; in fact many of the trends (including the trends in dresses and looks of wigs and such) do not originate in the US by any means.

I can personally only speak with any kind of authority about the dresses, of course. Dress trends are once again being set in Ireland -- at one point a few years back, it was New Zealand and Australia that set those trends. We’ve gone from barely embroidered dresses of the 40’s through the 60’s, to what one poster here once called “the jumping Book of Kells” to the all glitterball and silk dresses full of applique more Celtic than Irish.

I won’t make dresses that I feel don’t meet at least my own standards for good taste and competitiveness, though, but that does limit my market. And if you think the dresses are expensive, I think a canvas of many of the small dressmakers showed that most make somewhere between $0.10 to $10.00 an hour on the dressse.

The dresses will simplify again, I’m sure, simply because that’s the sort of cycle that trends go through. Talk a few of the World’s champions into wearing plain dresses, that’d do it right there. :)

Although, being from a place like Colorado, where there’s sun and temperate weather most of the year, long may it be before heavy velvet dresses come back in! Can’t count how many instances of heat stroke I’ve come close to! :)

Re: Irish Dance Schools make me sick

I am an Irish dance instructor, certified under An Comisiun, and I think you really have to do your homework before you send your child to an Irish dance school. I am sorry your poor dd had such an awful experience. Our school never yells or belittles a student. We only use positive reinforcement like stickers. Schools are very varied in their focus. Some do not compete at all (this kind of school sounds like it would appeal to you), and some are all about competition. Ask around for a school that does not compete at all. They are in every city.

I love the Irish Culture, and the music, but I also enjoy the sport of irish dancing. I don’t push my students to compete, but I had wonderful expereinces meeting Irish from all over the world when I competed, so I enjoy that aspect of dancing. I don’t think that makes it less Irish. Irish dancing has evolved for sure, as has many things in this world

We have had live music in our classes from time to time, but the school has to pay for the musicians, and that can get pricy. No one has volunteered to do it for free .

Most of our dancers wear wigs. We have offered hair curling classes, but its the parents who prefer the ease of the wigs.

We allow our parents to observe class once a month. I think its very distracting for the teacher and student to allow parents to observe otherwise. We get the least out of our kids the days the parents observe. The parents and kids feed off each other, and there are more distractions on those days. We only offer observation days because we want the parents to feel comfortable with us, but it is a bit counterproductive in my opinion.

And the Irish dancing world in Ireland is flashier than in the US. It is certainly not an American fabricated intertrepation of Irish dancing. Ireland sets the standards there.
