Added by Someone at The Session on April 21st, 2007

Instrument pecking order….

….or maybe what you think are the top ten, or eleven or whatever, number one being the most traditionaland/or essential for a session, and number 10 the least but still encountered in sessions.

It’s been getting a bit too civilised and tame and people being too nice to each other on here the last week or so, so I thought I’d get my tin opener (size extra large) out and open up this can of worms for my tea. Mmmm, juicy!

So here goes.

Pride of place at the number one spot should go of course to the Uillean Pipes, so despite the fact that tame pipers are a rarity and the pipes occasionally don’t suit a session environment (MY opinion!) I’ll go along with this and keep them at the top. Because they are a truly indigenous Irish instrument mainly, so they get the respect vote.

Number 2 - I’ll let fiddle have that, slightly grudgingly, since I kind of feel no sesh worth its salt is complete without at least one.
3 - Simple system flute, played half-decently in-tune and with sufficient volume.
4 - Button Accordion, preferably a dry tuned Castagnari.
5 - Banjo - 4-string of course.
6 - Whistle - half decently played of course.
7- Mandolin, mandola, bouzouki - lumped together (sorry) otherwise I’d be here forever - and you don’t want that do you?
8 - Bodhrán - also an indigenous “instrument” so it gets on the list. Not any further up because it isn’t indispensible in a session.
9 - Concertina - I’m told the Anglo is “meant” to be what Irish players play as opposed to the English, but I’ll let Jack and Dow argue the toss over that.
10 - Keyed flute - well, I had to get it in the top ten somewhere, didn’t I? Once again, can be disastrous in the wrong hands.

A few more?

11 - Piano accordian - shame to have to put this down here, but I’ve met only a handful of people (some of whom are members here) who can do this justice - and unfortunately many more who haven’t a fvckin clue.

12 - Harp. Should really be top of the whole list, due being ancient and cruit (sp?) being indigenous, but although exquisitely beautiful as a solo instrument, I personally haven’t heard more than a couple at sessions, and I didn’t feel they added much to the fabric of the sound. Sorry harpists.

13 - Harmonica - same reasons as above, and also very few people I’ve heard can get up to session speed. But I’ve only ever heard a couple of players at sessions anyway.

14 - Bones - which sound a lot better than….

15 - Spoons - which CAN sound better than ……

16 - Guitars - provocatively low on the list. Apologies to the good, sensitive, guitar accompanyists out there, but you have been grossly outnumbered by eegits and pop-idol rejects by so much that your voice is not heard in the melee. But that’s not all. It’s the ATITUDE of a fair few of these muppets, thinking that they are the very core, the key to the session, when they are at best superfulous, but usually excess baggage and extra noise.

17 - rainsticks, shaky eggs, coins on the table, people who sit in with the session personnel and do things like tell jokes between tunes, thinking they are an essential part of the evening’s entertainment (actually, in rural Ireland these guys probably ARE a fixture of the evening, so I’l let them off, there) various other hangers on, and so on.

List not to be taken too seriously. Feathers should not be ruffled. Please amend to your own specification.

Re: Instrument pecking order….

In a St Paddy’s session in Cork a few weeks ago I saw a clarinet being played. It didn’t detract from the session and wasn’t overly obtrusive. As far as I could make out, he was playing in the lower register, doing largely what llig on his viola would be doing down there. I’d put the clarinet in at number 11 or 12.

A viola (which isn’t common in sessions) I’d put alongside the fiddle at number 2, on the grounds that anything the fiddle can do a good violist can do it as well, and the viola can add a useful inner or lower part to enhance the overall sound (as llig is at pains to tell us now and then).

Very occasionally in a session I’ve come across a really good recorder player (now there’s a really traditional baroque instrument for you), and played well there’s no reason why it couldn’t make a decent contribution. Like whistles, recorders can have a variety of tonal qualities, depending on the make and how they are played. I’d sit the recorder alongside the whistle at number 6.

Re: Instrument pecking order….

You can’t separate fidde, flute and pipes in the pecking order, so they’re up there as joint No1, with whistle in at No 4. Everything else is optional, and sometimes fantastic.

For instance, I *love* good box playing, whether it be concertina, BA or PA. Banjo is great in the right hands. Even guitars and bodhrans can add something, provided they’re not too loud and not ‘thrashed’.

Other instruments are generally ‘iffy’ in one way or another. For instance, mandolin is almost always dreadful, for some reason. Zouk quite often is, but just about my best friend plays zouk, so they’ve got to be allowed (and he’s OK anyway - thanks for the ‘arboretum’! :-) ).

And, ‘iffy’ in another way, harps have never really been traditional in the tradition that we here on this site are part of - the tradition of the old Irish harpers was a separate tradition that, sadly, died, without even being fully notated, so we don’t even have the full dots to tell us what it sounded like. So they have some catching up to do. Would be great to hear them more though, wouldn’t it? They sound so pretty …

Other, less usual, instruments? Someone came along to a session in Cheltenham a couple of years or so ago playing sax. He was a great player, but my God, WHAT an abominable sound it was mixed in with ITM! (I suppose someone will cite the famous examples of this, but I don’t care - it was still dreadful.)
