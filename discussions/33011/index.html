The Ideal Pub Session…?

Those of you with experience of different sessions over the years, what’s your idea of the ideal pub session?

How many people ideally?
What sort of mix of instruments?
Would there be learners/improvers/newcomers, or do you prefer an established cast of solid players?
Songs? Percussion? Or not?
Private room, or more public bar?
Interval? Food available? Food break?
All through the year? Just winter? What time of the day? What day of the week?
Structured way of playing a repertoire (list or asking each person to lead); or just whatever tunes pop into members’ minds?
Have I missed out some other important consideration?

This is just an interested inquiry, ‘for fun’, really - no harm meant - but as an older woman, a learner, and (basically) an outsider, I am hoping to gain some insights too. It would be fascinating to hear of anyone’s experience, when a session has ‘changed’ in some way and either died out or become popular as a result.
Cheers.
Mollie

Re: The Ideal Pub Session…?

“How many people ideally?”

I think 12-15 is a good number. This ensures that the session keeps going even when some of the tunes played are a little obscure - at least you’ll probably get a few musicians joining in.

“What sort of mix of instruments?”

The usual mix of ITM instruments (or STM, ETM etc.) as the case may be.

“Would there be learners/improvers/newcomers, or do you prefer an established cast of solid players?”

A core of solid players is essential. But its also good to have learners /improvers / newcomers along - providing that they don’t screw things up for the experienced players.

“Songs? Percussion? Or not?”

Personally, I’m OK with a few songs. Also OK with percussion - providing that it’s in keeping and not too loud.

“Private room, or more public bar?”

Public bar. Good punters can often add atmosphere and make for a good session.

“Interval? Food available? Food break?”

It’s sometimes good to have an interval - maybe to catch up on the news, or perhaps to discuss what tunes might be learned before the next session. Free food is good - free beer for musicians is even better! :-)

“All through the year? Just winter? What time of the day? What day of the week?”

Am evening session the held on same day every week works best. If you hold a session (say) once a fortnight some folks won’t turn up in case it’s the wrong week.

“Structured way of playing a repertoire (list or asking each person to lead); or just whatever tunes pop into members’ minds?”

Organic sessions - the ones that evolve naturally - are usually the best.

“Have I missed out some other important consideration?”

Yes. You need a good session “leader” A strong melody player with a good repertoire who can strike up with a good set if the session begins to fade for any reason. Other tasks probably include liaising with the landlord, maintaining a session email list, welcoming / encouraging newcomers - but nonetheless dealing with them firmily (but tactfully) if they screw up the session! A rare animal who can do all that, though!

Re: The Ideal Pub Session…?

What follows is entirely my set of preferences:

How many people ideally? - I personally prefer smaller sessions, 3-4 minimum, but no more than 8-9.

What sort of mix of instruments? - The usual. Flute, whistle, fiddle, box, pipes, maybe a banjo.

Would there be learners/improvers/newcomers, or do you prefer an established cast of solid players? - One or two learners/improvers (I don’t know what distinction you mean between these two categories) and one or two newcomers are always welcome as they breathe some fresh air into the session - different sets, etc. But certainly a group of regulars who know each others’ sets is an essential central core.

Songs? Percussion? Or not? - The odd song provides a nice break. One song per half dozen sets of tunes is about right. No spoons, eggs, coins or bottles banging together. A bodhrán, maybe 2 max, but thumped by someone who knows the actual tunes. Bones are nice on occasion, played in moderation.

Private room, or more public bar? - Public bar, but a small one with just a few people there, actually listening.

Interval? Food available? Food break? - No need.

All through the year? Just winter? What time of the day? What day of the week? - All through the year. Time? The usual time, 9pm start. Tuesdays and Thursdays.

Structured way of playing a repertoire (list or asking each person to lead); or just whatever tunes pop into members’ minds? - Whoever feels able to lead should just lead a set, but give plenty of opportunities for others to lead. Formulaic sets week in week out get rather onerous, so changing sets is preferable.

Have I missed out some other important consideration? - You have to enjoy the company of the other personnel, or at least be able to endure them. Otherwise don’t bother attending. It will only end in tears.

Re: The Ideal Pub Session…?

Four or five are enough; a nice mix of instruments that isn’t possible with fewer players. Double figures is definitely too many; big sessions when driven by a tight core can be fun, but never have what it’s really about - being able to hear and respond to everything in the session. That also rules out percussion and guitars - they just get in the way of hearing the tunes. Maybe a very subtle guitar or bouzouki, but I prefer none.

Learners? Sure, but learn by listening, only play what you know. Improvers? If you’re not improving you should quit. Seriously. People who never learn a new tune, never play an old one differently are dead weight in a session. Newcomers? Definitely, always good to have fresh tunes and conversation, but if you’re all strangers it won’t be so good.

A few good songs are nice, but not essential. A public bar but quiet enough (that often seems the hardest part). The only interval you need is conversation between tunes - but you do need that, incessant tune-pouncing is a pet hate of mine. This connects with structure; the tunes should flow in the same way conversation does, nothing so formal as turns, some have more to say than others on a given night but everyone should be included.

Usually a mid-week evening as that’s most practical. A good pint. A free pint or three should really be part of it, but more important is a pub that understands what you need and makes you feel welcome.

That’s my ideal. Question is: why is it so hard to get a lot of the time?

Re: The Ideal Pub Session…?

Sessions are always on weird days like Monday or Wednesday. Sessions on Saturday or even Sunday evening would be awesome! Preferably only one bodhran player period, no turn taking, just one good solid drummer. Backing by DADGAD steel string guitar. I prefer guitar to zouk, but that might be just me. No more than 2 or 3 fiddles. If there are instruments that are limited by key, it’s nice when the flute player has an F flute for example to play in D minor or G minor or the box player has more than one box. Sticking to G and D all night can be quite boring especially when so many great tunes are not in G or D. Sets can be discussed beforehand or spontaneous. Preferably more people would be joining in towards the end of the set with obscure tunes at the beginning of the set. No shenanigans such as elementary school percussion instruments like shakey eggs or rainsticks, no sheet music. I used to think sheet music could be okay at a session until I was at a session where somebody was using it, I tried to read along (because they were picking obscure tunes from their books), but when you’re drinking it’s really not fun.

Also I prefer playing with fewer people as long as we have tunes in common. I agree that larger sessions are often better for more tune variety. I find that when my turn to lead a set comes around, I feel more comfortable with a larger session because I feel like no matter what tune I go into somebody will know it. In a small session you have to be much more careful of tune choices. Also agree we don’t want to play the same tunes week after week for years, there are just too many great tunes out there to be playing the same ones forever. In longstanding sessions every member is hopefully making an effort to learn each other’s old favorites as well as learning new tunes when somebody introduces them. Get the names of the ones you don’t know and actually learn some of them before the next session, yeah that’s nice.

What I’m reading here tells me I’m pretty lucky with the session I’ve fallen into here in my home town. We meet once a week at a restaurant/pub that whose management and staff has bent over backwards to make us welcome, and we appreciate them very much. We get about eight or nine players on average, sometimes a couple more or a couple fewer. Five or six strong core players, the rest of us learning and pitching in best we can. Button box, two or three fiddles, flute, whistle, concertina, bodhran, mandolin, and banjo. One or two songs a night. Sheet music? Who reads sheet music? Because of the university here we get a lot of skilled players coming through for a little while and moving on, so things evolve month to month. The atmosphere within the group is friendly and fun, but the bar is set pretty high--don’t play what you don’t know, learn or be left behind. We’re there to challenge one another and keep one another sharp. Our session leader even gives us homework now and then: new tunes or sets to learn by next week or the week after, so that we build a repertoire together. Oh, and we also get free beer.

Re: The Ideal Pub Session…?

For me, smaller is better -- 5 to 8 or 10 -- but also being inclusive and not scaring new people off.

Ideally it’s a group where people can impulsively keep a set going by starting up another tune.

Importantly for the USA, it’s in a place with a welcoming atmosphere, and where it’s comfortable to sit and play. Welcoming means not a ton of TVs on the walls and not a ton of eejits at the bar screaming and yelling at each other. Also, a place where you’re not sticking out into the middle of the room, or where you’re in a little alcove where it’s uncomfortable for listeners to sit.

Even if the owner and staff are nice folks, a place with a sports-bar atmosphere won’t be appealing to good musicians -- the types you’d like to come and join in. So your “core” will get a little lonely.

Re: The Ideal Pub Session…?

Ideal session?
Three strong players with a good repertoire as a core - one of them can be a backer. Not too many players - not more than 10. Not more than one (1) accomplished bodhrán player who knows the tunes. No bones. No spoons. No eggs. No congas (unless *very* well played by someone who is familiar with the tunes). Etc. Not more than two backers - and if more than one they should be able to “read” each other so that they synchronise their backing. Melody players should outnumber the sum of backers and rhythm players.
Open and friendly atmosphere, helping beginner and intermediate players to fit in. I usually ask beginner and intermediate players for tunes they know and would like to play and if they can’t drive along the tune set themselves, I’ll do it for them.
However, the session leader(s) must have the guts to tell disruptive musicians to stop whatever they’re doing in a friendly way, e.g. “please learn the basic chord structure of a tune before you try to back it” or “you should practise keeping a steady rhythm”. Obnoxious b******s can be told in less kind ways, since that’s probably the only way they’ll understand, e.g. “if you haven’t got a clue about the tune/rhythm, just shut up and don’t try to play along and ruin the music for everyone else”. Been there, done that.
Songs now and then (though not required).
Session with a regular (or semi-regular) schedule, so that you know that e.g. every second Thursday there’s a session.
No list of tunes to be played, but instead whatever pops up. However, individual musicians may have a “cheat list” if they have a hard time remembering which tunes they actually know.
