Associating tunes with a specific musician?

Some friends and I had a discussion yesterday which raised the question about how many people associate a tune with a certain musician. Maybe it was the person you learned the tune from, the composer, the first recording of it you. heard or just the group of people you play(ed) it with.

There was a difference in opinion about it whether this was a common thing, and I was just curious about other people’s experiences. And perhaps it’s influenced by whether you learned your music as an adult in the US, or whether you grew up with it in Ireland or in a place outside of Ireland but with a strong Irish music community.

For example, I had the privilege to take a workshop with Brendan McGlinchey at the Gaelic Roots camp in Boston in 1998, and he played the Gooseberry Bush for the class. So I always think of that moment, and his playing, when that tune comes up in a session.

Re: Associating tunes with a specific musician?

I can’t imagine the tunes I play *not* being associated with specific people. A big part of the richness of this music happens when playing with others, and that fosters connection, friendship, communion.

If you learn your tunes from other people, face to face, then the tune’s associations tend to imprint on that experience.

If you learn your tunes from recordings, you risk imprinting on whatever device is playing the tune for you.

Of course, regardless of how we learn a tune, we can create our own rich emotional ties and associations when we play that tune with others. So some tunes might conjure up a particular session or funny incident from ages ago, or a particular friend who had a special way with the tune.

Re: Associating tunes with a specific musician?

“If you learn your tunes from recordings, you risk imprinting on whatever device is playing the tune for you.”

Is that a bad thing?

In my experience, imprinting on the version that the artist on the recording plays, would be the same if I learned the tune from the same artist in person.

Considering how infrequently some of us far from Ireland get the opportunity to learn tunes from in-person source, maybe traveling far to the occasional festival or workshop, videos and recordings are the next best thing and often all we have available.

I’ve always found the question “Where did you get that version of the tune” fairly off-putting.

It assumes a mindset that we’re all supposed to be historical recreation cosplayers with little space for individual interpretation of a tune.

There’s absolutely a place for those who want to recreate the style of playing of specific players and who openly communicate that intention, but I’ve never seen the requirement to be an ethnomusicologist a requirement for being a skilled and legitimate player of this music. That feels like a plot by Big Ethnomusicology to sell more musicology.

I’m guessing back in the day, John Joe in his kitchen when asked where he got some version of a tune would say “from Sean down at the pub” and not from the version played by Patsy Touhey on a specific wax cylinder transcribed by so-and-so, and then published in 1926 by XYZ, with corrections in the second edition from so-and-so.

Re: Associating tunes with a specific musician?

Michael, I’m not at all talking about regurgitating a setting note for note.

I’m talking about associating the tune with the source (as in the OP’s example). When I play a tune with a person, I form memories of that experience. When I play along with a clip off the Dolby app on my phone or YouTube, it’s a far less interesting, in-the-round, human experience, and the memories (if there are any) don’t fire up my amygdala in remotely the same way.

Few recordings would have the depth and breadth of experience—stories, humour, sights, smells, diversions and digressions, etc.—as an in-person interaction.

That in-person encounter doesn’t have to be with some icon from County Feckit in the Auld Sod, it can be any reasonably decent player: your regular session mates, a visitor from the next session over. And we’re not beholden to always play the tune that same way. It should morph and grow over the years we play it. But we can still enjoy the associations of that in-person interaction.

I get that not everyone has easy, regular access to living, breathing trad musicians, and recordings are a handy resource, better than many alternatives. I also get that not everyone forms bonds with the people they play music with. No doubt our AI overlords will prefer it that way. To me, it’s missing the point of making music. (To be clear, another point of making music is self-expression, which begs for no small amount of time playing alone.)

Re: Associating tunes with a specific musician?

Michael, I’m not seeing anywhere that I characterized your experience. I posted about my own experience. I’ve learned a lot of tunes directly from people. I’ve also learned tunes from recordings—as you say, it’s du jour.

I *do* learn tunes in sessions, picking them up quietly on the fly. I also get together with people to swap tunes. I make these a priority because (1) this is how my earliest mentors did things, (2) I enjoy the experience, and (3) I far prefer it over learning tunes from mechanical devices.

Yes, many people learn heavily from recordings nowadays. Many people seem to live online, interacting mainly through so-called social media and posting on forums such as this one. For me (and I’m a textbook introvert), that’s a poor simulation of real human interaction. Society as we knew it is on shaky ground in part because of the alienating effects of such technology. Some people disagree, and some seem unaware that it’s happening (and that they’re wrapped up in it), but that’s my perspective.

Back to the OP: in most cases, the associations that come with tunes for me are twofold. I have vivid memories of playing the tune with the person who made it memorable. And I also have the experience of woodshedding on the tune on my own, as the oldsters used to say, “living inside the tune.” I suppose one is an experience shared with another person, in person, and the other is an experience shared with the tune itself. They’re both integral to how I play this music.

Re: Associating tunes with a specific musician?

I would say that maybe 25% of the tunes I know are strongly associated with a person, usually who I learned it from. For instance, I learned Grainne’s Jig, from Mr. Eskin, and I recall that fact every time I play it…

Maybe another 25% of my tunes are ones that I can recall where I first heard it, often from a recording (and even more likely if I actually learned it from that recording), but I wouldn’t necessarily say that I have a strong association between the tune with whomever made that recording.

On the other hand, I would say maybe 50% of the tunes I play, I have no recollection of where or when I first learned it. (Or what the tune is named, for that matter…) Those are often tunes that I just picked up over the years as they were played in sessions, and I don’t really have one person to attribute it to.

But I will say that I think having the association between a tune and a person is a good thing! This music is about much more than just the tunes, and having a strong association between tunes and people is part of the social aspect of the tradition, as well as helpful for being able to recall the tune. In an age where a lot of the world’s social interaction happens by very non-personal means, reinforcing the social aspect of this music helps us stay more grounded in real human interaction, and helps keep the tradition moving forward!

Re: Associating tunes with a specific musician?

Well, Michael, you can always say “it’s mine” (re:version) :-)

I also agree that learning tunes from recordings is not in any way inferior to learning them directly from a person. It may lack the visual clues making it harder to learn some things like the bowing and the posture (as well as form those fond social interaction memories that gimpy rightly mentioned) but it also adds the convenience of immersing yourself into someone’s music deeply and repeatedly. In any case, it’s still a living person who played that tune and they may not be able to play them that way now – if indeed they’re still alive! Some of the finest Irish musicians have been heavily influenced by the old recordings of people they’ve never met.

If we get really technical, “learning” a tune from a person can also mean many different things. Sometimes it’s an inspiring version that you’ve heard once and it stuck with you just like that. Sometimes it’s a boring class where you learn the tune note to note and still forget it. A person could also have influenced you in many different ways but you don’t really play a lot of their tunes or didn’t learn them formally. Pádraig O’Keeffe wrote out *half* a tune in his tablature notation to many of his students, playing it once on a student’s fiddle to check – and that was the end of the class. Then there were several of his star pupils who weren’t really students in the same sense – Denis Murphy, Julia Clifford and Paddy Cronin could all play tunes with Pádraig all night but they likely got most of them by ear, not from notation. The former and the latter both learned from Pádraig in some way. There’s plenty of nuance in that!

Re: Associating tunes with a specific musician?

I have a whole host of associations with most of the tunes I play (and many that I don’t):

-The name or name(s) of the tune
-The feeling I have about the tune and how much I enjoy it
-Any information I learned about who composed the tune (if known), or county/region/player(s) it’s particularly connected with (if any)
-Which recording(s) that it’s on that I’ve listened to and which ones I prefer
-Whose playing initially inspired me to learn it, whether a person I know in real life, or a ‘famous’ player’s version on an album, Youtube video, Soundcloud, etc.
-When/where I learned it, whether long ago or recently, at home woodshedding with a recording (and whether a professional recording, or one I made myself at a session), or ‘live’ from a teacher during a lesson or workshop, or by noodling along during a session
-Which of my two regular sessions a tune will be played at, often including which individuals play it and which don’t, if it’s not one that pretty much everybody knows
-If it’s a particular favorite (or bugbear) of someone I play with
-Specific memories of a time I played or listened to a tune

To the original question, some of my tunes might be especially associated with a specific person from any number of the ‘categories’ above, but not all

Re: Associating tunes with a specific musician?

I probably associate every tune with *something*. If I have learned it directly from a particular musician, whether in person or from a recording, the association would most likely be with that musician. If I have picked it up in sessions, the association could be with the person that started the tune the first time I heard it, the particular occasion or place when/where I first heard it, the person that was sitting next to me in the session, what the weather was doing at the time or what the family at the next table were having for lunch… I have half-learned quite a lot of tunes by browsing (mostly online) tunebooks and, unless some new association has been forged (in any of the aforementioned ways), their only association is with the dots on the screen or page, and perhaps with the era of my life when I ‘learned’ them (most likely sometime in my 20s).

As for *versions* of tunes, I am a lazy learner and rarely learn a particular setting down to the last detail – and I attempt (sometimes successfully) to introduce variations with each iteration (although, when following another musician, I try to stay as close to their version as I can). The last time I was asked “Where did you get that version (of ‘The Pigeon on the Gate’)?”, I felt I was being tested – I laughed and replied “It’s a combination of every version I’ve ever heard”. But perhaps I was mangling it to the point of blurring the boundaries between it and other tunes… Still, at least it was recognised as the tune it was supposed to be.
