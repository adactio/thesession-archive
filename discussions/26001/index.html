Musical Hopes and Ambitions

I’m curious to hear about your aspirations, goals, hopes, expectations, and maybe even ambitions for playing this music. What, if anything, do you hope to accomplish in your time playing the tunes?

When I first started playing this music, all I hoped for was to be able to play it well enough for my own enjoyment. It never crossed my mind to aim at higher “ambitions” such as playing in a band, recording a cd, or anything beyond simply playing the music for the sake of playing the music.

Somewhere along the way, I realized that I enjoyed playing in sessions, and so I decided to learn more tunes, and I learned to play with a variety of feels for the pulse and swing, so I could play *with* other people’s feel over a wide range of possibilities. In short, my goal was to become a decent session player.

That’s all I’ve ever really aspired to, and it’s enough. It never ends because there are always tunes to learn and lots to learn about the different roles anyone can play in sessions (e.g., supporting actor, lead, character cameo, craic head, etc.).

I’m not at all knocking more ambitious goals. I’d like to hear from people with all sorts of aims and ambitions with this music. Where do you hope to go?

Re: Musical Hopes and Ambitions

I’ve been asked this by my sessionmates before. I tell them that I have two goals: I want to play this music well - really, really well; and I want to enjoy every moment I have with my fiddle - that is, I never want to play out of a sense of obligation. This latter point means that not only do I not aspire to play in a band or such - it means that I specifically aspire to NOT play in a band. I know that if I ever take on such a commitment, there will come a time when I won’t be in the mood to play at a certain time or in a certain situation, but will have to do so because I’d promised someone else that i would. I like keeping this music as a hobby, something that I do entirely on my own terms.

Re: Musical Hopes and Ambitions

For me, all music is about communication. Usually it is communication that can’t happen with words. And, it can happen with other musicians and/or between the musicians and the listeners. I don’t think it makes much difference what type of music one is discussing; it is that communication that makes it all go, at least for me.

To be able to communicate you need to be able to speak the language. That is not a perfect metaphor, but it will do. That need, in turn, implies that you will strive to get as good as you can get at that particular language.

I get most of my joy playing with others and don’t like playing alone. I remember truly hating doing required solos at the end of term in college. It just isn’t my thing.

So, my hope is to go on playing all kinds of music with others who are like minded. I want to play that music as well as possible and I treasure the moments when it all comes together and we are grinning at each other.

Robert Shaw used to remind his choirs that the word “amateur” only implied love of what one was doing. It says nothing about technical skill. A useful thing to remember.
