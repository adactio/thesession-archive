What are the best ITM concerts you’ve been to?

This topic probably cropped up before my time on this site but sure why not answer again! What are all your favourite ITM concerts you’ve been to? I’ve seen everyone from Planxty to Dervish to Lunasa to Altan to Mcgoldrick/McSherry but believe it or not, none of these were my favourites. (especially not Lúnasa!)

The best concerts I’ve been to in no particular order were

1. Paul Brady, Paddy Glackin and Donal Lunny at the 2001 Frankie Kennedy Winter School. It was just billed as Donal Lunny and friends, first half was just Glackin and Lunny and it was absolutely sublime, then Paul Brady joined for the second half doing all ITM along with some of his best songs from his trad days Arthur MacBride, Mary & The Soldier, The Creel etc. It is fairly unusual to catch Brady playing trad these days and in the intimate atmosphere of Ionad Cois Luche it was just one of the best gigs ever.

2. Again in the same venue but a couple of years later was a brilliant concert by the wonderful Liz Carrol and John Doyle. They were on smashing form that night and I dunno there is just something about that venue at that time of year that makes great gigs even more special. Apparently Liz Carrol started up a great seisiún with some of the Altan crew later on that night in ‘An Chuirt’ and I bloody missed it! Went all the way back to Bunbeg and couldn’t find a decent seisiún :-(

3. The first time I saw Martin Hayes and Dennis Cahill they blew me away, hadn’t heard any of their records at the time so I wasn’t prepared for how unique they were and I never before or since experienced such a spine-tingling experience as the non-stop 45 minute set they did to finish. It was so different to any interpretation of ITM I’d ever heard yet so beautiful and hypnotic. Don’t care what the begrudgers think, what they did on that night was some of the most beautiful and pure ITM I’ve ever heard. Have seen them many times since and always enjoyed it, but it’s hard to get the same buzz again from the first time hearing that unexpected 45 minute set.

In those three concerts you have my 3 favourite fiddle players Liz Carrol, Paddy Glackin and Martin Hayes and probably my four favourite backers Paul Brady, Denis Cahill, John Doyle and Donal Lunny. Although I also love Steve Cooney but for some reason I’ve never seen him live!

Anyway, enough about my favourites, What were your best ITM concert experiences people?! Please nobody say the Corrs!!!

I too was blown away with Martin Hayes and Denis Cahill when they came to Canberra in the 90’s. However, since moving to the Alice, we have only had a few ITM concerts come through. It was amazing, Marcus Holden, Clare O‘Meara and Mark Oates and the local violinists who attended their fiddle workshop all came along. Although ITM only forms a part of their eclectic repertoire, their concert was lively and just lovely (concensus: the best ever at Araluen), and I’m still on cloud nine three weeks after the event. Thanks to everyone who was at the Firkin & Hound, you made one ITM enthusiast very very happy.

Re: What are the best ITM concerts you’ve been to?

Here’s 3 that stick in my memory, but there are so many:

“The Bothy Band”, Aberdeen 1975. Their first gig outside of Ireland, just before they made the 1st album. Never heard anything better.

“Maggie’s Leap”, Glasgow 1979. A Belfast band comprising Trevor Stewart [uilleann pipes], Desi Wilkinson [flute], Deirdre Shannon & Andy Dickson [fiddles], and Ian Robertson [bodhran]. Also on the bill were 2 singers, Len Graham, and [I think] Ted Hickey.

And finally, how’s this for a day out ?:
Ballisodare Festival 1978: an 8 hour marathon. Bands included “Oisin”, “Clannad”, who were a very innovative traditional band before they went all “new-age”, “Boys Of The Lough”, and “The Bothy Band”, [with Kevin Burke]. Solo performances from Christy Moore, Andy Irvine, Liam O’Flynn,[ but despite huge pressure from the crowd, no “Planxty” on-stage re-union ], American guitarist Dan Crary, Dick Gaughan, and also Matt Molloy accompanied by Donal Lunny.
What I really liked about this festival was that they also put some of the older local traditional musicians on stage, not just the current “top names” of the time, so we also got to hear Josie McDermott, Fred Finn & Peter Horan playing with Matt Molloy & Kevin Burke, and Cathal McConnell duetting with Packie Duignan. Sean-nos singer Nicolas Toibin sang a song that was a full 15 minutes long.
As if all of this wasn’t enough, we went to Collooney for food and a few pints and stumbled quite by chance into a flute session with Cathal McConnell, Matt Molloy and Seamus Tansey, not a combination you get to hear every day of the week.
I realise that these were all in the 70s, so I’ll just add 1 from Glasgow’s “Celtic Connections” a few years back - double-bill of “Danu” and “Llan de Cubel”[ straying slightly from the original question, I know.]
This year, the Scottish/Irish combination of Julie Fowlis, Ross Kennedy and 2 members of “Danu” in Aberdeen was the best I’ve heard in years.

Re: What are the best ITM concerts you’ve been to?

It wasn’t quite a concert, but neither was it a session. During the final afternoon of the final Green Linnet Weekend at the Knickerbocker Hotel in the Catskills, (back in ’95?) there was a wonderful gathering called “Music from Pepper’s Pub”. It featured the Tulla Ceili Band (at that time the Tulla held their practices in Pepper’s Pub in Feakle) which was then touring after the release of their 50th anniversary CD. The music began with the Tulla and quickly added Martin Hayes, Paul Cahill, and all of the other trad musicians who had performed for the weekend. As the group grew to about 30 musicians, the floor under their feet started to shake from the tapping of toes. At the concert which was being held downstairs, featuring Fairport Convention, the chandeliers started to sway and they had to send a runner upstairs to tell all present to spread out so that the ceiling would not collapse. I recall looking around during this wonderful afternoon to see many of those present with tears running down their faces. It was a once in a lifetime experience and certainly my best ITM experience!
