Which Requires More Skill In Music, Singing Or Playing An Instrument.?

Added by Michael Dineen on November 16th, 2014

Which Requires More Skill In Music, Singing Or Playing An Instrument.?

I always felt that while our song and instrumental music tradition are connected, they both require a certain skill. Musical ability is a great thing. It is nice to be able to pick up an instrument, and show a bit of virtuoso work on it. Singing requires more courage. It also comes down to the type of voice, a person has. If someone has a good voice, they can carry a song very well. However, if they have a poor voice it won’t come across very well. I suppose in many ways, singing comes cheap. If a person can sing, they will just open their mouth and the voice will come. They can then figure out the style they want to sing in. Playing instruments, means that a lot of money must be spent before anything happens. The other side to musical ability is, if a person gets nervous during a performance and makes a mistake, they could often add a few notes to cover it up and it will pass. Which of these talents requires more skill, singing or playing an instrument?

Re: Which Requires More Skill In Music, Singing Or Playing An Instrument.?

Singing requires a great deal of skill. The sounds that you make when you come out of the womb don’t come anywhere close near the singing that even untrained people can produce. However, it’s a skill that comes fairly naturally considering it’s part of core skills required for us humans - we also use our voice for communication.
Getting notes out of an instrument takes a while as well, the motoric skills required to play an insturment are quite substantial. However I gave my nephew a toy accordion for his birthday, he cannot talk/sing yet but he can produce fairly melodic sounds with the instrument.

Unsurprisingly, you’re comparing apples and oranges here. And putting all instruments on one pile won’t help this discussion either. I’d say when comparing instruments (incl. voice) the entry level might differ, and with that I mean the amount of time it takes to produce relatively simple tunes as opposed to just sound. For example, pipes take a long time, but a piano is relatively easy to play simple tunes on. But playing with two hands fluently on piano is not of any less skill compared to playing the pipes. Likewise, being capable of singing notes in different octaves in the right pitch with good volume requires years of training as well.

My point is simple: I don’t agree with your statement “If you can sing, you can go very far indeed without any training or knowledge whatsoever. The same cannot be said for an instrumentalist.”

And so far you are doing nothing to convince me otherwise. Elvis had Charlie Hodge as a live-in vocal coach, Sinatra had John Quilan.

It is true that McCartney and Wison didn’t have any formal training, but both studied and practiced obsessively, so you could hardly say they were singing “without any training or knowledge whatsoever” and the fact that both were accomplished instrumental musicians, having learned learned to play in exactly the same way that they learned to sing puts pay to the idea “the same cannot be said for an instrumentalist”.

The reality is that the human voice is an instrument, and like any other instrument, it takes study and practice to master it. Having a great vocal range doesn’t make a great singer. Practicing until you have complete control of your instrument, and knowing enough about music and prosody to know what sound you need to produce make a great singer.

Re: Which Requires More Skill In Music, Singing Or Playing An Instrument.?

“Singing requires more courage.”

Disagree. Singing requires more courage for people that aren’t confident in their voice. Some people have the confidence for singing, just like some don’t have to confidence to play their instruments publicly. Also, some people will sing in groups, but won’t sing solo, just like some will play instruments in groups but will adamantly refuse to play solo.

“If someone has a good voice, they can carry a song very well. However, if they have a poor voice it won’t come across very well.”

There are an abundance of tones and timbres of voices. This is too subjective to generalize. Some are soft and sweet. Some are heavy and raspy. Some people can work with a soft and sweet voice, and for others it’s too weak and nasally. No power behind it. Some people can work with a heavy and raspy voice, but it takes a lot of control to keep that kind of singing aesthetic and in tune, so many people can’t pull it off.

“I suppose in many ways, singing comes cheap. If a person can sing, they will just open their mouth and the voice will come.”

Disagree. I can sing, but it took years and years of training under the same people that taught me how to play instruments. Outside of personal experience, I’ve heard some really good singers become great singers after a bit of guidance and instruction from a professional. We all have room for improvement and singing is not special in that regard.

“Playing instruments, means that a lot of money must be spent before anything happens.”

I disagree. Singers also spend a lot of money, a lot of the same money us instrumentalist spend in terms of tuition. They may not have to spend money on buying and maintaining instruments of wood and metal, but they do have to take great care of their voice. Voice lessons aren’t cheap either.

“The other side to musical ability is, if a person gets nervous during a performance and makes a mistake, they could often add a few notes to cover it up and it will pass.”

No one in their right mind would “add a few notes” to cover up a mistake. That’s exactly the opposite of what anyone would want to do. It would make the mistake very obvious, more obvious than if you just let it pass itself. It’s best to keep the rhythmic flow going and get back on track melodically rather than throw in more to the mistake.
