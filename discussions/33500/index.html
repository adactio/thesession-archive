↳ Posted by Sol Foster on March 11th, 2014.

Re: Best key for a 3rd whistle, I have low and high D whistles.

1. “I’m wanting a whistle that is less shrill than the High D and more portable, easier on the fingers and less demanding of breath than the Low D.”

2. “It would be nice if it was in a useful key for Irish music.”

It all depends on your purpose. The two statements above don’t necessarily point in the same direction.

#1 seems to be pointing to something in the middle between Low D and High D due to pitch, ergonomics, and breath requirements. Whistles in G and A are right in the middle there, and many people love playing whistles in these keys. I have Burkes in G and A and they’re sweet players. If you don’t want the ‘pure’ tone of Burkes, Colin Goldie makes great whistles with a complex tone in every key.

F is a tad low of being in the middle. I love my Burke F. If you don’t want a ‘pure’ tone, MK makes a fantastic F that has a more complex tone, as does Goldie. Many players’ favourite whistle key is F… there’s something special about that key, don’t know what.

Bb is a tad high of being in the middle. There are great-playing Bb Generations around, or you can go with various other makers.

#2 is asking about useful keys for traditional Irish music. At sessions it seems like C might be the most useful, for those tunes in D minor mostly, but also some tunes in C Major and G minor. I love my old Generation C, it’s a very sweet player.

Also useful is an E whistle. Now if you go high E it’s even more ‘shrill’ than a high D. If you go low E it’s nearly like playing a low D, just a bit more ergonomic. But I like using a low E whistle for those fiddle tunes in A (I play them in G, on the E whistle they come out in A).

BTW high D whistles don’t have to be ‘shrill’. My old Feadog D plays extremely sweetly, not shrill in the least.

And Low D whistles don’t have to take a lot of wind. MK Low Ds are extremely air-efficient; about the same volume of air passes through my MK Low D as does my Burke high D.

↳ Posted by Richard D Cook on March 11th, 2014.

Re: Best key for a 3rd whistle, I have low and high D whistles.

Now that I think about it, if I were in you shoes, before I got a 3rd whistle I would get a sweet-playing high D, and a low D that didn’t have big breath requirements.

Because in looking back at your original statement it doesn’t seem like either your high D or low D are very satisfactory.

Jerry Freeman’s ‘tweaked’ Feadog D is the closest whistle I’ve tried to my old c1980 Feadog MK I, a very sweet nimble player, though not enough volume for some people. If you want to get away from your ‘shrill’ high D you can try that. The Burke narrow bore high D in brass is also a sweet player, very pure, not shrill at all.

Low Ds have a wider finger-stretch, it’s true. Conical-bore low Ds bring the lower-hand fingerholes closer together, you can try a Dixon all-plastic conical-bore low D for an inexpensive one. There are a number of high-end makers of wood conical-bore low Ds such as Nigel Richard and Jon Swayne. Or you might be lucky enough to come across a great-playing Copeland Low D from the 1980s or thereabouts. All of these will be more ergonomic than straight-bore Low Ds such as Burke, MK, Goldie, Overton, Reviol, Reyburn, etc.

Re: Best key for a 3rd whistle, I have low and high D whistles.

If you want a practical 3rd whistle to use in sessions and to have a nice solo pleasing whistle then get a Low G. An easy span, a mellow sound and ideal for tunes that sit low (below D under treble clef) as well as for tunes that are written with lowest note G on 2nd treble clef line or higher. The first example brings a strong full sound at the pitch of the D whistle where the high D whistles are fairly soft. The second drops the pitch an octave from the High D range and replaces shrill with warm. Using software to transpose the tunes accordingly for yourself will help if you want to avoid visually translating the fingering or learning in a new key by heart. Tunebook does this well as do some online web pages. I have a Susato Low G which is nice but I’m sure there are other good ones but don’t get a wide bore (they need too much air!)

If you mainly want a nice sounding, easy to play, comfortable lower pitched whistle then Low F, Low G, Low A , Bb and B are good. I prefer a few to alternate between but would find it hard to pick just one. Like someone else said - it’s hard to stop at just one more.
