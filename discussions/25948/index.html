I don’t think one single person or group has been the most influential, but some key people who have include:

Chief O’Neill and his collectors and sources;

Michael Coleman, James Morrison and all the other musicians who recorded 78s in the US;

the landlord of The Devonshire Arms in Camden Town who allowed, and the musicians (including Michael Gorman and John Vesey) who played at, the first regular pub session in London and thereby established a trend;

Séamus Ennis for his collecting and broadcasting;

the founders of CCÉ;

Ciarán Mac Mathúna for his radio shows;

Seán Riada, Paddy Moloney et al.

All have had an extremely wide influence.

Re: Who Has been the most influential Person(s)l in Irish music?

The “purists” laugh at O‘Riada, Planxty, The Chieftains, O’Carolan, so I think we can forget all about the “purists”.

In England the “folk revival” began in the late 1950s and early 1960s and Ireland was the same. So you had the Clancy’s, The McPeake’s and The Dubliners in Ireland.

Traditionally the McPeakes were probably thye best of these, but the Dubliners influenced the most. Barney and John Sheehan, irrespective of how good they were (argue about that elsewhere) influenced many, many musicians. Thousands of people began with the Dubliner’s songs and then progressed into the music. Thousands.

Now when the Dubliners were Kings in the mid 1960s I would say that very, very few of those listening to them had even heard of Coleman or Ennis or Clancy. Only the trad people had heard of them.

So for influence in getting people started…..for me the Dubliners.

In recent times it is probably Riverdance, The Corrs, Sharon Shannon and probably the Drop Kick Murphys in the USA.

These people sew the seeds, and once interested, players progress into the tradition.
