Re: Slowly building a repertoire of sets

Nobody said above that they cannot discern the difference (between slides and double jigs). They were debating whether it was OK to mix the two in a set. (Me, I say why the hell not - if you’re not playing for dancers, does it matter?)

But still, there’s a big difference between not being able to tell the difference in general, and occasionally coming across a particular tune that could go different ways. I’d say I have a very good grasp of different tune types and their rhythms and emphases and wouldn’t often entirely mistake a tune-type; but there are plenty of reels that work well being swung like a hornpipe and played at a hornpipe pace; some reels that have a bit of a polka feel to them.

In the Johnny O‘Leary tune book, he is quoted as saying that sometimes it is hard to tell the difference between certain slides and jigs. And Johnny O’Leary knew more slides than most musicians on the planet.
There are also quite a few tunes in that same book that, to quote it’s own notes, “fall into the fast hornpipe/slow polka category”. There are several instances of the latter that I would personally designate polkas (and play them as such) but the book calls them hornpipes. Whatever - it totally depends on how much swing the player gives it and what sort of tempo.

I would agree that such ambiguity is rare – but there’s a hell of a lot of tunes out there, it stands to reason you’ll get the occasional odd fish.

Re: Slowly building a repertoire of sets

I think there are two things going on here. The “Road to Lisdoonvarna” shows one of them. Many (all?) tunes can be played with more than one rhythm. Sometimes it’s easy to do, sometimes it’s a bit tricky. Sometimes the results are great, sometimes they are terrible. (Classic examples include “Around the World For Sport” (reel) / “The Leitrim Fancy” (jig) and “Ballydesmond Polka #2” / “Maurice Manley’s” (slide).)

So yeah, it’s definitely possible to take a slide and play it like a double jig. For some slides it even works pretty well.

This doesn’t mean that slides are supposed to sound like double jigs. If you’re playing all your slides so they sound like they go naturally with double jigs, then you are not playing them like slides. (Mind you, you could certainly make a set mixing the two. But if you’re doing it right, even very casual listeners will probably pick up on the fact something is different when you change from one to the other.)

I don’t know that I can put into words the difference, mind you. And I’m not from Kerry, I’m just grasping at straws from a distance, so I don’t know that I’ve got slides exactly right either. (Or hell, jigs for that matter.)

I don’t know about you, but that’s nothing at all what my double jigs sound like.

Double jig:
did-lee-dee did-lee-dee

Single jig
Dum-dee Dum-dee

Single Jig/Slide/Hop Jig all have the Dum-dee (long short) figure a lot, especially at the start of phrases)

Double jig/slip jig both have the did-lee-dee did-lee-dee a lot throughout.

Basically two different subdivisions of the same family. The Dum-dees and the Did-lee-dees.

I know it’s a hornpipe - I got it off of John Williams “Steam” album along with “The Peterswell Hornpipe” and added “The Lass on the Strand” to extend that set a bit. I play it as a hornpipe but dang, the melody just strikes me as the tune being something other than a hornpipe. The Lass on the Strand is a bit of a stretch for a damaged left hand but it’s good therapy. Eventually I’ll be able to play it in front of real live people! I hope…
