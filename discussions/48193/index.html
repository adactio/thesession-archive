Is a good tune a good tune regardless of the circumstances?

Added by 🎻Johnny Jay, Melody Player 🎻 on May 3rd, 2023

Is a good tune a good tune regardless of the circumstances?

I don’t mind if this discussion “drifts” into whatever you like and I’m sure there’s many examples we could consider… e.g. an inappropriate title, written specifically for some “dubious” person or event, played at an inappropriate event, by a musician(s) you don’t like or respect, or just played badly/over played. The possibilities are endless.

Anyway, what triggered this idea for a discussion was the fact that Ewen Henderson who is a musician I highly respect has recently composed a tune especially for an event which is happening this weekend.

https://projects.handsupfortrad.scot/musichub/2023/05/king-charles-iiis-coronation-strathspey-composed-by-ewen-henderson/

I’ve given it a “play through” and it’s a good enough tune even if it is arguably not a “classic”. However, my first thoughts were that playing this at a local session or during a performance could prove quite divisive. Personally, I’m not anti monarchy as such although I feel quite indifferent about the present incumbent.

There’s no need to focus on this particular tune though. As I say, the discussion can be opened up to cover whatever you want.

Re: Is a good tune a good tune regardless of the circumstances?

I’d say yes, but our feelings about it are rarely straightforward.

For instance, terrible people can make supremely good work, and there will always be audiences who can make the separation between artist and art more easily than others. It partly depends on the greatness of the work, how much time has passed since it was created (we’re all inescapably of our time), and at what stage it became clear that the artist was a vile human being (sometimes a work gets woven deeply into a culture before this is widely understood).

In the case of something like this, where it’s more a political thing, time also comes into it. Because I’m not much of a monarchist, I might not play that tune now in case it gave people the impression that I am. But if it’s still around in a few years’ time because it’s a good tune, I wouldn’t have quite the same resistance to it even if I knew the history. The moment’s passed.

Re: Is a good tune a good tune regardless of the circumstances?

This is an interesting topic for a lot of reasons. It certainly makes sense for songs to potentially be a source of controversy (political or otherwise), but I don’t generally think of tunes the same way. In this day and age, we seem to have the propensity to disavow, boycott, or ‘cancel’ anything that is related to something or someone that we don’t like or disagree with. That’s certainly within peoples’ rights, and in some cases can have a positive outcome. But, it is often a case of selective indignation for political purposes. So I guess it’s wise to be conscious of potential issues that people might have with a tune so that you can make prudent decisions about playing it in certain company.

↳ Posted by Barry Morse on May 7th, 2023.

Re: Is a good tune a good tune regardless of the circumstances?

With regard to Da Slockit Light on The Silver Bow, I would say that you can still definitely feel the pulse. It slows down temporarily at points but the main expressive element is in the flexible phrasing (shortening or lengthening some notes at the expense of others).
As mentioned, it’s much easier to do this with a small group of players who know each other’s playing very well (in this case, Tom Anderson, Aly Bain and Violet Tulloch) than with a large group of players at, say, a festival session.
But the tune works just as well played straight because it is a tune without any associated lyrics and was composed with normal bar lengths, rather than the air of a song which can have uneven bars (and/or meter) to fit in with the rhythm of the words.

I can think of several instances of the circumstances not being right for a particular tune or song, although in each case, the player/singer would have been totally oblivious to the situation.
There’s a guy singing a Kate Wolf song about dying of cancer, while one of our friends, sitting right next to him, is undergoing cancer treatment. There’s me singing “Goodbye Booze”, not knowing that someone in the room lost his wife to alcohol-related illness. There’s someone else singing a song about mixing up the tea caddy and the urn containing granny’s ashes on the very day we’d all just been to a friend’s Mum’s funeral. If any of these circumstances had been known to all, these songs would never have been sung on those occasions, but you can’t put a ban on any or every song or tune that might just possibly upset someone in the room.

And FWIW, my “Slockit Light” story: it was being played in an English session I was at, at about twice normal speed, romping along, quick march. Afterwards, I ventured to remark, very politely, I thought, that we usually play it a bit slower than that - to which “You play it how you like in your country - we’ll play it how we like here!” That’s me telt!
