Fiddle jigs, Flute reels, etc.

…is this true?

Yeah, Ok, many tunes may have been composed by a fiddle player (eg Ed Reevey {Hell, I’ll never get the spelling right!} or Josephine Keegan) or a flute player (eg Josey McDermott), but they aren’t the sole preserve of these instruments.

D’Ye’s ever hear at a session, some tune expert (probably like me - anally-retentive-stamp-collector-mentality-tune-geek,) after ye’ve played yer guts out on a tune, and yer man turns round and says, “Oh, yes, well that’s actually a fiddle tune”….If not fiddle, insert pipes/box/whatever.

I mean, if you can do a passable job on something and it sounds the biz, who gives a flyin’ fiddle?

Yep, and I know flutes only go down to C, whilst many Reevey and Fahey tunes go down to G, but so what, you go up for those couple of notes. It may lose a bit of the essence there, but you can make up for it, if you’re able to present the rest of the tune with dignity.

I’m merely questioning this notion of something being a flute/fiddle/whatever tune.

views?

Dan

Re: Fiddle jigs, Flute reels, etc.

What makes a tune a flute tune rather than a fiddle tune? Others have said that being in G major (or its relatives) and not having notes below low D are defining factors. Which they are. But another factor that’s often involved is the phrasing of the tune. If it’s a real notey tune without obvious places for the flute player to break the phrase and take a breath, then it’s probably not going to be a good flute tune. Not that this means it can’t be played (and played well) by a flute player. Rather, it means it will probably require a bit of work to master. The Skylark and Spike Island Lasses were like that for me. I needed to sit down and figure out where to breathe in them, but once I did they became two of my favorites.

I also think that a flute tune (for flute players) or a fiddle tune (for fiddle players) is one that will come right back under your fingers when it shows up at a session months (or even years) after you last played it, or even thought about it for that matter. The fiddle tunes that I do play (as a flute player) I often think of as “Peking Duck tunes”. Meaning that they are on my menu, but I will require 24 hours notice in order to produce an acceptable version of them.

Danny,

Okay, you have outed me. I’m a flute player, and I also own whistles although I rarely play them. And I agree that we are probably agreeing, but let me address some of your points anyway. Re “the flute goes down to C, normally”, well yes that’s true if you have an 8-key flute with working C/C# keys, but not if you only have a 6-key or keyless flute in which case the low note is D. And it seems that most of the 8-key flutes around are old ones (e.g. Rudalls and the like), while most of the 6-key/keyless flutes coe from modern makers. And in my experience older flutes are much more common in Ireland/Britain than they are in the US, where I am. So I guess it depends on where you live.

As for the phrasing and breaths just coming naturally on the flute, I’ll agree with that if you’d also say that stuff like cross-bowing, open-string rolls and such just come naturally on the fiddle. If you’re a really good player, maybe they do but I know a lot of fiddlers that find them hard to pull off. Same thing with breathing/phrasing on the flute. In some tunes it does just come naturally (those tunes I would probably call “flute tunes”) and in others it doesn’t (those are tunes that I’d say aren’t “flute tunes”). Sure you just leave out a note here or there to take a breath, but which one? Sometimes it just jumps out at you (comes naturally), other times it requires work and experimentation before it sounds right.

Another aspect of what’s a flute/fiddle tune and what’s not that I didn’t mention before is ornamentation and where it falls in a particular tune. A banjo player friend of mine just wrote a tune and asked me how it would be for the flute. Well, it’s in D dorian and has several places where there are three F natural eighth notes (quavers, crotchets, Bob Cratchits or whatever you call those in Europe I don’t know) right in a row. Not a lot that you can do with that on the flute, no matter how many keys you have. So I told him it’s probably not a good flute tune.

Of course, sure, any tune can probably be played on whatever instrument, given enough work by the player. And those standards are evolving. Why, back in the day Matt Molloy’s playing of the Toss the Feathers with the high D crans on the flute was considered revolutionary. Now, it’s run of the mill for pretty much any good flute player…

Re: Fiddle jigs, Flute reels, etc.

One point that doesn’t seem to have been touched on is that most of the tunes that are considered “fiddle” tunes are considered such because they are written in keys that use the sonorities of the instrument to advantage. Hence the commonest keys for the fiddle are 2 sharps, 1 sharp, and no sharps or flats, which will enable one or more open strings to resonate in sympathy with almost every note that’s being played - I deliberately haven’t mentioned specific names of keys because that will only lure us into the deep waters of modal keys, and confuse the issue. As a side issue, but still very relevant, this means that fiddle players should make sure their strings are exactly in tune and they should therefore play well in tune so as to take advantage of the resonances.
It’s also worth pointing out that if you play, on the fiddle, a tune in 1 or 2 flats (and there are a few), it seems to have a very special character which is lost if you transpose it to one of the “sharp” keys. Matin Hayes, at a concert in Bristol this year, remarked that he has a preference for tunes in 2 flats - I don’t know how serious he was, though.
Further, each note on the fiddle has its own particular tonal character, which is based on a number of factors including the resonances of the instrument and the type of string and how you play the note, and this is especially so with the better quality instruments.
Do the flutes/whistles have anything analogous to what I have said above about fiddles?
