The idea of an AI programme composing/compiling ‘traditional’ tunes seems to miss the point for me - as does the offering of self-penned pieces on this site. Certainly tunes that have features in common with this or that cultural style are being made up all the time - whether by actual people or software - but what is it that makes a tune accepted as ‘traditional’? What has made a tune composed by Paddy Fahey or Ed Reavy ‘traditional’ - apart from having been composed by a player brought up and well-versed in the genre? Was it traditional while the metaphorical ink was still wet?

Maybe the ‘traditional’ attribution becomes conferred with use; a cultural accretion, like lichen on a gatepost. Perhaps a new tune needs to spend time being being taken up, played by people within a particular community and passed around, in the process becoming amenable to players’ whims and stylistic idiosyncrasies while surviving to retain a distinctive shape and character.

Clearly the body of material embraced by a tradition needs to admit new entrants, otherwise it would be a bit like Early Music - musically valuable, of course, but circumscribed as to content and appealing to the musician’s antiquarian sensibilities. And there’s no rule about how long this should take: it could include a tune in a centuries-old rediscovered MS, or something more recent like a Maurice Lennon waltz: it’s the absorption by a bunch of players that makes for recognition as ‘traditional’.

Well I’m glad to know that ChatGPT does at least acknowledge that “the accordion” is one of the harder instruments to play. I have, on occasion, had people ask me about my B/C button accordion, along the lines of “Oh, that looks really simple - is it easy to play?” - to which I would answer - “don’t kid yourself - it’s totally illogical - requires developing lots of new brain connections” - or, as Chat GPT says:
“Some people may find the chromatic button accordion to be more challenging due to its complex button layout and the need to develop a greater understanding of music theory in order to play it well.” I do have the music theory from all my years playing piano and guitar, and yes, it does help, but there’s still an awful lot of learning to do!
