Re: your opinions please: on posting a closed session

Thing with a session is that not many punters come specifically to “listen”. They come for a good night out in the pub with mates and a few beers, and the live music is - hopefully - an added draw to that bar. However, at worst it seems to be something that’s annoying a few of them, which they have to shout over. The drunker they are, the worse that can become.

Musicians, OTOH, come specifically for the music. They’re there to listen, and maybe have a few tunes and enjoy the session. I’ll always look up the session if I’m going somewhere. If it’s one night only, I probably wouldn’t bring an instrument, so closed or not wouldn’t matter. If I’d moved somewhere, or was there longer term, I’d discount a closed session immediately, as what would be the point? I don’t want to go along to a session week after week to listen. I’d go and see a band for that.

Besides, if you close the session to musicians, and try to just get more drinking punters through the door who’re not participating, you’re likely to end up with a far worse session. Some nights a lot of regulars won’t turn up and you’ll end up with a flute and mandolin trying to be heard over a din of drunk punters.

For a session to be really good, there needs to be new people coming in, imo, and an element of not knowing what the night will be like.

Re: your opinions please: on posting a closed session

I can understand the need for a closed - or at least, not-completely open - session in certain circumstances: where, for example, the only people who play Irish (or whatever nationality or genre of) music are a small group of enthusiasts; there may be an abundance of musicians in the area but who are primarily interested in playing their own songs, Bob Dylan covers, aboriginal-bluegrass-jazz-punk fusion or what have you. So, designating the session “open” might unintentionally encourage musicians with no insight into, interest in or appreciation of whatever type of music you wish to play in your session.

There has been some talk of starting an Irish session of this kind where I live, as the musicians who have a serious interest in Irish music around here make up a very small proportion of the total number of session players; making it an “open” session would probably result in it being dominated by people who a have couple of token Irish token or a Christy Moore song, but with little else Irish in their repertoires and little interest in adding any more. This may seem like snobbery - perhaps it is - but I embrace all that goes on in my other local sessions (which are very mixed, with strong English and/or “contemporary acoustic” tendencies) and merely wish to have the opportunity to have a whole evening of good Irish tunes with a few like minded musicians - something which many take for granted (as I did when I lived in London).

Thus far, only one off Irish sessions have taken place, most of them in private, with no audience apart from ourselves (now, you can’t call that a gig). If I had sufficient mastery of Irish music and the requisite charisma and powers of persuasion, I would, without a doubt, welcome anyone whom I felt I could lure into the world of Irish trad - in fact, if I were a great musician, I would probably not care a fig what other music went on between the jigs and reels. But as it is, I am fairly average adult-inductee to Irish music and my grasp on it is somewhat shaky - and, if I may be so bold, I think that the same is true, to a greater or lesser degree, of my fellow Irish music heads - so it takes a few consecutive sets of tunes before the music really starts to flow.

There are, however, degrees of closedness. If Matt Molloy were to be passing through, of course he’d be welcome. So, in my book, would be any musician with a serious interest in Irish music, be they world-class, fair to middling or an absolute beginner. And, of course, assuming the session is in a public place, anyone, musician or non-musician would be welcome to participate as a respectful listener and round-buyer (< .

Stalingrad - Returning to the original question: Is it *acceptable* to post a closed session? It’s not against the site rules, as far as I know. If you really don’t want *anyone* except your select group of friends to play in your session, then there’s little point in posting it here, as most people who read these pages are, I think, players and, if they were looking for a session, would be expecting to play at it. As several posters have suggested, why not just call yourselves a band and call it a gig?

If, however, you are just trying not to encourage the “wrong sort” of musicians in order to keep your session Irish, then there’d be no harm in posting it. Sure, not *everyone* who visits this site is a bona fide Irish music lover, but I suspect most are. You might get a prima donna Bodhran player who speeds up, or a bass guitarist who plays a thrash rendition of She Moved Through the Fair; then again, you might get Dermot Byrne or Kieran Hanrahan - in my opinion it’s worth the risk.

Re: your opinions please: on posting a closed session

It’s amazing how myriad discussions here routinely circle back to what we mean by this word, “session.” It’s not just semantics, either, but people’s expectations. And when you get a group of 3 or 4 or 12 musicians with differing expectations, it usually turns ugly.

Personally, I prefer the sense of session that means “mostly Irish traditional music, welcoming to anyone who plays the tunes.” I’d rather err on the side of being open and inclusive than exclusive. But I can see good reasons why a session might trend toward being by-invite-only while still retaining all other aspects of a session (not a concert or band show).

How a session functions depends a lot on where it is and what the local culture/community is like. In many places around the world, totally open sessions can be quickly overwhelmed by boom-chuck guitar players or percussionists of every stripe (including vagrants randomly banging cobblestones together), and people who have little to no experience or understanding of playing THIS music.

One suggestion made above was to sort this out with each person as they come in. But in some places, you’d spend all your time week after week chatting with a parade of the clueless about your session and why two hours of bluegrass back up aren’t welcome or the need to limit bodhrans to no more than 6 at time (I’m not making this up), and so you’d never get a chance to play the tunes.

That said, one of the best sessions I ever experienced included a merry band of Latin American musicians who were in town for a gig, happened to dine at the pub on the right night, and after politely asking if they could join in, turned the evening into a great ride of Irish tunes with Latin rhythms and Latin music with Irish grooves. For me, that sort of serendipity is reason enough to avoid “closing” a session.

Re: your opinions please: on posting a closed session

I’m sorry, but I’ve read the whole thread, and I still say there is no such thing as a closed session.

The difference between a session and a gig isn’t in who plays, or what they play. The difference is in WHY they play.

If you are playing to entertain punters, then you are a band playing a gig. In that situation you are quite entitled to dictate who does or doesn’t play, but it isn’t a session.

Sessions exist for the sake of the music, not the punters. To keep the tradition alive, to share new tunes, and above all to bring in new blood. And for that reason you CANNOT have a closed session. If you don’t welcome strangers, you won’t learn their tunes. If you don’t encourage learners, the tradition will die out. Yes, you may have to have rules about ‘one bodhran at a time’, explain a bit about etiquette, and maybe point some people towards the nearest slow session. But you have to welcome everyone who wants to play, otherwise it isn’t a session, it’s a gig.

I know of two closed sessions in town. One was originally set up as a closed session, and the other has become a closed session to deal with problems that have arisen.

In practice, I don’t think there are any truly ‘open’ sessions. Several people have suggested that in a true session anyone can participate, if they’re good enough. And that qualifier is the problem: players who think they are good enough when they actually aren’t, don’t know the tunes, can’t play at the speed of the other performers, and so on. I’m not a guitarist - if I learn the three-chord-trick in D would I be welcome at your open session? How often would you let me come and annoy everyone, perhaps driving away good players, before you indicated that I wasn’t welcome. As soon as you exclude anyone, the session isn’t really open anymore.

Another reason for closed sessions is to keep out people who may be decent players but are very unpleasant individuals. Again, you can let them in the session, but you will lose other players who tire of being insulted, or having their playing criticized, or whatever else the personality problem might be.

The session that has just become closed was on the verge of collapse because:
-a couple of musicians who played Tin Pan Alley songs started showing up regularly, and didn’t seem to notice that everyone else was playing Irish/Scottish tunes
-one tremendously unpleasant individual was keeping a number of competent, sociable individuals away
Making the session closed was the only way to keep it going.

In a perfect world, fully open sessions would be ideal, and in some areas they may actually work. I would be curious to know how to discourage people who really can’t play (but are convinced that they can) or who are really not wanted
