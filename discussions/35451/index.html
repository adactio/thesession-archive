↳ Posted by Boyen on January 25th, 2015.

Re: Should everyone play secondary instruments?

I do not consider it my place to tell people what they *should* do. I do, however, think that playing more than one instrument has some advantages. Each instrument, by way of its particular technical requirements, tonal characteristics etc., offers a different perspective on a given tune. The characteristics of one instrument has a bearing on the interpretation of a tune, which can then be carried across to another instrument. Thus, it can enable you to ‘play outside the box’, so to speak.

On the flipside, the more instruments you play, the less time you have to devote to any one of them. But, for some musicians, variety is more important than technical proficiency. For some, on the other hand, their devotion to one instrument is so complete that they have no desire to play anything else.

Should everyone play secondary instruments?

No, not everyone cares to play multiple instruments. It’s not necessary. You don’t have to play multiple instruments to understand music.

“Why do so many people play more than one instrument at a session?”

We all have two things in common. We like the music and we like the instruments. Chances are, multiple instruments catch a player’s ear. I for one can say that I truly and confidently adore the sounds of the Piano, Fiddle, Concertina, Smallpipes and Uilleann Pipes. Some of us just like their sounds so much that we have to learn how to play them. Music being a career of mine, I don’t have to worry much about time or money as much cause I’ll always have the resources I need to learn what I like.

I also have security in knowing that you don’t have to be a master at an instrument to enjoy playing it. I accomplished my goals for piano, and for fiddle, now I can learn something new, and different. Since another instrument caught my ear, I decided to take it up. It’s not like the other instruments will disappear. They get the time we make for them.

Is it generally regarded as better to play more than one?

I guess if you have a small session that lacks melody players, or a backer, it could be useful. But if you don’t have an ear for another instrument I don’t really see a point in learning another one. If you love one, and you’re great at one, nothing is wrong with playing just that one.

I don’t see anyone getting any special treatment for playing multiple instruments. I don’t often hear players ask other players if they play other instruments. The only time I was asked is when I asked one of the members if I could try a tune on his piano accordion. The leader said, “Do you play piano Jerone?”. But most times I don’t think any of us care, as long as they sound good, cool.

Are those who advocate restricting oneself to 2-3 instruments really so much better in those than the others are in their best 2-3 instruments?

Are the most stubborn “mono-instrumentalists” really better on their only (sad and lonely) instrument than “poly-instrumentalists” are on their first?

(Inspired by http://how-to-learn-any-language.com/forum/forum_posts.asp?TID=31200&PN=133&TPN=1#359788 )

Don’t misunderstand me - I’m not saying that one “should” pick up a second, a third etc. instrument, but many instruments are VERY similar, you’re likely to play more or less the SAME tunes (unless you deliberately choose separate repertoires), the tunes themselves are often quite straightforward rhythmically and melodically (nowhere close to say, bebop or classical music) - and there is no pressure as in perfecting the tune like it was a classical solo piece.

This makes it relatively “easy” to transfer a share of your repertoire to another instrument (even if they are unrelated - fiddle/accordion, flute/concertina etc.). You know the tune, you know how the other instrument “works”. In about every session I realize I’m playing a tune I’ve NEVER before tried on the particular instrument.

I will opine that she could in truth play along with nothing, likely, but it seemed to me she had embraced a level of mediocrity at all things box-ish, and was satisfied with “Okay” in all of them. The only problem with that is social - while she was having fun, were those she played with doing the same? Perhaps she needed a mentor/critic to help her assess her true position in the musical universe, and realize she was not always at the center?

I am much luckier - I have you guys.
;-)
