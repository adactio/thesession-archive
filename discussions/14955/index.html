↳ Posted by Reverend on August 29th, 2007.

Re: Everybody Deserves their solos!

When solos come naturally in a session, it is fine, as when the others take a break to go to the bar or the rest room, or someone stops to have a bite to eat. Or when some people know a tune and others don’t. But when you start arranging these things in advance, it does become more of a performance. All discussed above by iluvpints is OK for a band, or for a round robin singing circle, but not really a good approach for an instrumental session.
One time it was just me on the harmonica and a fiddle player, and as I often do during longer sets, I dropped out during 8 bars to regain my breath. When I came back in, he dropped out, and left ME alone for 8 bars. It ended up sounding kind of neat, so I see the appeal for this sort of stuff, but one can go to far, and lose the collective approach that makes a session what it is.

Wow, that’s a wide open topic that should almost be a thread of its own. (I actually started a similar thread about that some time ago, although, it was mostly about the factors that contribute to that wonderful feeling).

Anyway here are some things I find enjoyable about sessions:

* Spending time with friends

* Finding that ‘perfect groove’ where everybody is on the same page. The music is great, the people are great, the playing is elevated to a new level, and the feeling is that of euphoria.

* Laughing and joking in between sets

* The sharing and learning of tunes

* Meeting new people who share some of the same interests as I

* The “free flow” nature of a session. Not knowing what tune will ensue next, and being comfortable with the fact that it will all work out anyway.

and last but not least:

* The alcohol!

So that’s not *everything* I find enjoyable about sessions, but that’s a start :-D
