A Study In Playing Alone

I played violin (but did not own one) for a few years in my youth and was considered good for the level of instruction I had at the time. However, I stopped when I was 14, and haven’t touched the instrument for a few decades. Within the last month, I decided to play the fidil and bought myself my first instrument and some materials for learning and reference. I figured I’d start as a beginner and could not rely on what I could do so long ago, but time has not been as damaging as I thought it would be.

My focus for now is ITM, but I am interested in another fiddling tradition, which I may pursue later. But my goals are the same: I only want to practice and play for myself, not socially or commercially. I’ve been reading this site and its informative and interesting discussions and I noticed that as appropriate for a site called “The Session” the focus is on social playing, but there are also discussions of commercial recording and performances. But the enjoyment of purely personal playing alone seems to be almost foreign in discussions.

I think playing alone is not something to overlook or think is wrong for those who may want to do this and be dissuaded from the recommendations from fiddling resources and traditional and folk music discussions which stress otherwise as almost a necessity.

This is not to say anybody thinks it is unusual or wrong here, but the subject is often neglected and not the subject of discussions except for people lamenting having nobody to play with and the resources promoting social activities as a goal at the expense of playing alone. We may practice alone, but we can also play alone and enjoy it as much as anybody enjoys doing anything else.

To me, practicing is playing and I am the player and the audience, and that is what I like. I do not live totally alone, and I’m told I sound good (I think that must be qualified with the level of expectation of how I would sound) and nobody objects to my playing and I do not mind others listening if they are around, but I am still playing for myself. I also play like others may play video games or on their blasted smartphones: my fidil is on my desk and I picked it up and play it many times through the day, working to get better in some way, to play a song I like, to learn a new song, to experiment with a new technique, etc. I enjoy hearing and feeling myself improve, so my playing is not aimless.

Anything repeatedly done is practice, and makes one better and more efficient, and with this, perhaps in the future I may be really good if I live long enough, or perhaps I’ll plateau at some level for an extended time (although I think I will always be able to work on getting better intonation and speed, if nothing else). It does not matter. I’m just playing and I enjoy it and I think many may as well. Playing for one’s household is where folk music comes from and it would be a shame to let the commercial interests let this be forgotten. And one does not need to worry about licensing and contracts when doing this, so it is free in every sense of the word.

We can learn from the allegedly fictional person Sherlock Holmes, as recorded by the allegedly fictional Watson, his roommate, who when after living with him he recorded in *A Study In Scarlet*, second chapter:

"…10. Plays the violin well….

I see that I have alluded above to his powers upon the violin. These were very remarkable, but as eccentric as all his other accomplishments. That he could play pieces, and difficult pieces, I knew well, because at my request he has played me some of Mendelssohn’s Lieder, and other favourites. When left to himself, however, he would seldom produce any music or attempt any recognizing air. Leaning back in his arm-chair…he would close his eyes and scrape carelessly at the fiddle which was thrown across his knee. Sometimes the chords were sonorous and melancholy. Occasionally they were fantastic and cheerful. Clearly they reflected the thoughts which possessed him, but whether the music aided those thoughts, or whether the playing was simply the result of a whim or fancy, was more than I could determine. I might have rebelled against these exasperating solos had it not been that he usually terminated them by playing in quick succession a whole series of my favourite airs as a slight compensation for the trial upon my patience."

Or do people just play the best they can together with a consideration of some base level of ability and follow someone leading the tune and enjoy it?

Also, I do not understand why a person who has listened to music habitually for decades is assumed to suddenly lose all sense of musicality when listening to himself.

And if I want to play with a group, I can pick a recording I personally like, and play along with it. All those problems disappear that were mentioned, plus all the downsides of playing with others live.

One of the biggest challenges with this instrument is that perfection is impossible, and players have to spend a lot of time working to develop a playing ability that does not have obvious flaws. Think about it: to learn the fidil, one has to listen to major problems in oneself generally for years before one can play what one wants. For some forms of music, it takes over a decade of constantly practice and direction.

Think about it: a player that starts at 3, gets private lessons, practices 3-8 hours a day for 20 years, goes to conservatory for years and gets constant criticism and performance experience and master classes picking apart music that is being played with proficiency, is not happy with how they sound after all that.

I think folk music is a bit less stressful and nitpicking than that. If it isn’t, then I would have no interest in it. I’m happy now, and I’m happy when I improve, and I’m happy to know I can constantly improve. The tunes I play now are the “worst” I’ll sound and I’m happy to play them and I’ll be happy to hear myself play them better tomorrow.
