What to do when a session grows out of proportion

I’ve been joining a lovely session in the center of the Netherlands ever since it started. The first year, every time about 6 to 10 musicians would join, sometimes we would barely have enough melody players to fill the night but usually we would have a good time.

Then at some point of the last year the word got out that there was a session and then it suddenly exploded, first we went from one table to two tables so we could host the 10-16 people that would join. And then on some sessions 20+ musicians would show up, at our anniversary session I think we even had close to 30 musicians! Now, this is not a tiny pub by all means, but we’re blocking the path way as soon as more than ~12 musicians turns up. Moreso, you can’t really hear eachother at one point and all the intimacy and great things about a session kind of evaporate. That’s not to say that the session with close to 30 people wasn’t a sight to behold, it’s just not quite as nice.

At one point the hosts decided to make it a biweekly session instead of a monthly session. Sometimes this helps. There is a core of musicians who would pretty much join every session and then there are people who enjoy coming every now and then that makes it variable. If by chance these musicians all decide at the same day that it is a good day to join, then we easily go over 20 musicians again.

There’s also a division in skill level that I should mention. The people in the core are quite evenly spread in skill level ranging from true beginners to people that have been playing for years. For the people outside this core, they are usually amazing players that play (semi-)professionally and leave the rest of us awe-struck. It’s safe to say that when people from outside this core join, the average level of the session goes up quite a bit.

So the problem is, we really don’t want to turn anyone away. We have a loyal core of friends and these amazing players that come every now and then, but when you put those together you easily get an overcrowded session. Any of you had similar experiences and did you manage to find a solution to keep everyone satisfied?

Re: What to do when a session grows out of proportion

I was once a part of a floating session that would be at a different location, monthly. None of the locations were pubs, but that is immaterial to my suggestion. Since there was a rather large contingent, we split to different rooms by skill level. We had enough advanced players that the second level was still fairly high and I was satisfied to be in the second group, since I didn’t have the repertoire or skill to be in the first. For you, I would suggest doing the split on alternate nights. It would be nice if some of the fairly advanced players made themselves available to lead the second group to keep things together and help expand repertoire. There will need to be consensus that this is a good solution. It really benefits everyone involved. It is a drag for beginner/intermediate players to try to keep up or have to sit out when they don’t know tunes and it is even worse for advanced players to have to cater to the lowest common denominator at the session.

Re: What to do when a session grows out of proportion

It sounds like you have a “good problem” on your hands. Talk to the pub owner about making it a weekly session - my experience is that a weekly session spreads the people out more than a fortnightly or monthly - you’ll have your core people that come every week, but by-and-large people won’t be there every week.

Perhaps having an “Improvers” session on another night of the week also would be a suggestion - this gives a hint that people should subdivide on ability, and means the better players will gravitate towards one night, and the newer players towards another. You may get two working sessions from this, and the players may be happier. Alternatively, you could do “improvers lessons” on an alternative night - give the very experienced players the chance to teach and earn a small bit of money, and the newer players a chance to play in a more structured environment that allows and encourages them to play and make mistakes.
