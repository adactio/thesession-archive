Re: Here’s why you should never trust sheet music

I think Dick has a valid point here. Some participants here (and I’ve done this myself no few times) have a tendency to make fun of other’s opinions to the point that it no doubt hurts people’s feelings.

There’s a difference between slagging and sniping.

Sometimes we cross that line in the heat of the moment. Sometimes we do it because we “don’t suffer fools easily.” And sometimes we snipe because a relentless troll deserves it.

But there’s another side to this, too. And that’s the notion that people should participate here in good faith. I think that’s a fundamental part of “being civil.”

Good faith means not misrepresenting yourself, not repeatedly using fallacious rhetoric, worrying less about “winning” an argument and more about contributing to moving a conversation forward. Trolling is just the most egregious example of NOT participating in good faith.

Finally, I think it’s healthy when the discussions here challenge people to really consider their opinions. Sometimes we hold opinions that are uninformed, poorly supported, poorly reasoned. Part of the value of freedom of speech is to allow such opinions to be aired precisely so they can be questioned and, if found to be lacking in substance, dismissed. That’s how human progress happens. In short, not all opinions are equal--some can be invalidated because they don’t stand up to reasoned questioning.

So participating in good faith also means being reasonable and reasoned, and recognizing the limits of one’s own experience. Which is why YMMV (“your mileage may vary”) is such a common, courteous disclaimer in online forums.

In the end, it’s Jeremy’s decision who gets suspended or banned, and who stays. But repeated suspensions at least suggest a repeating behavior problem, eh? If someone doesn’t like Jeremy’s decisions, they’re free to find another site or create their own.

Re: Here’s why you should never trust sheet music

I think wygal hit a really crucial point in her post.

Any classically trained musician reads sheet music with a mind full of the classical conventions for timing, phrasing, and even pitch. When such a player picks up O’Neill’s or any other transcript of a trad tune, they’ll impose those classical conventions. As wyogal points out, most of those conventions don’t apply to Irish trad. So the music comes out “wrong.”

For anyone who played another genre a long time, the conventions they bring with them will likely be difficult to override in favor of what their ear tells them Irish trad should sound like. Looking at the dots while playing only reinforces the old baggage. Far better to lose the baggage and let your ear take over.

Once your ear fully understands what Irish trad sounds like, and you can play the music with Irish trad conventions, then it’s easy to take a piece of written music and make it sound “right” for this genre.

In the mean time, ***use your ears.***

Good on you wyogal.
