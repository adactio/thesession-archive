How Many & How Often?

What is the general consensus on how many times a tune is played and how many tunes are strung together in a set? From my experience, most tunes at sessions seem to be played 3 times (4 times for single reels such as Drowsie Maggie, Boyne Hunt, etc) while the number of tunes is normally 2 or 3.

Personally I think the 3 tunes, 3 times option works best as any less and you feel “short changed” while if the set is further extended it sometimes seems to lose energy - maybe it’s my age! I should also say that this doesn’t apply at Fleadhs and other similar events where the adrenalin levels (not to mention the Guinness) can keep musicians going almost indefinitely.

Any views would be welcomed.

Re: How Many & How Often?

As soon as I hear a tune, I can tell if I know it or don’t, and can also tell (only personal preference) whether I want to join in (I like it) or only want to listen (don’t like it).

1 or 2 times through if with musicians who can follow or have a good repertoire.

If it is the usual session musicians and they have heard the tunes before, if they should know it and don’t join in - once, if I think they should join in, twice, if they do join in, three or four times.
If I am teaching them a tune - once through, then half a dozen times slowly. Repeat after an hour.

If no-one joins in, I don’t see the point of repeating the tune - why not go on to another.

Re: How Many & How Often?

In Scottish sessions, the tendency is to play each tune twice. Sometimes 4 part tunes like pipe marches etc will only get played once. I think once is enough for some slow airs. They can last too long sometimes. However, if there’s no repeat in each part, it’s good to play them twice.

I like the Irish session habit of playing a lot of the shorter jigs and reels etc three or four times. It gives everybody a chance to “get into the groove”. You might well know the tune but you can have a sip of your Guinness the first time around and listen to the rhythm or any slight variations before joining in. You can sometimes even learn the tune by the third or fourth time , if it’s new to you. Also it’s good to experiment with the tune a little e.g play in another octave etc as long as it doesn’t detract from overall sound.

In Scottish sessions, I’ve known many sessions where each tune is played once only but I’m not very keen on this. This works better for performances and they are usually regular sets which certain players have been playing/practising for a long time.

Re: How Many & How Often?

The Scottish “once through” thing is great when dancing - you have a new tune for each measure of the dance, and if there’s the odd repeat of the occasional fine tune, it makes it all the better for being unexpected. Some tunes only really work well when turned back on themselves. Sessions: it’s nice having a few times through, as John says, ’cos what you think might be one tune in the first part can actually be quite a different tune when you realise what the second part is.

I like larger sets (I remember a great session somewhere near Galway where the lady called “make this the last one!” and it went on for an hour), but
having shorter sets means you can vary the tempo and/or type of tune more, which is good too. I don’t care actually, I just love sessions.

My problem is that if I’ve started a tune and I’m playing, and racking my brains trying to think of a good fit for the next tune, I often forget whether I’m on the first or second repetition (how hard can it be to count to three?!) and confuse the others by changing too early, or not changing when they’re expecting it!

Re: How Many & How Often?

"If you can’t play a tune at least five times, dont play it’. Why did Joe Burke say this? I’ve got his album with Charlie Lennon, and he doesn’t play tunes through five times!
As a rather flute player I like short sets. From an aesthetic, as opposed to asthmatic point of view, I think two or three tunes put together well can make a sensible piece music with a beginning, middle and end. Ten tunes in a row can be fun for players, but I don’t think it often makes for good music. However, that’s the very personal view of a rather opinionated old fogey.
I quite like sets of one tune, played many times. I have a Martin Byrnes album where he plays the AshPlant over and over about six times, with some variation, but without any flashy ‘taking the tune for a walk’, and it’s rather nice. Maybe that’s the way the music used to played before the recording industry started telling the likes of Mr Colman to string three tunes together in a row … ?

Re: How Many & How Often?

I was lucky to sit with some of the great players, (no name dropping) and they used to say play it as much and as often as you like.
In Liverpool the session at the Irish Centre late 70s, early 80s, and most of the other sessions, was x2, but I found places where it was x3, which as a standard format I like, and would say is more prevalent everywhere these days.
Anyone who knows me will know that if a tune is “cooking” we’ll go through it a dozen times easily!
As regards sets, way back in ’81 someone timed me at 1 & 1/2 hours for a set, and I was able to name every tune. I still practice that way, and 1/2 hour practice set (unplanned, just play what comes into mind) really opens up the lungs and gets the fingers moving. It also helps keep my repertoire open, I’m always afeared I might lose a tune!
Mind you that’s all the thorough discipline of tone practice, scale practice paying off, that’s so important, and still is an important part of how I approach playing. I don’t just do tunes. Tone and breath control for flute players is so important!
Hopefully you’ll play a tune as long as you love it!

Re: How Many & How Often?

Interesting tendency to standardisation here! Surely not…

I never thought there should be a limit to the number of times a tune is played and have felt, subjectively, that a session was really kicking when the mob got the atmosphere going with a tune which keeps going five or more times.

Last I was in Cork, the atmosphere was great in a session in the Sin E, and a plodding Pipe tune went into the Bucks which got played five times and then Rakish Paddy which got played seven or so times. The tunes sounded different each time, and the atmosphere heightened throughout the set even though they were such well known tunes.

Here in Dundee (Scotland) the local session musicians tends to play tunes three times, but Mike Ward (an excellent and widely respected fiddler who used to play with the great Scottish band the Tannahill Weaver’s) turns up and plays through the Jolly Tinker six times into the Boys of Malin five times or, for example, the drunken sailor (a six part version) four times into O’Dowds (a four part version) five times. Short reels like the glass of beer or toss the feathers rarely get played less than five times. Repeats always have subtle, not gross variation which is very effective and heightened by good backing!!

Plus, I remember reading stories about Joe Cooley playing tunes as many as twelve times…..
