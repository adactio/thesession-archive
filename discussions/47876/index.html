“Tunes” composed by AI

https://thesession.org/tunes/22743#setting46103

Yes, there’ve been discussions here about AI “tunes.” But posting one to the tune archive is a leap of another kind.

This alleged jig isn’t a jig, it’s not even a tune in any recognizable, Irish trad sense.

I can see this becoming a trend here, and that would be a shame. Not just posting kack tunes, but posting any soulless AI-generated alphabet posing as a tune—it’s antithetical to what this music is about.

One hallmark of traditional music is that it arises from a community of people. The tunes are an expression of what it means to be living in that community, either directly or as a participant at some remove but nonetheless immersed in the music, it’s history, and the circle of people who make it.

Unfortunately, a ban on adding AI “tunes” to the archive here would be difficult to enforce, as the poster could claim they themselves, or some other person, composed the tune. Likely such “tunes” would be Artless and Inept and so could be culled after the fact. Maybe some guidelines in the tune-posting menu against posting inhuman abc spaghetti would discourage it. Or maybe the bots and their enablers are bound to win regardless.

Should AI debris be allowed in the tune archive here? What do you think?

Re: “Tunes” composed by AI

Technology continually presents us with change in our world, and presents us with ethical and societal challenges.

With regards to AI tunes being posted here, I would just say that the same guidelines should apply. If a tune is played regularly in sessions, it should get posted, no matter what the original source was. Since people are encouraged to write something about the tunes they post, I would assume that they should mention if the tune was “composed” by an AI.

I think in general, those guidelines will keep the tune archives from being overrun by AI “written” tunes. But we’re also really early in the AI game still, and it’s hard to say what the world will look like in 5 years, or 10, or 20…

I spent some time thinking about whether it would be possible to try to spot tunes composed by AI programmatically (similar to the detectors that have been written to try to detect when text was generated by AI), and my conclusion was that while it might be possible, the AI is going to get better and better, to the point where it may be impossible to detect.

So we just need to rely on the time-tested, old fashioned way of having tunes spread within our tradition. If a tune is a good tune, people will play it, and the masses will determine how far it spreads rather organically. If AI generates an amazing tune, and people play it to the point that it spreads, I guess we don’t really need to care that it wasn’t created directly by a human.

Re: “Tunes” composed by AI

I respectfully disagree that an AI tune can have “merits,” at least within a traditional music community. As you say, Reverend, AI may someday be adept enough to generate a pleasing string of notes. But an Irish trad tune is so much more than that.

Here’s the story behind Ed Reavy’s tune, The Whistler of Rosslea, as recounted by his son Joe:

“Ed was to a fair once in Rosslea and heard this whistler. He had a whistling range that could easily match the register of a fiddle or flute. And he had all the embellishments of a great traditional player, too. He sold fish in the market places and charmed his customers with choice tunes—many of his own making.”

Likewise, there’s a story involving real people behind Reavy’s tune Hughie’s Cap:

“A favorite story of Ed’s tells of Hughie, an easygoing sort who had a fierce look when he wore a certain cap. Friends would take Hughie along whenever a fight was anticipated. One look from Hughie and his cap would silence the most quarrelsome bullies. One night, as it would happen, Hughie’s friends found themselves in the worst sort of mix and quickly looked for Hughie’s help. But Hughie, alas, had ventured out this time without his menacing capeen. ‘Oh God,’ one exclaimed, ‘somebody go fetch Hughie’s cap, or there’ll be hell to pay for all of us this night!’”

Imagine instead an AI rationale for a tune:

“Input: 6/8, 16 bars, D major.”
Or worse, some AI-generated babble that passes for a folksy story.

@Gobby “I have never believed that a thousand monkeys sat at typewriters for ANY amount of time could inevitably re-create a Shakespearean masterpiece”

It’s not a thousand monkeys, it’s an infinite number of monkeys for an infinite amount of time. The point of that idea is to convey a better understanding of probabilities and the concept of infinity. If the universe consisted of nothing but monkeys sitting at typewriters for the last 13 billion years, the odds of one of them typing a complete work of Shakespeare is very low, but is *not zero*. If you expand that out to an infinite amount of time, it then becomes basically inevitable (and there are mathematical proofs for this).

Applying that to this discussion, AI is given huge data sets to build its model of what something is. So it is not just randomly spitting things out (as in the case of the proverbial monkeys), it is guided. So the probability of an AI being able to “compose” a tune that we would recognize as a good tune is a lot higher than just random chance, assuming that it has been “trained” on good tunes.

However, the probabilities are lower that it would come up with a new musical phrase that is both unlike anything we’ve heard before and also something that evokes an emotional response in the listener (and sounds like an Irish tune). This is because it’s harder for it to come up with something completely different other than by random chance, and it has no capability to understand the emotional implications of a musical phrase. This is something that a human composer can do much more easily, and once a composer finds a turn that conveys an emotion, they can use it and build on it. Respected Irish composers (Fahey, Reavy, O’Brien, Keegan, Broderick, et al) seem to have regularly come up with phrases that we’ve never heard before, but immediately feel like Irish music and evoke emotional responses in the listener…

But even though the probabilities of an AI composing the next amazing tune are significantly lower, they’re *not zero*…

For example, we learn the difference between major and minor keys and apply that knowledge. It’s an intellectual process, not an emotional or “human” process. It is but one of many tools used to achieve a result. The genesis of the composition may have some emotional origin, but we use learned skills to get the result we want. AI uses the same process and thus can potentially come up with a better product or, in this case, tune. No writer’s block!
