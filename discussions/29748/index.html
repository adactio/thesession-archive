Am I hearing right?

A few weeks ago, I submitted this discussion

https://thesession.org/discussions/29296

which related to the purchasing of musical instruments and how much one should pay.

Anyway, earlier today I had occasion to visit a well known violin shop in Edinburgh to buy one or two accessories for my fiddle and, while I was there, I thought that I might try out some of the more expensive instruments.

However, none of these which ranged from £2000-£4000 sounded as good(to me) as my own fiddle which I had actually purchased from the same shop a few years ago for around £500. They all seemed to have an “echoey” sound whereas my own instrument was more mellow and actually felt easier to play…better action etc.
Now, I do realise that adjustments can be made to suit and I’m also probably used to the sound of my own instrument.
I’m also perfectly sure that the violins costing £3000 will be better instruments and capable of projecting the sound much better especially in the hands of good players.
However, as far as I’m concerned, I’m happier with what I’ve got and it actually sounds better to me.

Now, what I’m wondering is whether listeners might hear things a little differently, i.e. would the more expensive instrument sound better to them even if not to me? If so, should we consider this when choosing an instrument or just stick with our own feelings?

As I say, I realise there’s quite a few considerations to take into account, i.e. the set up of the instrument, type of strings, style and ability of playing, and just the familiarity with a regular instrument. However, as I’m unlikely to ever become a virtuoso classical violinist, I feel that I’d be as well sticking to what I’ve got.

Re: Am I hearing right?

Various frequencies from the fiddle come out from the instrument in all different directions. Some, particularly the high-pitched noise frequencies (esp. bow hiss), don’t project far but go straight into the player’s left ear. This is why it is so difficult for a violinist to hear his or her own sound as others hear it. There is also the other aspect, which you’ve got to learn to overcome, which is that your brain “hears” what you want it hear and edits its input accordingly. But that’s really a subject for a psychologist to take up elsewhere.

I now regularly put cotton-wool in my left ear when playing so that I can hear more clearly what is going on around me (important not only in sessions but in orchestra) and so that I know that what I’m hearing, mainly with my right ear, is closer to what other people are hearing from me.

I have in the past in my practice room – or “phrontisterion”, a wonderful Ancient Greek word for a “place for hard thinking”! – tried headphones linked to a mic about 10 feet away. It worked, after a fashion, but it coloured the sound, picked up extraneous noises, and was inconvenient in other respects, so I’ve rejected it in favour of the bud-in-the-ear approach. It really helps me concentrate.

Re: Am I hearing right?

Let’s forget all the subjective issues here for a moment … you can mull over these at your leisure in a few weeks time, if you buy something straight from a shop without a proper trial :)

A couple of things spring to mind here … you really can’t judge how good a violin is just by playing it in the shop, regardless of what it sounds like at the time. You need to take it away and play it at home, to be fair to yourself. You + the shop can negotiate on this, if you find one you really like.

Here’s the bit that bites - the 90% factor. Meaning, the sound comes mostly from the player. If you play a more expensive violin, and are really taken with the tone, and think it might work for you, negotiate and take it home.

OK, after a week of playing it at home, does it do what you want it to do? Can you get as good on your own violin?

Over to you :)

That’s how I got my (as near as perfect) violin.
