What! No Jazz?

Just imagine if we had all stayed put in our own countries over the last couple of millenia, instead of all trying to take over the world, what would ITM actually look like today?

Here are a few musical thoughts:
Just think, we would have no Irish/American music now, No Country Music, No Bluegrass, No Blues, No Jazz……..
No Cape Breton either.
No Liz Carroll!
We would not have the Coleman 78’s to cherish.
‘Jaff’ would be making Uilleann Pipes in the Glens of Antrim, instead of Oz.
I’d still be back home in Scotia.
Kerri wouldn’t be writing to us from Guatamala (:-))

On a basic level too, I wonder what instruments we would be using for ITM?
Even the fiddle wouldn’t have travelled to Ireland, so I guess the main instruments would be the Whistle & the Bodhran - after all, everything else was imported & then modified, wasn’t it?

Hey, look on the bright side though - No Banjos, Accordions or Piano Accordions though!

Perhaps Jews Harps & ‘Tin Whistle Hornpipes’ would be the BIG trad instruments by now, with clubs of their own.

Can you imagine the Dublin Jews Harp Club meetings, discussing the old players & listening to old 78s of the great old ‘twangers’ in action.

Willie Clancy might have ended up a legendary Jews Harp player instead.
*thinks* - Anyone ever heard ‘The Fox Hunt’ being played on a Jews Harp?
Which reminds me, when are they going to start Jews Harp classes in Miltown?

So:
Let’s try & keep this one nice & friendly, we don’t want any squabbling or name calling now do we?
So when the bell goes, come out - nice and friendly like, with flower chains round your necks, skipping & singing, with bells around your ankles & waving handkerchiefs of peace & love!

*DING*

OK you’ve pushed me, so I got out my crystal ball and gazed back into the far distant present and I predict we would also have been playing:

1) The Percussive Pipes - a pig’s bladder which is kept inflated with a goatskin foot pump and out of which a set of hollowed willow pipes protrude. It is played by beating it with a shillelagh to produce a rhythmic boom-squeal-boom-squeal. The virtuoso would vary the pitch of the squeal by the force of the hit and how near a given pipe the bladder is struck.

2) The Inflatolin - A pig’s bladder inflated to form a resonance chamber and attached to a pair of hinged sticks. A horse intestine string is tensioned between the sticks and played with a bow. The pitch is varied by opening or closing the sticks to adjust the tension.

3) The Harpsycord - A stag’s antler strung with strings of different length which are plucked by blunted fish-hooks on springs. The fish-hooks are actuated by pulling the cords (geddit?) to which they are attached. The antler is attached to a pig’s bladder for ressonance to make it heard above the Percussive Pipes (see above).

4) The RamORama - invented by Mr O’Rama from Dublin, it is a set of tuned ram’s horns arranged in a circle and attached to a turnable disk of bog oak. The disk has one hole in it. A pig’s bladder is inflated via a willow mouth piece and the instrument played by dialing the appropriate horn.

5) The BogOPhone - a set of tuned blocks of bog oak whalloped with a shillelagh but amplified with a … you guessed it… pig’s bladder resonator chamber.

6) A huge vertical horn with its mouth shaped like a beast’s head - that’s a real one the Ancient Brits used to scare excrement out of the Romans - and without a bladder in sight!

Or did I see these for sale on eBay recently? :)
