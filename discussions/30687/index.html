New and Unique approach to learn Session Music

Ceol Tracks have produced a new and unique approach to learning traditional tunes. The book teaches basic music theory in staff and ABC method. Has finger charts for whistle, accordion, concertina, fiddle, banjo, mandolin and open guitar chord chart. Each page displays a single tune clearly laid out in staff and ABC method. The tutor book comes with 3 CDs. CD 1 contains all 63 tunes played at learning pace. While CDs 2 and 3 plays the tunes with accompaniment 3 times over, at session pace. All CDs have a 2 bar count-in to give you time to play from the start of the tune. The tunes in the book can be downloaded individually if you don’t want to buy the book. Go to http://www.ceoltracks.com for more information. It’s the Irish music tutor you’ve being waiting for all your life.

Re: New and Unique approach to learn Session Music

Arguably, “session music” is a fairly recent phenomenon.

Surely, sessions were originally musicians, albeit coming from different circumstances perhaps, just getting together for a tune.

They didn’t play “session tunes” but just brought the tunes they happened to know and had already learned.

Even today, you will get a variety of people attending sessions who have experience in Irish and other music(Some may even be professional and/or gigging musicians) along with amateurs, enthusiasts including beginners.
They just play tunes they enjoy and the repertoire is “agreed” and developed in a “de facto” manner for that particular session rather than being prescribed by the leader or organisers(Although I’ve known some sessions to be strictly controlled like this!).

However, it’s not “session music” or “session tunes” but just music and tunes which happen to get played in the session.

Of course, the very well known tunes tend to be ubiquitous in many sessions but they were never necessarily intended to be “session tunes” as such.

So, what I’m trying to say here(rather incoherently, I admit) that we should be thinking along the lines of learning Irish music (Or Scottish etc whatever happens to be your preference) in general as opposed to “session tunes” rather than considering the latter to be a genre on its own.

“”“is this a session tutor, or a celidh band tutor?” As a learner are they significantly different ?"

As a learner, the difference would be in learning to create the intrinsic rhythm of a tune *within* the melody line, instead of playing a simple string of notes while following an outside rhythm reference. Which, in an actual Ceilidh band, would be far less mechanical than these samples.

Just my opinion, but I don’t think it’s helpful to start beginners with a flat mechanical rhythm, when you can just as easily introduce the actual rhythms of the music at the same time. It’s one way to keep beginners interested, as well as reducing the chance that they’ll end up like this performance (the infamous “Celtic Violinist” rendition of Drowsy Maggie):

http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7w65438PCGM

At some point you have to start learning how the rhythm pulse works in this music, so why not at the very start? I’ve known people who were still struggling with playing the notes, but had a great feel for the rhythm and were fun to play with. The opposite (note facility without a rhythm feel) is almost never a fun time, in my experience. I don’t think a rank beginner should have to wait a year or more to pick up on how the rhythms work, especially in this music.
