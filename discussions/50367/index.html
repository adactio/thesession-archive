Session etiquette for the bar patrons.

We had an odd situation at our weekly session last night. A regular patron of the bar, who is a lovely older man, and a big supporter of our sessions, misbehaved.

We had a guest fiddler who is a very accomplished classical player sitting in. The older gent was trying to talk with her as we started a tune, so she didn’t hear him. He got up and physically grabbed the end of her bow to get her attention while she was playing. I intervened by calling him out and telling him never to touch the musicians, but he got all defensive about what he had actually touched. He did back off, but no apology or acknowledgement of his inappropriate action.

It was toward the end of the session, and he had a few pints in him by that point. I don’t think he would have done this to a male player. I plan on having a talk with him at the next session. I don’t want his behavior to keep players from feeling safe and welcome at our sessions. How do you handle bad behavior by patrons, especially those that are very supportive in every other regard?

Re: Session etiquette for the bar patrons.

I play regularly in a pub that gets heavy tourist traffic and often gets very crowded. If you can think of it, I’ve seen it. Punters touching musicians, punters grabbing instruments, punters sitting on instruments, punters throwing things at musicians, punters deciding that the session is their own personal sing-song audience, tons of flash photography/videography directly in our faces, (absolutely awful), and a host of other issues.

Usually, being firm but not aggressive gets the job done, and keeps the rest of the crowd on your side. Once they’ve stopped or understood, being a bit conciliatory and recognizing that they didn’t mean to be rude usually gets even the perpetrator back on your side, and everyone’s fine. When people get aggressive back, it helps to have the rest of the crowd on your side, and I’ve had other punters back me up and help de-escalate. On the rare occasion we’ve had to go to the security/bar staff and had someone thrown out, but that’s a last resort. Usually, if the perpetrator is really put out they’re just stomp off eventually and that’s the end of that.

Re: Session etiquette for the bar patrons.

“How do you handle bad behavior by patrons, especially those that are very supportive in every other regard?“

Not in a session, but I’ve had to address this a couple of times in various work places, and 3 tips if helpful (but obviously personal opinion etc).

1). Focus on the behaviour/issue, not the person.
Don’t infer an issue with him as a person (even if justified) but highlight the exact behaviour, why it was inappropriate and why it needs to stop.

2). You can almost never predict the reasons why someone you don’t know does things…
In your example, it’s probably highly likely it was a gender specific thing, but I personally wouldn’t mention it as you don’t know, and that behaviour would have been as unacceptable to anyone.

3). It’s very cliche, but the “s*it sandwich” approach, I find, always works well for conveying any negative news.
Essentially bookend the bad bit with 2 pieces of good news/complimentary bits that make them realise they’re still valued at the session.

Re: Session etiquette for the bar patrons.

“How do you handle bad behavior by patrons, especially those that are very supportive in every other regard?“

First things first: Keep a cool head. In no case would I suggest harsh words. While you might get away with calling out a drunken old man in public, calling out a younger drunk might turn out differently for a lot of people. Or maybe the 35 year old grandson is within earshot.

I’ve had drunks fall or dance into me over the years. You can usually see drunks coming and mitigate the encounter. That’s something for a session player to learn.

Sometimes you can catch a drunk’s eye and shoo them away with the back of your hand or support them so they don’t tip into you before it’s too late. Sometimes they’re going to land in your lap anyway. It’s not good, but you’re in a house full of alcohol.

From your story, it sounds like you already intervened. Beyond what you did (risking escalation), if you, as session leader, really don’t want the patron there anymore because his behavior disrupts your sessions (but this event sounds more like a momentary nuisance), I think all you can do is inform the proprietor.
