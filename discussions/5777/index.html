Retired? Is it possible?

At a session after a festival I was at I met an old mate I hadn’t seen for a few years. This fella had been a very good flute player, in fact from a musical “Family”, but no amount of cajoling could get him to join in even for one set.
He said he’d retired.
Besides now having a respected post in academia, thus I guess no further need to “prove himself”, upon closer questionning he said stuff like there was too much bitching, people with no scope further than playing the music, general pettiness, big egos over no big deal. That kinda thing. And of course how right he is when I read some of the stuff posted here.
But didn’t he just like a small session, playing tunes for their own sake?
There was no convincing him. He wouldn’t be drawn. The Egos got him down. I can see where he’s coming from but I can’t buck the habit yet…..nor do I intend to. The only hobby I no longer do is rock climbing, but I wouldn’t mind some nice easy N.Wales V.Diffs (oxymoronically named!), but let’s kill this 2-month long lung infection first.

Re: Retired? Is it possible?

I can understand it, Danny. Sometimes when you’ve got lots of other stuff going on in your life, the music doesn’t seem all that important any more. And I think the London scene can be very competitive. It’s a fairly unique situation; you have hundreds of players crammed into a city where, if they’re reasonably mobile, they rub up against each other all the time. Tensions develop, people get territorial and the higher up the hierarchy people ascend, then the greater the tension and the greater the territoriality. (I imagine there’s some sort of ceiling which people eventually burst through and then they’re acknowledged as being as good as it gets and then they can relax; they no longer have to prove themselves and no-one sets out to test them … but that’s probably just a flight of fancy on my behalf. I bumped into Paddy Keenan in the loos the night before he did a gig in Hammersmith and he said he was bricking it because half of the people in the audience wouldn’t just be listening to his playing, they’d be micro-analysing it. On top of that there was a rake of his relatives in the audience and he had to be on the top of his game cos there were pipers among them who reckon they’re every bit as good as he himself is and he had to concede that they had a point!)

And then there’s the compromising over speed and styles and whether you want to be in the same pub as a bodhran player and whether yer man’s allowed to join in on guitar and then when he does it’s jaysis why did you let him into the circle? And who’s being paid to keep this session afloat? Yer man? Sure he couldn’t play to save his life. And so then there’s a breakaway that gets a session in the pub down the road and visitors to each are (subtly) warned to avoid the other session cos it’s too fast/too slow/full of culchies/full of pretentious gits who’d prefer to spend all night high-falooting rather than playing the tunes/unwelcoming to newcomers/rubbish cos they’d let any Tom, Dick or Harry into their midst.

So … yer wee head’s reevin’ with all this nonsense and you decide not to bother with the sessions for a bit. And then you realise that your playing relied on the crack to keep itself fresh. But neveretheless, you don’t want the bad that comes with the good. So you don’t play as much as you used to, even in the house and one day you notice that your instrument case is thick with dust and you open it up and, jaysis, you can barely get a note out of the yoke. So you close the case and a few months later, the coating of dust thicker, you shove it in the back of a cupboard …

Yeah, I can understand it, right enough! Shudder!!

Re: Retired? Is it possible?

I like the definition of an amateur as someone who keeps doing what they love even though the results are disappointing. But I can also understand, from personal experience, how wearing that can be. At least once a month I catch myself saying that after 30 years I’m no where near the musician I would like to be--I find shortcomings in my tone, timing, intonation, bag of chops, repertoire--you name it, I can identify a flaw. :o)

Thank goodness for those brief, fleeting moments of delusion when it all clicks and sounds just the way I imagine. You can build a session career on them. *grin*

That said, the minute you’re satisfied with your playing, you’re dead in the water--no more improvement. So it’s healthy to learn to live with the inner critic.

All of which may not have any bearing on why Danny’s acquaintance stopped playing. Sometimes I’m mindful of how the music is *always* there--tunes in my head, tunes I need to learn, a flute begging to be played every day so it stays “played in,” a session every Tuesday, another slow session another ceili, another gig…sometimes it feels like the 800 pound gorilla squatting on my real priorities--family, work, health.

And then I come back to reality and play a tune….
