Members’ Details

An important point was raised today in a thread (https://thesession.org/discussions/2557) by one of our more senior Members, which I think needs further discussion.

Many of us have probably felt a little uneasy (I know I have) when replying to someone’s posting and you realise you know nothing whatsoever about them, and there is nothing posted in their Details to give any hint. It’s a bit like talking in the dark to a stranger you can’t see, or getting anonymous email (which I bin, anyway).

In a live session, if a stranger turns up their sex and approximate age are immediately obvious; if they start playing everyone soon gets a fair assessment of the stranger’s expertise on that instrument. During the craic and over a beer you then learn a bit more about the stranger (and he about you and the others) - where he’s from, how along he’s been playing, perhaps whether he plays in a band somewhere, and other things. Possibly even his full name, although privacy in this respect should always be respected. And then he’s well on the way to being a welcome member of the session. This is how it should be; it’s all part of civilised social interaction.

Is it too much to ask that Members of this virtual session, *before* they post in a discussion or submit a tune, session details, etc, likewise give some basic information about themselves in their Details - something along the lines mentioned above, but of course nothing that could compromise their privacy if they so wished? It is useful to know whether you’re replying to someone who knows next to nothing about ITM or music (but who would like to) or someone who has been playing for 30 years and is currently leading a busy ceili band, or whether the Member is young or an adult.

Trevor

As usual, a happy medium is probably best where it comes to reacting with others around here. You have to take everything with a grain of salt online, yet we’ve found it’s also best to take most people at face value (or at least interact with them as if you do).

It’s also important to know that we have many, many, many lurkers, and many, many, many players who don’t know much about sessions or the culture of sessions (which is largely the culture of Ireland, although often layered with the mores and culture of wherever the session takes place). We also have players who have played this stuff since year dot. And of course everyone has strong opinions about everything, a great many of them directly opposed to other people’s strong opinions. When you put a bunch of people together like that, you’re going to have crossed lines and miscommunications and problems (not the least being that you shouldn’t mouth off or make jokes around here without you being very clear as to whether it’s a joke or not, because, no matter how ridiculous you thought you were being, someone will take it seriously and perhaps get themselves into hot water at some session somewhere following your “advice” which may or may not have come off the way you intended it to).

A few lines in your bio about what you play, how long you’ve played, and your home culture/country will help to smooth some of those bumps out. Like it or not, one tends to treat beginners differently from intermediate players and expert players differently from intermediates, and there’s some good reasons for that. Certainly I’ll be more careful to explain things to someone who hasn’t the experience in sessions or with this stuff to know how things work than I am to someone who has played for long enough to know better. I’ll give more weight to the words of an experienced player; conversely, I’ll be somewhat more forgiving of the beginner giving out to those who have been playing longer. I might use a bit of shorthand patois or a certain sort of joke for someone who has played for years and years that I know a beginner wouldn’t really “get”. I’d slag a person from Ireland much earlier on than someone from the States, for instance, knowing they’ll generally take it for teasing rather than an insult, more or less, whereas Americans need more time to get to know you before you can slag them indescriminately, if ever. And, for a beginner who honestly needs help, I’ll take the time and the heat for explaining some of the more unpleasant aspects of playing at sessions where I won’t bother for someone who should know better or who presents themselves as someone who should know better..

Conversely, an experienced player who doesn’t put anything in their bio and then comes out with something weird is going to deserve the lowering of estimation that they will receive rather than an interested hearing of why their experience gives them impetus to go against the flow.

This is a global community, and a rather small one, you can usually find someone who knows the person you want to know about, nickname or no, unless they play at home by themselves all the time and never get out to play. Don’t think people don’t check, either, because we do. So I’d also think carefully about misrepresenting oneself in a bio here…
