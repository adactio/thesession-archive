Re: Resources for learning how to accompany tunes/songs with a melody instrument?

It just takes practice, but a fair amount of it! Chris Smith’s “Celtic Backup for All Instrumentalists” describes several a formal exercise similar to what Ian describes: record the melody on an instrument several times through, or loop the recording, and progress through alternatives of backup styles -- drones on the tonal center, rhythmic punctuation, arpeggiation, root/fifths, more or less chord motion, walking basses, all kinds of options. Definitely start with simple melodies you know REALLY well!

You can even loop the MIDI on this site now -- just click the repeat checkbox after clicking “play,” slow it down to a comfortable level, and experiment.

Example, to develop your ear for harmony: loop the Kesh. Start with droning on a G for a significant amount of time, and listen for where the melody clashes with it -- that’s a cue that notes from the other primary chords of the key of G would be good options (C and D major, E or A minor).

Then try playing a B or D when the melody is on a G, rather than the G itself. You’ll be on your way to improvising countermelody. But it takes concentrated practice and careful listening to what works, and to what other instruments are doing around you! In ECD/SCD, you’ll definitely want to listen closely to the pianist if you’re playing with one. And perhaps consider checking out the book “Interview with a Vamper” which focuses on ECD and Contra accompaniment.

I adore the sound of harmonizing when done right. If you can maintain balance with the singer or other melody players, and not overpower them, harmonizing with the melody can really give some dynamic personality and color to the music.

One thing that I like to do is simply practice harmonizing. If you can find an anchor note, you can develop the corresponding melody line from there. Give yourself a chance and try not to get frustrated if a harmony doesn’t come to you right away. I find that they come eventually as you develop the melody line. Sometimes very slowly. It takes practice, and some music can be trickier than others, but I’ve found it to be something I do a lot recreationally nowadays because I’ve had so much fun doing it.

Re: Resources for learning how to accompany tunes/songs with a melody instrument?

When accompanying a singer, the least intrusive method is to play in the gaps. Usually the singer sings a line and then there is a gap and then another line and so on. A good first strategy is to echo the line, or part of the line, the singer has just sung. Of course if the chord has changed you adapt to not conflict with the chord.

However you are talking about tunes and Irish tunes are not famous for their gaps. Lots of tunes do break down into four small sections and playing the first section when the main tune has moved onto the second might work, a bit like a round or cannon.

A different strategy is to play a high harmony. Two scale notes above the melody note is a good place to start. Again, you might have to adjust one or two to fit chords that may or may not exist under the tune.

The big rule is, if it sounds good it’s OK and if it doesn’t then chage it.

Unless you have a very thick skin you shouldn’t try to learn this in a session. It has to be a woodshed thing.

Re: Resources for learning how to accompany tunes/songs with a melody instrument?

For a couple years I made my living doing Studio work in Hollywood.

It was very common for them to want the Uilleann pipes in the mix just to add some texture.

I had to get good at reading Lead Sheets and using the information to improvise harmony parts.

A Lead Sheet, if you’re not familiar, has the melody line and the chord symbols.

It’s not rocket science! You only need to know how to think of every other letter, that A means A C# E and Bm means B D F# and so forth.

The chord symbol tells you the three notes you may play. The melody line tells you the note you must not play (you don’t want to “step on” the singer, especially with the pipes!)

So you only have two choices, if you’re staying within ordinary harmony, which is what they usually want.

About counter-melodies, I’ve mentioned before the arranging device of coming up with a harmony part for a song which is melodic enough to be perceived by the audience as a standalone tune, to be used as the intro and break.

Then in the verse immediately following the break the melody instrument repeats the counter-melody, the melody and counter-melody now being heard together for the first time. It’s quite effective both in vocal song and in instrumental pieces.

Re: Resources for learning how to accompany tunes/songs with a melody instrument?

I think it’s fair to say that this sort of thing is more common in other styles - and as such may be better learned from a study of them.

I think that Scandinavian fiddle music, for example, has a great tradition of close harmony, where two fiddles, or fiddle and accordion, etc. will play through the melody, usually following the same rhythmic phrasing, but introducing fluid harmonic context. I have learned a lot from listening to this stuff, as it shows you a pretty good principal, but also demonstrates that just going a third above the melody often will run you into a brick wall, and at certain points you are often have to make more dynamic choices.

Alternately, klezmer has a big tradition of what is called “Sekunde”, which usually involves a single line instrument, playing some kind of rhythmic counterpoint, often improvised, or even a full on counter melody in places. This is often done on sustaining instruments like bowed strings, trombone, or tenor sax, but a lot seems like an adaptation of what the Tsimbl (hammered dulcimer) would’ve done in a more traditional accompanying role.

I hope any of this is helpful - sometimes even sitting at a keyboard, playing the notes of a major scale and trying to harmonize them with notes from a I or V chord is a useful way of developing your ear for this kind of thing.
