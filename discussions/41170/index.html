My weekly session

Before you read any further I’ll tell you about my session if you are willing to reciprocate. Fair play?

Recently I have posted about the challenges and the rewards I experience playing in a session.

Here are the rewards;

I love the company of my session mates. We meet, play, banter, swap tunes (& stories), share too much and never enough about everything under the sun. My session has a wide range abilities and musical experience. This lot constantly surprises me (which is only sometimes rewarding). There is not one single person in the session who I have not learnt something from; I appreciate each of us for her or his individual contribution. Some have given me a greater appreciation for music and I savour & explore that as best I am able. Others have contributed in non-musical ways and I thank them when I recognise the value of what they have to offer.

A few challenges;

It’s not a tell-all account. This is only the OP and I shouldn’t spill everything until a few of you have reciprocated. The more you show me yours; the more I’ll show mine.

Here are a few tidbits from my session. Sheet music is allowed. We have a bass player (amplified). Last night there were 2 bodhran players (although not always playing at the same time). I suck (not totally, but more than enough to irritate sensitive folks). Ocassionally someone will play through a tune even though he does not know it (notice I said he, I believe most of the women in our group would not do this as freely as the men)… That’s all. Probably more than I should have written.

I look forward to your responses. I hope I have been civil-minded in putting this discussion up on the board.

Cheers,
Ben

Re: My weekly session

Let me preface mine by saying a couple things. First, everybody has been incredibly awesome. Second, even though I don’t agree with some local ideas of what constitutes an Irish session, I am grateful to have an opportunity to play with others. Third, this is a new session, and only monthly, so it’s got a lot of settling-in to do.

AB, ours is a lot like yours. Mixed levels, mixed repertoire, sheet music, all instruments welcome. Ours is held in a violin shop that is friendly toward fiddlers, so no drinks. No crowd noise either, so it’s fair. Kids are welcome, and we’ve had a couple of them play a few tunes. Things I like. Some of the attendees have a cheery joyful outlook on the music and the gathering. Made me realize I am way too serious at times. Finding tunes in common, even dusting off old favorites that I’ve forgotten about. Watching violinists learn about fiddle playing. Songs. I love songs. One lady sings us a song or two every time. I don’t care if it’s Fields of Athenry or Black Velvet Band. I love it all. One night she closed with a miserable dirge of a song, and it was the highlight of my night.

There have been some struggles with people playing too fast for the group, and non-Irish repertoire, and I just might be the only player who uses rolls and cuts, but overall this has been a good experience.

Edit: this area has a heavy fiddle bias, and it’s at a violin shop, so most players are obv fiddlers. We have bodhran and a couple whistles. Last time we had small group. One flute, two whistles, one fiddle.

Re: My weekly session

Fiddler George Rubsamen and I have been co-hosting the Tuesday night session at The Ould Sod here in San Diego since January of 2000. It’s a small dive-ish bar in the Normal Heights district a few miles north and east of downtown San Diego. We have about 20 or so local players who might show up on any night, with about 10 regulars, including George and I who come most weeks. The space we play in is quite small, we really only have seats for about 8 people so sometimes we swap out players, but occasionally, as happened last week, we had many guests and as many as 16 players, which was quite crowded. Fiddles seem to be in vogue right now, but in the past when Ben Power was living in San Diego, we’d have a lot of flute players as well. I generally play Uilleann pipes/whistle, concertina, and some B/C box. We have some “early” players, as Noel Hill would call them, who are just starting out, and we encourage them to come for the first hour of the session to play the tunes they know at their pace, and when the more experienced regulars show up around 8 or 9, they know to give up their seats and listen for the rest of the evening. It’s not a paid hosting, but the pub has always provided all of the musician’s drinks for free, a privilege we don’t abuse, and we generally give whatever tips we get from the patrons to the bartender. The music later in the evening is at a pretty high level, and for some unknown reason, jigs seem to dominate over reels in tune frequency compared to other sessions I co-host or regularly participate in. One down side is that because it’s a bar, you have to be 21 to even go inside, so we can’t include any younger players, which was possible at some of the other sessions that were at restaurant/bars in San Diego in the past. The session is every Tuesday from 7-11 PM, and we started doing a more relaxed session on the 1st and 3rd Sundays in the covered part of the new beer garden they built last year behind the pub. Visitors are always welcome, just give me a heads-up that you’ll be in town and we’ll take very good care of you, give you a ride to/from the pub, setup house sessions, etc.

Re: My weekly session

We have a large, albeit cliquish, Irish music scene around Denver. There’s somewhere between 15 and 20 regular sessions along the Front Range (from Ft. Collins to Colorado Springs). Some are monthly or bi-weekly, but most are weekly, and most of them are in pubs or restaurants. The sessions come and go - in fact, we had a situation a couple months ago where two of the longest running sessions lost their homes about a week apart, because both businesses closed their doors (for different reasons). Thankfully, both of those sessions have found new homes.

I founded and anchor two of the weekly sessions.

Sunday nights at Burns Pub is our “higher powered” session. Most of us have been playing together for 15+ years, and the core group of people have moved the session around when pubs decided they didn’t want us there anymore. But at Burns Pub, we have finally found a home that loves having us. The pub was not originally open on Sundays, but I talked them into giving us a shot, and they loved it. It was a money loser for them for some time, but over the past 5 years, we’ve developed a regular following of punters, and it’s now their busiest night - almost always packed to the gills with an appreciative crowd. (Which isn’t too hard, because the main room in the pub seats about 35 including musicians). But the fact that we’re mostly experienced players who have been playing together for a long time means that we’ve developed a reputation as the snobby session (when, in fact, we’re quite friendly as long as you play the music the way that we like - ok, that sounds snobby even to me… ;-) ) The reality of it is that we play a fair number of pretty obscure tunes, and we tend to play faster than most of the local sessions. And we’ve played with each other long enough that we generally know each other’s repertoire. So we tend to make up sets on the fly, and (sometimes) call out the tunes as we’re going. A person who was trying to use sheet music would not be able to handle that, because by the time they had found the music for a tune, we’d likely be on to the next tune. We’ve also had the joy of having dancing there fairly regularly. There are a couple of champion dancers in town that also play the music, and it’s a blast when they come. The crowd really responds to the dancing! But even when the dancers aren’t there, we often have people dancing to a set or two, or at the very least, little kids who can’t help but dance to the music.

My Wednesday session is at Westminster Brewing Company (one of around 300 breweries in the state). It’s a brew pub, not an Irish pub (although, they do a nice Irish Red, amongst their fantastic portfolio). Their brewmaster saw us playing at Burns Pub and offered us a night at the brewery. My goal for this session was to make it much more open and friendly to players of all skill levels - a place where my students could ‘get their feet wet’ in the session scene. And we’ve developed a nice group of regulars that keep things going. The thing I like the most about this session is that people bring up a lot of tunes that don’t generally get played at my Sunday session, and I really like the variety. But we do also try to hold the session to the standards of a fairly traditional Irish session, without too much in the way of non-Irish tunes or non-traditional instruments. There have been a few people that found that they didn’t fit in because of that. We occasionally have dancing at this session too!

As I stated in my profile, if you’re ever coming through Denver, give me a shout - I’d be happy to help you find some music!

Re: My weekly session

In my week, I go to one large, open, session in a local pub, and two small, private, “invite only” sessions, as well as a band gig. They all have the following in common:
-no sheet music
-all Irish traditional music and instruments that fit within that tradition

The large session can have anywhere between 6–26 players in attendance. The pub is very big and open, and has a strong following of punters as well. This session has been going for something like 19 years (I’ve only been going for around 6 or 7). There is a bar tab for the musicians. This session is usually very fiddle heavy, lots of fiddles, mandos, guitars, whistles, flute, a few bouzoukis, occasionally pipes, and banjo. A number of multi-instrumentalists in this group, so usually not more than one whistle/guitar at a time. We have a few singers, but they respectfully wait for slow points, and never interrupt a good flow of tunes, (maybe 3-4 songs, in the three hours we are there). We have a variety of ability, and it’s first come first serve for seating, which awkwardly, at times, ends up leaving the best players back a row or two, and those who should offer to give up their seats refuse. Even so, this is a great, fun session, and I look forward to it every week.

I play in a smaller, private session that ranges from 4–7. This one is also fairly fiddle heavy, but almost everyone plays two instruments, and attempts to keep things varied. We have fiddle, whistle, flute, guitar, bouzouki, occasionally banjo, and very rarely piano. This one is probably the most fun. Plenty of conversation, jokes, whiskey, and tunes. And we all share tunes that are new to the group, and there is quite a push to learn new repertoire. (Some of the tunes I don’t care for, and won’t waste time learning though.)

I also play about once a week, with two flute players. I love this session too, because of the repertoire of tunes, and the friendships. I have the most similar taste in tunes, I think, with these two. Although, one is moving away and it will only be two of us. Not sure if that qualifies as a session?

Additionally, for the last two years I have been playing a gig in another pub. There is a core, who’s attendance varies, and special guests, usually 3-6 of us. This is set up as a performance, and we’re on a small stage, but it isn’t paid (tips only), and it essentially works like a session, and is very casual. We get free drinks, and dinner, and although we have attempted to map out regular sets, it always ends up being totally on the fly, and no one ever really knows what tune is coming next until it happens. We could have fiddle, flute, whistle, piano accordion, guitar, bodhran, bouzouki, mandolin, and occasionally concertina or pipes, depending on who might be passing through town, or who is invited to sit in. We have occasional songs in this one too.

Between all of these, I’m feeling incredibly satisfied, and lucky. The tedium of my day job is quickly forgotten. I know nothing lasts forever, but right now life is grand, and I wish it could last forever!

Re: My weekly session

I can testify that the session scene in Colorado is pretty good. I first encountered it 30 years ago when there were only about 2, maybe 3 sessions, and I noticed that it had absolutely exploded when I returned a couple of years ago. Riverdance fallout, is my theory. (Sort of like Starbucks pioneering good coffee in the US).

I will contradict John (reverend), only on one point. The session scene is not as “cliquish” as he suggests, and I report that from my experiences as a “mere intermediate” who has dropped in on relatively advanced sessions (such as the Wednesday Burns). Certainly sessions have their own repertoire, and the Burns & Boulder sessions in particular have a lot of tunes specific to them, as you would expect from people who have been playing together for 30 years.

Also I think the more advanced session players have consciously made an effort to avoid cliquish or elitist behaviors. Or, alternatively, they have enough maturity or self-confidence that they don’t need to prove anything or put on airs.

Crazy session census: 9 flutists at the Boulder session last week. Also, only one bodhran, one backup guitar (two melody steel/resonator guitars) and one backup bouzouki. But, also a harp, couple of banjos, couple of boxes, and a typical number of fiddles. Couple of whistle players, but they stuck mainly to flute or their other main instruments.

Re: My weekly session

I go to quite a few musical things in a month, but only 2 of them I would describe as sessions.

The rest of them are sing-around play-around type things, more like an open-mic, but everyone is welcome to join in.

I run one of the two sessions I go to, at the Allied Arms in Reading.

It’s mixed in terms of styles of tunes, but there is always a bit of ITM, thanks mostly to me and a super top whistle and pipe player who comes to it quite often.

The original session they had there stopped, don’t quite know why.

I organised it with help from a friend, because the QueenVic session stopped and I wanted something close, that was easy for people to get to who don’t drive.

The QueenVic session was a couple of miles away from the nearest station, and the walk was pretty tricky for me as a VI person. So that meant a taxi and all the fun that involves!

Anyways the Allied session is usually in the front bar, but if that is ever booked out we are put in the main bar.

People are welcome to come and listen and most weeks we have a couple of folks who pop in.

Some weeks we get lots of people and have dancing, but it’s usually drunk people leaping about :D

This excites my Guide Dog Archie, so he has a little dance as well if I let him!

It’s usually mostly tunes, but it depends on who is there.

I try to monitor the vibe on the night and taler it accordingly.

Some times we only have 4 people there, sometimes it’s 14 + listeners, which is a real squeeze in that bar!

No free beer, but the pub staff are very good to us and sometimes people who come to listen request tunes and buy us drinks :)

The Lands End session is at a pub near Twyford.

I usually get a lift to and from the station with the organiser.

This is mostly people who play for morriss sides, but it’s very friendly and anything goes. It’s only tunes though.

I like this one because we are in a large room, the drinks are good and no dogs apart from mine.

I usually am fine with dog friendly places, but at this session’s former home a dog was allowed to run lose and went for Archie :(

That’s about it for now, until I join a new session!
