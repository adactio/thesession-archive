On a personal level, I’ll always remember one morning in the 90s when I called by the house of a friend on my way to work. My friend was a gigging singer, and when I arrived, he was still in his “pit” from a late night. The postie had left a postcard stuck on the outside of the door. I just wonder how many of the world renowned singers would make the time to send a card across the Atlantic to someone they didn’t know, and taking the time to find gigs for them.

Re: Pete Seeger 1919-2014

I assume that the major media will attempt to “neuter” Pete, as we saw happen to Nelson Mandela. He will be presented, like Mandela, as a kindly old gent, a loving grandfather, a simple soul, an inoffensive folk-singing do-gooder. But Pete was much more than that.
Pete was a sincere Communist at one point in his life. He was a life-long friend and colleague of such people as Gus Hall, Paul Robeson, Bettina Aptheker, Woody Guthrie, Leonard Peltier and many, many more people of similar militant beliefs. He remained committed to social justice, redistribution of wealth, civil rights, feminist rights, nuclear disarmament, unionism, anti-capitalism, world peace, the green revolution, the Occupy movement. He absolutely refused to compromise his principles.
He joined the Young Communist League (YCL), when he was 17. I joined it at the same age. In 1942 Pete became a member of the Communist Party USA (CPUSA). He eventually “drifted away” (his words) from the Party in the late 1940s and 1950s.
For most Americans the thought of a humanized communist is inconceivable. Communists that I knew were far more idealistic and sensitive to injustice than the average citizen. In the more civilized countries of the world being a Communist does not mean that a person is a Stalinist or that a person believes in violent revolution. Those of us who love Pete should keep in mind that in the 30s and 40s the Communist Party formed the vanguard for social change that resulted in Civil Rights, unionism, social programs, and the anti-war movement.
True, he was not a Communist when he died. But it is also clear that he supported “the Communist agenda.” He, like Woody Guthrie, was a singer who cannot be separated from left-wing activism. Pete embodied those ideals so well that we tend to forget how radical he really was.

Re: Pete Seeger 1919-2014

Like many great men, Mr. Seeger was also a complex man. He never claimed to be the most talented of musicians, but he used what talent he had with tremendous power and drive. He was a generous person, always looking out for the little guy. He showed personal bravery during the blacklist era, standing for his ideals in front of congressional committees that were running amok. And he was a loud voice for environmental causes, in a time when those voices were needed.
But, like many idealists of his time, he found it easier to find fault with his own country than he did finding fault in the communist countries, the trap of ‘familiarity breeds contempt.’ He did, however, come around on that issue when it became more clear that these countries were not living up to their professed ideals, but instead trapped the little guy in a hideous totalitarian state.
In the balance, I think he did far more good than harm. And even when he made mistakes, I think his intentions were good. But in the end, it will be history that judges him, just as it judges us all. I hope he rests in peace.

Re: Pete Seeger 1919-2014

It’s unfortunately predictable that whenever Communism is mentioned, especially among Americans, the names Stalin and Mao are almost immediately brought up. You don’t often hear the words “Scottsboro Boys” or Sharecroppers’ Union“ etc. Anyone unfamiliar with what the Communist Party meant to people in the Deep South during the depression would benefit from reading ”All God’s Dangers: The Life of Nate Shaw (Ned Cobb), by Theodore Rosengarten, or “Hammer and Hoe: Alabama Communists During the Great Depression ”, by Robin Kelley. Read up on the Scottsboro Boys, and how the American Communist Party brought in a Romanian Jewish Democrat, Samuel Leibowitz, to act for the defence, exposing deep prejudice within the judicial system. This was the Communist party that attracted Pete Seeger. Not the totalitarian leaders drunk with power. It’s all too easy to point the finger with hindsight. That Communism has failed in so many ways in the way it has been implemented and exploited by national leaders doesn’t detract from the fact that, in the days of Pete Seeger’s youth, it represented a fight against the way he saw his country developing. Pete’s ideologies grew up with him. To paint him as a naïve idealist is showing one’s own naïvety.
I deliberately omitted “RIP” from my opening post, because (IMO) Seeger’s spirit will not rest, and will live on as long as there is injustice and inequality in the world. There are people still living like Pete’s long time friend Tommy Sands, who will continue along the same path. Trying within their own capacity to make the world a better place to live in.

That’s me off my soapbox now. Time for some music.
