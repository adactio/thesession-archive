muso or non-muso?

I’m talking about long-term relationships here. I know this has been covered intermittently, but I"m dredging it back up.

The argument for the muso/muso compatibility is obvious. Or is it? Some famous success stories, heated passion, some very famous, infamous & not-so-famous interpersonal disasters….

The argument for non-muso is more subtle. The stable support staff, stays home with kids on session night, holds down stable job, feeds dog, inspires & sustains. Or one-way ticket to jealousy & lack of common interest, assuming the muso lives & breathes the stuff?

What do you think? I know you can’t make sweeping generalizations, I’m just fishing for war stories. Go figure.

Re: muso or non-muso?

Em:

I and spousal unit, both musos, are at 22 years and counting. We met playing music and “hooked up” (as the youngsters say) musically before we ever did physically, and have played together, off-and-on, formally and informally, touring internationally and bashing it out in the session, ever since.

Playing music with *anybody* for decades--regardless of whether you’re sleeping together or not--is a unique and profoundly joyful thing.

That said, and assuming--a big assumption!--that the relationship would be healthy and valued even if music were no part of it, make damned sure that you

(a) find additional playing situations in which spousal unit is not involved (nothing is worse for other players than to have the two who are a couple bring their relationship battles into the music, says he who’s been guilty of same)

and

(b) seek, develop, and maintain ways of furthering the relationship that have nothing to do with music--don’t make it a substitute for all the hard psycho-emotional work a LTR needs.

$0.02

chris smith

Re: muso or non-muso?

I’d find it hard to contemplate a relationship with someone who wasn’t interested in the music. I fear that they would come between me and it. That’s not to say I wouldn’t try - but they’d have to understand how important it is, and preferably have some sort of equivalent in their own life.

I did live with a muso for 7 years, which had good points and bad points. He encouraged me to sing, but on the other hand, I was over-influenced by his taste and judgements. I was floating around the periphery of Irish music, but he pulled me away from it, because he, after a decade of immersion in it, had grown discontented and cynical about it and had a ‘been there, done that’ attitude. Since our taste for Eastern European music coincided, that’s the direction I got swept into. I should say in my defence that he was a brilliant player and a professional musician, as well as a lot older than me - I didn’t have a firm notion of what I was doing, and it’s not surprising that I was strongly influenced.

Then after that, I lived with a punk-rock bass player - we met because I was in a band myself at that point. Since punk-rock was the principle thing we had in common, you can guess what musical avenue I followed while we were together…

It’s in the last few years, since I’ve been single, that I’ve found my path - which led straight back to Irish music. I feel a little as though I was weighted down by circumstance; and then when the weights were removed, I bobbed up to the surface. And reaching the bright sunlight of the surface, ladies and gentlemen, I found myself in the glorious world of Irish music, and now I’ve got here, I’m damn well staying… ;-)

If I’d been more focussed in the beginning, or discovered sean nos earlier, I’m sure I would have meandered less. But in my case, it took a lot of wandering to get to this point. So the last thing I want is to fall for someone who’s going, even unwittingly, however subtly, to draw me away from it.

BUT, there’s one great drawback. I’m sure I won’t be the only person here to reflect that, if you’re looking for a stable, sensible, reliable man, ‘musician’ is a profession that might be low on your list. Especially ‘musician who spends most of his time in pubs’. You see where I’m coming from, here… sigh…

Re: muso or non-muso?

Tish, that’s a spot on (though brief) taxonomy of troublesome relationships. Gigging for money as an amateur demands almost as much work (or more if you’re the insecure type) as being a full-monty professional, yet the financial return is too skimpy to outweigh the time and passion invested. It’s one thing if you can buy your darling the car of his/her dreams when your global concert tour sells out and the cd goes platinum, but passing the hat to 15 college kids in a coffeehouse ain’t gonna make that happen.

A session (or maybe 2) a week, and no gigs (or maybe two or three a year, and only local ones) is a much more sustainable schedule, and it’s understood as a hobby, which means you as the muso know you have to miss a few sessions for the kids birthdays, a heavy homework night, fambly time, when the dishwasher leaks, etc.

I gotta say, it’s great to be able to set aside the instrument when something more important (heresy!) comes up. It’s nice to have the flexibility that comes with being an amateur and a generalist in life, rather than a specialist.
