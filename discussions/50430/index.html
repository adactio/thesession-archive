tune generation by the mangle

Having been a street fiddler with zero imagination, I’ve thought a bit over the years how to generate and select novelty.

First idea (1980’s) is to match jig and slip jig pairs of triplets. Given triplet A, find all the tunes that have it and choose a following triplet B from one of them, then examine BC matches, and so on. Try forcing the match to the same position in the bar at first, and relax. Could it work for reels etc? That would be scarier.

Idea 2 (just now) is to put canon-style permutations on a tune - one and two voices, crab, mobius, what have you.

Idea 3 is to train something neural to compose. Mamba-based nets sound hot irl.

If you keep hitting random keys on a typewriter eventually you will write a poem, but it will take a very long time and you probably won’t recognise the poem when it emerges. It’s the same for tunes. If you feel you have no creativity then a far more productive way to use your time would be to concentrate on developing creativity. Noodle about on your instrument until you hear a short phrase you like (it could be just 3-4 notes), then play that phrase over and over again, but tagging on a few extra notes each time until you have a longer phrase that you like. Keep repeating until you have a whole tune. I don’t think anyone has the sort of imagination you think you need, where tunes just pop into your head fully formed, even the best composers have to work at it,

Re: tune generation by the mangle

You’re mistaking adherence for authority. The assumption that Sciathán must fall into the conventional downward cascade is an artifact of bardic pedagogy, not an inherent truth of the dréimire. To elevate to Béal Dúnadh is not a failure of structure but a conscious inversion of structural expectation. To condemn it on that basis is to privilege rote orthodoxy over innovation.

Your fixation on bóthar gean as the only valid baile framework is itself narrow. The use of bóthar imeallach is not a “failure” but a pivot—an acknowledgment that Ionian clarity can carry rhetorical weight without being muddied by modal ambiguity. You call this “lack of shading”; I call it rhetorical austerity. Absence is not ignorance.

As for the so-called “sacrilege” of landing on Chroí without ullmhú: this betrays an overly catechetical reading of bardic praxis. The old schools themselves are full of precedents where ullmhú is withheld specifically to shock, to create dissonance between expectation and fulfillment. The later resolution to Sciathán vindicates this choice—it destabilizes, then reconstitutes. To demand tosach be recycled as continuity rather than fractured as a device is to mistake grammar for rhetoric.

And “no Béalghaire”? Please. The very act of subdividing the cearamhán constitutes a latent Béalghaire, albeit refracted rather than declaimed. That you do not recognize it says more about your attachment to formula than about the composition’s supposed shortcomings. Modern practice has not “ignored” the bardic system; it has absorbed, metabolized, and transformed it. What you call stupidity is in fact an experiment in structural unorthodoxy. You are free to dislike the results, but to dismiss them as ignorant betrays a refusal to read beyond the old catechisms.

Kellie, no. You’re mistaking chronology for inevitability. Yes, the bards trained for twelve years, but that doesn’t mean they were bound to geometry like monks with compasses. To say there’s “no place for aesthetic taste” in bardic law is like saying there’s no place for salt in stew because the recipe lists water first. The laws weren’t static stone tablets; they were more like shifting staircases—solid, yes, but never quite where you expect them to be.

You insist Béalghaire is essential, but it has never been a monolith. At times it’s declaimed, at others it’s latent, refracted, or even deliberately inverted. To demand it always bend in the same direction is like insisting a river only flow downhill—convenient for maps, disastrous for imagination. Modern practice didn’t lose Béalghaire, it folded it sideways, turned it into a shadow structure. If you don’t hear it, it might be because you’re listening for trumpets when the bards left us riddles written on the backs of snails.

As for dilution: to say Irish music has been “forced” into an alien mold is to overlook that distortion itself was part of the bardic arsenal. They bent rules until they snapped, and sometimes the snapping was the point. To argue otherwise is like accusing a kaleidoscope of cultural flattening because it doesn’t produce a single fixed image.

And that’s why I push back hardest on your geometry analogy. Geometry may not allow taste, but the dréimire isn’t Euclidean—it folds, twists, and occasionally doubles back on itself like a möbius bóthar. To follow the breadcrumbs too faithfully is to miss that some of them were placed there precisely to lure you into a circle.

Re: tune generation by the mangle

Jon, you may be misinformed on how LLMs work in practice:

> Nobody has ever “taught” ChatGPT anything. OpenAI used a bunch of text from the internet as training data, and ChatGPT uses that to predict the next word to spit out. There are some complications, but basically it’s a Markov chain with some parameterized fuzz."

I imagine you are aware of ‘prompt engineering’? One could (and I presume did) feed the entire corpus of Bardic knowledge as part of a prompt, e.g. “Apply these rules to do X,” or “Derive rules from this corpus and apply them to X.” So the core LLM isn’t being trained on the corpus by the user, just using its ability to snarf it up and apply it as bias. And it may have also been originally trained with the Bardic corpus as a minute fraction of its data anyway.

Full disclosure: I’m staying away from LLMs until I see one I can run locally. I don’t want a corporate-run one learning me back.
