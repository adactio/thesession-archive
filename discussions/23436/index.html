Re: A humble request for the *Tunes* category of thesession.org

Random_notes, there is effectively a “moratorium” between tune submissions and the publishing of the sheetmusic; usually a day or two (sometimes three). That’s not by design, it’s just depends on how busy I am.

When I then go to create the sheetmusic, I check that the ABC notation is valid. If it’s not, I see what I can do to fix it. Usually the issues are pretty obvious (a missing note length or pause). If the ABCs are invalid and I can’t fix them, then I delete the tune and ask the person who submitted it to test their tunes in some ABC software before submitting them.

So it’s not like I’m just blindly converting whatever is submitted into sheetmusic; there’s quite a bit of editorial control already.

A point of clarification.

Abcs are a representation of the tunes. When I referred to misrepresentations of the tunes I was implying abcs &/or sheet music which is either
1) hastily written down with errors. Errors which the transcriber, upon proofreading or attempting to play, would want to edit, after the fact.

or

2) very carefully written down, yet the transcriber; rather than represent the tune as it is played, attempts to make corrections. An example of this is the desire to create consonance in music. The transcriber, upon not hearing the desired consonance, makes the *correction*, thus misrepresenting what she or he hears.

The 1st case has been my concern during this discussion.
Yes, someone who understands the tunes is best suited to play the tunes when what is written contains errors. Conversely, anyone who has less understanding of the tunes is more likely to take what is written at face value. Thesession.org is primarily in written form ~ Gif images & Midi files each being generated from written text.

The 2nd case is difficult to resolve. At this late date I have surrendered to the fact they are contained in printed hard copies, such as O’Neill’s. Join a session for sure, but a session designed after cleaned up versions of the tunes? Yikes!

My primary concern is situation #1 which can be resolved much more easily.

The consonance issue I notice a lot. You have to be able and willing to spot the clear and not so clear reasons for a dissonance in music, and also accept it when you can’t fathom the reason. There are definitely a lot of watered down tune settings that find some way to pass by and alter some beautiful parts in music, as much as dressing up when it’s not needed.

I of course mostly learned to read music in the context of tunes and always found as many versions of the sheet music as possible. The session tune section has helped me a lot in my quest to piece together more possibilities of some tunes. and also induced me to rediscover tunes that I knew from listening and found in the database and recognized but had never learned.

In this sense the discontinuity of the music to reality is second nature. The imagination and memory are necessary. I am now and always will be frustrated at jumbled transcriptions with imbalanced measures, excessive accidentals, syncopation, and all the rest, which I now edit continuously in abc explorer. Even the most experienced transcribers, myself included, make poor choices in how to structure a tune on paper. In you personal collection, you can always go back and rewrite something, but once on the site it would be impossible to go back and change it all.

Well currently I am working on smoothing out the bumps in 700 reels from this site, variations included, but I decided to dump partial variations most of the time. Or if the posted a whole line I just cut and paste the rest of the tune to fit where described, but I am growing weary of this. This involves combining reposted tunes, and deleting or moving tunes that aren’t reels. So far I have about 25 tunes in the trashcan and am a little more than halfway through. Once I get through all the combined reposts I project 700 reels will turn into something like 500. Eventually I will start adding my own settings, or move on to jigs, or polkas.
