Concurrent guitar & bouzouki, yay or nay?

What are your thoughts on this? Is it ok for both to accompany tunes at the same time, especially if one is primarily chording and the other is doing countermelody and/or cross-picking? Or should the players alternate? I’m as asking as a guy who’s played bouzouki in the same session for about 13 years now, and trying to navigate around a guitar player that started showing up a couple of years ago. There’s been some tangential conflict to all this that I won’t go into, and really isn’t relevant other than how it has created a slow build up of tension to the overall situation.
My understanding has always been that the general consensus is melody players prefer one bodhran at a time, and one guitar or bouzouki at a time, and I’m curious if that still is the consensus or if I’m mistaken about it ever being the consensus.

In almost all circumstances, Nay.

I’d say that potential ‘Yay’ circumstances would involve a combination of (a) skill, (b) preparation, (c) coordination, (d) musical sensitivity, (e) social awareness, and (f) the agreement/indulgence of other players at the session. Re: Concurrent guitar & bouzouki, yay or nay?

There are no rules, but my personal preference is for fewer accompanists in the same octave so that there’s more room to use altered chords, chord extensions, suspensions, and countermelodies without risking dissonance with another player. Guitar and bouzouki also sound more similar than (for example) either with piano, accordion, or bass which by virtue of their dissimilar timbre may combine more pleasingly. Huge sessions with a multitude of each instrument don’t appeal to me, but they may be the next person’s preference. It takes a practiced hand and ear to keep out of another’s way, so the more players there are, the harder that becomes. Ymmv, imho, etc etc.

Re: Concurrent guitar & bouzouki, yay or nay?

This thread shows that different people have different preferences for the number and type of backers at a session. So what’s tolerated or accepted at one session may not be at another. And that’s the prerogative of those sessioners.

In short, and in direct response to the OP, there isn’t really a consensus on this. Each session has its own expectations, and even these may change depending on who shows up on a given day.

In the OP situation, there are several ways to resolve the problem.
- The backers could grin and bear it, playing simultaneously.
- The zouk player could assert his/her tenure at the session and tell the guitarist to bugger off.
- The zouk and guitar players could take turns which days they show up.
- The zouk and guitar players could come to all sessions but take turns backing sets.
- They could poll the melody players and let them decide how many backers (and which ones) can play at once.
- One or both of them could learn to play melody.

The answer will likely have to come through honest, good-faith communication, either between the backers, or with the session anchor if there is one, or with the core melody players, or the whole group.

At some point, a regular ongoing session has to decide what it wants to be. In some sessions, the anchor sets and enforces the expectations, and in others it’s a group decision. In my experience, it rarely goes well when one player (who’s not the anchor) steps on another’s toes. Sessions are as much or more social circles as they are musical, so it helps to resolve the inevitable clashes as gracefully and with as much agreement among the regular players as you can muster.

Re: Concurrent guitar & bouzouki, yay or nay?

I think a lot depends specifically on the players and their style of backup.

In our session, the primary backup player plays mandocello, and uses a sparse walking scale style against a drone that effectively has the instrument acting as a bass more than a chord instrument.

With the mandocello filling the role and register of a bass, it often works very well if we have a visiting guitar player or bouzouki player filling the mid-range spectrum. Because the mandocello is seldom playing fully formed chords, they generally won’t clash.

Now two guitars or two bouzoukis, that can be problematic and we’d most often encourage them to take turns, but I have seen the same dynamic work with sparsely played bouzouki plus guitar, completely depends on the player’s style and their ability to work together.

Re: Concurrent guitar & bouzouki, yay or nay?

mandocello8, I play guitar, but never in Irish sessions (I play fiddle or another melody instrument then), so I’m not one of your backers desperate to hang onto my turf. But I do have thoughtful reasons for strongly preferring, at most, only one backer at a time in a session.

- A lot of Irish trad tunes are ambiguous when it comes to what chords might fit. So two backers may have really divergent ideas about which chords go where. To the point of clashing. It’s bad enough when *one* backer plays a chord that doesn’t at all fit what the melody players had in mind, but when two do it, and do it with chords that don’t blend besides, it sounds like two accordions dropped down a stairwell. It’s one thing when a melody player does a melodic variation, but a wall of sound from a backer on a detour is nothing less than jarring.

- I savor that harmonic ambiguity, when a tune wanders between modes or even keys, when the chord could be major but just as well minor or some other color. As a melody player, I enjoy inserting a quick “sweet” note, a passing note, or a double stop here and there to imply one color or another, and then changing it the next time around. For a B quarter note, I might grab a G to go with it, or an F#, or an E. For a dotted quarter B, I might play BAB or BcB or BdB or BeB or some other combination. Backers can’t read my mind, so they can’t know what tonal color I might favor at any given moment. Often as not, it clashes.

- The tunes work just fine without any backing at all. The reverse is far less true. Without a backer, a melody player is free to hear (and play) his or her own harmonic background, his or her own pulse, swing, etc. Without any melody players, a session is DOA, or it’s a song circle.

I’ve sessioned with some fantastic backers, on guitar, zouk, piano, and bodhran. But I’ve also really enjoyed many sessions without a backer at all. “Zero guitar” doesn’t harm a session. One guitar may or may not enhance it. In my 40 years of sessions, more than one backer has almost always muddied the sound, made it harder to hear the other melody players, and made it harder to adjust pulse, swing, and tempo on the fly.

Of course, these are nothing more than my personal observations and preferences. You can do what you want at your session. But I have no problem building my session around my preferences, and those of my session mates, rather than suffering when accompaniment isn’t helping the overall sound.
