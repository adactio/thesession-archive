Flute: Low D too flat!

I’ve recently bought an old Buffet Crampon 5 key simple system flute without tuning slide. Supossedly, the flute was restored by a well known woodwind technician (the pads and keys are actually restored and don’t leak) so I must assume that the cork is in the right position too.

The issue is that I’ve noticed, while practicing my embouchure with a chromatic tuner, that all the flute is almost a semitone lower, with the low D being more a C#.

It can be somewhat fixed if I align the center of the head-joint hole with the center of the middle of the finger holes (but then is harder to blow in the center and difficult to get a dark tone). If I turn the head inwards, aligning the edge of the head hole with the center of the finger holes, the sound goes too flat.

Is that an embouchure thing that I have to correct, or the flute can actually changes it’s tuning in such a noticeable way just by turning the head-joint a few degrees?

Re: Flute: Low D too flat!

Oh, this could go into such complexity! But at the root of it your embouchure needs adjustment. While looking at the flute from its foot end, put all of the centers of the tone holes and the center of the embouchure hole all in a straight line, then hold the flute straight out horizontally sideways while placing all of the holes vertically straight up, and never mind about the Irish embouchure rubbish, You asked for it and this is how a flute really gets played, professionally. Then, strike an A@440hz tuning fork, treat both of your ears to it and then put exactly that same A tone on your flute. OK, once you have done that then harmonically match all of the other tones on your flute to that same A tone, yes, the A tone then becomes somewhat of a drone tone against which all other tones get played. Think of bagpipes and how a chanter plays against its drone pipes. Do not be concerned about the tuning of a flute and Buffet Crampon happens to make wonderful flutes. Many years ago I learned how to play at A@440 pitch standards on an A@435 flute. Yes, if your embouchure is in tip-top condition then it is easy to “bend” the tuning of a flute by a semitone.

Re: Flute: Low D too flat!

The answer is simple: Turn the embouchure hole away from you to play sharper. That’s pretty much it. Be sure you are blowing with enough force to get good tone without overblowing. Obviously, if the blow is weak, the tone will suffer along with the pitch. Use an aural tone, not something with lights or a swinging needle, to get your baseline A and E in both lower and upper octaves. If you can get the pitch to your satisfaction on those four notes, the rest should be okay. With practice, it’s pretty easy, but in time you might want to get a flute you don’t have to fight with, unless you are just a bit inexperienced at this point.

Your embouchure can do a whole lot more than that! Never mind tuning slides! Wake up first thing in the morning with flute playing on your mind, then strike an A@440 tuning fork, treat both of your ears to it, pick up your flute, exactly match the tuning fork’s A@440 tone and then harmonically match all of the other of the flute’s tones to that A@440 pitch. Its true that all sorts of things are possible and that working them out will improve your skills as a flute player. On the other hand, many of the old flutes were made to play sharp or flat of A 440 and will play at their best at or near their intended pitch, at least for most players. I had the opportunity to hear Hammy Hamilton last year; he played a lovely old Rudall that he found hopelessly out of tune for ensemble or duet (except percussion!) work but kept for the gorgeous tone. He played it full blast and sounded great. Had me kicking myself for letting a similar flute go after years of trying to blow it into tune at modern pitch.

So by all means use the Buffet as an opportunity for focused embouchure development, but also spend time exploring its tonal capabilities at or near its original pitch, whatever that is. If you want to play with fixed pitch instruments in modern tuning, get a modern simple system flute. There are lots of them out there, even on a budget.

Re: Flute: Low D too flat!

Well, let me admit it is quite true that a flute pitched at A@440 is best suited for playing music pitched at A@440, nice and easy, falls right into place immediately. But what I am getting at here is embouchure flexibility and what that can do. An A@430 flute can easily be sharpened and an A@450 flute can easily be flattened, both to play at A@440 pitch standards. Irish traditional music is not so much of a training ground for that, especially as the dance music is fairly monolithic and little embouchure adjustment is required. But flute music of the Baroque era changes all of that, where sometimes music is played loudly but sometimes played softly and sometimes a single tone can shift from soft to loud or from loud to soft. Yet a flute played softly tends to go flatter in pitch while a flute played loudly tends to go sharper in pitch and so a player needs to adjust their embouchure to compensate for the differences between a softly played tone and a loudly played tone in order to keep both of those tones in the same tune. Yes, that is the beginning of where a player learns how to “bend” the tone of a flute. And once you learn how to do that then you will discover how easy it is to “bend” A@430 and A@450 flutes into A@440 tuning.

Everyone here probably realized this long before I did, but I could not play my 1832 flute with a 40 cent flat low D in tune when I tuned to G,g and tried to bring the D and d up. Everything changed when I decided to move the tuning slide to bring the middle D into perfect tune, with the low D about 20 cents flat. This note I can raise by 20 cents by raising my chin to blow more across the hole.

Of course tuning to the middle D makes the other notes sharper. However, with good breath support, and having the upper lip protrude past the lower lip, I find that most of the remaining notes are in reasonable tune. For the very share A,B, I tuck my chin on the A,B to bring flatten them further, and for the a,b tuck my chin and back off on the amount of air.

As an aside, I have the embouchure hole in line with finger holes and this gives me better resonance, and gives me more leeway to make small changes in tuning by changing the direction of the air stream, and larger ones by tipping my head up or down.

Re: Flute: Low D too flat!

Yeah, positioning a flute’s head joint cork is somewhat of an art. The Golden Rule here is to adjust the downtube face of the cork, the end of the cork facing the embouchure hole, to a position where octave leaps (up or down) are as easy as possible. And that could take some experimentation! A good place to begin is to measure the bore size of the head joint at the embouchure hole and then position the end of the head joint cork at a distance of the same as the bore size to the center of the embouchure hole. That should get you quite close but maybe not ideal and so some slightly further adjustment could be necessary. Oh, and always use a large diameter wood dowel to shift the position of the cork, nothing skinny or pointy, and that way the face of the cork will remain nice and flat, just the way it should be.
