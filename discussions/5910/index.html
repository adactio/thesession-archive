Re: Tune name

Jack, when you are in a hole, stop digging. The Titanic was indeed 1912, add on thirty years and we have 1932. The bodhran was brought back into vogue mostly by O’Riada in the 1950’s.

However William Wallace and his “terrorists” may well have had bodhrans, because the instrument did not fade away until the early 17th century, along with the language and other parts of the culture. As with the language however, the bodhran survived in more remote parts. And we all now know that the precursor of the bodhran arrived in Ireland with the North African Celts.

I realise Jack, that coming from a country with a 229 year old history this time span may be hard to grasp.

One eminent historian attributes the “invention” of the wheel to the bodhran, when supposedly someone put spines (now called spokes) onto a bodhran, and the rest is history. Despite his well argued case I doubt this, because the bodhran preceded the wheel by centuries, and somebody would have thought of it earlier.

As for the tune on this thread I think it is an old Spanish tune called “Barcelona”,which was brought to what is now California by Don Sebastian de Montoya in 1526, or maybe 27. This was well received by the locals because St Brendan had brought Irish music to the east coast centuries earlier.

However in California at that time the “Mexican” bodhran was played. This links up to the “Dingle Regatta” because the Mexican players used to wave during the second part of a tune, thus giving birth to what is now universally known as “The Mexican Wave”.
