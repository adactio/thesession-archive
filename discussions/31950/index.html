Let’s introduce ourselves !

Reading the last few big posts recently (« The Mustard Board » and « Mining for gems in the mustard » in particular !) I realized that I didn’t know anybody on this website really and I actually found myself too lazy to go and check the profile of all the persons taking part in these discussions (or the profiles don’t give you any information - like mine…).

So I thought it could be nice and handy to have a sort of an « introduce yourself » post… Most of us probably don’t have a biography ready to use, so I thought of a few questions that might help giving people an idea of who we are and what’s our approach to music… Shouldn’t take too long to copy and paste + change the answers… Obviousy, anybody can add stuff or not answer other questions… it is just a guideline.

And obviously, the whole point here is to avoid going into arguments !

I’ll start by introducting myself anyways !

Name : Charles
Age : 26
Home : Born in Switzerland, living in Doolin, Co. Clare

Instrument(s) : Concertina and a bit of tin whistle

Inspiring musicians : Terry Bingham, James Cullinan, Dermot Byrne

Favourite setting for playing : small and quiet sessions. I enjoy a bit of madness from time to time too !

How and when did I start ITM : Came to Doolin for work for the summer in 2006 and I didn’t have any notion about irish music then. A friend taught me a few tunes on the tin whistle that summer, and I started playing the concertina a year and a half later.

Goal within the music : reaching a level so that when I come in to a session in Clare other musicians think « Hey cool, Charles is joining us tonight ! ». Not really looking for gigs, even though it is nice from time to time to be under a certain pressure. It is also important to me that I am passing on some music to other people, but not necessarily through teaching.

How do I learn tunes : a mix of ABC and by ear., mostly from session recordings. I can’t read music scores. Actually, I can’t learn a tune from ABC if I am not familiar with it before hand.

Alright… I hope to « meet » some of you this way, and maybe it might help a bit with a few issues that have been brought up recently !

Cheers

I learn tunes by mainly listening with help of ABC or dots. I’m not fluent with reading music, but I do use it as a guide if I am not getting the tune. I am in the same boat that if I don’t know what it sounds like, then I probably can’t learn off of dots alone.

Actually my name’s not really Gobby, and I’m not quite an alcoholic, but for personal reasons I prefer to not expose my real name and persona on cyberspace. That’s not because I have anything to hide, it’s just how I am in real life;- a recluse who has withdrawn from society. Believe it or not, you lot are my only community, and I enjoy (or sometimes otherwise) your company every morning.

All of this stuff, I would suggest, belongs in the profile pages, where I do see some merit in the suggestion of having a more formal layout of personal introduction (see below). I Just don’t see how it would work though. Ultimately, real name or pseudonym, we (mostly) don’t actually know each other and it can at times be equally difficult (real name or pseudonym) to tell reality from imagination. Let me put it another way, and then I’ll explain why I see this suggestion as having merit (albeit just an ideal):-

Fundamentally, I don’t actually know who you are, and neither do you know me, and for the most part (I imagine..??) most of us don’t actually know each other. We are free in cyberspace to adopt any persona and act out any role, and no matter what we say about ourselves to large degree we can only be just imaginary friends (and maybe for some, imaginary enemies).

I’m not trying to be negative here, and I hasten to add that I have faith in the accuracy of most of your cyberspace apparitions. The reason for me thinking this out recently is more to do with this:-

Referring once more to our recent couple of disheartening melee’s, it strikes me that there is something about cyberspace discourse that can be akin to the road-rage that some otherwise perfectly civil and good natured person can experience once behind the wheel of a car. Perhaps, as somebody recently suggested, the inability to perceive body language in the dialogue has some effect here. But more than this, I believe is the effect akin to road-rage, where our ego’s can too quickly become over imaginative and consequently over sensitive. And this happens because we simply don’t really know the other person. Our ego’s can too quickly fill in the gaps of ignorance with feelings of being insulted, or disrespected, etc. The insult may be imagined or it may even be real. But whichever, in the end, why let the ego attach itself when (for the most part), they are just an unknown driver to you, and you to them. Just let the car pass.

So feel free anybody to respond to me with (e.g.,) “Gobby, you’re a feckin idiot!” (truly). This wouldn’t actually upset me in the slightest, because you don’t even know who it is you’re talking to. But consider it the other way around. If my ego attaches to something and I respond negatively (like road rage), being in cyberspace I don’t even know who it is I’m angry with, and my anger in fact would merely be with my own imagination, and ultimately, because when I go to bed at night I know who I am, more damaging to myself than to anybody else (from experience).

So sorry if I’ve diverted too much from your intention Charles. I think it’s a nice ideal, and I’m sure to enjoy reading the responses, but whatever name I or anybody else uses, it makes no difference. We are only who you imagine us to be by the pictures we pant of ourselves. I must add BTW, that I don’t hide behind Gobby O’Gobbo. I always use my real name on personal contacts. Good luck with your idea. I must go now and fill in my profile (again).

My first Irish teacher suggested that I learn to pick a couple of tunes on the guitar, so that I would know them well enough to figure out accompaniment. Re: Let’s introduce ourselves !

This post is actually in response to a question asked of me on another current thread (Ciaran Carsan). Albeit my third comment in this thread I thought my reply would be more appropriate here. Key Maniac Lad, who apparently watches Noddy video’s, gave a link to one and, referring to Noddy’s arch enemy Gobbo the Goblin, he asked me, “This isn’t you Gobbo, is it?” My answer adds a little more info about myself…..

Well KML, believe it or not you are exactly right. Gobby and to a lesser degree his mate Sly were my favourite characters when I was a little kid. I liked them because they gave that poncey Noddy such a hard time. The Gobby part of my chosen pseudonym was given to me by an Irishman when I was a little older (13) and roaming the Stretford streets of Manchester. He was a night-watchman I discovered in a tent outside a hole in the road, and for three nights he gave me massive sized cups of tea with an equally huge chocolate biscuit, while he entertained me with stories and lilted tunes from Ireland. He also let me smoke his pipe (hence my long resisted but still persistent desire for a Peterson pipe stuffed with an Irish mix…. sigh!). I was greatly disappointed the night I found him gone, and missing him is what first made me start paying proper attention to Irish music. I always wish I that I knew his name and where he came from, but all I remember was that his name was Paddy (and what’s the chances of that?). But anyhow, the first night I met him he said, ‘Your a gobby little shite, aren’t you?’ Nowadays I don’t really have the attitude of either Gobby or Gobbo, but try and play my fiddle as if I did.
