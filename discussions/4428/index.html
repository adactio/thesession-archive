Re: Quite possibly the most obnoxious flute recording ever

Jim, interesting point about Lindeert de Jonge’s playing of Bach. When I play Bach, or other composer of that period, on the cello now I feel I naturally want to put in the occasional roll or other ornament which my old cello teacher would have been surprised to hear (and probably delighted as well, since he had another life as a jazz musician). Of course, in an orchestra I mustn’t do that, because of the orchestral discipline of everyone playing from the score, but the unaccompanied stuff gives far more scope for individual interpretation.

Having said that, Bach and Handel, to mention but two composers of that era, didn’t put much in the way of ornaments or phrasing in their scores (what we see now are the efforts of modern editors), not much more than broad indications of speed or whether something is to be played loud or soft. The musicians of the day would have put in the ornamentation themselves, as part of the culture. I suspect that playing Irish trad music today gives an insight into that 17/18th century tradition, and it shouldn’t be too surprising that there is a cross-over effect.

As mentioned above, one of the 2 CDs is supposed to be “traditional”, and I don’t think anyone’s heard anything from it yet. You can be sure that Flatley didn’t record this particular track with the Irish traditional music community in mind. Time will tell. The dilemma is, do you buy a 2 CD set when there is a strong possibility you only want one?
