My First live performance playing ITM

Two of my friends got married and wanted me to play fiddle for their wedding. Since ITM is all i know, it was all i could play.

The guy on the piano is a guy i’ve duetted with before on piano. He seemed to pick up the tunes pretty fast(i picked something i thought would’ve been simple enough) The guy on the guitar i *re-met today. He goes to the school i dropped out of and remembers me playing piano there.

Unfortunately, i wasn’t recording when him and i tried “Boys of Malin” for the first time(because we tried other tunes and they didn’t work out too well. This was really new to him). It was AWESOME! but, now it’s just a memory.

So we decided to play that one for the Reception. And after that, i figured i developed some *slightly better judgment, and led them into the “Steeplechase/The Graf Spey” set, which was unrehearsed. We did better than i thought, but i wish i had followed instead of lead(just because the Guitar had the rhythm and i didn’t. You’ll hear my rhythm mistakes in there.)

I’m not looking for criticism this time, but if you want, it’s still welcome. Just showing you all what i’ve been up to over here. I hope this isn’t wasted space. And to encourage that i will now say several other things.

Isn’t it awesome that we can get together with strangers and just play music? That’s what happened with the Guitarist and us today :)

I’ve seen musicians like us(who don’t have a background in ITM) get slashed on this site. So is what i did a bad thing? It’s ok for me to play this music right?

Re: My First live performance playing ITM

I’m a bit late joining this one, but here’s what I have to say, anyway:

You have great spirit in your playing - music without spirit is like a balloon with no air in it. Also, as others have already said, it takes a lot of courage and confidence to get up there and play in front of a crowd. So, “Chwarae teg!” as we say in Wales.

The two main issues with your playing, to my ear, are timing and intonation (two things I also struggle with on the fiddle). These are not entirely unrelated to one another either - you have a tendency to race ahead and, of course, the faster you play, the less time you have to attend to the intonation (tuning) of each note. Now that you’ve learned to play fast, go back and learn to play slow - *really* slow, keeping the rhythm rock solid and getting each note sounding just how you want it.

As regards your style - one note to each bow stroke - it reminds me most of Johnny Doherty from W. Donegal. But, to recap what others have said, some variety in the bowing would not go amiss. Bowed triplets, cuts and rolls are all things that can be incorporated into your playing over time, but I would suggest first experimenting with slurring notes, changing bow direction at different points in the tune, and seeing what effect it has on the rhythm and feel. Using longer bow strokes also gives you the option of accenting notes just by quickening the bow speed, without a change of bow direction. Perhaps a single-bowed style is what you’ll end up coming back to, but it is as well to have a bit more technique up your sleeve that you actually use.
