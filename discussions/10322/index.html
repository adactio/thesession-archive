Re: Flute teachers and Flute classes in Cork ~

Vanessa: What I will tell you has been discussed in various forms many times on this board, so bear with the repitition. First, you must listen to this music constantly. If you do that, you will have it infused in your soul. I do mean constantly. If you love it as much as you profess, you ARE listening to it much of your day. One of the most important ways to listen to the music is to do it live. Go to sessions and listen. Leave your instrument at home and just listen and watch the dynamics of a good session - for a long time.
Secondly, don’t bog yourself down with notation. If you are listening to the music, and really “hearing” it, notation will fall by the wayside. You have to learn it by ear, your listening tool! Knowing that learning the music by ear can be difficult for most people, there are many technological tools that can help you slow recordings down in order to attain what I would call the “crutch” of notation, and they are great helps.
Actually, I’ve gotten ahead of myself a bit. If you live in Cork, there MUST be many tutors who could help you get started with the basics of the flute, posture, and ornamentation. I should think that Hammy Hamilton knows of such. But once you figure out yourself, or are properly instructed in the basics, becoming proficient is a matter of LISTENING, and practice. Lots and lots of the latter. I wish you perserverence and success. The flute is a wonderful instrument.

The German Flute / Die Deutsche Flöte

Having worked with people who thought themselves tone deaf, or who in later age thought they were useless musically, I know something of the sort of houtspa/bravery required to have made your reaches out, to this site, and to finally have secured a teacher. Your description gave me ‘faith’ in you, the morse code in the previous comment, or I would have been more hesitant in starting this thread.

I almost think you need a nickname, like maybe “Deutsche Flöte”? Having, amongst others, enjoyed the company of many old flute players in Eire, I know they said ‘German Flute’ with affection, the old wood flutes often referred to as that, whether or not they were English or French or American or otherwise made. The instrument was very popular in Germany too, including finding a place in the village bands, before -you-know-what’… There were many fine German players and makers, including, of course, the Bavarian responsible for the modern flute:

The Boehm system (1832 / 1847), Theobald Boehm (1794-1881)
http://www.flutehistory.com/
http://www.flutehistory.com/Instrument/Boehm.php3

Rick Wilson’s Historical Flutes Page:
http://www.oldflutes.com/
http://www.oldflutes.com/german.htm

You’ll find other sites focusing just on the folk or Irish flute on the Internet, as simple search will give you that added joy, though I suspect you’ve done that. There are also links under ‘Links’ here on site.

Your teachers sounds a charmer, infinite patience and common sense, that is ideal. That makes me smile ear to ear, and it isn’t easy when I’m sneezing so much and rubbing eyes and gasping for air ~ hayfever and asthma (am I ;-) seeking sympathy? ~ No! )

You do need to credit yourself more though, try to get past the idea of ‘difficulty’. It is one of those bugaboos, like a little monster that sits on your shoulder. The reason why the little basturd remains is because there is a comfort in having an excuse for not doing something, or for it not going as planned. I know, I’ve got one just like everyone else does. For some of us it is more vocal and persistant. You need to work on having the upper hand there, not it…

Yes, for a child completely divorced of music all their life you might be able to find that as a limitation, except ~ you are born with a heart, and for most of us that doesn’t include an irregular beat or murmur. So, internally, the clock that makes us musical is an integral part of you. I remember an experiment made with small group of musicians, wiring them all in. They started playing and the weird wonderful happening was that suddenly as you watched the machines recording their vitals, their hearts started to synchronize ~ I mean WOW! That may not be the norm, but still deserves a WOW!

If you do have a German accent in your playing, that ain’t bad either. Eire, Ireland, and it’s music, is drawn from a wide field. Some even suggest the vacation of certain eminent composers to Kerry is responsible for the ‘polka’. That is a bit a a stretch, blaming them for the whole thing that is, but these tunes didn’t just pop out of the ground, as neither did the potato originate in Irish soil. The Austro-Hungarians, the French, traveling Irish returning home ~ the input of ideas and forms is amazing. If you know the Irish, well, they don’t limit themselves, they are world travelers many of them, and even those that don’t leave the soil tend to have minds that see beyond their little breadth of Eire.

Actually, where do you get off saying you are a “very difficult student”? Did some teacher tell you that? You have the passion, what more could a good teacher want? Yes, I know, I have known a few that want medal winners and won’t bother with a student they don’t see that potential in, and wanting that to show ‘quickly’… I tend not to play music with those sorts if I am given my druthers… Not being a fast learner is not bad. That often makes for a good teacher. There are some really superb musicians there in Eire who were slow to start and who struggled, I know a few who have recorded as well, and won contests and medals, and because they have the memory of their own struggle, or that of a family member or friend ~ are damned good teachers I’d recommend to anyone…because of the insight that gives them patience. Please, work to not count you limitations and to focus in on your accomplishments. Look how far you’ve gotten so far ~ I’m amazed and inspired by it, so should you…

I know it is easier for me to be saying these things, but I do feel the things you’ve said because I know them myself ~ it is always easy to compile a list of ones failings and limitations, and they make great excuse for being afraid to move forward. Buy hey, you are doing just that, moving forward, and it does not matter what speed that growth takes, you are growing and finding yourself and your musical self is opening up. Music is, if that isn’t already clear, ‘magic’ to me. It can be a best friend, and I see that as being the case with you. You don’t have to be a virtuoso with lots of tricks. Just find the voice, with help, that musical spark inside, and give it voice through your flute or however else you need to. I would also suggest dance if you can, as that helps strengthen the physical and mental understanding of the music we play and share.

I almost forgot, we used to play some German dance tunes amongst the Irish, in praticular Zweifacher, but also the odd mazurka or polka… One of my favourite teachers was Bavarian, and what a wonderful kick she was, and she had a very pronounced German accent, and she was also very, very bossy, but maybe that was what I needed… ;-)

Another bit of myth is the idea of being ‘tone deaf’… Again, some folks have put a lot of work into testing this thing, and there’s not one I can think of that proved it, to the contrary. What they found was that on the whole it was either due to an untrained ear, or to insecurities and lack of confidence ~ or both. They found that with a little concerted effort, directed teaching, they could help the person who thought of themselves as tone deaf, to gain confidence and to overcome it and to sing in tune… BUT ~ tone quality is another thing altogether…

“Clumsy fingering” ~ we are all clumsy when trying something the first time, or at least those of us who are honest about it will admit it. Yeah, I know, for some that clumsiness is short lived. We’re virging on the toroise and the hare here. You know, for some for who it comes early, they give up early and nothing more happens, while for those who struggle, it often develops into a lifelong passion…

Keep asking those questions, it is how you learn, it is the active child in you. I’ve found that those who are overly irritated with questions it is because they are frustrated in not knowing the answers. That’s me remembering some of my math teachers. Keep that child and freshness alive, don’t sacrafice the ‘newness’ of it. Most of us appreciate questions, but it might be a good idea to aportion them out in dribbles instead of a flood… But I suspect you were exaggerating. It is easier to learn a few things well, one at a time, instead of trying to learn everything at once… Don’t rush it, that too easily can become part of your technique, not something I have any appreciation for. Relax your way to good technique.

Your tutor, a good tutor, isn’t bearing up with you despite your short comings, they are there because they believe in your strengths. It is not an imposition. Sometimes strength of heart is all that is needed… Maybe soon you will be able to learn in a group too? There may even be a slow session somewhere in your area. Some of the Comhaltas branches have classes where you just learn one tune at a time at a relaxed tempo, or one part one week and the second the next. The joy in group lessons is that you find people with similar insecurities, you discover you are not alone… YOU ARE NOT ALONE…

~ on how you can bring the seemingly impossible within your own grasp… You!

Yes! ~ It isn’t about finding what you see outside of yourself and aspiring to be something or someone you aren’t. It is about uncoving who you are and channeling that into your own distinct identity and way with the music. Some musicians have made and art over time out of their limitations…their ‘distinct’ way with the music. The music is there in us all, that rhythm. For some of us it is a major excavation to find, reclaim and animate that part of our soul. For others it may always be on the surface, but more chance it is taken for granted or neglected when so easily at hand.

About your ‘marchiness’, so, go for it, ask your teacher to teach you some marches, find some marches to play. What is really a joy about them is, aside for giving you an outlet for those tendencies you’ve suggested are in you, you will discover that marches are not so regimented, that their great kick to play. Yes, a defined and powerful basic beat, but within all limitations there are amazing freedoms.

Marches are a great discipline, and they will help you with other things, and if you gain an understanding of their limitations and their possibilities ~ well, they will help you across the board and as already said, they can be a hell of a lot of fun to play too, despite the marchiness of them… There are some great ones here on site ~ Irish, Welsh, Scottish, Breton, Galician, Swedish… ~ including quite a few I’ve contributed, along with other things. I recommend them highly for beginning and developing players. As said, they will also give you an outlet for your claimed tendency…and ground you in one of the most important foundations of all music ~ a good strong sense and control of rhythm…
