Tin whistle to help playing other instruments

I have read on here before for people who want to play the tunes but don’t know which instrument to learn, that a good idea would be to start on the tin whistle. It’s doesn’t take much to start getting some tunes out of it and they don’t cost all that much for a starter whistle. I guess the advice is to learn the tunes on the whistle and that will make it easier when you switch over to the fiddle or concertina or whatever since the tunes are already in your head.

Are there any advantages to picking up the tin whistle as a secondary instrument? The portability of it is an obvious advantage but what are thoughts on how learning tin whistle could improve your playing on your main instrument, whatever that instrument is?

↳ Posted by sean mac aonghusa on July 31st, 2014.

Re: Tin whistle to help playing other instruments

It will help you with “the flow, the flow” which is what my fiddle mentor Danny Meehan often told me to work on in my early days.

Other advantages: Nothing can compare with the sound of a whistle played on the banks of a lake at dawn or after dusk. You will realise why so many great fluting and piping tunes are in G. You will have a fantastic amount of fun with it. You can practise on car journeys and even while driving yourself (NB only when stopped in traffic jams, and beware of exploding airbags :) ).

Re: Tin whistle to help playing other instruments

I play multiple instruments, but I started as a string player.

For me the whistle was like some sort of alchemy. Making the notes strait from my breath and having to overblow to get the upper octave was a foreign concept for me. So the simple little tin whistle gave me a completely new perspective.

It also helped me to understand why so many of the old tunes were in the range that they were, and I also got a new perspective on ornaments being a string player grappling with a wind instrument

my experience has been that playing a new instrument improves your overall musicianship, and that leads to playing your main instrument better. Its sort of a funny thing, but you aren’t taking time away from your main instrument like you would think.

so yes, I think its worth picking up a tin whistle, even if you don’t intend to be a whistle player

Re: Tin whistle to help playing other instruments

I recommend it for learners on fiddle and pipes because it allows them to play tunes and learn them so that when they attain the ability to blow/bow steady and play scales in tune they the already have a bunch of tunes in their head and under their fingers . its a relatively simple matter then to transfer over to the more complex instrument. People like Patsy Touhey for eg suggests that beginning pipers avoid at all cost playing dance music on pipes for at least 6 months , just to attain the basic ability to play the instrument. I myself spent a couple of years really just practicing scales and the like , knowing that at some point I would be able to play tunes I already knew from 20yrs at the banjo and the like . Once the tune is in your head its only a matter of transferring it onto different instruments .
I also feel the whistle really helps with phrasing in that you need to breathe and this means by necessity you break the tune up in some fashion and forces one to think about this aspect of the music . I feel Its also a lot easier to approach many ornaments through the medium of the whistle , rolls grace notes etc , and as other posters have mentioned ‘the flow’. Another thing is the directness of blowing through a tube , its quite an intimate connection denied fiddlers , closer to singing than any other instrument .

I think many of these aspects apply to the flute but really I know nothing about the flute so would not feel able to comment on it and I presume its much more of a complex instrument to get and maintain a good tone , seems that way as an observer.
Traditionally ithe whistle is pretty much the first instrument anyone picks up and It seems to me that its a part of a holistic approach to the music, just as is playing simple tunes with little to no ornaments at first , as a kid would do. As a teacher of decades I feel that simplicity is the key to everything , the main difference between a master and a beginner is that the master does the same things plays the same tunes , only much better .
I feel that with time and patience a simple approach allows one to develop in a natural unforced way and that this offers more room for personal growth. Bit verbose here, just back from a cracking session :-)

@ Ailin

Try covering the holes of flutes and whistles another way. First, get your fingers in such a position that they do properly cover the holes, and then, adjust your grip to keep the fingers exactly where you want them to be.
