Re: Best Guitar…

MikeS, I noticed on another thread that you were asking what brands various famous players play.
It sounds like you’re seriously thinking of buying a new instrument.
Be very careful how you take the advice given on this thread. Nobody here would intentionally try to steer you wrong, but you still have to take it all with a grain of salt. “Brand X is twice the guitar that Brand Y is at half the price” is no doubt an attempt to be helpful, but there’s a degree of nonsense about those kinds of comments, too. We are all products of our own limited experience, and we all have our personal preferences.
It’s not brand. Do you like bright? Mellow? Small body or large? Does a booming bass turn you on, or are you more about balance? Are we talking fingerstyle guitar? Flatpicking? Would you sacrifice a bit of fingerstyle subtlety or flatpicking power for the sake of an instrument that was perhaps more versatile? These are all matters of personal preferences.
Most major guitar makers produce different models that target those and other qualities of personal preference, and I suppose some brands may do a better job with some of those qualities than others do. When someone here tells you Brand X is the best, do they mean the best overall, or do they mean the best fingerstyle, or the best brightness-for-a-mahagony guitar, or the boomiest-bass-considering-it’s-not-a-dreadnought?
Play as many instruments as you can before you buy one. Take along a friend who plays like you, or like you wish you could, so you can hear the instrument from the “receiving” side. And of course play ’em yourself, so you can see whether they can deliver what you’ve got. As you look and shop, do it without looking at brand name or even price, until you get an idea of the sound you want. Then spend your time trying to find an affordable instrument that best delivers that sound.
Whatever you do, don’t buy it off the Internet or from a catalogue without getting a chance to hear it, unless there’s a liberal return policy.

Re: Best Guitar…

I’d second the suggestion that you look at the pickup if the amplified sound is your concern. There are a lot of good pickups that have come available in the last few years - I’m partial to the K&K I’ve got in my Martin, but there are many good ones - so poke around, read up on the various sorts of systems (soundboard transducers, undersaddle/piezo systems, magnetic, and internal microphones are the big four, and there are plenty of combo and hybrid arrangements), and try to compare instruments with each system.
Be careful not to put too much weight on the sound at a big concert like Lunasa - a dedicated sound man with a rack of gear can make even a stock pickup sound pretty good in a concert setting, but is that your situation? For bar gigs, you want something that you can plug in and play without spending a lot of time tweaking, ducking out in front of the stacks, and so forth.
Also, have you tried a pre-amp? Most stock pickups sound pretty bad without them, but can be brought up to snuff with them. I like the Baggs ParaAcoustic DI, which has some good EQ to play with, but there are again other things to look at as well. The other thing that might help is a bit of compression, but there you’re getting into stuff that requires a lot of attention which you might not have to spare when you’re on stage.

Re: Best Guitar…

I’ve noticed that a good guitarist can make a bad guitar sound good, has anyone else? There are good reasons for this, to do with the technique and the ability to hold a note down properly for max sustain etc.

If buying a guitar, decide what characteristics you are looking for, and also decide if you want to buy it in a shop or from an ad/auction. In both cases pick a price range.

If the former, go to a shop and try out EVERY guitar that meets your requirements. You will be amazed how a brand you have never heard of compares against a “name” instrument. (This is how I came across my Yamaki guitar which I had for 13 years until it was stolen. No other guitar in the shop came close in the price range).

If buying on eBay, you will probably only be interested in a “name” guitar and even Martin makes the odd “dud” so beware. If looking through ads, the potential for a bargain is always there but it usually involves a lot of travel and wasted effort with disappointment if you miss out on a bargain.

I’ve done shops and ads, generally buying second hand from a shop can also be a good idea.

Lastly if you know exactly what you want to buy NEW, there are lots of Internet music shops selling instuments at heavily discounted prices. Thomannn is one such site (in Europe), I’m I’m sure there are others.
