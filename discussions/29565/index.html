Did you get a chance to have some tunes on St Patrick’s Day?

So did you get to have some tunes on St Patrick’s Day?
I had been tipped off that Mr Vulpes & the Fiddlin’ Sisters would be having a sneaky sesh starting at about 2pm in the Hibernian Sto’Vo’Kor. I got there and there was no sign of them by which time some football match had started on the big TV. They arrived about 45 mins after me and it was decided that we would wait for the sports game for ugly half-naked brutish scarred moronic men squabbling and grappling in the mud to finish. I waited and waited, these games drag on boring everyone forever. It eventually stopped and we sat down and got on with the business in hand: playing tunes. The pub’ was busy, raucous and increasingly busy. And then to my horror I found out another blinking football match (this time involving Ireland) was to start imminently. So we had to stop playing.
I left.
I went to the Wig Museum. It was completely rammed with hundreds of people I’ve never seen before. I went round the back and got in via the backdoor and kitchen. It was heaving. The same sports thing was occurring and the sound pounded out of the sound system.
But I was invited back into the kitchen away from the pandemonium and Norman Vader (box) and I played for about an hour to the pub’ landlord and a few select guests. So it turned out nice again!

Re: Did you get a chance to have some tunes on St Patrick’s Day?

I had a restaurant gig with a 3-piece. A few too many crowd pleasers for my liking, but the diners went wild.

After returning home, I went out to a friend’s 50th birthday do in a local pub. There was a line up that included various musical acts, ensemble and solo (ranging from W. African drumming to contemporary singer-songwritering), ending, ostensibly, with an ‘Irish’ session, what with it being Paddy’s Night. Having come from another gig, I only turned up at 12.30 - just in time for the session. As it turned out, there were only two traditional players there by this time - myself and a concertina player who plays mostly English music (what few Irish tunes he plays, he makes them sound like Morris tunes - but makes a great job of it). So, the two of us had a little sessionette until about 2.45am - until the landlord started to get nervous. Hardly a note of Irish music was played - just Morris tunes and umpty-dumpty English jigs - but it didn’t matter a jot. What was important was that the bar staff all had their cardboard Magners hats on and I have a beard and look like a leprechaun (alledgedly).

Re: Did you get a chance to have some tunes on St Patrick’s Day?

A local bar had a band cancel a week before Paddy’s. A rock/pop singer songwriter was given the job if he could find a fiddler and a rhythm section.
I (a fiddler) heard about this from some buskers and knocked on the fellow’s door. Over 4 evenings we cobbled together a set, found a drummer and then a bass player the day before the gig.
Voila! Nelson BC’s newest folk rock band.
We played 3 sets between 7:30 and midnight. Repeated some of the scant fiddle tunes we’d roughed out (very rough).
We butchered Glasgow Reel, I shouted out Whiskey in the Jar and Fisherman’s Blues but the majority of the tunes were the guitarist’s own rock songs.
All you ITM purists would have been horrified but we pulled it off; pleased a mostly young crowd.
I got a hundred bucks, a cheeseburger and 3 pints of Guiness out of the deal then crawled back into my hole until next Paddy’s day perhaps.

A fantastic session.

Re: Did you get a chance to have some tunes on St Patrick’s Day?

I can’t afford NOT to gig on St Patrick’s Day. I need the income!

This year was more quiet than most, only two gigs, one playing Highland pipes at a church hall for 15 minutes ($100) the other playing at a smallish quiet house party for around two hours, on uilleann pipes, SSPs, and various whistles ($300).

This house party has been my evening St Pat’s gig for over a decade, and a good one it is too, as I avoid being in a crowded noisy pub with thousands of drunk people.

Once again it was a “dry” St Pats, as I don’t drink while working, and music is my work.

No the church isn’t interested in the uilleann pipes! I have them with me and ask them every year but they only want the big pipes.

Then early Sunday morning it was a church gig with a trio (fiddle/guitar/uilleann pipes) that we’ve been doing for 7 years now, playing during the church service then playing in the church hall for a couple hours ($600).

Some St Pat’s I’ve had six or seven gigs, running around all day, but not this year! I often do golf course gigs in the morning (every golf course has a St Pat’s tournament it seems) but this year all of them were cancelled anyhow due to heavy rain and high winds all morning.
