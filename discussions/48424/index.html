Where are your eyes?

…silly question - or perhaps not so…?

Fiddle players, where are you looking when you play? (Apart from around to see all the admiring glances…)

Over the years (both decades on the mandolin) and decade-plus on the fiddle, I’ve got into the habit of watching my left-hand fingers - which is pretty stupid as they know where to go. It was recently suggested to me that I would be much better off keeping my eyes on the bow position on the strings. Apart from sending me cross-eyed, it seems to make sense, and perhaps even a difference.

Just curious to know what other people’s experiences are…

Re: Where are your eyes?

LOL, Judy and meself, glad you enjoyed it, just wanted to inject some whimsy into the notion that us fiddlers should be free to NOT watch our fingers or bows. There’s so much else to look at! :-)

For fiddlers, both hands play by feel—proprioperception—the ability we have to know where our body parts are without looking. It’s what enables us to toss a tennis ball over our head and catch it unseen behind our back. The same “magic” guides our bow hands in response to our ears reporting whether we’re producing the sounds we want.

Left-hand fingers know where to land because the ear tells them that’s the right spot, the right spacing from the other fingers, the frame of the hand, and the shape and feel of the neck.

The right hand can be a bit more challenging because it’s floating at the far end of a stick, far removed from the strings it’s trying to vibrate. Most of us go through an early phase of checking our bowing in a mirror, but it’s crucial to use that visual feedback to focus on how our bow hand and arm *feel* when they’re doing everything right and the sound is good. It helped me tremendously to think of my bow hand itself playing the strings, rather than the intermediary of the stick and hair. Once you have the feel, you won’t need your eyes to guide the bow.

When playing fiddle, your eyes aren’t needed to properly work your fingers and hands. Your eyes are free to look anywhere or nowhere at all.

There’s so much to listen to when playing an instrument, so many aural details; to me, a more revealing question would be, “Where are your ears?”

Re: Where are your eyes?

“I find that when I look at my left hand, my concentration on the tune improves.”

Yes, that’s probably why some people do it. Not so much as an aid in placing their fingers but as a point of focus that’s specifically about the tune. Looking around the room presents many opportunities for distraction. (Which is why I tend to look around the room. :-D )

Of the better fiddlers who seem to be looking at the fingerboard, I’d be surprised if any of them were actually visually paying attention to their fingering. That would be an unnecessary mental loop, likely to just get in the way. In fact, once you’re adept at fingering and bowing, focusing on the mechanics of how you’re fingering and bowing (while playing in a session or gig, say) is an excellent way to trip yourself up. To play fluently, the mechanics work best on autopilot.
