Dilemna!

How do you go about deciding which session to go to on any one evening?
Take today for instance.
Five minutes walk from my home will be a bijou but classy session with good friends; ten minutes on the ’bus is a fun but raucous session with friends and acquaintences; twenty minutes train ride and I’m at a large mainstream session with, usually, people I don’t know so well.
Do you go for comfy hyper-local (walk), party-like freakout (‘bus) or the ’I-wonder-what-will-happen?’ (train)?
Three great sessions, all different, spoilt for choice!

I hasten to add the tunes will be good at all. It’s the flavour of the sesh itself I’m thinking about.

And also there is another one about 30 mins on the train which I’ve never been to (although I know the pub’) and fancy checking out one day.

Additionall: I believe ‘dilemna’ really means two (di) things. Is there a word ‘trilemna’ or ‘quadlemna’?!

Re: Dilemna!

Personally, I would probably rule out session #2, unless I had a particular reason to go, as raucous isn’t really my thing, with or without friends. So we’re back to a *dilemma* (as opposed to a trilemma or tetralemma [‘Lemma’ being of Greek origin, the number prefix ought also to be Greek, hence ‘tetra-’].

How frequent are these sessions? If your local session is weekly, then you’ve not much to lose by checking out the further one - if you don’t mind travelling. Even a session with seemingly the perfect balance of friendship and musicianship (not that there need necessarily be a trade-off between the two) can lose its lustre if it becomes too much of a routine - just as a session benefits from the occasional visiting non-regular (or even, dare I say it, the occasional absence of a regular - not *yours* specifically, but anyone’s) , so a session regular can benefit from an occasional change of scene. So, if you’re feeling adventurous, I say go for the unknown; if you don’t like it, well, your round-the-corner session is the perfect excuse never to go again.

But even words formed from words in the first category with an added suffix, the ‘m’ and ‘n’ are both pronounced:

The only example I can think of with intervocalic ‘mn’ consisently pronounced ‘m’ is: damning

Re: Dilemna!

The “lemma” part of “dilemma”, in the sense we use it today, comes from the Greek word “lemma” (with a long “e” - eta). There is also a different Greek word “lemma” (with a short “e” - epsilon) that has the entirely unrelated meaning of “husk” or “skin”. In neither case is there a variant word “lemna” listed in Lewis and Short’s Greek Lexicon, which is probably the most authoritative dictionary of the language.

Dilemma- as it happens!

The session/ pub’ choices were (in no special order):
The Kentish Branch Line Tappers & Shunters Club
The Tooting Old Testament Re-Enactment Society
B B Kings’s Wig Museum (Finchley TX)
Plus the new unfamiliar one at ‘Mud-Door’

In the end I went local and had fab tunes (and songs) with John- box; Daire- flute; Tom- pipes & all manner of whistles; Peter- six string devil & vox Hibernia; Vincent- box (no singing this time!); myself- baritone ukulele & vox Londinium (plus a chap on yet another box whose name escapes me). We played and played and played and it was quality stuff despite my continuing mission to wreck it. On the whole there was only one box player at a time. We attempted to drink the pub’ (and to some extent the pub’ next door- Mr Choudhry’s Halal Deep Space Nine) dry and to fill the whole back garden with ash whilst putting the whole world to rights, including discussing cosmology, antique furniture & campanology as well as bragging about our hot water bottle collections. It must have been a great night judging by the lack of paper money left in my Levis this morning! And you know one must have a good one when the first thing you do when waking up the next morning, is creeping downstairs to check your instrument got home with you and was not left in that comfortable privet (Ligustrum ovalifolium) hedge halfway home! (Hello hedge! zzzzzzzz!)

Another reason for going local is this: every time I’ve been elsewhere on a Sunday night and I stroll in the local the next evening, John always always says, ‘Ah! You missed a grand session here last night Dave’.

By the way- I haven’t been doing well with spelling lately having recently got both ‘dyad’ and ‘dilemma’ wrong!
