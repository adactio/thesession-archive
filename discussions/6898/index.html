Where’s the Tune?

At the last couple of festival sessions I played, they were similar in that they were held in delightfully atmospheric pubs, serving great beer, the only disadvantage being a somewhat limited capacity for seating.

At the second of the two sessions there was space for maybe only 15 musicians. We’ve had some absolutely cracking sessions there in the past so everyone that goes there really wants to get in and play some tunes. Naturally the tendency is to arrive early to ensure you have somewhere to sit.

I arrived early (more by luck than judgement) when there were perhaps 3 or 4 seats remaining.

So there I was, pint on the table, back to the bar, feeling very pleased that I was all ready to go. I looked around the room and my immediate concern was that there appeared to be a lack of “lead” instruments!!! Just myself with a box, a fiddler, and a flute and …………………………………

………..9 bodhrans!!!!!!!!

Now I have nothing at all against goat bashers, in fact when in capable hands the drum can be a welcome addition to any session, but I lost count of the number of banjos, fiddles mandolins etc that I saw come into the bar only to discover that there was nowhere for them to sit before walking back out again.

Needless to say 9 bodhran make one hell of a rumble and you can guarantee by the laws of physics that something’s is going to rattle loose after a while. Unfortunately it was my patience ……and after about an hour I quietly left the bar, no doubt making room for a pair of Venezuelan guiro players or similar, who would have provided the perfect compliment.

Is this a worldwide phenomenon…..that the bodhran is on the increase? I’d be pleased to hear from session goers (including bodhran players) regarding their observations in recent years.

I don’t wish to stereotype the players, but I have noticed that this instrument does seem to have been an championed by “women of a certain age”……. Do you think it could be attributed to successive mild winters or the effects of global warming?? :)

Re: Where’s the Tune?

The secret is to pick your bodhranists. The tune session I go to has, out of 7 regular attendees and 2 to 4 irregulars, 4 bodhranists. HOWEVER, all of them are skilled, experienced, and *tactful*. We never get two of them playing at once, let alone all four, and usually only one tune in 5 or 6 gets accompanied at all. The general instinct of all four of them seems to be, “I should wait until we start a tune I can enhance instead of detract from”. It probably helps that three of the four play other instruments also (guitar & whistle), but in any case they are all very careful not to overwhelm or spoil the tunes.

So it seems to me that we need an Emily Post of bodhran etiquette to help the less tactful members of the breed to learn how to be more tactful.

And then, for those who decline to learn tact, there’s the knockout drops in their drinks strategy… ;-)
