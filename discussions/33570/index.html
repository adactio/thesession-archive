This feels more like a confession. Especially after reading 5stringfool’s comments about his sister and world peace….sometime’s I look at Lowden’s blackwood/redwood fan fret at 2 am and ignore my friend Ryan as he’s texting me (he has cancer)….I’d probably even choose it over world peace if while gazing at it my laptop screen spoke to me and gave me a choice between the two….:-/

Forgive me father, for I have lusted after other people’s instruments for a very long time….

It’s almost a year since I procured a concert pitch half set from Tim and Steph Benson in Ebony, Nickel-silver, and Boxwood. I highly recommend these guys, as the wait is a fraction of the time for your set, and they are passionately pursuing excellence in their art. Their work only continues to improve, and the set I have now is drool worthy for sure (Martino Vacca of Full Set drooled on my chanter at a recent seminar, and the president of our club remarked at the strength and beauty of the drones’ sound). I’m currently playing a loaner chanter in bubinga and brass, and it’s really good. The reed is great too, but the chanter is just what you want; volume, excellent feel with the scalloped holes, crispy responsiveness for ornaments, and last but not least, tone. My first ebony suffered some very slight warping due to some extreme humidity, and Tim is offered to replace it immediately without hesitation. I can’t wait to see the new one, and my 3 yr old boy will have a great chanter to play when he grows up if he should choose to wrestle the Irish octopus like his crazy old man (the warping was so slight that it can be adjusted and tweaked into near perfect tune).

I told my wife before we were married (one of the smarter things I’ve done in my life- notice it was BEFORE marriage ;-) ) that I would “be at peace” with about $30,000 more in instrument purchases. I had a good pile of instruments then, and we had a good discussion. It’s paid off, since shocking her with that big number right off the bat conditioned her to be less alarmed when whistles, a half set, a flute, and soon to be bari/tenor regulators, started coming through the door.

Guitars…sigh…many loves here. I love my Lowden in Cedar top and Mahogany back/sides, and it’s about 16 yrs old. The main guitar porn that comes across my laptop screen these days is from George’s FB page. Now that he’s celebrating 40 yrs in the business, he’s done some lovely new videos and pictures, showcasing new designs. Any model in blackwood back/sides and California Redwood top would have me drooling to my grave…sigh..
Here’s a link that is worthy of a view for guitar lovers, my “lotto-ax” is at 8:00: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lov_ystdnUA

An excellent review of a Blackwood/Adirondack Spruce guitar w/vids: vhttp://onemanz.com/guitar/reviews/acoustic-guitars/lowden/o50c-african-blackwood/

I told ‘c’ once that I would have no more and no less than 7 full sets of uilleann pipes if I hit it big. Of course I would mix up the makers. Rogge, Wooff, K&Q, would get me started. My favorite woods are Ebony and Boxwood. Some new makers are fashioning sick sets of pipes, like this guy (grab a handkerchief or don a bib): http://www.nakatsuipipes.com/Pages/default.aspx

The MK pro low F I have is great, as are my Overtons (Goldie). I had two beloved whistles stolen years ago: a Bernard Overton session D, and a Burke ’00 composite “session” C. They were, in my mind, perfection. I can’t really fathom better instruments in these pitches, they were so good. I wish I didn’t know the person who stole my C, who moved away just after the heist.
I would certainly put at least a handful more Burke whistles on my list should I win the Lottery, first purchase being a composite low F…

A Grinter and an Olwell flute…not sure which I’d choose in the “flat” key….

Lastly I’d pick up a Fender strat and Fender amp. It’s funny how long I’ve gone now without these two things since I sold them to buy traditional instruments years ago. Kind of a joke amongst my friends, as I played guitar 2 nights/wk at our two biggest music pubs in town from ‘07-’10 and just used whatever was there. Would be nice to let off a little steam now and then on one…;-)

BB-a lute looks pretty much like an overgrown mandolin, which makes sense because technically a mandolin is a type of lute. A Baroque lute, however is a very special animal-it has between 10 and 13 courses(pairs of strings like a mandolin, except the highest string which is single.) The last few bass courses are above the neck and can only be played open. So, between 19 and 25 individual strings to tune.:) The one in the link above has very ornate mother-of -pearl inlays on the fingerboard and neck and is in my opinion very beautiful. Here is a link to a fine performance of Bach on the lute.http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z_SdaFwQXKI&list=UU5QaoO9pYtGfyC39EEsa_Tg
For other videos just type in Baroque lute in you tube. There are pages and pages.
