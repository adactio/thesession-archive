If the concern is about a potentially violent situation, then it’s worth remembering that someone is in that situation already. It’s important to not be stupid, but ethically speaking if you’re worried that this particular junkie might become violent then I’d think it’s imperative to find an effective intervention, not to turn away and ignore the situation.

One tip, if you’re thinking about intervening in a situation of this sort, is to keep in mind that you don’t need to engage with the other party at all if you don’t want to. If you see someone who seems to be trapped in a conversation with someone, you can talk to that person and offer them a way out - there’s lots of options, most of them are fine. Some examples - “Jane, I’ve been looking for you, can you come here for a minute, I want you to look at something for me”. “Helen, I hear that there’s some good tunes at the Rusty Bucket, do you want to come along?”. “Sue, that was a great tune you played earlier, the e minor jig, can you come over here and show me how it goes?”

Really, anything will do, and there’s no need to engage with the offending party at all. In fact, it’s probably best all in all to just go right around them. If the goal is to get someone out of an uncomfortable situation safely, then there’s nothing to be gained by mixing it up with the harasser at that time.
