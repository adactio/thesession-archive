Reel Rhythms

As I have been spending some time lately comparing players from previous generations to “modern” players it seems to me that they play reels differently. Older players tend to accent the second beat(I’m counting in cut time) while younger players will accent the up-beats.For example on Paddy Taylor’s wonderful Boy in the Gap LP he takes breaths on the second beat roughly 80% of the time while Kevin Burke is much more likely to accent the “and” of beat number one. Am I totally off-base here? Is this a generational thing or maybe a regional thing? I would be the first to admit that as an American my grasp of the subtlties of regional differences is nowhere near as firm as someone raised in the tradition. Or is it just a personal difference between players?

As a flute player, it seems to me that the most easily identified styles are a Sligo style that tends to follow the rhythm of a galloping horse in playing reels and the northern Belfast style that favors more of an oom-pah, oom-pah type of rhythm. The Sligo style is a smoother, kind of cascading sound, whereas the Belfast style has a more pronounced punch to the rhythm. I’m not Irish, so I hope I’ve got the regionality right.

Re: Reel Rhythms

I’m big on clarity, and what’s happening in this discussion to muddy it is 1) some people are talking about polka rhythms instead of reel rhythms and 2) people are talking in circles because they’re using different time signatures for reels 2/2 v 4/4 etc.

If we stick to reels the way they’re usually written, with four eighthnotes to the bar, what I think of as American Old-Time bowing (or perhaps the caricature of American Old-Time bowing) is

one two THREE four five six SEVEN eight

This might be what you guys mean by “backbeat” or “offbeat” or “syncopation” with certain Irish fiddlers.

I thought that Kevin Burke, with that album, was purposely and consciously mimicking American Old-Time fiddling. Could be wrong, but that’s what it sounds like to me.
