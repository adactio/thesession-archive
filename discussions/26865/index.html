Re: G# on flute

To bend a note, you should not be turning the flute, but rather adjusting the airflow with your lips. Hence the phrase “Lipping it”. Try turning the flute at speed and sooner or later you’ll drop it.

I have fair success with XXoXXX or XXoXXo for low G# and XXoXoX for middle G#. Half holing works too. This is on a fairly large holed Pratten-style flute. Eflat/Dsharp is impossible (in any sense of a usable, working note) on a D keyless flute.

Venting the Eflat key on a keyed flute is only a good idea if the flute was designed to do that - most antiques were, but many modern ones aren’t. Check with your maker if it is a modern flute.

Re: G# on flute

You seem confused about what half-holing means. It is not lipping. Lipping G# is a terrible habit to get into because it affects your tone. You definitely don’t want to roll the flute! Half-holing means putting your finger down so that the G hole is half-covered. I play in pipers grip so for G# I tend to put my finger down so that it lands to the outside of the hole, but you can also half-hole by setting your finger down above or below the hole. I’ve also seen people go to the far side of the hole by arching their fingers “past” the hole.

With creativity, many G# can be avoided with variations. As others mentioned, you can also slide through them, though if you do it for every G# your session mates are going to tire of that rather quickly.

Re: G# on flute

Ben, let me first say that a fingering chart will indeed show the Eb (or, more properly, D#) used for venting. But for purposes of this discussion (ie, ITM), it would be almost impossble to use because it is extremely awkward. As proof that Irish players don’t use it, take a look at almost every notable Irish player (including Matt Malloy) and you will see that almost all of them twist the tail piece so that the D# key faces forward. In other words, they place all the keys on the tailpiece into an unplayable position.

Venting gives a very slightly more open sound that is only apparent by direct comparison to having the key closed. Also, the notes that can be vented are arguably more open that the D, C# and C that cannot be similarly vented. If you compare the vented E to D and then compare the unvented E to D, the timbre of the unvented E better matches the D, IMO. In the end, though, I think you will find the difference negligable.

Re: G# on flute

The notion of keeping the D# key depressed seemed so weird to me that I decided to check Grey Larsen’s book “Essential Guide To Irish Flute and Tin Whistle” to see what he had to say on the subject. Grey takes a fairly academic approach in his book, so I imagined his opinion would be beneficial to the discussion. He notes that keeping your pinky on the key is okay if it is comfortable, but that it should not be depressed as it would be on a Boehm because it is “rarely workable” on a simple-system flute. I also noted that his eight-key fingering chart does not show the D# key used to vent the flute. Oh, and he also notes that venting tends to make the note, especially E, sharp.
