Re: Where have all the flower’s gone?

>>“I’d be interested if you could find this usage common before 1960 or even later. I sincerely doubt it. ”

From wordorigins.org:

"The fact is that woman has been used adjectivally since the Middle English period. The earliest citation in the OED of woman as adjective is from a Wycliffite translation of the Bible from before 1382, which renders 3 Kings 17:9 as “a womman widuwe.” (The 1611 Authorized (King James) version flips it, rendering 1 Kings 17:9 as “a widow woman.” Note that some versions of the Bible label the two books of Samuel as 1 Kings and 2 Kings. So what is 1 Kings in some versions is called 3 Kings in others.) But there are even earlier uses known. The Ancrene Riwle, a monastic rulebook for women written prior to 1200, has in at least one manuscript—Cambridge Corpus Christi College 402, copied around 1230—the following:

For swuch ah wummone lare to beonne luuelich & liðe & selthwenne sturne.
(For such a woman teacher to be kind and gentle and seldom harsh.)

Nor is the attributive use limited to centuries past, but has continued through the ages, with writers like Dryden, Pope, and Dickens using woman as the first element in a hyphenated noun, such as “woman-warrior” or “woman-doctor.” Others have used it as a straight-up adjective, like Fynes Moryson who in 1671 described Sappho as a “woman poet,” or Matthew Prior who in 1706 wrote of Queen Anne, “the Woman Chief is Master of the War,” or The Guardian itself which disregarded its own rule in 1979 and described Margaret Thatcher as “Britain’s first woman Prime Minister.”

So neither history nor current usage, which seems to find nothing wrong in the adjectival use of woman, is on the side of a general proscription. "

Re: Where have all the flower’s gone?

I peeked into the discussion forum after not looking at it for maybe 5 years and this is the first thing I see. Before that I had given up on it, probably around the time facebook arrived on my computer because I found the discussions of the music much more genuine and positive there. Also, there isn’t any anonymity that tends to insulate people from irresponsible and uncivil discourse. I think the Session Dot Org is a very useful site with the tune database… warts and all, but the discussion forum might have fallen victim to the more advanced communication technology that other sites present that lends themselves to a healthier expression.

I enjoyed this forum in the early days when it was the fun place to share antidotes and opinions related to trad music, but I became weary of the pedantic overreach from a few contributors. I was told (by some of the people being canonized in this thread) that the sessions we have here in San Francisco aren’t “real Irish sessions” and I should inform visiting musicians of that fact before we commence and mislead them. It seemed odd to me that anyone would have the audacity to make such a demand, but that was sometimes the nature of the discourse in here.

Another thing I grew tired of was all the negativity expressed towards stalwarts of trad. It was disconcerting to see someone like Martin Hays getting so much grief from musicians that could barely play a few tunes at a session let alone ever rise to the accomplishment of someone like Martin. It seemed like people were staking their reputations in here by making bold negative assertions about players on the world stage. I never see that sort of thing happen on facebook. There were also people who delivered things like anti-bodhran dogma as if it was handed down from God on a stone tablet. There was a fair amount of bullying too if someone didn’t agree with the assumed forum consensus. It just became a huge waste of time for me because discussions would spiral out of control only to be detonated into oblivion long after something could have been done about it with member’s bloodied body parts strewn through the debris.

I can only speak for myself as to why I left, others will have their own reasons, of course, but I’m sure the response I get to this post will only underline what I’ve said. But on a lighter note, I’ll leave you all with this article in The Drone that reminded me of the Session Dot Org discussion forum.
