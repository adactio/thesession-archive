Democracy or autocracy

Things seem a bit too calm in mustardia so I thought I’d revive a dead horse and take him for a wee gallop. :)

The paid session….. Yes, no, or it depends?

Pro: it’s good for pubs because it is a way to guarantee that musicians will show up and not leave it to chance, which from a landlord’s perspective is a crap business model. It’s good for the musicians who are being paid, as they get compensation for their efforts. Who wouldn’t want 20 to 40 quid for just having some tunes?

Con: it can create weird and not entirely pleasant power dynamics at a session. The paid musicians assume a kind of ownership of the session and run it like their little autocracy. There gets to be tension boiling just under the surface between the paid musos and the unpaid ones.

And away we go. I’ll bring the popcorn.

Re: Democracy or autocracy

All of the sessions around here are paid sessions - there are two or three people who are getting a modest fee to be there and make sure there’s at least a few musicians playing. All of the musicians get at least some consideration from the bar - usually, there’s a tab for the session and you’re on your own after that’s used up.
The sessions don’t seem to be hurt by it. It ensures that at least a few good players will be there, and nobody seems very bothered if we stop and talk for ten minutes at a time, so I don’t think it’s interrupting the chat very much. (Usually if someone gets antsy and starts playing, it’s not the paid anchors)

There’s also no sense of “ownership” in the sense of the paid anchors dictating what tunes are to be played or any of that. Of course, they’re pretty strong players, so they play a lot, while those of us who don’t know a bazillion tunes might play on fewer tunes, but that’s down to ability, not ownership.

It seems to work pretty well this way here, I’m sure other customs work fine elsewhere.
