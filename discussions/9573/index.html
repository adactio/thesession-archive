Re: Which guitars…

Spruce….what kind John? I am a recent convert back to Sitka tops, I know, uncool in these days of Engleman, Adirondack , German, etc. Nothing in my opinion beats a wide grain sitka top, more projection and depth than the other spruces.

i’d say if you want loud and projection and power try some in stores with the wide grain. Even a laminate cheapy can sound good if the top is solid wide grain sitka.

just a humble opinion from someone with way, way, way too many guitars. The top wood is the most important, more than the maker. Go drive a salesperson in a music store nuts and try them all one day, LOL!

Must say I never met a Gibson I liked though, there’s something dull and heavy about the sound, but maybe good for rock or blues or something I am not quite getting.

Re: Which guitars…

Several of the best guitars I’ve met had Sitka tops and I just ordered a customized Martin with Sitka top. More than one luthier has told me that the value of the tight-grain spruces (Adirondack, Engleman, etc.) is as much cosmetic as acoustic. I remember playing a really great sounding D-18 with the widest-grain (Sitka) top I’ve ever seen. The grain wasn’t even straight and it fanned out to more than a quarter inch at the end. Based on the stuff you can read about choosing tone woods, it should’ve sounded lousy and probably crumbled under the string tension. It was actually magnificent.

Iris, there are some great Gibsons out there, but it took me a few decades to realize it. One of my friends trades constantly and he’s had a few Gibsons that could hold their own anywhere. The other 97% were just as you said.

Re: Which guitars…

Interesting Bob…. I am getting very educated these days, just finished making my second guitar. Both OMs, same guitar. The first is Indian Rosewood and Engleman. It’s loud, bright and sweet, very balanced. Great fingerstyle guitar, louder than most any dreadnaught I have played it against if you want it to be.

The new one, my teacher asked what do I want in the sound…I said I want a great backing guitar, deep, warm, lots of punch. He said try wide grain sitka, I got the widest I could find in his shop. The back and sides are walnut, with a three piece back, a brazilian rosewood center. He says it’s the top that matters most. I got just what I wanted, it’s even louder than the first one, great for fingerstyle and backing too. It’s way warmer and deeper and louder than the first. He says it’s the top mainly. Walnut is a really overlooked wood in guitars and it’s great sounding and beautiful if you get the figured woods with great patterning.

Also apparently the sitka mellows with age and the others pretty much don’t or not near as much. I will only use sitka, wide grain from now on. The next, am thinking koa, just to see what will happen. Maybe maple center strip, who knows. That’s on schedule for August.

have lost my mind, swore I wouldn’t buy any more guitars. But making them doesn’t count, right? Don’t know what on earth I will do with them, certainly don’t want to work as a luthier, but it sure is fun to make them. Right now the second one is getting a French polish….that’s fun too, and a beautiful finish. They will all be played for sure!

Re: Which guitars…

What many people don’t realise is that a lot of players don’t necessarily bring their “best” guitars to gigs (for what I hope are fairly obvious reasons….!!!).

For example Arty McGlynn has a prized Yamaha FG180 which is exclusively used for studio work. For the gigs it’s a Takamine or equivalent. I’m sure a lot of other name players are the same.

The responses above show a huge amount of variation in what people are using, to me it very much depends on the sound you want. I have a Joe Foley guitar which is great for ITM because it has a big bass sound and so it can fill the “hole” in acoustic sessions. But for amplified gigs I use a Guild D30 mainly because it has a superb Fishman pickup built in. It’s a nice guitar as well…….!!

But when at home I have another Joe Foley guitar with Brazilian rosewood back/sides which is so good it doesn’t get outside the house that often….

So I guess that the guitars used for gigging may not always be the ultimate choice of the musician playing them.
