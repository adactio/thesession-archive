Nice Slow Albums

Hi everyone,
I’m trying to brainstorm some CDs that are notoriously slow that perhaps learners can play along to. I’m learning fiddle, but my technique is so slow that I can’t really play along to too many recordings. (Playing too fast for one’s own good isn’t a very accurate way of progressing, and I just end up practicing mistakes). I also don’t want to impede on the other musicians at sessions yet, so I’m getting quite lonely just playing fiddle solo at home.

So far I’m playing along to Seamus Creagh’s Tunes for Practice and Martin Hayes & Dennis Cahill’s the Lonesome Touch. Any other suggestions? (I’m not trying to copy their style blindly, just for the physical practice of listening to others while playing).

Thanks!

Re: Nice Slow Albums

There are some great slow records, but I don’t think they’re really the best for this sort of thing.
I mean, you might not want to sit down with the Mary Bergin and the Frankie Gavin to start off with, but if you’re having trouble hearing a tune it’s probably because you’re having trouble listening, not because the tune is too fast for you. Get used to hearing the beat, and how the tune is placed against it. Grab hold of a piece of the tune and use that for leverage to get the surrounding bits. Get the tune in your head and use your brain to slow it down.

Verena Commins and Julie Langan made a fantastic record of tunes, which are played at a perfect pace and with a perfect touch, and I’d have you listen to that record, but that’s because it’s a great record with great tunes - not just because it’s a little slower than average.

Jon, jcawley isn’t a beginner musician, just a beginner on fiddle. I doubt she has any trouble hearing the tunes at tempo. But playing along (with an unwieldy bow) can be a challenge.

Anyway, it does help to play along with another musician--live or recorded--but never underestimate the progress that can be made just playing slowly by yourself, making the music on your fiddle come ever closer to the music you hear in your head. Ultimately, that’s what will get you where you want to go.

Re: Nice Slow Albums

If you want to play the tune at a slow pace, play it at a slow pace. What’s hard about that? If you want to play with someone, play with someone. Again, I don’t see the difficulty.

As far as changing the tune, it is true that slowing down a tune artificially does change the ornaments beyond recognition. If you drop the pace enough to make a difference (“Even half speed is too fast”…) then you’ll start hearing the ornamentation as taking place in time, which is wrong. This will throw the whole pacing of the tune off.

I say, just play the tune.

Re: Nice Slow Albums

taking no position on the pros and cons of “playing along,” here are some, er, “slow” cds. the “er” is in there because these records not “slow.” they are at the relaxed end of “to speed.” the fact that the tempos on the list i’m making are often termed “slow” (by those my teacher called “the dipsh$$$$ts” and i myself call “the turistas”), is a reflection of an extremely regrettable trend over the last twenty years wherein hyper-speed playing has come to be thought of as normal, and relaxed-speed playing has come to be thought of as “slow.” these records aren’t only great for practicing. they’re great for getting into your dreams and your cellular/molecular system, the beat, the swing, and the emotion of this wonderful music. even if you later intend to become a hyper-speed person, the rhythm of this music is the basis of the essentials of good high-speed playing (it’s not just playing to time signature, that is the least of it---steady beat and swing).

These are recordings of or including fiddle. Some are in flat keys. That means the “D tunes” are in C, the “G tunes” are in F, the “E minor tunes” are in d minor, etc.
there is computer technology to make copies moving the keys up if you need that.

1. “The Green Mountain,” solo fiddle, Pat O’Connor County Clare
2. “Conversation at the Crosses,” Pat O‘Connor on fiddle, Eoin O’sullivan on accordion, County Clare
3. Solo fiddle CD by Galway fiddler Breda Keville, “The Hop Down.”
4. Joe Ryan, “An Buachaill Dreoite,” You can hear the tracks at CD Baby. aren’t they great? http://www.cdbaby.com/cd/joeryan
5. “The Thing Itself,” Maeve Donnelly (Galway) on fiddle, Peadar O’Loughlin (Clare) on flute
6. “Tracin’ ,” duo by Patrick Ourceau on fiddle, Gearoid O Hallmhorain on concertina
7. Both duo cds by fiddler Caoimhín Ó Raghallaigh and piper mick o’brien……these are mostly in b-flat. that means the “D tunes” are in b-flat, and the “G tunes” are in e-flat, etc. again, you can use software to copy them up to conventional keys if you are not ready to learn flat keys…..or, just listen to them. you won’t be able to stop doing that anyway.

“Kitty Lie Over”
“Deadly Buzz” (Just out)

8. Self-titled first solo CD by accordion/concertina player John Williams, guest tracks by Martin Hayes on fiddle, MIcho Russell on whistle. [John Williams can and will often play very fast, but this CD is full of gorgeous relaxed-speed music….]

9. Somebody already mentioned the east clare concertina player mary macnamara. all of her recordings are wonderful for what you want, just mentioning the first one, “music from east clare,” or something like that, has numerous tracks where there is fiddle by martin hayes and/or his father, pj hayes.

10. “Melting Snow,” a solo fiddle CD by east clare fiddler Orla Harrington. Available through Custys, Ennis, Clare.

A couple not featuring fiddle. There is lots more, but here are a couple:

11. The solo cd by the late concertina player Kitty Hayes, and her duo cd with piper Peter Laban, “They’ll be Good Yet.”

12. “An Crann Carraige, Concertina Melodies from Tuamgraney, East Clare,” by concertina player Martin O’brien. I think Custys in Ennis, Clare, might still have it or know how to get it.
