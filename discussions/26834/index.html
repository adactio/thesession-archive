The Explanation: A New Musical Journey

(Please read all)
A basic explanation of what has been going on, is that i’m coming up on a new musical season and i’ve been trying to get prepared for it. This upcoming year, i’m changing my focus completely from Piano, to Violin/Fiddle. So before that year started i’ve been trying to collect information, and find some good resources, because i didn’t know how to research this music. Google, Wikipedia, and Bing, werent friendly to me and too many people post junk on youtube, so i couldn’t find the good stuff no matter how hard i tried.

So why have i been so impatient? Because i haven’t felt this much inspiration and motivation in a long time, and i was afraid that the inspiration would leave me at any moment, and take the motivation with it. So before it went away, i at least wanted to collect an abundance of information for my studies this year, so i wouldn’t be discouraged at how hard it is to research this stuff. But now thats not a problem anymore, because you all have given me all the resources i need :)

I’ve learned a lot here, and i will be using the information i’ve gathered here for this new season of music, which begins the 28th of this month, So i’ll eventually have seen all the clips and videos that have been sent to me. I’ll eventually have re-read and studied all the comments here, and documents sent with them.

Over this year i’ll also be visiting sessions that i’ve found out are in Austin. I called the place that was sent to me, and they told me to come on that night any week, but to call before i go because i’m underage. And i have some other emails and phone calls to make.

Why haven’t i made comments and replied to every little thing sent to me? Because i didn’t expect to be this popular and recieve this much help. I couldn’t keep up with everything given here because i didn’t have time to. I have a job, and other obligations that keep me occupied. And i also don’t have internet at my house, so i have to post and do research, limited to only a few hours a day…. if that many, and find a place with free WIFI. And recently i haven’t even had my computer.

So have i really been not listening? No, i have been. But it seems like i haven’t because i’ve been taking note of everything said here, as well as disconnecting here and there to actually do a little bit of the research.

SO tell me if i’m right about these things
The most important thing is to listen, be patient, and absorb the music, and eventually, i’ll begin to understand it. -The Majority

Listen listen listen is what i’ve gotten here. And thanks to some of the members here, you know who you are, for sending me all of the videos and Websites with videos that i will be on intensely! I can’t wait till i start working on “Bad fiddle playing vs. Good fiddle playing.” But 1st i’ll get an ear for the good fiddle playing.

Also, thanks for all of the Artist Names, spread all over my threads. I definetly didn’t know any Artists to start with, which also hindered my research. But now i have a few names. Tommy Peoples, Paddy Canny(and haha, more Paddy’s) John Dougherty, and many others!

What seems to be the most important is finding people who play that i can visit sessions with and see and hear what is actually going on. I plan on visiting one place that i mentioned a couple paragraphs ago, as well as getting in touch with some folks given to me a couple threads ago.

You all have also given me books to help me with my technique, and books to help me learn if i can’t find a good teacher. I will try and find one to take those 1-2 1st music lessons. As well as books that i should stay away from. And i will stay away from tune collections in sheet made by non-Irish artists

I have also learned that it will be better for me to learn by ear than by reading, because in THIS music what we hear, can’t always be written down. So i’ll keep training my ear and won’t worry about the notes right now. I’ll take my time, because the ear training i’ll be doing with this instrument, will definetly help me with piano.

I’ll also take a look at the dances and check out all what tune styles that are being played with them, because some of you have let me know that all of this is DANCE music, outside of the Airs.

During the later part of this year, hopefully i’ll have been exposed enough to this music, that i can actually pick some dots, and play the music, not the dots. Just use the dots for reference when learning… Later.

And finally, i’ll be listening to singing as well, so i can actually begin to get a feel of how the music should sound, and how i should play it, because i’ve learned that the tunes come from the language. I won’t worry about translations and lyrics. But i will still collect them, because Gaelic words are pretty to look at(Really, i like reading them, even though i can’t understand them)
So is all of this correct?

The Last Rant: If i haven’t said it all, i’ll say it here. This upcoming year, i will focus INTENSELY on Irish Traditional Music, to set up a good foundation for the rest of my life in This music, as well as all of the other music i play. Thanks for helping out a younger Musician get prepared for a New Musical Journey. I don’t think i’ve ever been on a musical journey like this. And also, don’t be frightened when i use words like “research” and “study” All those words mean to me, is “Learning about something that i enjoy experiencing, so i can later have enough knowledge to actually do them.” I love listening to the music but nothing is like “playing” it: “Enjoying something that i have learned how to do, and having fun with it”

I’ll post a thank you to everyone that helped me out, but as i’ve been taking note of names…. there are just too many to do that with right now. Thanks everyone! for Everything! Now i’m ready to actually begin this New journey!

I want to learn ITM, Irish Traditional music, because i’ve always wanted to. It’s fun, intriguing, different, I love the culture, and the language is beautiful. I want my musical expression to extend past the boundaries of my own mind. I want to feel the same thing you all feel when you play. Joy.

Thanks again everyone. You have been a great help.

Ah, so you are who I thought you were … You’d still prefer to be called Jerone, I guess?

Anyway … honestly just trying to help here: you appear to have many good traits, but one of the most annoying is your tendency, not just with the term “musicologist” but with other things as well, to repeatedly insist things are the case when knowledgeable people here know that it isn’t and tell you so.

Let’s have one, very brief, crack at the “musicologist” thing … The problem seems to me to be that you’ve got the definition wrong. You started off (on another thread) saying that it was someone who studied how to make melodies. But it isn’t. You’ve now changed that to someone who studies music. But it isn’t. A musicologist would, in very brief terms (though I agree with cboody, you’d need a course of study even to properly define the term) be someone who HAS STUDIED (i.e. has already put in the decades of work) music and has compared the different musics around the world and understands the differences. *Not* just someone who is in the course of studying such things (which it doesn’t appear to me that you are, but that’s another story …).

So, you confused Gaels and Celts and implied that the Welsh were Gaelic, in an early post. A musicologist would not have done that. You repeatedly called “tunes” “songs”. Now that is a basic mistake that even a 4 year old kid starting out in the tradition of any of the musics of Britain and Ireland would not have made, let alone a musicologist. You don’t appear to understand how music links together over history - which is in fact the very first building block for someone *aspiring* to be a musicologist, as opposed to someone who already is.

In short, the term “musicologist” implies as it is generally used, someone who knows a lot more about music than you do … yet … Maybe one day, in thirty years’ time or so, provided you have spent almost every waking hour not just living and breathing music, but *studying* it and the way it fits together over history and between different cultures, you might end up as a musicologist. In the meantime, let’s be a bit more humble.

To put it in context, I was very fortunate to know intimately one of the most knowledgeable people there has ever been concerning the music of Britain and Ireland. He died aged 82, having spent his whole life - *from birth*, literally, having been born into a family of famous musicologists - studying the music of these islands. If you went round his house, you could find that you’d just spent 18 hours or so just talking about music, and the time had just flown. He was known around the world for his knowledge of the music. And yet he would not have claimed to be a “musicologist”. He was too humble for that.

Just saying …

The Frog in the journey

Is this a parable? Should I use the real story or make adjustments?

I’ll make adjustments to protect the innocent… ;-).

There was this frog that desperately wanted to get to the other side of a very large gorge, steep sides both ways. Now he could have made his way slowly back down one side of the rise he was on, and them back up the other. There was a clear path, no problem, and this was a young and healthy frog. But he was set in taking what he thought was the quickest path, to jump the gorge. So he began an intense program of exercise, building up muscle, and practicing high and long jumps. He was intent on doing this. In the end he started having problems with bursitis, but he ignored the pain and continued, then he tore a ligament, but he considered that minor and with great focus moved past that pain ~ until he resiously did in his carilege in the joints and on one particular high jump came down hard and broke his knee completely in half. No amount of effort could get him any further. In fact his jumping ability reduced in height and length until he couldn’t jump at all. Now even the idea of going the well trodden path was too much effort. He was stuck where he was, still a frog, but a frog that now couldn’t even jump. He was am miserable old fart of a frog and he never did see the other side except at a distance.

I know a piper whose intense study lead to RSI and he having to give up the pipes completely for a few years, and then completely retraining himself, slowly, and to take it more at ease, without the tension that intensity has a tendency to build in…
