Re: Plaiyng ITM outside Ireland

Here in Vermont (Republic of: US) there has always been a rich tradition of fiddle playing as entertainment when there was little else. Who are Vermonters descended from? Well, you can guess there is no such thing as a “real American” around here that didn’t come from somewhere else. New England style music has flavors of Irish in it for sure. Am I stating the obvious? So this new influx of traditional fiddling that has moved into Vermont from outside Vermont (to a large extent), this younger generation is now merging with what is left of the older players. And since Vermont is still largely a rural state, traditional music can still have a life in local town halls and kitchens. I would not say it’s thriving everywhere…there are pockets where the revival is strong…but it is not thought of as being something wierd. Any “real Vermonter” will smile to see a fiddle being pulled out of its case under most circumstances, and whether its an Irish tune, or an Applalchian tune, a French Canadian tune or something Uncle Frank used to play…its sure to spark the memory of many rural folks who used to expect such live fare on the long winters evenings of their youth.

Re: Plaiyng ITM outside Ireland

Back to the topic for a sec (but not totally abandoning the Portugese connection…) As an immigrant to Canada I’m sensitive to not trying to force my exported culture on the neighbours. I seek out folks who enjoy playing or listening to ITM or anything close to it, but I avoid getting irritated if people either want it to conform to stereotypes (Danny B, Whiskey itj, Unicorn, etc.) or just plain don’t like it (“playing the same tune all night long.”)

Portugese content: I find fado singing a bit inaccessible (and, yes, maybe a bit “the same tune all the time”) and I wouldn’t want someone to impose their expectations of my intelligent appreciation of it on me if I chanced into a “fado session” in full swing in my local pub.

Zina, even in the seventies many people discounting the Chieftans because they weren’t “traditional” (i.e., they “arranged” their tunes. Round about ’76 I remember a lot of the hard core trad audience heading for the bar when the Chieftans appeared on stage at the Lisdoonvarna folk festival.
