Re: Ryanair & instruments - what’s the current state of play??

my younger son lately went to Rome by RyanAir for the rugby, and had a cheap trip.

You need to choose your destination with care, not take any check-in baggage and not have any ongoing connections - because if you miss them, and RyanAir flights are often problematical in that respect, you have lost the price of your onward journey and have no recourse at all, because that is one of the “frills” RyanAir DON’T provide.

Nor do they accept any obligation at all, even the ones they are clearly required to by law, in respect of passengers stranded by weather or volcanic ash, as they clearly demonstrated a couple of years ago.

What you get for your money is a seat on one specific flight, and no other, the timing of which may vary at their sole discretion and with no liability implied or accepted for the consequences. If the flight is cancelled, you need another ticket.

They have a very bad reputation at Stansted for problems such as being unable to check in online and being charged £25 ( I think at present ) for being able to check in.

Personally, I don’t favour them because my travel patterns don’t suit their business model, YMMV as they say. There used to be a much-detested “cattle-truck class” commuter flight from Luton to Aberdeen, much favoured by contract workers on fixed per diem allowances.

I have successfully travelled on Ryan Air for the first time on a direct flight from Dublin to Frankfurt-Hahn (Germany). Re: Ryanair & instruments - what’s the current state of play??

A couple of rules if your hand luggage is a bit over the limit

1/ Never stand near the front of the queue for boarding (sometimes they seem like they have a quota to achieve, let them achieve it with someone else, never do priority boarding)
2/ Hide yourself in the queue for as long as possible, in behind others
2/ If you feel they may check your bag while borading, always ask an easy question that they can answer positively as you give the boarding pass “can I get a sandwich on the flight” “does it matter if my boarding pass is ripped, I’m so sorry etc..” , stupid questions tend to knock them out of their procedure
3/ Never say you have a musical instrument (theorectically you have to pay for a musical instrument regardless of whether it meets the required size, a concertina player got stung on that rule once.
4/ Never show your hand luggage at the check-in desk, say you don’t have any.
