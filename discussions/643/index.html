Martin Hayes

Reading a recent discussion on influences I was fascinated to see that a lot of you regard martin hayes as a major influence and my question is why ?

Personally, Ive never been able to take to his style, its never done anything for me as a trad musician Its just very slow and gives me the dragged out feeling I know that up north here we apparently play too fast for dancing but surly isn t his way too slow ?

I think there just isn t much life in it. Soul yes, but life, very little. And I don t know why either because I ve had a great session once with Martin Hayes, Mairead Mooney and Dermot Byrne and he played in what I would describe as a Sligo type style with great life and flair which I found a joy to listen to. so much so that the next day I went out and bought one of his albums -the lonesome touch, But I was shocked to hear the difference. In his albums and stage performances, there is none of the joy and lift to be found, and I don t mean that he doesn t play fast enough either, he plays about the same speed as Noel Hill & Tony Linane but the difference is unbelievable. So I ve come to a conclusion that its just a stage act .

I don t think that it represents clare music properly either in style because clare music should have loads of life and rhythm in it for a perfect example noel hill and tony linnane , Frankie Gavin, paddy canny etc.(whos playing I admire) Compared to them, martins music seems dragged out almost like the playing of Dublin fiddler tommy potts .Traditional music has more emotions to it than the lonesome touch.

I respect that people have different tastes and I hope that this doesn t offend anybody that likes his music. Its just a question that been bugging me and I ve listened to his albums to try and understand what it is that draws people to his music and hopefully now you ll let me know.

Thanks.

↳ Posted by b.maloney on June 17th, 2002.

Hey, somebody INVITED me to climb on the soapbox!

Heh -- sorry, it’s a bad habit that when I tease someone, I add “ly” onto their name. Anyway, how should I know, Bradford? I’ve never heard every single Clare style player and player who plays in Clare. Have you? I heard people whose names I don’t know in pubs I can’t quite recall the names of, playing similar things to the way Martin plays things while I was in Clare, with certain differences. I asked whether Maftin Hayes’s style was Clare style while there, and they told me it was ONE Clare style and that Martin played Clare style like Martin and that Pat played Clare style like Pat, and so on, after they finished taking the piss out of me for asking such a stupid question and got serious again, and who the hell am I to disbelieve somebody from Clare when they seriously say that Martin’s Clare style is only one way of playing Clare style?

And, did I say that Martin Hayes’s style of playing is the penultimate Clare style? No. I’d have probably picked out (fer instance, god knows there’s many a great fiddler out of Clare) his da, amongst the older ones, or Pat O’Connor amongst the younger ones.

Don’t mistake an older way of playing for the only way of playing a regional style. For years and years, and years past that, the Irish have picked and chosen from the influences that offered themselves. When the dancing masters, many of whom played as well, came back from France, they brought some of that influence with them. When the bothy workers came back from Scotland, they brought that influence back with them. Spain, Wales, even England, everywhere there were Irish who came back home, they brought bits with them. Hell, most historians think the reel came to Ireland from Scotland.

Sure, each region has its own style -- East Galway is different from Clare, for instance, but only those who know can tell the difference, I sure can’t (George could tell you, and says he probably wouldn’t play with a group of East Galway players because “I couldn’t really add anything to the playing”) -- and overall one county might play slower than another, or faster, or with more triplets, or whatever.

However, each fiddler has always had freedom to add whatever they saw fit to the local style, and each listener has freedom to decide whether they like it or not. George Keith tells me that the first thing a fiddler will say about another fiddler (when passing judgement) is whether they like their ornaments or not, but that a fiddler has to take a stand on what they like and how they decide to play regardless of anyone else’s thoughts on the matter.

I suspect Martin Hayes could out-fiddle anyone on this list in any style they liked, given that I’ve heard people talk about his and other fiddlers’ parlor trick of sitting round playing like other famous fiddlers. I’ve heard people say that they don’t want to fiddle like any of the Glackins, yet they’re fantastic fiddlers. Does that make Donegal a “bad” style? Tommy Potts was iimpossible for some (most?) people to play with, but what a fiddler!

What is the use of sitting about wondering why somebody might be inspired by a fiddler you don’t personally care for? Sure, it’s always good to know the roots of where something sprang from, and it’s good to be able to play pure when you like, but music is an intensely personal art form for people at the levels of Hayes and Burke and Carroll and Ivers and such. I don’t think they should have to limit themselves to one style all their lives to satisfy our need to keep a regional style pure, and they could care less about our disdain for their personal ways of playing.

I’ve taken lessons from folks from all over Ireland (as well as the US), and will continue to do so, and I get so much conflicting advice it’s not even funny. Don’t cross the strings, do cross the strings, don’t change bow when changing strings, cross strings on the same bow all you like. Short bows are better here, no, slurring here is better. Triplets should never be used here, of course you can use a triplet there, what’re you talking about? As George told me, you have to decide which what you like and damn everyone else’s opinions. Joan Hanrahan in Ennis told me that I was not to do something a certain way, and then shrugged and then said, “unless you do.”

Was Martin Hayes an influence for me? Sure. So, good buddy Brad, are you. So is Will. So is Dirk. So are tons of players who have said things or taught me things or gave me examples of behavior that I liked or that repelled me, some of which I agree with and some that I don’t. That is the way Irish playing has been since year dot, and the way it’ll always be. It’ll survive all our petty little wishes. Long may it live.

Like An Ceoltoir (Martin, right?) said it’s not him, it’s the stage schtick. I’m not saying he’s bad - I’m just saying you could be misled quickly if you don’t realise it’s a party trick. Any trick that is overused than becomes, well over-used. This music is about refreshing suprises, not using a single party trick as a crutch for every single thing that you play. I’m not saying that his style should be completely avoided, but not used to the point he does. I also don’t think that everyone should play a single style & that’s it. The styles haven’t changed that much that I’ve heard since recordings became available, so old & new (the only style that I have noticed a bit of a difference is the Donegal music) isn’t all that different. I must admit most good players have a notable style, one that’s traditional. There are a whole bunch of pointers that tell you what kind of style something is being played in. Some tunes sound best when played in a certain way, like “Bean a ti ar Lar” played with a northern Donegal feel, in fact I can think of no other way I’d rather hear it. Some tunes can get different treatments & still sound great, but some are regional best rendered in it’s own regional style.
Sure Martin Hayes is a “kind” of Clare style, much in the same way that Kenny G is a “kind” of jazz, or Air Supply is a “kind” of rock. But I say cut the mustard & go buy an album with solid music on it.
