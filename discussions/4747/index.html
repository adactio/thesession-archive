Re: Ways to get rid of annoying people in sessions

I have an idea. When somebody no-one likes joins the session table, ask them to start a tune. Then, while they’re playing, stand up en masse and move to another table. Make sure it’s in a corner - that way you’re protected on two sides and it won’t be as difficult to keep the intruder out.

If they don’t get the hint, you could put a bucket of hot tar over the piano bench, attached to the keys by a string. Ask if the annoying person can back you on a tune, then when they play, they get a shoulder load of tar around their ears. That way, your suffering at being annoyed by the presence of an annoying person will be alleviated by the hilarity of his screaming and burning until the ambulance comes.

If he’s so oblivious to the rules of session etiquette that he returns next week (when he gets out of the hospital) after he’s been shunned and tarred, somebody will just have to sit down and talk to him because he’s obviously too stupid to read your subtle cues.

Of course, communication (that is, communication with the offender himself as opposed to complaining to the other musicians in your ITM club *about* the offender) is an absolute last resort. We all pray it never comes to this. Thankfully, if we all work together to maintain a healthy atmosphere of jerkiness, backstabbing and snobbery most other musicians will leave us alone, and you will never have to sit down and explain how your session works to a newcomer.

Re: Ways to get rid of annoying people in sessions

I really think that no matter where you go, there will always be someone annoying someone else in an *open* public session. I’m starting to not worry or care anymore about the inherent dangers of playing in open sessions. (Currently, I spend more time playing in situations that are smaller, more private and intimate like house sessions since that’s what I personally prefer). But last night I went down to our weekly “public access free-for-all” after a 5 week break. I decided not to get upset or disgusted over anything or anyone. I just focused on a nice fiddler who showed up and we had a nice time playing some tunes.

So what’s my point. I think it’s a lot healthier to just accept that open session playing is always going to be unpredictable and sometimes dodgy. But sometimes that’s half the fun. I’m really trying to be more laid back and open minded about our weekly local session. But I don’t go every week since it’s not exactly helpful for my playing. But hey, it’s fun socially when I do decide to go!

Joyce
