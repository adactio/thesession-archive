Custom Trad Tunes Composed by Cedar Dobson

Do you have a friend or family member that you want to give a meaningful gift to? What about a custom Trad tune named in honour of them? I can compose a special tune just for them!

My service includes:
Tune type choice (jig, reel, hornpipe, air, slide, polka, etc, I can help with this if needed)
Choice of key (ex. A major, D minor, or specific chord changes/modes as well)
Name for the tune (anything you’d like)
Receive an MP3 recording of the tune performed on the Irish tin whistle
Receive a PDF of the sheet music of the tune

Thank you so much and feel free to comment on this post or email me at cedardobsonmusic@gmail.com, Sláinte!

Link to video of sample tunes: https://youtu.be/TMuyL95uMj0?si=3IWlpLvUaisMso3h

Sometimes people will pay for naming rights of an existing tune, but then it’s usually called a “tune auction”. Phil Cunningham’s “Sarah’s Song” was named from an auction.

For commissions I always start with a blank page. The commissioner might have some some idea of the type of tune they would like, but other than that it’s up to me.
I’ve had commissions for weddings, significant birthdays, anniversaries, memorials, places, events, stage shows, etc. I’ll usually offer several tunes from which to choose and, occasionally, one of those might be recycled from a previous commission, but there will always be something new.

Re: Custom Trad Tunes Composed by Cedar Dobson

One of our more famous ex members once commented that any significant meaning relating to the music was “all in the mind”.

Unless you knew the person for which a tune was composed or really thought about the subject, the title would be meaningless. So, identifying a tune with a particular person, event, or whatever is really just in the imagination.

For example, when I hear or play “Rolling Waves” I think of waves rolling on the shore and “The Butterfly” flying around when I play that one. However, if the tunes had other titles, would I still do the same?

So if a man wanted to commision a tune to dedicate to his wife, mother, daughter etc, as long as it’s got a nice soft and beautiful melody (that’s subjective, of course) it doesn’t really make difference. If the purchaser likes it, he will probably automatically always associate the tune with his family member or whatever other subject he has chosen. However, an outsider won’t be any the wiser unless he/she has been given the information.

Re: Custom Trad Tunes Composed by Cedar Dobson

A work of art doesn’t have to be meaningful to *everyone* to yet be deeply meaningful to a few.

Some people think instrumental tunes are pure abstractions. I prefer to feel tunes. This music we play expresses emotions. Not in words or visual images, but in key, mode, intervals, timbre, pace, pulse, lift. If the tunes didn’t have names at all, most of us would still feel a sense of happiness or lightheartedness when listening to this:
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7-Fg6UA5g1g

And a tinge of sorrow or longing in this:
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8kXSKrHpU04

If you don’t feel the mood change in this two-tune set, then you may be in the “pure abstraction” cohort. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7VxQpebTnGs

Can you always pin a tidy label on the mood of a tune? No. But that doesn’t make them purely abstract. It’s more a matter that we don’t have words for every degree of emotion. Some tunes span a gamut of feelings. Whole novels have thrown 100,000 words at evoking the emotions around the loss of a loved one, yet a mere melody can be just as powerful:
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8fX8qqotwqk

The notion that “it’s all in your head” applies to how we respond to any stimulus, whether art or the hard “realities” of daily life. How we respond to, say, facing a large tiger shark in the ocean, is in fact all in our minds, no less that how we respond to a tune.
https://www.youtube.com/shorts/od-YT-Bu-KY
