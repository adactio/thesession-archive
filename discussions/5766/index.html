I remember a couple of years ago, me sporting a fuller beard than usual, talking to him at the bar during a break between the tunes and he was gaunching away back to me, very guardedly and weirdly and with the most fearful look on his face. My sister eventually twigged the crack and explained that he was talking to Aidan, “… y’know, yer nephew, over in London this years, my brawr … God’s sake Patsy, get a grip!” He nearly cried with relief. “Thanks be to Jaysis. Now, I’ve been under the influence many’s a time myself, but never to the extent that I thought I was conversing with my dead brother in the middle of a session … that’s some drinking!

“. . .this ”silly question“ has so far received more replies than any other thread on todays page. . .”

Well, all that really might serve to show is that we’re all a bit silly here. Of course I consider that a virtue.

In any case, I think he’s wrong. I do not in any way admire Janis Joplin, I am, however, astounded by her. Being around JC (coincidence? You decide) could be, as Phil says, “an emotional nightmare” and as the friend who attended the concert with me remarked, “Can you imagine having to grow up as Johnny’s brother?” I don’t find anything to admire in this.

It is, however, one of the personal attributes that can tend to make one larger than life. Johnny is larger than life because of the way he approached life, he cut a swath through it like Sherman marching to the sea.

Which means that when he was around you often didn’t have time to admire him, you were too busy running like hell to get out of the way of the cavalry and people who didn’t make it often got flattened.

But then there was this other Johnny too. . .

It isn’t even really true that I admired his playing. Johnny was fast. I’ve been reasonably up front here about not being an admirer of fast playing.

But Johnny had a way of being fast that transcended the mere speed at which he could play. There was a quality to his playing that came through at any speed and he could play a slow air to make you cry.

I had a mere passing acquaintanceship with the man (as did many thousands of others), and spent much of that time avoiding him, and yet he seems to have had some sort of profound effect on me that is impossible to explain and his passing did not affect me like the passing of someone I admired, but like the passing of a close and deeply loved family member.

And *that* is what makes him larger than life.

(The passing of Welsh sailor Tristan Jones had a similar affect on me, and I had never even met the man, simply read his books. Tristan was also larger than life)
