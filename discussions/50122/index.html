Tunes Posted on Social Media Channels

Perhaps I’m getting old but or maybe the world is changing. Firstly, I have to say that there is some great quality stuff being posted on social media platforms. Some of these are great snippets of great sessions etc and others by certain well known traditional musicians but equally and unfortunately,there is stuff being posted by players who are not so good. I fully understand that these players are doing their best and have to be commended for their enthusiasm and love of traditional music but I just feel that you have to crawl before you can walk. I have taught music for many years and I know people need to be encouraged and I would always refer to a young student I had who would say ‘read the room’. The ease of recording a video on your phone and the speed at which you can publish it online may be another reason. I’m by no means a purest but I know some videos of solo players make other music teachers cringe. What are your thoughts on this?

I think the constant stream of nothing but All-Ireland winners and the top 0.1% players in the world on some Irish music groups and video services while impressive and delightful, also has some aspects that can be very off-putting in some ways to those early into their playing of traditional Irish music.

I appreciate the courage of those who put out videos of however they are playing at this point in their journey with this music, warts and all, in the face of the constant barrage of top-level playing videos knowing that they may get negative comments or cruel criticism of their playing.

Social media has much to answer for, I’m afraid, but there are as many positive aspects are there are negative ones.

Posting videos and recordings with the purpose of helping and informing players and listeners is not a bad thing in itself as long as those who visit and use such resources are aware of their potential short comings and flaws.

As for for emerging musicians and singers using social media to promote themselves, this can also have mixed outcomes. It is very easy to make a film and recording now and we can now often have the privelege of viewing or listening to a musician, singer, or band who isn’t “quite ready”. So, it’s possible that we may judge them unfairly as a result or not be prepared to give them a chance.

In the old days, musicians and singers “honed” their craft outwith the view/glare of the media and audiences in general. They might play small gigs, do supports and on(That still happens, of course) but most listeners etc would be quite unaware of their output until they were relatively accomplished.
Unfortunately, I have been guilty of dismissing a lot of emerging talent over the years and unduly criticising their performances, ideas etc only to be really impressed by their output further down the line. So these days, I say nothing unless it’s to offer encouragement.

Another negative aspect about social media and recording opportunities online is that there is so much competition out there now. When everyone else is “doing it”, this makes it very hard for even some of the more accomplished musicians to get noticed.

Re: Tunes Posted on Social Media Channels

Well, some terms start out as literal phrases and become terms of art, and I would say that “social media” is one of those. For me, this is more of a forum site. The most obvious difference is that a forum site is typically content-specific, focused primarily on a particular topic like programming, trad music, stamp collecting, or what have you, while a social media site is content-generic. Social media is in principle aimed at communicating with people you already know about whatever it is you choose to communicate, while forums are about communicating with people you don’t know about topics in a particular semantic space.

Ironically, considering the above, forum sites tend to develop really strong communities. I started visiting this site when it was a “tune of the week” site, and as you can see from my user ID I was one of the first people to join when it became the forum site it is today. I’ve seen the relationships that complete strangers have forged here. I bought my B/C box from a total stranger based on the good word of someone I’d only ever “met” in this space. Called the guy, chatted with him, sent him a check, he sent me the box, I’m still playing it. Would you do that on Facebook? Not likely, I think. Another anecdote: when a programming forum that I used to frequent closed down, a number of the regulars formed a slack channel. It’s been five years, and we’re still using that channel, mostly for the sort of things that people say they get from facebook - life updates, complaining about work, funny anecdotes, and the like. The difference is that on that channel, and here, the people we’re talking to are mostly actual people.
Not being a social media user, my information on this is second hand, but as far as I can tell there isn’t anything similar on social media sites, probably because that’s not what they’re for, and more to the point because community doesn’t make money. Also, bots aren’t very good at community.

Then there’s the UI and UX aspect. Social media is characterized by “feeds” which tell you what you should be looking at, typically presented in the form of a bottomless pit of increasingly useless and inane nonsense. (Pro tip: as a software engineer, I can tell you that there is never any point in scrolling through a feed. The reason is pretty simple: recommendation engines are pretty well understood, and they work, so if there’s anything you want that you have to scroll for it’s because someone’s read Skinner and understands random-reward reinforcement, and you’re the pigeon being trained. In general, with social media, you’re the pigeon.) Forum sites, on the other hand, are typically organized by topics and threads, and allow the user to find the topics they’re interested in.

Also, there’s the ads. Social media sites are basically places where you can go to look at ads for things you don’t want or need, and occasionally see some content, some of which will occasionally be relevant to you and most of which will be engineered to be momentarily distracting to people whose behavior resembles yours. Social media sites are literally an API in reverse: the consumers are the ad bots and the service is you. Forum sites, on the other hand, are typically not ad-funded, or if they do serve ads they’re sidebarred and easily blocked (Pro tip #2: Use adblock, and if a site objects to that, don’t go to that site)

But the main thing, which explains all of the above, is simply this: social media sites are not designed to serve their users, they’re designed to use their victims. Facebook, Tiktok, and that one that sounds like a drug delivery service all have engineers whose annual bonus depends on increasing users’ average “engagement” by some fraction of a second. If you ever wonder why you go back to one of those sites, it’s more or less for the same reason that you buy another bag of Doritos even though you actually don’t like Doritos. This is why the concept of the “feed” exists: it’s there to keep you on the site looking at ads for a little longer. If you could go to facebook and find what you were looking for and leave, it would be useful, and Mark Zuckerberg would be drawing a comfortable but not particularly impressive salary. You can’t and Mark Zuckerberg is a bazillionaire. This is not an accident. A forum gives you a place to use some of your attention for your own purposes. A social media site exists to take your attention and hang on to it regardless of your desire or attention. The contrast with a site like this one is pretty obvious, I think.

Does that answer your question?

“Perhaps I’m getting old…”

Millennial here, speaking for my generation:

The one redeeming factor about social media is the speed at which it allows us to exchange information with each other. For example, say you have a young aspiring musician who lives in a town of less than 2,500 people, and in this town there are no thriving musical communities. This person can use social media to access musical communities from around the world, so that they don’t have to trudge alone through what can be a very challenging and difficult experience. They could even go as far as building their own communities of supporters and fellow learners. Their journey could encourage people, bring people together, and support each other. And there are communities for literally everything, whether it’s about caring for houseplants, or local board gamers. I just went to Facebook and randomly typed “Polka Lovers” in the search bar, and dozens of groups popped up, some with over 9k members.

Social media isn’t just about entertainment, it’s about exchange; whether it’s cultural, intellectual, political, philosophical, or artistic. There are Tiktok creators that do everything from explaining mathical formulas, to sharing ghost stories that run in their family. There are Youtubers who show their hand drawing process, and others who talk about the lore from their favorite fantasy video game franchise. And hecks, some people are just lonely and want to be apart of something. If they’re not hurting anyone, I don’t see what the big deal is.

And don’t get me wrong. I can get pretty snobby about the quality of the content in my social media feeds, and yea, there is actual “slop” content that’s out there for us to complain about. But I personally don’t see anything wrong with someone sharing their musical journey, even from day 1. Matter of fact, “personal journey” content itself has a very firm position in social media, whether it’s musical development, physical fitness, or… home renovating. There is so much that can be misunderstood about what an experience may be like, and people who share their personal journeys give others a clue about what it may actually be like. I personally think this is essential for musical communities, since so much of the work and effort happens behind the scenes.

Even still, whatever the case, some people just want to share. Things that give them joy, things that helped them, things that meant something to them, etc. There is still value in the content, regardless of the level of talent of the creator.
