Re: Cure for fiddle hicky … please!

This is “Fiddler’s Neck”. Players of other instruments which press against the body get similar conditions, for instance “Cellist’s Leg”. I have seen it on a Computer Programmer’s wrists where they were touching his desk. It is a terror to people who worked any machinery for a living because no-one will employ them if something like this gets onto their records.

It might be an allergy, in which case you need to identify what’s causing it and stop using it. (It might, for instance, be your rosin rather than your chin rest, or some kind of cleaning product you use). It can be caused, however, just by the pressure of the instrument against the neck. Some people have skin which reacts to pressure. (Mine does. If I press on my upper lip for a a minute, for instance, it starts to swell).

I think the clinic is right and there is a strong argument for not playing at all until you have healed. If you can’t bear not to play - and I think everyone contributing to The Session understands how that could be - then the only option is to change everything. Get a fiddle and bow made from different woods. Change the rosin you use. Change the strings. Keep everything in a different case. Play old-style against your chest as in the clip at http://video.aol.com/video-detail/niel-gow-replication-pochette-fiddle/20167333 - and move the instrument often and play less.

I wouldn’t put anything on the rash until it has healed unless prescribed by a doctor. Don’t use soap, talcum or anything like that anywhere near the area of the problem. You just have to wait, but if the cause is removed then you shouldn’t have to wait long.

Playing the fiddle for a long time in a warm situation can trap a thin layer of perspiration on the skin of the neck. This could have encouraged the growth of some sort of fungus that triggers dermatitis. Or it could be an auto-immune situation where you’re sort of allergic to your own perspiration (this has happened to me).

There’s probably no alternative to taking a rest from playing. I know how hard it is. I was once out of commission for over two years while recovering from a wrist injury. Couldn’t play anything but harmonica. Right now, I’m just starting to play again after another wrist strain five months ago. I recommend spending the time with a different instrument or maybe singing.

Re: Cure for fiddle hicky … please!

Sounds like more than a mere fungal growth to me, and I’m not medically trained at all…..

:-/

I agree with Ionannas--try the off-the-neck position and keep playing. Do be careful though--lowering the fiddle also shifts your bowing arm angle and motion. You don’t want to over do it and end up with a bow arm strain or worse. Monitor your bow arm joints and muscles and make sure you’re not stressing anything unduly. Stop if anything starts to hurt, and regardless of your posture/position--stay relaxed.

FWIW, I let my fiddle slip down below my collar bone now and then so I can more easily talk or look around the room, or just to wiggle any kinks out of my neck. It’s healthy to be a bit flexible with where the fiddle sits--treat it less like an appendage and more like the prop (or at least prosthetic) that it is. PLAY with it. See if you can’t find a comfortable alternate place to stand underneath the fiddle while you play, that lets you play without aggravating the dermatitis.

To be fair. I’ve never gotten a fiddle hickey of any kind, despite playing for up to 12 hours straight on occasion. Maybe it’s my beard. Maybe I’m just thick skinned. Maybe my luck has run out and I’m about to sprout an oozing boil the size of Old Faithful geyser tomorrow morning where my lymph node on the left side of my neck used to be…. Regardless, if my skin protested that much, I’d find another way to prop up my fiddle and keep sawing away.

Good luck!
