Buying a fiddle

Re: Buying a fiddle

As a newby it seems to me that there are a couple of issues. When it comes to guitars, for instance, everyone knows that certain makes are highly valued but that it is also very much a question of taste and that individual guitars from the same makers can vary as well. With fiddles it seems even more difficult. First of all the level of competence of the fiddler makes a huge difference, the combination of bow and fiddle is important, and of course the quality of the instrument itself. My first question to you would be; why do you want to buy another instrument ? What is wrong with your current instrument ? It seems to me that it is important to have a good idea of what you want and why you want it before you can set off down the purchase path.
My own fiddle was not expensive ( an Eastman) and my bow is an Orchestra, but occasionally I manage to coax them both into making a decent sound, which tells me that the problems of tone, etc, lie with me, more tha with them, and only when I can always get the best out of them will I consider spending more money.

Re: Buying a fiddle

To me, definitely a case of try before you buy! :-)

I think a typical scenario is to…
A) find some good luthiers by word of mouth ( local musicians will know)
B) tell them your price range ( this helps to narrow down possibilities a lot! ) and make an appointment
C) probably you will wind up in a room with 6-8 fiddles around your price range - maybe a few slightly above.
D) play them all, take your time…
E) take home your two favorites if you can’t decide…and play them some more ( the shop may take your credit card info as insurance)
F) enjoy your fiddle!

If you are not in love with anything at your appointment, you can up your price range (yes, painful) or try a different shop. It could take awhile, but it’s enjoyable…

If you can not play well enough yet to do serious “test playing”, bring a good player to play them for you. It’s also good to bring a patient Listener… Sitting a few feet away, they can vote on what sounds nicest to them…since what you hear and what others hear is different.

Also- If there is something specific you are not liking about your fiddle, maybe show luthier first? It could be that a soundpost adjustment, new strings, or a better bow will solve the issue (vs buying an entirely different instrument)…

( Sorry I don’t know about FB groups, I mostly just go to local luthiers for “tune-ups” or shopping.)

Unless I’m misreading your comments so far, you seem to be concentrating on the idea of buying a new instrument from a luthier. Which is great for the maker community, but may not necessarily be best for you at this stage.

If you give a rough idea of where you are in the UK, we might be able to recommend a good local-ish dealer who would give you the opportunity to try a number of instruments in your price range and decide which one feels right to you. And if you have a knowledgeable friend / teacher take them with you for a second opinion and pair of ears, but always remember at the end of the day it’ll be you who is planning to playing the instrument for years to come, so be guided by expertise but choose the one *you* prefer.

Re: Buying a fiddle

I think one point making really clear is that although quality and price are related, the relationship is quite loose. Price is mostly about lineage, workmanship, and a bunch of things that don’t actually mean much to the average string-scraper, of whatever genre. I’ve played instruments worth ten times what I paid for mine, and I wouldn’t take them at any price. I’ve played instruments cheaper than mine that I’d swap for mine on the spot (and I’ve also played much more expensive instruments that I would also swap, to be fair!)

Set a budget, but don’t break the bank, because you probably do need a fiddle upgrade but you almost certainly need a bow upgrade as well. Which should come first is probably something a teacher should advise on but honestly, once you’ve got a basically decent fiddle the bow is what I’d throw money at. Yes, a lot of performing players do use trash bows that they replace on a regular basis but that’s because stage performance often requires a certain violence of style, which you do not need to replicate.

Re: Buying a fiddle

I worked in a big fiddle shop for years, we sold lots because we had lots.

That hundred year old german fiddle may be the the best sounding fiddle in a shop, think about getting it set up really well before moving on.

One issue is any good sounding fiddle in a shop will have had a lot of money spent on it getting it that way. The 2 grand fiddle in the shop will have come in at a fifth to a tenth of that price, 500 quid in labour and bits to set it up, a third profit and shop running costs then vat gives you the sale price.

Buying a new fiddle direct does give most of the cost to the maker, but unless it is already made, you cannot tell what it is going to sound like. Either way you need to expect not to get a return on your money, a fiddle will not return your outlay which a good set of pipes or concertina may.

I would also echo the above regarding bows, once you have a fiddle that works, getting a good bow is a must. There are no good players out there with visibly cheap bows, they just dont work for all the fiddley bits.

If you’re not in too much of a hurry, I would look in antique shops, pawn shops etc. and see if a bargain comes along in time. I’ve never looked at fiddles in auctions, though I know some people say there can be good finds at these. I always find the prices of second hand fiddles in music shops depressingly high, often for instruments in pretty poor condition. I’m very limited in that I play left handed and would have to either pay someone to redo the bass bar, peg box etc., do this work myself (only done this once, on an old broken instrument), or buy one from a maker that actually does left handed ones. In the end I bought a brand new one from Gliga, in the U.S., and feel like I did pretty well for $300 or so. I’d say that trying to get a better sound by working on playing is a good way forward, and trying out different fiddles and bows without a need to buy one immediately should give you an idea of what you might be looking for.

Re: Buying a fiddle

Junk shop finds are normally just that - junk. You don’t find good violins in junk shops for the same reason you don’t find fine antiques there - the shop owner knows exactly who to pass the good stuff on to, it never reaches their shelves. The best you can hope for is a medium quality instrument that needs more work than would be viable for a dealer. Auctions can be a source of bargains, but it is always a gamble and you have to know your onions. It’s OK for dealers who are buying several instruments, they will nearly always find a customer that suits each one, but if you are only buying one fiddle for yourself, the chances of finding one that suits you are slim. And remember that if you win a bid it is because the dealers have dropped out, because the price has gone above what they think it is worth.
