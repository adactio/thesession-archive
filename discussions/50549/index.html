Hi!
Silly Wizard was one of the greatest bands of all time, and for sure one of my favourites:)

They were quite unique, hence combination of Andy’s vocals and impressive banjo/whistle playing with mastership of Cunningham brothers (Phill Cunningham on piano accordion, whistles, bouzuki, and whatever, and Johnny Cunningham on the fiddle) and quite solid backing section of bass and acoustic guitar really moved people!
Closer to the end of their journey they’ve released an album Glints of Silver, that was quite visionary, as for my, playing with some nice synths…
It can be found on YouTube, as well as couple of full videos of them playing live concert, including all jokes and stories… You may contact me in private messages, and I may try to send you those in some way you can get this files…

Fun fact #1 - the band name was taken from some book for kids, that they just took from the shelve
Fun fact #2 - in live performances brothers from time to time were left alone on the stage to just play set of reels, and, competing, sped it up to some unbelievable bpms, still playing brilliantly. Andy has joked afterwards, that, when brothers play full speed, only dogs can hear them :)

Re: Please tell me anything about Silly Wizard.

Silly Wizard were a Scottish folk band that began forming in Edinburgh in 1970. The founder members were two like-minded university students—Gordon Jones (guitar, bodhran, vocals, bouzouki, mandola), and Bob Thomas (guitar, mandolin, mandola, banjo, concertina). In January 1972, Jones and Thomas formed a trio with their flatmate Bill Watkins (guitar, vocals, fiddle) and performed (occasionally unpaid) under various band names in Edinburgh folk clubs. In the spring of 1972, Watkins returned to Birmingham and, in June 1972, Chris Pritchard (vocals) came in as his replacement. In July 1972, this newly formed trio were offered their first paid booking at the Burns Monument Hotel, Brig O’ Doon, Scotland, and needed a band name in a hurry. The name “Silly Wizard” was chosen and the continuing stream of bookings ensured that the name became permanent. In September 1972, the trio recruited Johnny Cunningham (1957–2003) (fiddle, viola, mandola, vocals) and Silly Wizard started to take off.

They went through several changes and in late 1976, Freeland Barbour left the band and was replaced by Johnny Cunningham’s younger brother, Phil Cunningham . Phil is still around on the Scottish Music Scene, Johnny died in 2003.

My wife and I used to see them when we were students back in the mid 70’s.

Re: Please tell me anything about Silly Wizard.

Posted above :
“Fun fact #1 - the band name was taken from some book for kids, that they just took from the shelve”.
Actually, this is incorrect. I’m listening right now to a recording I have of a programme made for BBC Scotland by Bruce MacGregor called “Pure Wizardry - The Story of Silly Wizard”. It was broadcast on Christmas day 2001. On the origin of the band’s name, Gordon Jones states that at one time he shared a flat with some bloke who quit university to be a wizard. They didn’t think that a sensible course of action, and at the time were taking bookings in short-lived bands which required a name to go on a poster. They did a couple of gigs under the name of “Silly Wizard”, and got repeat bookings, so had to stick with the name.
Andy M. was never a big fan of the name, telling me once, “I wouldn’t have cared if we’d been called something like ”Daft Bastard“, but we’re stuck with it”. Having said that, Andy was rightly proud of the music he created with the band over the years, despite the unfortunate choice of name.
I have far too many stories about the band to post here, someone really should write a book. [ but nobody would believe it ! ].
