The seven secrets of the successful (organized) musician

What do you do to remember your tunes - is the list in your head, or do you carry around a sheet of paper? Do you keep notes on how the tunes start? A couple of bars of dots, maybe, or a snippet of ABC?

I’ve seen people haul around bags full of tunebooks, lists on scrunched-up balls of paper, Post-Its stuck to accordians, even a rolodex with a card for each tune and the first two bars written out in musical notation. Then there’s the spreadsheet types. Haven’t seen anyone use a Palm Pilot yet.

Share your secrets!

This might be just a question for those of us with CRAFT disease. (Don’t ask me what it is - I forget.)

Re: The seven secrets of the successful (organized) musician

What is it you need to remember your tunes? (And I’m watching you “O” -- you’re better at calling them up than I am.) Here
are some ideas:

1) Lists are useful. For example, I have a spreadsheet with most of the tunes I’ve ever learned (the list of tunes I “know” is quite a bit shorter), but it doesn’t do me much good unless I look at it once in a while. Doesn’t matter if it’s on paper or digital or whatever. It’s very handy to have a list at the session to remind you of those well-loved favorites that you’ve forgotten that you ever knew. An address book with lots of room to write tune names is one way to do this.

2) Association is the way we remember most things. On the whole I’m with Brendan: the best way for me to recall tunes is by some association with an event, a person, a place, anything. Sometimes tunes come with a built-in association to something I’ve actually experienced (The Cliffs of Moher), or some kind of made-up constellation of feelings (The Old Bush -- don’t ask). It doesn’t matter what, and it doesn’t have to be logical, as long as it’s clear and personal. Generally this kind of thing is a lot more effective than trying to keep a mental list of fingering patterns, first bars or whatever. The purpose of life is to create meaningful experiences to hang tunes on…

3) We spend a lot of time practicing tunes, but not much time practicing recalling tunes.

4) The other half of this process is the collective memory part. If you actively contribute to a community of players, you can rely on the collective memory to help keep track of your tunes (at least the current ones). I guess that means a long-term committment to participation in the community/session and becoming a reliable source of tuneage yourself. The more deeply and the longer you spend immersed in that community, the better and more reliable your access to the tune memory becomes. (Which might just be another way of saying what Kerri and Rae said.)

Can’t remember 5 to 7, but they were good ones too.
