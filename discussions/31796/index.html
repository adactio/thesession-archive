i think that it is not that unusual to find CDs out of print. there are plenty of CDs that went out of print in the 90’s, before the advent of iTunes and mp3s. case and point: james kelly’s capel street was never even released on CD and is completely unavailable today.

in the case of traditional music, i think a lot of the unavailability is due to artists funding their own CDs without a label. i know i have had a hard time getting a few CDs because the artists themselves have just chosen to front the cost to have it reprinted. additionally, many labels for trad have minimal resources, and may have trouble keeping up with demand.

if this situation was truly caused by iTunes/etc, then you would see these CDs listed on iTunes with minimal effort at reprinting. the fact that such CDs cannot be found on any medium points to market factors beyond the arrival of a new media distribution channel. such factors may be relevant for music that is pirated extensively, but i think that traditional music has always been subject to availability issues.

Re: Has Apple won? Are CDs well on their way to extinction?

As a small publisher (not of music) I can see both sides of the argument. I sell my products both in paper and digital form. Digital products are a real challenge - I would like my customers who buy a digital file to be able to make copies for their own use - backup, other devices etc. But on the other hand I don’t want them to hand on copies to all their mates or worse still, make it available as a download on the net. If I was to accept these practices, I could easily have to double, treble or whatever my retail costs. Whereas I’d rather charge a lower cost and spread that across all who want to use the material - that seems fairer. How you balance this is very tricky and perhaps impossible. So I sympathise at that level with the ‘music industry’ trying to put some structure on their sales.

There are significant downsides to Apple’s growing dominance, are far as I can see they are creating a substantial monopoly. And monopolies tend to have drawbacks: for artists, consumers and publishers. As far as I can tell, anything that has to go on an Apple device has to go through their store. I’ve looked at it and they want a 30% cut - for what, allowing it be used on their devices? I don’t object in principle to them taking a cut as a reseller but I do object if they are the sole reseller and the only means of getting product on their devices. They’ll role out excuses about quality and compatibility etc…. but do we really want to see one corporation holding & exercising this type of power.

Some has observed above that every artist can be their own producer, publisher and do their own sales and marketing etc., through the wonders of the web. Maybe in some cases but it’s bloody hard work and very little thanks. I suspect that many musicians and others would prefer to simply concentrate on their crafts and employ/ sub contract all the commercial end of things to others. So in that sense, there will probably always be room for the ‘music industry’.

As regards CDs, that’s where most of our personal music collection lies, I’ve never bought a music download..

Re: Has Apple won? Are CDs well on their way to extinction?

I still buy CD’s from artists who are only distributing their music that way, and at concerts, but almost all the music I buy is online. Usually from Amazon in non-DRM’d MP3 format (I’m not a big fan of iTunes). I know it doesn’t send as much income to the artist, but it saves the time of ripping the CD’s and verifying the metadata, before it goes into our home media server.

Years ago, my wife and I ripped our large selection of CD’s to high bitrate MP3 (256kbs or 320kbps). I’ve done ABX testing against the original WAV files ripped from the CD’s, and I can’t tell a difference in audio quality.

The huge bonus for a trad musician with digital files is the ability to search metadata, since so many artists cover the same tunes. It provides a different perspective and various points of entry, when you’re learning a tune for the first time. I can call up Foobar2000 and type a tune name in the search field, and it will show all the MP3’s we have by different artists, or the same artist on different recordings. Doing that with a pile of CD’s would be a PITA. I’d have to remember who did what and when, or have everything cataloged in a database, then go look for the CD. With digital files like MP3’s, FLAC or other formats that support metadata, it’s all right there for quick searching on the hard drive.

I still miss some things from CD’s (and LP’s), like album cover art, and especially the album notes with detailed info on tune origins, list of performers and so on. But for me, the quick search on different versions of the same tune is well worth the trade-off. And I don’t think that’s something I could get from a cloud streaming service like Spotify. Just make sure you understand how to maintain a backup system if you go all-digital. I use a mirrored hard drive for the main media server, and every six months I write the collection to a stack of data DVD’s for archiving.

No great wisdom on my part. I love this site. It is amazing how many of our colleagues on the Session are Whiz bang computer scientists, or at least Whix bang computer folks who can reveal many secrets to the digital universe.

Ffeg= actually Ffmpeg. Can’t type for shite… A plug in for audacity that lets you glom onto ITunes converted recordings and do the modification things described above which would otherwise be blocked by the coding Geniuses at Apple.

It beats the heck out of the old Wollensak reel-to-reel tape recorders I used to lust for! Though my Teacher wished I would record more in any format… I find that a really humbling experience though.
