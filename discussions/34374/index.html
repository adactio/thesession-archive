Re: Wanting to learn all the tunes from an album.

I’m very sorry some of you that are stuck in dreadful backwaters and are starved of a real breathing vibrant scene and have to put up with just looking at moving pictures with sound of other people doing it.
But you will never get it unless you are lucky enough to play week after week with great players who never play a tune or set the same twice.
None of the sessions have ‘tune lists’; it’s all ad hoc. Over time you hear tunes over and over again by all sorts of different players in different places in different combinations puting their own spin on each tune. So you learn slowly and organically. And you learn your own version: not some bloke’s forty year old version or wotteffah. One doesn’t visualize (Auralize!?) a particular recorded version or versions, you hear a definitve and abstracted version.
Additionally: there is a dialogue between the players, mimicking each other, musical jokes and winks. You influencing what’s happening and the other musicians effecting you.
None of this happens in you bedroom in Chelmsford or Florida listening and playing along with some old LP over and over again.
Sorry!

Re: Wanting to learn all the tunes from an album.

I sat next to somebody at a session, he told me he had learned all his tunes at sessions, not from recordings. I played a tune, a well-known tune, and he said “I didn’t know it had all those bits in it”. But I was playing a pretty unornamented, straightforward version of the tune. I don’t think you learn a definitive version of a tune at a session, I think there’s a tendency for tunes to be simplified, blurred, because of the surrounding noise, because people are playing slightly different versions, because of the different levels of accuracy people aim for and achieve.

What matters about music is sound. A recording sounds much the same as the original.

When I learn a tune from a recording I usually find that after I play it myself half a dozen times, it starts to become my own version. I don’t try to play just like the person on the recording.

I’m not starved of a vibrant scene, my session is great, I could learn all my tunes there if I wanted to, but I think it’s generally better to learn tunes from recordings.
