Talking while someone is playing.

I started off a set of jigs the other night. All was going well till it came to the 3rd or 4th one, then no-one knew it so I had to carry it solo. There’s me just back from the holidays, hadn’t touched the flute for over 2 weeks…I was going not too bad, a few heads were listening etc,…. then the 2 lads (players!) one on EITHER SIDE of me start up a conversation! I kept playing, but did they not raise their voices to hear each other! I was pretty damned determined to finish my set now, which I did. I didn’t say anything, but what that experience does to me is make me think a little less of the culprits.

…or… maybe they were trying to tell me something???

I reckon it was lack of thought and manners, but there you go. I wouldn’t want the whole pub to lapse into silence, nothing like that. In the right setting, the sound of conversation is part of the craic and brings the whole thing to life. But when I struggle to hear myself, as people who should know better are shouting either side of me, I begin to think something is not right.

Danny.

Ok Danny,
There’s no such thing as a ‘perfectly good Empire’ - that’s a bit of an oxymoron.
I remember a wonderful example of battling against a couple of shouters in the Half Moon in Oxford a couple of years ago. A group of young men sat at the next table to where we playing. It wasn’t a big session - 2 Fiddles, 2 flutes, Mandolin, mandola and concertina. They seemed quite appreciative of what we were playing, but the volume they were talking at crept up as the night wore on and drink was taken. As it got louder (no one else in the quite busy pub was particularly audible to us), we just cranked up our volume, we’re all quite used to playing in big crowded pubs, so this was no problem. After a while we were playing at approaching maximum volume, and the four guys at the next table were shouting at each other to hear over the music. At this point, Eddie, the fiddler, put down his fiddle and said, “look, if you want a chat, can you do it somewhere else in the pub, because we canna (he’s Scottish) hear a note we’re playing.”
The noisiest lad (An American, though of course that’s completely irrelevant), immediately put on the most grovelling display of contrition I have ever seen, with the result that we actually ended up apologising to him(!). He LOVED the music. He had only travelled ALL this way to hear us wonderful Celtic people…. And, of course, as soon as we started playing again - up crept the volume again, until Eddie slammed his fiddle down on the table and shreiked, “Will you shut the f**k up!” to cheers from the rest of the patrons in the pub. The noisy lad’s face was a joy to behold.
