What testimonials do you have?

Ok, so there was me on that 2/2 versus 4/4 thread pontificating right, left and centre with my pearls of wisdom. But, let’s face, I could be any old Tom, Dick or Harry who thinks he knows it all but, in fact, knows diddley squat.

So, to put your minds at ease regarding my suitability as a dispenser of arcane knowledge, I offer up for your edification and enlightenment the following testimonials:

“Such a gifted guitar player – Donald learnt to play in a day.” (Bert Weedon)

“When I learned there was a secret chord it was Donald I turned to for the answer.” (Leonard Cohen)

“What a man - his transcriptions are something else. He could score with me anytime.” (Marilyn Monroe)

“Donald’s melody playing is decorated with a vast array of beautiful ornaments.” (Michael Doulton)

Top those, if you can.

Re: What testimonials do you have?

It’s Ireland, in 1974, me and my girlfriend were travelling on bicycles. It starts to pour, so we seek shelter under the verandah of a bank. The rain gets heavier and heavier - looks like we’ll be there for hours. So we pull out flute and concertina and play a few tunes to while away the time. Other people join us - literally a captive audience. We play everything we know, several times, to great general merriment. Finally the rain starts to ease, and people start slipping away. One chap turns to us before leaving and says “I reckon you’re better than the Chieftains”.

I sometimes wonder if he meant to say “I reckon you’re wetter than the Chieftains”!

Re: What testimonials do you have?

Last year, I went to a festival with strong Apallachian leanings. I was lurking on the periphery of a late night Old Time session with my mandolin, trying to pick up the tunes on the fly (Me? Noodling? Never!), somewhat overwhelmed by the unfamiliar musical galaxy opening up before me. As I was leaving, sometime after 3am, I thanked the other musicians for their music, and for tolerating my attempts at participating. Feeling the need to justify my incompetence, I explained that this was a new musical language to me, that I played Irish music, blahdee blah… Then the ‘pack leader’ (an Irishman, as it happens), no doubt impatient to play the next tune, cut me off, saying,

“Cut all that self deprecating s#!te. You’re a very nice man and you’re quite a good mandolin player.”

Talk about building up and knocking down. I was asking for it, though.

↳ Posted by AlBrown on June 30th, 2014.

Re: What testimonials do you have?

A few years ago when I was busking with a friend, an old chap gave us some change and I THINK he said to me that I’d be the next Sean McGuire (MacGuire?). At the time I played tunes out of books but hadn’t done much listening to fiddlers, so I didn’t really know anything about fiddle music, and didn’t know who Sean McGuire was but I assumed it was meant as a compliment! Now that I know a good bit more about it all, I’m chuffed by his sentiment, though I’ve reigned in my style a lot and go for a less virtuosic assault on the music - and much as I admire him, I don’t aspire to play like Sean McGuire!

Other than that, I’ve not done enough in the scene (haven’t played at sessions etc.) to really know what many people think, other than various kind words when I would busk in my uni holidays and being approached to play for gigs, events etc.

Still, I was chuffed when I had a lesson with Kevin Burke about a year ago and he seemed moderately pleased with how I’d handled The Morning Dew, though I began to rush (probably nerves), which he did pick up on. A friend of mine had a lesson also, and the three of us went for lunch afterwards, where Kevin also said that it was good to see ‘such talented musicians’ being interested in traditional/fiddle music, and seemed to feel that it would be in safe hands, which was a really kind bit of encouragement. He may have just been being a nice guy, because it would be unlike him to be really savage about it, but I was pretty chuffed all the same :) still got such a long way to go though…
