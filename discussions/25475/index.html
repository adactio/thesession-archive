Re: The Irish Washerwoman

I’ve never heard a polka I didn’t like, though I’ve heard polkas murdered in ways that I hadn’t thought possible. The Irish Washerwoman was the one tune I thought was inherently flawed, not through laziness or lack of ability, simply that it was structurally unattractive. I’ve heard lots of ‘overplayed’ tunes used as demonstrations of technical ability or as vehicles to transport over-inflated egos, and more often than not they come across as contrived and unconvincing at best, and pretentious and unbearable at worst. This is just a lovely wee tune.

Re: The Irish Washerwoman

I have to think about the audience on this one. I think that there are very strong feelings about it among the older immigrant population here in the States at least.

My example as a case-in-point. A few years ago we had a St. Pat’s doing with an effort to avoid the commercialized “Irish” trappings. A very competent young non-ITM fiddler I know was asked to play some “Celtic” for a group that included some older Irish immigrants. He played Washerwoman.

Several older women with very powerful brogues were there. THey were very gracious, smiled, and complented the young man how well he played it….and outside made several much less than gracious remarks about the tune….

My sense is that unpopularity of the tune is deeper than it just having the overexposure it had- I remember it being played as characteristic of Irish music when I was a kid in the old Polish neighborhood.

For some reason, around here, non-Irish bands who dabble in Irish music (play one or two Irish tunes as “crowd pleasers”) ALWAYS player Irish Washerwoman, and they ALWAYS do the same thing, starting off the tune slowly and speeding it up faster and faster until it’s a jumble of notes.
For some reason they think that’s how Irish Washerwoman must be played.

There’s a “pirate band” that plays at Disneyland and they have a couple Irish tunes in their repertoire, including this speeding-up Irish Washerwoman. The other thing they play is the set dance St Patrick’s Day, played far too fast to dance to.
