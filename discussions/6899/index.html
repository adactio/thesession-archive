My Missus started out more on the American trad./contemporary folk music end of things, first with guitar and then more exclusively to Appalachian dulcimer. When she started hanging around sessions, as well as listening to innovative dulcimer players like Lorraine Lee, Leo Kretzner and John Molineux, she developed a taste for Irish/Celtic stuff -- and when she met me (through the fiddle player in my band, a mutual friend), her downfall was complete.
Actually, we tended to do more songs than tunes, largely because we both play fretted string instruments, which don’t offer much in the way of contrasting sounds. But music was definitely part of the foundation for our relationship: About four months after we started going out, we had the opportunity to go to Ireland for about 10 days, which we spent largely going to sessions. She even joined my band, which by then was a trio, i.e., her, our mutual friend and me, in its last stages.
Sadly, music is not something we share as often nowadays. She became immersed in managing our property (well, someone has to do it, after all) and in school/community matters, and just didn’t have the time or motivation to keep up with it. After a considerable lay-off myself, I’ve become very active musically again and try to go to sessions regularly -- but most often on my own.
Still, there are times when we do musical things together. Just the other week, in fact, we went to a house-party session and she had a great time. And there are those evenings when she’ll hear me practicing, break out the dulcimer and join in; we still have our repertoire of songs we can summon up, and to my ears it still sounds pretty damn good.

Re: compatibility… again!

It was my wife, Min Gates, who brought be (back, sort of) to ITM. We were in Bethesda, Md., I was managing a recording studio. The one thing that we didn’t share was music. I have played almost all my life, but Min had only listened casually. In Bethesda she became immersed in the studio life and musicians’ company all the time.

I loved all manner of international musics for a long while, but had attempted only some Latin and African ones. She found a pub that was on her way home (Flanagan’s) and with live ITM during happy hour. She had never heard ITM and when she heard it bounding out of the pub onto the street it captivated her immediately. Later on there in Bethesda, we met Zan McLeod (who was then new to Md) and he introduced us to many of the local players and situations as he met them, and we became more involved in ITM, while I still worked with all sorts of music in the studios.

We returned to Indiana and Min said she’d like to learn bodhran, so in about ‘92 she began to play and ’came up‘ in a lively session that began here with the guidance of veterans including Jamie Gans, Sam Bartlett, Grey Larsen and Chris Smith. She, and many of them, continually encouraged me to join in, and finally in ’99, I began to play at home before joining the session. This session crew eventually came to include Eric Merrill, Erin Schroeder, Danny Novek, and has now endured about a half-dozen venue changes and goes on and on.

Now Min and I travel together, to Ireland, to sessions, and to gigs together, and it is purely wonderful. I really enjoy and respect her talent and her playing! Since those ’80’s in her first studio experiences, Min has been a good, perceptive, intelligent and trusted contributor and critic to my work in music and recording, and now we’ve made two records together, along with contributing to lots more. Music, most especially our Irish music, seems to be the most elastic and least troublesome part of our relationship (in a relationship that isn’t particularly troublesome at all! <GG>).

I’ve seen a lot of relationships between a music devotee and a non-musical person, and many of them crash horribly, but many of them meet on good terms with the folks just reconciled to being involved in different interests. The degree of involvement matters, because if one can’t abide the travel and odd rhythms of the profession of music, that can provide a stress that can do some real damage.

Re: compatibility… again!

Dragonfiddle’s “respect for individuality” as far as I’m concerned, is now my absolute Numero Uno predictor for compatibility, waaay ahead even of religion.

And there are degrees of “in common-ness” - it’s not a digital parameter. You can appear to have music or religion in common but have it be a constant source of aggravation. He says “Martin”, she says “Taylor”; she says “Gibson”, he says “Fender” :-) The fact that you both hold what you have “in common” dear can just make these “small” differences worse. Personally I’d see better prospects for a happy union between say a Catholic and an atheist than between say an “orthodox” Catholic and a “liberal” one. Or between a muso and a non-muso rather than a trad head and a “Celtic” player.

My SO is an amateur group singer and took up instruments after meeting me. We do church music and consort early music together, paid and unpaid, but there have been “moments” over me playing music he can’t be part of and doesn’t like. When we met I was a working trainee with part-time degree studies and there wasn’t time for anything other than our church music. I don’t think it occurred to him then that my horizons were broader, that music for me was not something exclusive to the relationship. Perhaps that realisation was a bit of an uncomfortable surprise.

He doesn’t want to play trad but isn’t agin me going to sessions - quite happy to drop me off and collect me and be supportive that way. He finds it a fast and furious barrage, but he doesn’t mind Lunasa too much and can survive most of Kevin Crehan’s solo disc. He bought a book of Irish & Scottish tunes for cello and plays them at home every now and then, and occasionally asks me to write out something like Autumn Child or Crested Hens in bass clef. Last time I tossed out my out-of-date thesession tune book he salvaged it and said he’d have a go at some of the jigs on mandolin. He likes a lot of the tunes, but at his own pace.

Our different approaches to practice can create friction though. In group situations it’s rare that any fluff he makes is particularly noticeable or brings the performance to a halt. He’s also working at home and can practice here and there if he chooses. Different story for me, so I need to be more obsessive about practice - I have more of it to do and can only do it in a block at day’s end. He also always has a script put in front of him and most of the time, it’s me that has to write it, so I need thinking/messing around space to come up with ideas, which sometimes is interpreted as noodling to fill in time. So when I’ve had it after work but am under pressure to pull the next gig together, I get a bit crabby at a tendency to interrupt me to sign this cheque, tell me about that conversation, remind me to do XYZ, blah blah. That said, he does cook dinner while I’m practising! ;-)
