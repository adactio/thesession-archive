Help Plan My First Business Trip.

I successfully finished my first big music project this last weekend. The project was an experiment to see if the rumors were true about how much money professional buskers could potentially make in large cities. After quitting my mundane factory job, working as a full-time musician for the last several months, street performing being my main source of income, and experiencing the push and flow for myself, I have no reason to believe that those rumors aren’t true. I won’t mention numbers, for the sake of modesty, but so that you understand how serious I am about this, I will say this: My goal for the project was to hit the bottom-end of the rumor. I hit that goal, semi-locally.

So my next project is to see how close I can get to the high-end of the rumor, and how consistently.

(This street performing thing is what I like to call a “short term means, to a long term end”. I don’t intend to live the rest of my life as a street performer. My ultimate goal is to be able to live comfortably selling music. That’s why I call it a project, because it’s short term and is more like a huge assessment rather than an actual plan.)

To do this, I will have to plan a trip to one of the large cities across the country. But I don’t know anything about planning trips. That’s why I’m posting this here, you guys travel a lot and I’m positive you’ll know what I need to know.

The target is Las Vegas, Nevada. Large city, where busking is common, there are actual laws and ordinances in place in an effort to keep things professional and civil, and it’s a tourist city, where people go to gamble. I’m not sure if there is a more perfect place. People talk about New Orleans often, it’s apparently the Top City in the country for street performers. But my personal target is Las Vegas.

Now, since it’s cross country, I see either one of two options. I can either fly over, and stay for a weekend to test the waters. Or I can go on a cross country road trip, with Las Vegas as my destination, and stay for a week or two. There are pros and cons to each that I’ve figured out. Maybe you guys can help me weigh things, or look into things I haven’t thought of or should consider.

Flying, Weekend:
It will be cheaper, but it is almost guaranteed that I will make significantly less profit.
I won’t need to save up for booking a hotel long term, but I also won’t have a car.
I will be able to make it to and from there safely, but I won’t have much time to explore my busking options, and then work.
If nothing works out. I won’t have lost much money, and I will be able to get back home.
And what about all of my music equipment and the planes? That’s a huge concern.

Driving/Tour:
It will be very expensive, and take loads more planning, but I can make profit the entire tour.
There’s the possibility that I could do well on the tour, but have a hard time in Las Vegas. Weather, police, hidden challenges, competition, etc.
A tour gives me an opportunity to build my fanbase and start letting people know my music exists, but the tour is definitely for profit.
I’m gonna spend a lot of money on hotels, and in some cases, maybe even parking.
I have to do more research on busking laws and ordinances for the cities that I stop in.
I can go anywhere I want to whenever. And my music equipment will be the least of my concerns.
If I’m doing well in a certain place, I can stay for a few days.

The plan is to be there sometime between May and June, but I have to be there before July, as it’s when they have a psychotic weather season.

Any advice helps. I don’t travel at all. I’ve never flown with anything other than luggage and a carry on. I’ve never even bought a hotel room. This will be an excellent learning experience, and worth a try regardless, but I want to profit if I can. Thank you for your time!

I don’t know any specifics about the US, never having been there. But I do have experience of busking around the UK and elsewhere in Europe. One thing that occurs to me is that, in places where a permit is required (and it seems like Las Vegas might be one of them), it usually takes some time (a week, maybe a month) for a permit applicaion to be processed. So, if you were to go with the ‘flying visit’ option, you might find that you are unable to get a permit in time and end up either not playing at all or, if you have the audacity, playing anyway but being constantly moved on or even arrested. Again, I know nothing about domestic flights in the US, but with a lot of equipment (keyboard, amp, seat, generator?) you are probably going to be hit with excess baggage charges - and it will be a lot to cart around at the other end without your own car.

Since you don’t have a job to go back to, and your objective is to learn, I would go for the road trip option, even if it does work out more expensive. The joy of travelling is not just in reaching your destination, but the whole journey - so you ultimately get more for your money. Busking in small towns along the way could be a good experience, even if not very profitable. As long as you manage to stay fed, sheltered and safe, and have the money to continue travelling, that’s all that matters…. and, in years to come, it will be something to tell the grandchildren ;-)

I wish you all the very best on your quest.

I’ve driven across country on tour with my bands many times. One thing to keep in mind if you intend to drive for this trip is that the Golden Rule is NEVER leave your musical equipment unattended in the car. If you’re traveling solo this can be difficult since it’s not realistic to drag everything into the bathroom with you at gas stations/rest stops. Not an issue if you’ll be traveling with a friend, but definitely something to consider if you’ll be doing the drive on your own.

As already mentioned in other posts, maybe try to network beforehand and make some connections for places to stay overnight on your journey rather than shelling out for hotels the whole way. My band would just put a call out to friends to see if anyone knew people in such and such town who’d be willing to put us up for the night. I’ve made great friends that way too.

Driving through the heart of the country has it’s other challenges that some of the other folks commenting might not have to consider, but to be frank, ones that you and I do: I’m mixed race - both myself and several of my bandmates attracted a lot of undeserved attention/hassle from local cops and on one occasion were literally escorted to the edge of town and told to keep driving. At least we were traveling as a group though, so things felt safer than if I’d been traveling on my own. We did have a cop who was hassling us change his tune though, when he heard my Irish accent - he was so taken aback, not expecting that accent to come out of my mouth, that we got out of getting a ticket for a broken tail light.

Absolutely not trying to dissuade you from deciding to drive for the trip - I’m all about choosing the adventurous option meself, just some food for thought!

Re: Help Plan My First Business Trip.

Unfortunately, youth hostels are a lot harder to come by in the States than elsewhere, and are generally more concentrated in larger cities than scattered around the country.

I’ve done quite a few long road trips, and I’ve done a lot of busking, so here’s my 2 cents:

Do the road trip idea, and pack a tent along with your gear. This website helped save me a lot of money during a 6 week road trip in the South, when the weather was good enough most nights to pitch a tent: https://freecampsites.net/ . Most of them are easily accessible in a car and either free or dirt cheap. With a blow-up mattress, you’ll sleep pretty well (or at least I do!).

Also if you haven’t already heard of couchsurfing.com, get on there. It basically connects people who are looking for a place to stay with people who have a spare bedroom or couch, and is a great way to find people and places along the way. One thing I will say is that people think this is a way to just get free lodging for the night, but please don’t treat it that way. Hosts will open up their homes to you, bring a little gift and be ready to socialize. You’ll probably get great info on where to busk, neat spots to check out, etc., and meet a bunch of really nice and interesting people along the way.

For hotels and such, I’ve always used kayak.com. If you have the app you can search your location and book at any time. My GF and I often see where the day takes us, then book an inexpensive motel over dinner, which generally costs less than $100 (usually more like $60). Airbnb.com is also a good source, people rent out rooms by the night, sort of a paying couchsurfing idea. They generally require more notice but can be cheaper and more comfortable than a Super 8 or Motel 6 (although both of those are just fine, too!).

One outlet I have not looked into but I’ve heard interesting things about is Tindr. I’ve heard of multiple people who essentially set up a Tindr profile and rather than looking for a date, say that they’re looking for a place to stay the night. Apparently it’s a good way to find a place, and if you’re single and ready to mingle it’s a good way to meet new “friends,” if you catch my drift. Not being single, I don’t have firsthand experience, but that might be an option.

Check out indieonthemove.com as well, it’s a site that lists tons of places to gig in the US. You might be able to pick up a couple nightly engagements along the way. They probably won’t be for much money, but would help supplement what you’re making busking, wouldn’t be weather dependent, and often come with a free dinner.

Look up farmer’s markets along the way; these are often open to buskers. I used to make $150 in a couple hours at a weekly farmer’s market, and chances are there’ll be at least one nearby wherever you are. Some of them you might have to contact in advance, but it’s worth it, and some of them are even paying gigs, which would be sweet.

Try out different combinations and rigs if you’re going to be busking in different places. Some don’t allow amplification, some do to a point, some are completely open to it, some have limits on time spent in one spot, or how much you can block a sidewalk, or how easily you can pack up and move if needed. If the weather is looking iffy, being able to go out with a light and easy to pack setup might mean the difference between making a little bit of money that day and none at all. Absolutely bring your fiddle, it’s the perfect instrument for those quick setup busking sessions. I’ve busked with a 6 piece band and by myself on tin whistle and everything in between, and I’ve always made at least a bit of cash.

Leverage facebook, twitter, any forums you’re on (like this one!) and other connections to find people and information in the areas you’ll be visiting. Once you have a general itinerary, finding people you know (or “know” online) along the way can be a real boon. You already have fans, why not go out and meet them? Just don’t spam, obviously, and don’t take advantage of people who are trying to help you out.

And finally, if you are anywhere in the vicinity of Sparta, Illinois, and you are looking for a place to stay, do not be tempted by the low, low price of the “Sparta Motel.” Unless, of course, you’re someone who really wants that “low budget horror film” ambiance paired with a thriving insect and arthropod community, with a side of indistinguishable stains on every piece of furniture. Speaking from personal experience.

Re: Help Plan My First Business Trip.

I should add a word of caution, too. I’ve been to Nashville, and although I didn’t busk there, I saw the amount of foot traffic the city gets. I will warn you that the vast majority of cities in the US have nowhere near that amount of foot traffic, and are therefore much less amenable to busking than Nashville. You will probably not be able to rock up to just anywhere and make much of anything, even in larger cities.

Some buskers focus on population totals as a way of gauging income from a city. I’ve heard a basic figure of 100,000 as a suitable size for a city to busk, although certain circumstances can make smaller cities viable as well. This works in Europe, since most cities have pedestrianized areas and a culture of walking or riding public transit. In the US, where the car is king, a large city could have relatively little foot traffic in its downtown area.

I’d suggest looking at college or “alternative” towns known for things like artist communities, music scenes, or historic downtowns. Places with relatively large tourist populations are also good. These, while smaller than nearby cities, will probably have much more foot traffic and a population more amenable to giving money to a busker.

For example, in my home state of Massachusetts, Boston is great for busking. But the 2nd largest city, Worcester, is terrible, mainly because everybody drives everywhere. There’s nowhere with nearly enough foot traffic to support a busker, save possibly for the arena on big event nights. About an hour to the west, the town of Northampton is known as being decent for buskers, despite having 1/6 the population of Worcester. “Noho” is in the middle of a college area and the downtown sees a lot of people on foot and bikes rather than car. The same is true in North Carolina, where Raleigh and Durham are not great, but Asheville is well known as a busking spot, although there’s plenty of competition. It pays to get local info, which is where your network and services like couchsurfing can be invaluable.

Look for festivals as well. In the spring, many places have festivals, often called the “Such and Such Blossom Festival” or something else related to flowers and crops and spring. These will have the large concentrations of people you need to make some decent money in otherwise dry spots. And I don’t have to tell you to look for Irish festivals; you’d be sure to get some cash at those! Again, research pays off.

My busking experience

I personally supported myself for about three years busking in the subway in Boston, Paris, and in the Queen’s Old Castle shopping mall in Cork city playing ITM. For a time I was working with a girlfriend who operated a homemade marionette​, which proved to be very effective in drawing people into our audience. This is of course critical, since most people won’t give you a dime if they don’t stop to give a listen. The way it worked was a mother would pass by with a three or four year old child, and the child would become enchanted with the marionette. This then became an impromptu street theater thereby causing a fairly large crowd to gather and watch. The end result being a fourfold increase in our income. Good luck.
