No shortage of finding popular session tune lists. I see these exhaustive ones such as

https://thesession.org/discussions/110

but that doesn’t narrow matters down much and doesn’t say anything of their difficulty on whistle.

I am conscious now of not overreaching as I did before and remaining stuck on tunes that are too difficult so I would like to have a nice little cache of easily accessible tunes but at the same time ones that are still well liked in most sessions (i.e. not out of favor ones like irish washerwoman or kesh jig, or out on the ocean etc.)

So far the reels which I know which fit the criteria are:

-Dunmore lasses
-Kiss the maid behind the barrel
-Cooley’s

So 7 more for reels if you please and still I have not picked any for jigs yet as all the ones (that I can recall) I know are from when I was deliberately trying to find the hardest, or most obscure tunes and which I would not make much progress on so would like to start afresh with jigs.

So 7 more picks for reels and the full house of 10 for jigs fitting the above criteria - good for whistle and not ‘worn out’, but rather popular, in sessions.

Hi Windmusic - I hope you’re well!

You can’t go far wrong with Michael Eskin’s collection! There is a healthy mix of tunes there. Some can be off the beaten track - but it is an excellent resource to get you playing well.

Also - what is ‘old hat’ or ‘out of favour’ varies wildly from session to session - and more well worn tracks even come back in fashion. They are well worn because they are of an excellence!

Any session is going to welcome a set of these tunes played nicely from the ‘greats’ - and appreciate a dedicated learner battering the classics. Everyone can join in. As a visitor and traveller, these tunes will teach you the shared repertoire and give you a feel for so much.

Playing an obscure piece that nobody knows is not really what a session is at its core.

Playing these tunes with others will also give an idea of what solid playing sounds and feels like - mostly everyone can play them well!

Re: 10 easy-medium difficulty popular session jigs and reels for tin whistle?

I’ve recently been focussing on some Vincent Broderick tunes. They are very rewarding and they fit very well on wind instruments. For example: The Rookery, Flagstone of Memory, Milky way, and Crock of Gold. They are tunes you can work up to speed on whistle - especially Rookery which is super-easy, right up there with Sally Gardens.

Other favorites of mine are: Virginia Reel, Poor but Happy at 53, and Drunken Landlady. Those last two are good for building up skill on rolls.

Another great whistle tune is Dusty Windowsills. Easy on whistle, whereas fiddlers have to struggle with some string jumps. On whistle it helps train you on breath pulses, but once you get that, you’re golden.

I don’t think any of those tunes would fall under the oldy-moldy category like Butterfly, Kesh, Cup of Tea. I would recommend building up skill on Cup of Tea. Maybe it’s a bit oldy-moldy, but as Cheeky Elf says, it is one you should have under your belt.

Edit: I just read the Eskin list. A lot of great tunes, some fairly challenging, Most of them are not in the top-100 oldy-moldies, but I recognize many of them as being popular in sessions I’ve been to. Not all are suitable for whistle (Tam Lin? really? More of a fiddle-fest-tune).

Re: 10 easy-medium difficulty popular session jigs and reels for tin whistle?

I think there’s a balance to be had between learning the warhorses and learning tunes that really speak to you. My point about not making lists is that if you feel pressure to learn things on your list instead of things that inspire you (not that things on the warhorse lists can’t do that too), then it’s going to feel more like a job than a passion.

In general, you shouldn’t beat yourself up about anything related to the music. It’s common for even very experienced players to feel a bit of panic about all the tunes they don’t know. And especially when you’re first starting out, there is a lot of comparing one’s own progress against the progress of others, and some panic when someone appears to be progressing faster than you. In my experience, that never completely goes away. There will always be people better than you, but it’s not a competition. So it’s best not to worry about what you need to learn, and just embrace the learning as a life-long journey of discovery and joy.

Re: 10 easy-medium difficulty popular session jigs and reels for tin whistle?

Just because a lot of sessions don’t regularly play the warhorse standards doesn’t make them “useless” for sessions, because undoubtedly, almost every player in a session is going to know those tunes (and so should you). As Will mentioned, most sessions end up cycling through tunes, and most players like to keep learning tunes all the time. So certain tunes will come in and out of vogue at any given session.

But when someone new joins a session, a little dance is played to try to find tunes in common with that person. If the players are playing their current “in vogue” tunes, and the new player isn’t playing along, they will invariably start gravitating toward the more common tunes specifically to get the new player more involved. And they’ll probably ask the new player to start something. If the new player pulls out something obscure, then there’s a chance that they’ll be playing solo, which many players find uncomfortable. So that’s another reason to have some good standards in your repertoire!

But again, I don’t think it’s necessarily worth limiting yourself to learning only tunes on “the list”, either. Over time, you will pick up all the standards anyway, because they’re often played a lot at festivals and such, because those are groups of people that don’t necessarily know each other’s repertoires. Something you can do to achieve some balance in your repertoire is to go to local sessions (post pandemic, of course) and learn what their current “in vogue” tunes might be, and start adding those into your learning queue…

Which brings up another point. A lot of people feel like they need to be “ready” to play in sessions, and spend a lot of time practicing to get there. While you do need a certain level of proficiency to be a productive member of a session, you can’t get good at playing with other people solely by practicing and learning on your own. (And you certainly have enough skill set to be playing in some sessions!) The real learning comes in those very sessions, where you have to learn how to play with other people that might play with different swing or at different tempos that you’re used to, and they play tunes you’ve never heard before, etc. So I think it’s important to get yourself out there (after quarantine) and get your feet wet, even if you end up sitting there for most of the night with your instrument in your lap…

Re: 10 easy-medium difficulty popular session jigs and reels for tin whistle?

> I have learned about 8 in the last few days

Your brain needs a “tag” of some sort that it can associate with a particular tune. That tag is for most people something to do with the circumstances in which they learnt it, so I have tags like “the four part jig I learnt from Claire” or “the reel I learnt at SCAT in Aberdeen” or “the intro hornpipe we played in 2001”.

When you learn half a dozen tunes together, you are giving them all the same “tag”. If someone reminded you of one, you’d remember it, but you haven’t a way in to enumerate all eight of them.

So, when you learn tunes, decide on a tag and think of it while you learn the tune. This tune is a Ford Cortina. This tune has long, slightly greasy hair. This tune smells of freshly-cut grass.

Don’t worry overmuch though. It tends to sort itself out.

Re: 10 easy-medium difficulty popular session jigs and reels for tin whistle?

When choosing good tunes for the whistle, you have to be fairly selective. A lot of fiddle tunes don’t work well on the whistle if they feature a lot of notes in the lower octave - they just go under when you’re playing along with other instruments.

One tune I would recommend - and hasn’t been mentioned in this thread so far - is the reel The Sally Gardens. It’s in G major, but hexatonic - so you don’t need the C natural note. I think you can get up to a fair speed on it quickly.
