Re: trad in modern music?

What usually happens as far as I can judge is that up-and-coming musicians take what they can from whatever source they want -- which is fair enough -- and make what they can from it. Sometimes it works, and sometimes it doesn’t. It’s like a cook trying out recipes. But they must have the raw ingredients to start with. Trad, blues, jazz, swing -- you name it, have all been refined and shaped by the best musicians for generations and constitute the raw materials for the new musicians to work with. As long as they don’t destroy the ingredients in the process by mixing them all together, I don’t really care what they do, but I rarely hear anything new from them. Most of them are the musical equivalent of ‘conceptual’ artists, who try desperately to come up with new ideas just for the sake of it. Occasionally someone comes along who can contribute -- like the Bothy Band -- but people like that are rare, and understand and respect what they are working with.

Re: trad in modern music?

christofloffer, the tradition isn’t the music, per se. It is the tradition of playing the music. It is *us*, the people who play it. If you play the music as if it’s a historical piece, or because it reminds you of times long past, then IMO, you’re looking at it the wrong way. It’s not the music that ties us to the past, it’s the line of people that have played it over the years, and passed it on down the line, all the way to us, and beyond. That’s the tradition. And we’re all part of it. It isn’t like we’re recreating a long-lost tradition, it’s that we *are* the tradition.

Whether “modern trad” has a place in the tradition, well, I look at it like this: Modern music that draws from trad as an influence certainly has a place in this world. (Who are we to say that people can’t do something). But I choose to look at is as being separate from the tradition, not part of it. There are certainly instances where music is on the fringes of the tradition, but like I said, the tradition is us, not the music, exactly. So it’s up to each of us to decide what we consider to fit within the tradition. Now, if you’re looking for consensus, forget it! :-/

Re: trad in modern music?

Caoimhín Ó Raghallaigh
‘…what happens when you stop playing tunes…’:
“I guess that a lot of traditional music today is ego driven. The focus is on what do you do to a tune, what are the variations you can put in, how cool can you make it. A reversal of that for me is playing a tune, one tune, a very simple tune, that maybe takes 20 seconds to play. I would play it over and over and over for maybe half an hour. For the first ten minutes, my brain churns out all the possible variations and different things it could possibly do – that was very much me doing my thing to the tune. But then it would go through a period, again probably about 10 minutes, of the tune just being completely empty of everything, just the bare notes. And then something happens – things would start to come from nowhere. I think that’s probably pretty common with all sorts of artists, where you are no longer in control. Everyone talks about that, but it happened for me at age 17, and since then that for me is what music is about.”
http://journalofmusic.com/article/661
