Deaf

I’m 63, I have tinnitus and I’ve lost my high frequencies. I have hearing aids that help a lot, but not really enough. I’ve given up playing music in the pub with my mates. Our venues are small and quite noisy and there’s nearly always a background hubbub. I find it next to impossible to banter with the other blokes and I can’t be doing with sitting there all night with a face as long as a gasman’s mac. If everyone’s playing melody instruments I can cope quite well, but as soon as someone picks up a guitar to do something fancy, or if a banjo strikes up, or if the only other melody player is a fiddler ten feet away, I’m all at sea. I started late at this game and I’ve learned almost entirely by listening and interacting. It sounds grim but, as I’ve been unhappy with my lot for quite a while apropos of my hearing, it actually came as quite a relief when I decided to pack it in, so I’m not miserable!

I’d be interested to hear about anyone else’s experiences with this problem, which can’t be rare among older musicians. But please don’t don’t tell me to do kitchen sessions - no chance of that ever happening round my way!

Re: Deaf

Steve, you are only one year my senior (old bugger!), so I empathize. My hearing is fine, but I suffer from peripheral neuropathy, which is a condition in the nerves that causes a loss of sensitivity in the fingers and feet, often accompanied by pain. I have no pain, but it is now a challenge to play flute. I can’t play whistle at all anymore and have sold my prized Copeland whistle. I have worked diligently to manage around the condition and now own a Siccama flute that features two more keys than an eight-key. The keys are operated by the two ring fingers, which are the weakest. Having only to activate the key rather than seal the hole with my finger reduces the occasion of missing a note due to air leakage. The extra keys also help to keep the entire hand aligned. So, barring the condition getting worse, I can still play, albeit with increased concentration and care in fingering the notes. My Dad had the condition and I hope it levels off on me as it did with him.

If I had been just a whistle player, I would have been sidelined by this. All one can do is to manage around life’s curve balls the best one can. My best wishes are with ye, me old mustard-mate.

“I find it next to impossible to banter with the other blokes…” Me too. I miss a lot of the jokes and slagging. If the pub is at all busy or loud I feel isolated. So my advice, dear Steve, is to get a fiddle and play it like mad. It’s under your ear and as a player you can hear it just fine. The fiddle is tough but you’re young and disciplined enough that you have many years left after you’ve mastered the basics.
I am a lot older than you are and I am still hopeful. So… happy days!
