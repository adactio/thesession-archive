Are sessions boring for the listener?

I’ve been listening to Comhaltas Session CDs as an example of what i should be aspiring to and a means of learning tunes by ear.I have to admit that though they’re useful for this purpose,there’s a monotony about them.And these are skilful players.I’m wondering whether ITM is best served by the typical session playing,which is more pleasurable for the players than the punters.This music was composed for dancers.When music is played all night in repetitive dance form,usually in either keys of Dor G,it can sound boring.There is little room for expressive playing.And often there is a lack of backing/cords unless provided by often frowned upon instruments like guitar or piano.I’m wondering whether better quality ITM is produced by breaking into smaller groups where there is more scope for individual expression.And more satisfying for the player.

Re: Are sessions boring for the listener?

From the pub punter’s point of view it’s probably true to say that the music coming out a session can sometimes sound very “same-ish”, although it obviously wouldn’t to the players. To many punters it may be a sort of generally fairly agreeable background music that is basically there to be heard, but not listened to, as long as it doesn’t intrude on conversations. I suggest it could be instructive to a sessioneer to sit out a session for 10-15 minutes and go to the other end of the pub to hear the music from the point of view of the pub punter (I’ve done it myself).

In our Tuesday English* session we regularly have people sitting fairly close to us, obviously listening to and enjoying the music for most of the evening, and then afterwards coming up and talking to us about the music - and they are not necessarily players or musicians themselves.

*Btw, our “English” session is fairly eclectic and includes non-English folk music, including Irish, Scottish and French.

Re: Are sessions boring for the listener?

“I’ve been listening to Comhaltas Session CDs as an example of what i should be aspiring to and a means of learning tunes by ear.”

Learning by ear? Very good. As for what you should be aspiring to? Wrong. You should find and listen to music and musicians you find inspiring.

“I have to admit that though they’re useful for this purpose,there’s a monotony about them.And these are skilful players.I’m wondering whether ITM is best served by the typical session playing,which is more pleasurable for the players than the punters.”

Yeah, the typical session is more about the sessioners pleasure than the audience. Some music is like that, and trad isn’t alone there. Someone once said, “I’d fly across the world to sing sacred harp music, but I wouldn’t walk across the street to listen to it”. Luckily trad is a lot more pleasing on the ears.

“This music was composed for dancers.When music is played all night in repetitive dance form,usually in either keys of Dor G,it can sound boring.There is little room for expressive playing.”

There’s much more then D or G, especially if you consider different modes, who do have a different color. Not to mention, there is lots of room for expressive playing. In fact, isn’t that the entire point of this music?

“And often there is a lack of backing/cords unless provided by often frowned upon instruments like guitar or piano.”

Well, most of this music was not really created with backing chords in mind. If they are there and good, fine, if they aren’t there, what’s the problem?

“I’m wondering whether better quality ITM is produced by breaking into smaller groups where there is more scope for individual expression.And more satisfying for the player.”

Depends on the tune(s), the size of the group, the people in the group, the mix of instruments, the atmosphere, the mood, sign of the moon, and the numbers of people playing all at once. Personally I prefer a session that is comfortable, cozy, and social.

I figure if the musicians in a session are having fun and enjoying the music at least some of the people listening will enjoy it too.

Every picture tells a story …and every tune for that matter.
If you can’t hear the story in the tune though I suppose it can be boring. Irish music I’ve read described as an ‘acquired taste’, and you can see this reflected in the flat affect of most pub punters who aren’t used to the music. Their ears droop the more they listen, seemingly.
Interesting phenomenon I saw recently at a session. Tunes great, good playing, flat-affect punters. Hypothesis: punters can’t really aurally analyse and understand a tune by itself…put something with it, like a guitar and a bodhran (well played of course), and then it translates the tune into something the punter can relate to. Hence you get the punters involved and interested. A great challenge and a huge mystery to be enjoyed over a settling Guinness.
