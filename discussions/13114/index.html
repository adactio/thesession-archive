Should we expect to be paid for Recreational Activities (Or The Session Leader, Part Two)?

*The other thread was getting a bit lengthy and I thought we could focus more closely on one or two points here in a new thread*

Firstly, I’d like to state (as I did elsewhere) that I don’t disagree with the general principle of session hosts or core regular musicians who have been “invited” or previously agreed to turn up to a pub or similar to play tunes.

However, I can’t help but being uneasy about the concept of people being paid to do something which is considered to be a recreational activity or hobby. If you go along to a pub to play darts, dominoes, or even watch the football on TV, you wouldn’t expect to get paid for this. So, what’s the difference about going along to play a few tunes?

Ah, you’ll say that the punters and management are getting the benefit; i.e hearing music and possible increase in bar takings. Of course, we’re assuming that the punters *want* to listen in the first place and you could also argue that bar takings will also increase if there is a darts/dominoes match being held or a big football game on TV.

Fair enough, if the musicians are there because they have been invited to “entertain” but, in that case, shouldn’t they be doing so and interacting with the punters etc rather than “diddling away in a corner”?

Another argument is that a host’s duty is to “facilitate” and “welcome” visiting musicians, perhaps even encouraging and (in a few cases) offering a few tips here and there. However, you might be playing doms/darts and invite a visitor to “join in” and they might have a few games with you and buy drink at the bar. Would you really expect to get paid for this?

In most cases, playing music isn’t our “day jobs” and, even for full time musicians, “the session” is surely just a place to relax and unwind. If someone wishes to make a living or earn money from music, oughtn’t they be seeking proper gigs or engagements no matter how modest? I know these can be difficult to find but “playing in a session” shouldn’t have to be their prime source of “bread and butter”.

Now, I realise that I’ve offered some extreme, arguably even ridiculous examples, here. However, I do believe this is an area which is extremely blurred in that it(playing in a session) is regarded by some but not others as “work”. Moreover, it’s only regarded as “work” when it suits them. Rather strange!

Thanks for all the responses. They are many and very interesting especially as this is just a supplementary discussion.
A couple of points….

Ptarmigan, I don’t think I’ve missed the point although I agree that we might be at cross purposes. It would take another discussion (maybe several) :-) before we could agree on a definition for a “professional” musician. However, if we’re talking about those who are involved in music on a full time time basis, you’ll probably agree that the majority aren’t particularly “well off” and a session is another source of income. I was merely commenting that it is a shame that musicians are so undervalued that they can’t make a good living from (probably) their first choices of work e.g live concerts, CDs and so on. Of course, I do realise that yourself and many others prefer to be involved in teaching and so on.

Anyway, I’ve no problem with professional musicians(or anyone else) being involved in sessions or being eligible for payment when it is available or appropriate.
Again, I do realise that some of my comparisons in the first post might be considered a little far fetched, if not spurious. Of course, I can understand why “anchor” or “host” musicians who turn up to a session on a regular basis or musicians “invited” to play in premises by the management for a particular occasion(s) should be paid. They are obviously viewed as providing some form of entertainment, service, etc and deserve to be remunerated.
However, if I(or another musician) wants to join a session “for a tune” or go and start a “one off” session somewhere, that’s a different matter. It is possible too that they just wish a place to play on a regular basis and might be quite happy to do it for free. Of course, as Danny observes, if this obviously results in attracting people to the pub and/or increasing the bar takings, then negotiation with the management for some form of payment is quite reasonable. They’d be foolish not to do so.

Basically(and I’m probably not that much out of step with you all…..it’s just having to communicate via this board), all I’m suggesting that while, on occasion, playing in a session might be regarded as a job or “work”, this isn’t always the case and it shouldn’t necessarily be regrded as “the norm” for everyone and in every situation. There are so many different possibilities.

In the foreseeable future, I don’t see that sessions will become too exclusive but, perhaps, they might in another generation. That’s my main concern. In my previous career(in which I didn’t have a “whale of a time” :-)), we always used to get extremely irritated when these “hobby bobbies” or special constables used to turn out and work for free when it suited them(or the senior management).
I’m sure you’ll argue(I wouldn’t disagree) that it’s different “ball game” as far as music is concerned but I’d hate to see the day when all sessions comprised “specially chosen” musicians who were all overly protective of their turf and visiting musicians(both professional and amateur) might feel reluctant to join them or start their own session in case they were standing on peoples toes.

Of course, as long as there are plenty of suitable session venues and amenable land lords this might never happen. However, I’m sure that you’ll agree that “session friendly” pubs are becoming much more scarce.

Re: Should we expect to be paid for Recreational Activities (Or The Session Leader, Part Two)?

I also admitted very early on, even before I finished my first post, that these comparisons were a bit extreme/silly. Of course, if you were to invite Eric Bristow or Jocky Wilson to your local for a game of darts…hoping it would bring in a few punters..then I’d expect they’d want paid too.

“Session is a Public Performance”

We’ve argued about this one very often and I’d suggest rather vaguely that “it is and it isn’t” or “it may be or not be depending on the circumstances”. However, if it is perceived to be so by the publican and punters or they are deriving some enjoyment however slight, then I’d argue that it was.

As I say, I think it all depends on whether the music has been “encouraged” and/or the musicians invited or not. Even in cases where musicians set up their own sessions, they still have right to negotiate(and arguably should) if they feel that the management are obviously benefitting from this or taking advantage eg “Come on lads, how about a few more tunes….” etc.

“who else would work regularly in a Pub …. for nothing?”

The key words here “regularly” and “work”. Both of these imply that there’s an *expectation* that the musicians will be there to ensure that “the music happens” or “to lead, facilitate, encourage” or whatever. As I’ve now said several times, I’ve no problem with this. However, if I just fancy going out to my local session pub for a few pints and a tune, I don’t expect anything.
However, if I got a couple of free pints for my trouble, I wouldn’t object.

Again, I doubt if our views are really that much different even although we’ve been clashing here a wee bit. :-)
