An ocarina made for ITM- but do ocarinas belong in ITM?

I’ve been heavily practicing my 12 hole ocarina lately. Unfortunately it doesn’t have the range for most Irish tunes. If you want to play ITM in Irish music, you really need a double chamber ocarina.

So while window shopping for double ocarinas I stumbled upon this beauty.

https://pureocarinas.com/shop/pure-double-alto-d-ocarina

Very similar fingering to a tin whistle, in the right key for ITM. But this brings us back to a discussion I see has been brought up before- do ocarinas belong in Irish music? Sure it’s not an ITM instrument right now, but could it be one day? It wasn’t that long ago that playing mandolins at sessions was controversial, and now they’re almost universally accepted.

On the other hand, if you start allowing ocarinas at a session maybe it’s only a matter of time before you’ve got people bringing castanets and saxophones and pretty soon you’re not playing ITM anymore.

I’ve heard lots of people playing Irish tunes on ocarinas- some of them are fairly appropriate, some are quite blah. It can be done if the ocarina is in the right hands.

Personally I’m in the pro-ocarina camp, but I’d like to see what other people think.

Re: An ocarina made for ITM- but do ocarinas belong in ITM?

Although I guess I wouldn’t mind a skilled enough ocarina player in a session, I can think of a couple of downsides to taking it up in preference to something else:

It’s not tunable except for some variation in breath pressure (?), so if a session is drifting slightly off A440 you may not be able to follow where the rest of the group is going, the way you can with a whistle or flute with a tuning slide. And if you can’t manage A440 to begin with, due to ambient temperature or some other factor, it can be annoying for groups that are stable at that tuning. There is nothing worse than a whistle that’s out of tune with the group, because you can really hear it in that pitch range that stands out above the other instruments.

Also you have no other options to extend the range and scale. For me this would be the major disadvantage. Whistle players sometimes bring a collection of whistles in different keys to facilitate certain keys and modes, like a C whistle for tunes in D dorian or a low F whistle for Lúnasa tune arrangements. Low D whistles are available as a flute alternative. Of course an ocarina player could switch off to whistles for those purposes, but it would seem more logical to just stick to whistles in that case.

All things considered, the ocarina seems to me to be a one trick pony, and I’m not seeing any advantages that would lead one to choose it over the whistle for this music.

Re: An ocarina made for ITM- but do ocarinas belong in ITM?

IMO wrote “New techniques and instruments get accepted as time moves on.”

Yes, but not *all* new techniques and instruments win acceptance. There have been excellent sax and clarinet Irish trad players for decades, but they’re not popping up at sessions everywhere. Same with bass—Trevor Hutchinson has helped give Lunasa its signature sound for 25 years now, yet few other Irish trad bands feature bass, and they’re rare to nonexistent at sessions. (FWIW, I’ve played in sessions with a sax, clarinet, and bass (not all together), and several other non-trad instruments, and enjoyed it just fine. The players were top notch, knew the tunes, and were sensitive about blending in rather than overpowering everyone else.)

My sense is that the instruments that do make the cut have two things going for them: (1) they can produce timbres suited to trad sensibilities—reedy, chiffy, “dhurty,” and (2) they can be played at a volume that isn’t overpowering. Interestingly, that second point is one that’s commonly raised in screeds about banjos, keyboards, accordions, and sometimes even guitars.

Yes, traditions evolve, but they do so, by default and by definition, slowly and selectively. Otherwise Irish trad wouldn’t be distinguishable from techno or Afro-Pop or jazz.

I’m glad to hear from another ocarina enthusiast Aldon!

I’ve seen tuning slides on ocarinas, I have no idea how much they effect the pitch or how they even work. However, it doesn’t seem necessary to me since the ocarina is completely chromatic, unlike the tin whistle. The only problem with the ocarina is the limited range, but that’s not a problem once you add a second or even a third chamber. Quadruple chamber ocarinas exist, but they’re rare.

People have mentioned the inconvenience of a fixed pitch instrument, but it’s not a problem with the ocarina since the tone of it is so flexible that you can play as sharp or as flat as necessary to be in tune with other instruments simply by slightly adjusting breath pressure. I tend to play flat when I’m by myself but have no problem moving up a bit to match other people I’m playing with.

Re: An ocarina made for ITM- but do ocarinas belong in ITM?

@Wodanaz Ocarinas are a lot louder than you may expect, and part of the problem is that ‘the’ ocarina is a collection of different instruments with one name - you can not classify all of them by one you may have encountered. This especially applies to 4 hole pendant ones - which really aren’t worth anyone’s time given that the transverse ocarina exists.

Italian (menaglio) ocarinas can easily hold their own against a french-style G bagpipe, and most Asian-made 12 hole ocarinas can do the same on the mid to high notes, but they generally lack power in the low end (the range extension comes at the cost of quiet low notes).

Pure Ocarinas, which I am the maker of, were designed to balance with a typical pub session and even then I sometimes get people complaining that it is too loud. The ‘pure’ tone is cutty, its easy to hear through other instruments.

I play in sessions frequently but have few recordings of it. My playing style and instruments have been developing alongside each other for years, and I’m doing what I can given the time I have available to learn and practice music.
