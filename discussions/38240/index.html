Exploding Fiddle

There I was, quietly sitting by the fire on Monday night. Wifey Kate had gone to craft group, I had the TV soaps turned off as soon as she was gone. Just me and the dog, toasting our butts, fire blazing. Me swigging a glass of red wine, Spike (the dog) having a wiggle, then BANG !! Scared the wits outta me, and made the dog jump too! Saw something flutter up and down again, then figured it all out - my fiddle was sitting on a stand beside me. The tailpiece cord had snapped, so the tailpiece leapt upwards as it was held in tension by the strings, smacked the bridge and sent it up in the air. Bridge flew up about 4 feet. I had shifted the cord about a year ago to add another knot as a friend reckoned my tailpiece was getting too close to the bridge. I also thought that maybe 2 knots would have been better. Well we’re never going to know now! I’ve got a new tailpiece cord to install, no knots needed, this one has little threaded ferrules. Had hoped to install it tonight, but went to an Open Mic night instead. Fit it tomorrow instead. Any one here ever had this happen?

I was at a session one evening just before Christmas a good few years ago in a country pub - open fire going full blast and pretty near standing room only - and well into a set of tunes (eyes shut and concentrating) when I heard an almighty bang on the table in front of me. Scared the life out of me.

And there it was. A fiddle, in two pieces, the neck broken clean away from the body of the fiddle with the pieces held together by the strings.

The owner of course was devastated. I commiserated with him and wondered what had happened. It turned out that the guy had ridden his motorbike to the session (in sub zero temperatures I might add) with the fiddle on his back and it must have been the temperature differentials that had weakened the joint I supposed.
Luckily, if you can call it that, the break looked pretty clean so, I would have thought, repairable although I never did find out if it was.
Count your blessings Mr. Gurgan!

I’ve been around a few catastrophic failures in sessions before. About 8 years ago, in between sets, a very expensive bouzouki hanging on a stand next to the player made a horrendous sound. At first we thought a string had spontaneously broken. But unfortunately the top had split and collapsed, and the sound was a combination of a cracking sound with all the strings twanging from the change in tension. The bouzouki was rebuilt but the same thing happened again and it never recovered.

On a more humorous note (so to speak), I was playing my Silver Bell banjo in a session a few months ago, and it was behaving strangely. I was having to tune it between every set. I blamed it on the changing weather. But then, in the middle of a set of blazing reels, I felt myself go out of tune rather drastically, looked down, and had all my strings go slack with a sproing sound. Turns out the nut holding the tailpiece on had fallen to off. Never could find it, so who knows how long it had been gone, with just the tension holding the tailpiece. But it was a very unexpected development during while playing. Thankfully, unlike a fiddle, it was a banjo that could be fixed with a wrench and screwdriver ;-)

Re: Exploding Fiddle

The bridge falling over can be pretty alarming - even though it can usually be easily put back in place.

A few years ago, I went to a festival in South Wales. I hitched across from just over the border in England, having been visiting friends in Herefordshire, and got caught in some of the downpours I’ve encountered. I’ve been drenched on the road many a time, but my ‘showerproof’ instrument cases were no match for this rain and, when I arrived, I found my instruments *wet* - i.e. dripping with water. I dried them off as best I could, although I had no choice but to put them back into their wet cases. Despite this, they seemed to perform OK over the weekend. When I got back home, I went to the local session. After the rough treatment, my fiddle had gone very flat; as I gently coaxed it back up to pitch, it emitted a loud SNAP, which made everyone both jump and gasp in horror. Once I realised the bridge had fallen over, I thought nothing of it - this can happen sometimes when doing any dramatic retuning, if you don’t keep an eye on the bridge feet as you tune. I stood the bridge up and tried again - it fell over again, and everyone jumped and gasped again. After the third attempt, I realised that the excessive moisture had caused the bridge to warp, so it was never going to stand up. A new bridge was the only thing for it. I really ought to thing about getting a waterproof case.

My other exploding instrument incident was also weather related. I built a mandolin out of a coconut shell, designed to be packed into a rucksack for a walk from London to Portugal. It sounded good - like a mandolin played through a valve radio - although it was rather awkward to hold. Still, I busked on it along the way, and it never failed to arouse curiosity. I had got as far as Lower Normandy when I was busking one Sunny afternoon and my playing was interrupted by a loud CRACK, then another… and then another. The soundboard had split in three places, and folded under the tension of the strings. In retrospect, it was a design flaw - I never imagined that anything as rigid as a coconut shell could possible flex, so I had not put in any longitudinal reinforcement. But, under the tension of 8 steel strings, flex it did - more than the cedar soundboard could bear. It would no doubt have happened eventually anyway, but it was probably accelerated by having the full glare of the afternoon sun upon it. I carried it with me the rest of the way to Portugal - and back. I still haven’t got round to fixing it.
