banjo plunking

t’was a splendid banjo plunker in the pub last night and I was thinking about what it was that made it not give me a headache … like the vast majority of banjo plunking.

And thinking about it being a percussion instrument it was obvious really … it’s the rhythm. Or more specifically, the timing.

And then, of course, the same thing goes for the music on any instrument … it’s the rhythm. Or more specifically, the timing. (Though I suppose you spot it more with a percussion instrument … just ’cause there’s nothing else to it really. But that doesn’t mean it’s any less about the timing on a fiddle or a flute, of course.)

When the timing is spot on the percussive aspect of the banjo magically disappears, as does the click of a metronome when you play precisely in time with it. [*This is your pet subject this banjo bridge thing! *]-Solidmahog

I think it’s got to do with what the ear gets used to. For years I attended sessions where the banjo was played loud, near the bridge, and the multiple triplets immediately reminded me of “drilling” (in case it wasn’t obvious!).

After that, for a while I played with a 5-string bluegrass player, and the tonal differences were phenomenal, When he played a break, or sometimes even a full reel, dpending on what we were playing, he’d be up close to the bridge, ripping out the melody really clearly. Other times, (even during full “tunes”) and in accomp. mode, he’d be well away from the bridge and mellow, yet still clear. I think that’s what I was trying to get at. I don’t hear much variation in tenor banjo volume/tone in sessions, and there sure is room for it. That’s all.

I do agree with llig in that because banjo is so percussive, it’s less forgiving in timing/intonation faults. Just like an electric guitar/electric violin can be unforgiving. It’s right up front - in your face. It needs to be good :)

Don’t put words into my mouth, even using spoons.
I’m referring to the initial attack of the banjo string, which is very short and relatively quiet. When the notes are regular and on time, the attack is masked by the attack of your own playing, but the louder note still carries on, however briefly.
