Sessions

Years ago people used to play trad in houses now they play in pubs. People forget that pubs are commercial business. I play traditional music but I’m not playing in some ones pub unless they pay me. I can play for the love of music at home, I don’t see what “crack” there is in exploitation. Also dealing with musicians is hard work why should I bother?

Re: Sessions

The sessions I go to where I live are not paid. We are there playing in the pub on sufferance. I’m thoroughly happy with that. It means one can stop and start, go out to the smoking area or wherever, come back, doze, chat, let a newbie in to play Egan’s Polka slowly (or whatever), and various other things that might be held to short-change punters for whom a session might be an advertised or indeed a paying attraction. In other words, there’s no condition that we perform: we’re there to enjoy ourselves. That means, among other things, being relatively free from demands to play The Wild Rover, etc. It helps, admittedly, that in two relevant pubs we occupy rooms aside from the main bar.

The music-making varies between desultory and astonishing. We *do*, actually, want to play well, and improve our game. And people do come to listen to us.

Re: Sessions

In London, most sessions have 2 or 3 paid musicians - usually very good ones. The rest of us (musicians) turn up for the pleasure of playing with them, and each other. Punters also turn up on session nights specifically to listen to the music - and buy a few shots, pints or mineral waters while they are there. The paid musicians are paid to turn up and play on a given night at an agreed time, in order to bring in punters, and do so. The other musicians have no such commitment - they have the choice to turn up or not - so it would be unreasonable for them to expect payment. Sometimes, there may be visiting musicians that are indisputably better players than the paid regulars, but the same still applies - they are there for their own enjoyment (If they happen to pull in a good crowd, it’s a bonus for the publican.)

Where I live in Wales,on the other hand, i. good trad players are few and far between and ii. keen trad listeners are few and far between. People like to play in pubs anyway, with or without payment or audience - and do. So paying people for playing a session is money wasted, as far as the publican is concerned.
