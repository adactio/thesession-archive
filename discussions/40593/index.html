waiting times

Hi everybody!

This is mostly a request for advice (and also a little bit getting stuff off my chest because waiting is soooo hard :) )
Two years ago I ordered an uilleann pipes chanter. Had a nice chat with the maker on the phone, we discussed all the options etc. He said he’d sneak it in between other orders and I’d have it in a few months. Two years later and I’m still waiting. Sigh :)
Then six months ago my flute developed a crack. The flute maker said he’d take a look at it, so I sent it in… and I’m still waiting for the confirmation that the flute even arrived. Sigh :)
So… after three months of waiting, I thought I’d buy a backup flute to be able to at least play SOME music, can’t be that hard really! Found a maker who said he had a flute all ready to go in 1-2 weeks. Sent the money, and three months later… I’m still waiting. Sigh :)
So, what’s your advice?
Am I doing something wrong?
Obviously being a pushy customer wouldn’t help at all, but I feel that I fail to understand something important.
And:
Where’d you get a flute while waiting?

PS Makers’ names left out for obvious reasons. They’re awesome guys and hopefully/probably swamped with work.

Long waits are often the way it is with sole-trading instrument makers and repairers - things get pushed down the priority list, machines break down, materials run out, personal and family circumstances interfere with workflow etc. But failing to deliver on a promise without explanation or apology is bad form - they should give realistic estimates in the first place. By the time an instrument maker gains enough skill and recognition to become known as a serious producer of instruments - they’re also old enough to start experiencing personal health problems and if not their own, then health issues of spouses, relatives and friends. Then there’s funerals, kids and grand kid graduations, seminars, vacations and delays caused by changes in regulations regarding finish products, ivory trade, import and export regulations and probably more things than I care to think about. Even local humidity against humidity at the destination can play a part. I’m not surprised at delays themselves but I shudder that any responsible person in the trade would sideline a repair or a purchase for years and not offer to return the deposit if they can’t deliver in a reasonable amount of time.

↳ Posted by Robert Ross on March 31st, 2017.

Re: waiting times

Adding to the frustration over the wait is the scenario that’s happened to me more than once with more than one sort of instrument:

1) you play a few instruments by a particular maker and they’re consistently brilliant.

2) you put down your money with the maker and wait.

3) after a number of delays (the reasons for which have an impressive variety, like “the Canadian Postal strike” when your instrument is travelling from the UK to the USA) a couple years later you get your instrument and it plays nothing like the ones by the same maker you’d tried.

4) you contact the maker and he says some jaw-dropping thing like “well, you know, I decided to try a bit of an experiment, not sure it worked very well…”

After a number of instances of this happening with various flute and uilleann pipe makers, and determined to not have it happen again, before I ordered an uilleann chanter I told the maker of my previous bad experiences with other makers and wanted reassurance that the chanter he would make for me would be like the few I had played (which were extraordinarily consistent). “Oh no” he said “I have my design totally settled, I’ve been making it for years and I never change a thing”.

The wait was bizarre because I’d call him every few months (beginning at the promised date, which I never imagined would be met anyhow) and he’d always say he would make mine soon.

After a couple years, during one of my usual calls he amazed me by saying “Oh it’s you, I wondered what had happened to you. Your chanter has been done for ages, been sitting in a drawer here for a year.” (I guess it hadn’t occurred to him to post it to me!)

So the chanter finally arrived and guess what? It didn’t play like his others. Laying a couple other chanters by him, and mine, side-by-side it could be seen that the finger-holes were in different places, and from how it played and the non-interchangeability of the reeds I think the bore was different too.

3.5 years is short in the uilleann world.

Re: waiting times

In many ways I totally agree with you steve, I play amazing music on a 35e Uke And I play badly on instruments that cost 50x that. :-) but I have to point out that, as a piper, what I play is not ‘folk music’ in the sense that the music is really high culture . The view that it’s ‘peasant music’ is merely an external cultural imposition. Yes it can be played by anybody , but so can any music…..
I acknowledge that my chanter is just a stick with holes in it and a bit of grass tied together to make it sound. But the way those holes were made was the result of years of study, practice and experience, of superior technique and is a result of cumulative learning over centuries.
When we pay high prices for the results of these skills , we are paying for the decades of work , frustration, breakages late nights, hungry kids, tooling and materials and not just for a stick with holes in it.
Having traveled in Africa and makeing my own instruments I freely acknowledge that it’s the person playing, not the instrument,yet the pleasure of playing a functional, in tune, instrument that sounds awesome is worth as much patience and money as we can afford.
It’s all relative .
My top quality whistle cost less than my student cello , my Uke cost less than one Fiddle string , the price is in some ways irrelevant beyond can I afford it or not.
Get the best instrument you can afford but at the end of the day it’s the hand that plays that matters.

A full set of pipes used to cost the equivalent of a house, only the wealthy and the horse dealers could afford the cash to procure them , they are the result of an incredible amount of work and numerous skills, they are not cheap and they are not ‘folk instruments’ they are an expression of high culture and mastery of skill and so is the music. To disdain it as ‘folk music’ is merely Cultural snobbery based IMO upon a lack of understanding, propaganda and ability. The apparent simplicity is illusional And superficial , it’s like examining a work of art from a distance , only when you get really close can you see the intricacies and detail and much will forever remain hidden to those without the skills and understanding to see .
