↳ Posted by Arthur Gondolor on December 13th, 2015.

Re: Timing tips?

If you can’t play with a metronome, tapping your foot will not help the issue. As you become more proficient at keeping time, foot tapping will come naturally when you play. If you are having to practice doing it, you are not ready to do it.

More advanced performers may decide to tap their feet in certain ways to mimic a sort of dance step or to create a rhythmic addition to their playing. You see a great deal of that with Canadian fiddlers, and also with American Old Time fiddlers, both in regions where the music is still commonly played for dancing. Once you are able to keep time without the metronome, and have developed the ability to tap your foot naturally when you are playing, you may decide to develop a certain style. But for now, stick with the metronome.

Re: Timing tips?

The reason for tapping every 4 notes is because this is dance music. It is designed (for lack of a better term) for a certain walking or dancing pace. Tapping at that pace helps a lot of players stay on track. And it’s not just about timing, it’s also about getting the groove and rhythm right. The phrasing and flow of a reel all revolves around 2 beats per measure.

Re: Timing tips?

I have a variety of ways which I tap my foot. While I aim to keep it steady I don’t always tap exactly the same way everytime. Doesn’t mean I do it randomly , or that it’s not intended to give me a steady beat. The goal is to internalise the beat, not be distracted from keeping it steady & to tap (or use any other body movement)
only when it reinforces the first two ~ internalising¹ a steady² beat.

The different ways I tap my foot &/or feet is a subject in itself. Suffice it to say watching how musicians tap their foot/feet may not get you started on the right foot (in a manner of speaking). Before attempting anything too complicated work on establishing a steady foot tap while playing a reel with as few *surprises* as possible.
After you can do that put on a recording of one of the tunes with a dotted crotchet & tap your foot until you’re able to stay on the beat. Then play the tune, tap your foot & try not to think about it. If you flub your foot, just stop tapping but keep playing. Don’t tap again until you feel comfortable.

What’s probably happening (when you get thrown off) is your foot is wanting to follow what is happening in the rhythm & not sticking to the beat. Down the road you can do that. For now, if you want to internalise the beat, a steady foot tap will help reinforce that. Eventually it should feel natural. As with learning anything give it the time you need & try not to be too frustrated during the process. Just be aware when you do make progress.

Re: Timing tips?

Nate, I’ve said this before and I’ll say it again. People play reels properly because they know how a reel should sound, not because of how it is notated. That is only important to the novice who knows the difference between 2/2 and 4/4 and finds things don’t sound right if they play in 4/4. That is a simple fact. One time signature can never sound like the other, which is why both time signatures exist. Therefore, only one can be right for reels. If you know how to count time, there is no way you can play a reel in 4/4. Or, to put it another way, if you had a conductor sit in on a session and conduct what he or she was hearing during a reel, the conductor would conduct the time as 2/2 or cut time. There is no option for doing 4/4, even if you can tap your foot four times per measure. But all of this requires that you know how to count musical time.

If you have worked as a copyist, you must know musicians who know nothing of Irish music. Get one to play a reel with no time signature and as him or her to play the piece in each time signature. It might not sound very Irish stylistically, but there will be no mistaking which time signature used is correct. Since the notes used and the placement of bars does not change from one time signature to the other (probably one reason for the confusion on which to use), there will be no tip-off as to how the piece should be played time-wise. You both can just listen to the results of your test and you will have your answer.

Re: Timing tips?

I consistently fail to see the point of these arguments about 2/2 vs 4/4. What is the point? It’s an aural tradition, if you are relying on sheet music to learns tunes you have bigger problems than which notation expresses the inherent rythmn better.

Sheet music (or abc notation) is a fine tool for recalling tunes that you haven’t played in a few years but the ideal method would be to listen to themto them to ingrain them in your head and play them a few times to get your muscle memory on track. It’s not a good way to learn tunes wholesale, especially if you are a novice player. There is a great deal of subtlety involved in Irish music and I at the the least have rarely found a collection of tunes where everything the player is doing is written out. They certainly exist, but that sort of thing is more suited to analyzing where a player puts certain ornaments or variations to create a specific sound, not to doggedly copy precisely what they do.

Back to the task at hand. Arthur, why do you so frequently want to work on shortcuts to try to shore up your playing? Any accomplished musician will tell you that the most important parts are the basics. A more productive use of your time would be to practice playing in time with a metronome (2/2 vs 4/4 aside) until you can play in time with it, and then practicing the same until you can play in time with it off. Once you have that, foot tapping will be a breeze and won’t be something to worry about practicing.

Hi Will. Apologies for my tardy response to your post of 7:18am, but Christmas shopping reared its ugly head.
Anyway the question you posed got me thinking hard and I suppose I would have to say that the way I back tunes and, in this particular case, reels is driven by the way the dancing (ceilidh rather than Scottish Country, although they are pretty closely related) is done over here, which is very much two steps per bar, hence the heavy use of marches (even what are called 4/4 marches tend to be played with a duple time feel). The proper notation is 2/2. There is nothing to debate. That is not my opinion. Re: Timing tips?

I think the reality is that a reel is both 2/2 and 4/4. It is a rhythm that doesn’t fit the strict rules of classical music that notation describes.

If you vocalize a reel - I’ll use the words ‘alligator propagator’ but you can use whatever words you feel comfortable with.

If you play the reel completely straight the rhythm is ALL-i-gat-or PRO-pa-gat-or - 2/2
If you play it heavily swung it becomes ALL-i-GAT-or PRO-pa-GAT-or - 4/4

But if you play it normally the effect is like saying those words as you would in normal speech - there is a strong emphasis on ALL and PRO, but also a smaller emphasis on GAT and GAT - it is a mixture of both 2/2 and 4/4.

Re: Timing tips?

4/4 or 2/2 doesn’t make any difference in how a tune is notated. A reel is still written in eight notes either way.
You have your ABC written in 2 groups of 4 characters, so there are 8 “time slots” to account for. If the quarter note gets the beat and there are 4 beats, then writing 8th notes puts 8 “time slots” in a bar. If the half note gets the beat, and there are only two beats, then we can still put 4 eight notes in each half note.

Where all the foot tapping comes in is that if you were strictly tapping your foot on each beat, then tapping your foot twice in each measure would mean there were two beats, and so 2/2

thing is, where people tap feet doesn’t have anything to do with notation, so if something can be just as easily written in 4/4, the common practice is to write it in 4/4 and leave it to the players play it as they like.

Re: Timing tips?

>“4/4 or 2/2 doesn’t make any difference in how a tune is notated.”

That’s true, eight time slots in a bar is eight time slots. But the time signature does give the player some more information on how the tune should be played - Jigs and waltzes both have six time slots but very different rhythms, and it is the time signature that tells you which is which.

With dance music, regardless of the time signature and the natural rhythm of the tune there tends to be an undercurrent of 2/2, because you need to emphasize the beats where the foot-falls happen.

As to foot tapping, watch an audience. You’ll see people tapping once, twice, four times, on the down beat or back beat, and even with two feet doing any combination of the above, all in the same audience listening to the same tune. I don’t think it really matters how you tap, as long as you keep it steady.
