Re: The dark side of Arthur’s Day

OK. It’s a sweeping statement not based on fact, in other words. First of all, there is no legal definition which correlates to a “class A narcotic”, “narcotic” being too vague a term to be of much use. Drugs, at least here in the UK, are regulated under the Misuse of Drugs Act 1971, which gives three classifications: A, B and C. The criteria which the UK regulators seek to apply when classifying drugs are:

* The aid in policy and management of public resources
* To “send a signal” to young people about the seriousness and harmfulness of various drugs
* To deter producers from producing and supplying them

However, this paper, produced in 2008 - http://www.ukdpc.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/Briefing%20-%20Submission%20to%20the%20ACMD%20ecstasy%20classification%20review.pdf - suggests that the classification system doesn’t work too well in achieving those objectives, so maybe we shouldn’t be classifying drugs at all.

The only paper that I can recall which suggested that alcohol should be classified alongside such drugs as heroin, and way above ecstasy ( :-O) ), was the paper published in 2007 by a team led by Professor David Nutt. This was the paper which, according to Wikipedia (I know, I know) got him sacked from the Government’s Advisory Council on the Misuse of Drugs. (Oh, I tell a lie, Nutt did another paper later which essentially said the same thing about alcohol, but appeared to me to base its conclusion on relative harm to the public on the fact that many more of us drink than take heroin. To which I am inclined to respond with, “Duh!”)

Alcohol has been around longer than humans (it occurs naturally, and you should see the drunk bees in my garden when they find some :-) ). We’ve evolved with it there. Who knows? We may even need it.

Cheers!
