Re: Tutor book/cd for Scottish fiddle?

There are three main books you might find useful. None are exactly tutors or follow a pedagogical progression, but they are all a font of knowledge.

The Caledonian Companion - Alastair Hardie (there is an associated CD)
The Fiddle Music of Scotland - James Hunter (more music than tutor)
Traditional Scottish Fiddling - Christine Martin (the blue book)

The Caledonian Companion is probably the best of the three but it is quite strongly ‘one true way’ - which is, in these ever changing times, no bad thing. The Martin book delves a lot more into regional style.

Budget a little to take them to be spiral bound.

Oddly enough, Alastair Hardie is also a specialist in Baroque violin.

Re: Tutor book/cd for Scottish fiddle?

There is also “Aly Bain - 50 fiddle solos” - book with music for 50 tunes, plus those tunes on a CD. Not really a tuition book as such, but you can always slow down the tunes if you want.

http://www.amazon.co.uk/s/ref=nb_sb_noss?url=search-alias%3Daps&field-keywords=aly+bain+50+fiddle+solos

I’d also recommend Chris Haigh’s book “Exploring Folk Fiddle.” The book is written by cross-genre player Chris Haigh, and although it is not Scots fiddle focused, it gives a great insight in the wider areas of fiddling (including a section on variation and improvisation). It focusses more on fiddle playing at a root level, rather than any specific genre, although I’m sure you’ll recognize a load of the tunes. It comes with a CD with 101 tunes altogether. Think of buying an Irish fiddle book, then a Scots fiddle book, then Cape Breton fiddle book, etc etc - instead of doing that, then this might save you a bit of time and money too :)

http://www.amazon.co.uk/s/ref=nb_sb_noss?url=search-alias%3Daps&field-keywords=chris+haigh+exploring+folk+fiddle&rh=i%3Aaps%2Ck%3Achris+haigh+exploring+folk+fiddle

Last, but not least, is The Skye Collection, with each tune having the melody line and the piano line too, if you happen to know any pianists ;) ;) ;) A superb book.

http://www.amazon.co.uk/Skye-Collection-Best-Reels-Strathspeys/dp/0786665106

Re: Tutor book/cd for Scottish fiddle?

I think the argy-bargy comes from people having different approaches, and the right answer for you will depend on what you actually want to achieve. If you normally play another genre but want to play a few Scottish tunes and have them sound Scottish, then the generalist Chris Haigh type approach is probably right for you. But if your aim is to develop a personal style that is inherently Scottish, then you will need more detailed, authentic sources, some of which have been mentioned above.

Be aware that Scottish (and Cape Breton) fiddling is much more technically demanding than Irish or American. There was, in past, a very strong classical influence on Scottish fiddle music. Many of the ‘old masters’ were classically trained violinists, and their music contains a lot of high position work, off-string bowing, weird keys and other techniques that are generally avoided in Irish and American playing. Classical violin is probably a better starting place than Irish fiddling if you want to become Scottish.

And yes, attending any workshops you can lay your hands on would be an excellent way to start. (Together with listening to as much Scottish fiddle music as you can find, of course.)

“Be aware that Scottish (and Cape Breton) fiddling is much more technically demanding than Irish or American. There was, in past, a very strong classical influence on Scottish fiddle music. You are speaking of “Scottish fiddling” in monolithic terms, always a dangerous this to do. There are several different styles of Scottish fiddling, just as there are different styles of piping, and some of these styles have little or nothing to do with a strong classical influence. Many of Scotland’s finest traditional fiddlers do not at all approach the the music from a classical viewpoint. Think Alasdair White (Battlefield Band), John Martin, Derek Hoy, et. al.

Do not confuse Skinner et al. with West Highland traditional fiddlers.

Re: Tutor book/cd for Scottish fiddle?

@ Matt Buckley:
“Do not confuse Skinner et al. with West Highland traditional fiddlers. ” or, from another perspective, don’t confuse modern hybrid Scottish/Irish fiddlers with thoroughbred Scottish fiddling.

It’s true that there is a lot of the repertoire that can be played with minimal technique, but there is also a whole swathe that cannot. And when people talk about Scottish fiddling as distinct from Irish I tend to think they are talking specifically about the distinctive sounds and rhythms that we call ‘East Coast’ rather than the ‘West Coast’ sound which blurs into Irish.

Yes, there are as many different styles of Scottish fiddling as there are Scottish fiddlers. To be honest I’ve always found the East/West highland/borders classification to be nonsensical, a better way of classifying fiddlers to me wound be ceilidh band/trad. But even with this diversity of styles there are certain common traits in Scottish fiddling - watch Duncan Chisholm or Aly Bain (the most ‘highland’ of fiddlers), and see how much time they spend doing vibrato half way up the neck, bouncing their bows and generally doing stuff that would have Martin Hayes or Tommy Peoples calling them ‘classical snobs’.

Re: Tutor book/cd for Scottish fiddle?

The “Traditional Scottish Fiddling” by Christine Martin arrived today.

The tunes and ornaments on the CD are all done on solo fiddle, so the sound is nice and clear.

The typeset is very clear and easy to read, and there is a lot of detail on the tunes, both in the markup of the music and in the text that goes with them.

There are 25 detailed explanations of left hand techniques and numerous type of bowing too.

One minor niggle for me - there a section on double-stopping which covers both literal double-stopping, and simply playing two notes together (one note stopped and the other on an open string), but does not differentiate between the two types. Misleading for some, who may not know that there’s actually a difference.

All in all, a very good book, imo.

Re: Tutor book/cd for Scottish fiddle?

‘Most people do not realise how far Scottish folk-fiddle music was influenced by classical music: it is usually thought of as an indigenous growth, untouched by civilisation, transmitted by illiterate farm-workers and vagrant players. But in fact folk-fiddle playing, as it exists in Scotland today, was almost entirely an eighteenth-century creation; and it was developed by educated musicians, most of whom were at home in the classical music culture.’ - David Johnson, page 111, ‘Music & Society in Lowland Scotland in the Eighteenth Century’, 2nd Ed, Edinburgh 2003

This cheers me up - I feel glad that I’ve spent three and a half years on baroque music now, and glad that it wasn’t a waste of time! :)
