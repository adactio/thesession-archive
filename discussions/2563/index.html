Re: Fiddle Tunes

Er…well, if you can’t tell, Mike, opinions vary widely as to what would impress. :) I’m most emphatically NOT an expert, of course, but my opinion is that what tunes you choose in order to impress will depend on who it is that you wish to impress, because they’ll be impressed by widely differing things.

For instance, if it’s the punters and your audience you want to impress, then you’ll choose stuff that they like (like the much maligned Orange Blossom Express, which here in the States is the province of the Old Time and Texas fiddlers, not Irish). If it’s other musos you want to impress, then you’ll choose stuff to please them. If it’s adjudicators you want to impress, then find out what they want to hear and give them that, up to and including style, ornaments, variations, and repertoire. (I wouldn’t choose Mason’s Apron for a competition, though, as it’s been done to absolute death and you’d better have an absolutely drop dead killer setting and variations thereupon to manage well with it.) At least, that would be true for fleadhs around here, anyway, from what I hear.

There is, of course, absolutely no hope of impressing all three groups at the same time. :) So good luck, and let us know what you choose to do for your competition.

Re: Fiddle Tunes

A word of advice, Mike - do not try to impress the musicians who go to Sandy Bell’s, - they won’t be, and anyway, nobody goes there to impress anyone else. Just join in and play the music you know, and listen to and enjoy the music you don’t know. I’ve always found them ready and willing to share both advice and tunes, but if you blast away with the “Orange Blossom Special” or some such other showpiece, you’ll do yourself no good at all.
First impressions are often lasting, and credibility , once lost, is very hard to recover. Incidentally, I know of no competition in Scotland where you wouldn’t be instantly disqualified for playing something like “Orange Blossom Special”. If you’re thinking of TMSA competitions, or Fiddle & Accordeon Club competitions, stick with some of Scott Skinner’s greatest hits. One of the few tunes he wrote which I really like, is “Robert The Bruce”. It has entered the Irish tradition, having been recorded by Liz Carroll’s trio “Trian”, and I first ever heard it played by Derry fiddler Dermot McLaughlin years ago at an Edinburgh Folk Festival, by coincidence. An interesting piece. I’ll suggest one other tune which goes “up the neck” for you - Johnny Doherty, the Donegal fiddler had a great hornpipe called “The Japanese Hornpipe”. I don’t have music for it, but will request it. Good luck if you go to Bell’s , but “ ca’ canny”.

Re: Fiddle Tunes

Oh Lord. Mike, better talk to that friend of yours that won the comp last year before you choose a tune and/or settings.

Variations and settings are, from what I hear, THE make-it-or-break-it point at the fleadhs, since most people have a certain technical competency or they wouldn’t be competing anyway. The best Irish musos are all as a group famous for the inventiveness and individuality of their variations and settings of tunes…it’s what actually makes this music hard, that level of playing. (Granted, sometimes the variation is only two or three notes different, but the greats do it in such a way that the tune shows in an entirely different light than it did before.)

Settings of a tune, btw, are different from variations of a tune. The way *I* think of it (and someone else’s mileage may vary) is that a setting changes the chord structure inherent in a tune, and so it’s hard for two musos to play a tune if they have different settings. A variation does not change the chord structure and will work with anyone else’s way of playing the tune so long as it’s basically the same setting of the tune.
