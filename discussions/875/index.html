Added by Eliot on September 10th, 2002

What is the worst session you ever went to?

For me it was the session with 3 flutes, a fiddle player and…

3 bodhrans (none of whom understood the rhythm of the songs, and all of whom wanted to play loudly, together, giving somewhat the consistency of a 60’s drum circle)

and…

2 didgeridoos

and they were tuned to something between E-flat and E, respectively.

And I went out of my way, while travelling, to plan my visit to this session… oh well, maybe it was just a bad night.

--Eliot

Re: What is the worst session you ever went to?

I’ve been to a lot of very dodgy sessions, but very few where there has been absolutely NOTHING positive to say about them. I once persuaded a load of people to meet me at a session which I hadn’t attended for a while and which it turned out had shut for the Summer, which went down very well! I’ve been to sessions which were supposed to be Irish but were full of massed ranks of Melodian players churning out dismally cheery morris dance tunes. I’ve been to sessions where at least half a dozen guitarists insist on accompanying each tune, all playing ‘full’ chords, but not all in the same order, or in some cases, key. I’ve been to a session where the flow of the tunes never really got going because a fiddler would use any pause to launch into a long and very scratchy slow air, and on the occasions he didn’t, a hammer dulcimer player would entertain us with Sonny’s Mazurka or such like, tapped out at an excruciatingly slow pace. I once went some considerable distance to what I had been told was a good music session, and didn’t realise until I introduced myself (sounds very formal!), and sat down, that it was actually a rather weird singaround, composed almost entirely of people who couldn’t sing, but had folders with reams of songs typed out which they would groan (men) or screech (women) through all 25 verses of. When it got round to me they insisted I played a tune, which I did, made my excuses and left.
But I think the worst sessions I’ve ever been to have to be St Patricks Night sessions. You know the score. You’re at your normal session and the landlord says, “Why don’t you lads come down next Tuesday and give us a few tunes for St. Patrick’s Night?” and a warning bell rings somewhere in your mind, but you think “What the hell!” - you don’t want to let him down. On the night, the pub is packed with drunken students out for ‘the craic’ who scream at the top of their voices at each other, and howl with demented laughter at each other’s jokes. You can’t hear the person sitting next to you. Some drunken buffoon elbows your flute into your teeth. The fiddler at the far end of the table starts a set which filters down the musicians like a musical Chinese Whispers, and by the second tune one end of the table is playing a completely different tune to the other, but it doesn’t matter, because no one can hear anyway. I could go on …

Re: What is the worst session you ever went to?

I wasn’t going to say anything because I feel we have been focusing on the negative too much. Then again - there are session I would love to forget ever happened.

I hadn’t been to a particular session for over 6 months. I showed up and nothing was happening. I decided to start things off with a few jigs and a couple of people started pulling out instruments. Good enough. The guitarist couldn’t find the right chords to Stray Away Child. He wanted me to provide all of the chord changes. I don’t know how many of you have this same problem but talking and playing just don’t mix. I can’t do it.

I stopped the tune and was already anticipating an early evening. The second guy pulled out an American Indian Flute and started playing a Jig on it. I touched a couple of the notes on my fiddle and decided not to play everything in a different key and 1/3 step off.

The guitarist informed me that everybody had to tune to the flute. The guitarist had already done so. I had nothing left to loose so I retuned and played another number with them. It was wholly unsatisfying. The Flute player pulled out a second flute and blew a note. The guitar player started retuning. He told me - ‘Now we all have to retune to this flute.’

I am sure that I had about 30 explicatives lined up in a row. I packed the fiddle up and spoke with a friend for the rest of the evening. I never sessioned there again. How could our rocking session have gone that far down the tubes in 6 months. Oh the Pain.

↳ Posted by Mark Cordova on September 11th, 2002.

Re: What is the worst session you ever went to?

There was once a young couple who were briefly residents in the Dallas area. The young ladies name was Jody and she was a very talented but raw fiddler. ‘Rough-But-Right’ is the American Old-Time fiddler expression. She certainly had the gift! Anyway, I was having some tunes with her and her boyfriend at a local pub (deserted but for a few souls).

As we were playing, a gentleman at the bar bought us a round of beer and I thought to myself ‘YES! This is the reason I’ve picked up the fiddle!’. Anyway, several tunes are played and the gentleman who bought the round approaches and, in a thick Irish accent, declares…

My name is Sean Patrick Burke and I’ve lived in Dublin for 30 years. and I’ve been listening to all of you at the bar while you’re playing….Anyway, so I’ve been listening to you play and there’s something I wanted to say to all of you…

(at this point, we’ve all got rosy checks and glossy eyes. he did buy us a round after all)

So, I’ve been listening to you play some tunes and what I wanted to say is, ‘not any one of you has a f****ing clue of how this music is played!’

(witness now, our jaws dropping and color going out of our cheeks.)

‘Not one of ya!!’ He exclaims again, ‘Except YOU! (points to Jody). You’ve got a clue! But none of the rest of ya!’

Now in all fairness, ‘Sean’ was about 9 sheets to the wind at this point. Despite his exclaimation, he was in fact perfectly friendly to all of us the rest of the night. And to be even more perfectly honest, Sean was absolutely right! None of us but Jody had a clue! Anyway, I knew that long before he said it.

So there you have it. The worst session I’ve been at, and the worst session I’ve been responsible for! And truthfully, when Sean said ‘we didn’t have a clue’, I swear I’ve never had a bigger laugh in my life! I was nearly rolling on the floor!

I’ll never forget his admonisment and I’ll always try to take Seans advice to heart. ‘ Ya gotta put some swing into it!’

Fantastic!
