Re: How do you remember all your tunes?

If you have learned a tune in the past it should come back to you fairly readily if you hear it in a sesssion. I do a couple of things to keep track of tunes I’ve learned.
1) Write the name of the tune down and reference to notes that are close (i.e. Bulmer and Sharpley, Foinn, etc)
2) Transcribe the tune. You will find it easier to memorize tunes that you’ve take the time to transcribe. Doesn’t have to have all the ornamentation (unless that’s the point of the exercise), the basic tune will do, then keep them in a folder.
For memorization
3) Flash cards - tune name on front, first two measures of the A part on the back

Smart people develop entire paths of study around memory. It’s not that simple. Few of us can remember more than a few tunes at any one time, able to lay them down with panache. Some we can bring forward with a little mental “digging”. Past that, we can recognize many more and then recall some of them well enough to play them well. Re: How do you remember all your tunes?

Human memory is a strange thing… When we’re building a memory, we’re actually building a neural pathway in our brain that strengthens connections between neurons. And to cement that memory in place, it’s actually the act of recalling the memory which strengthens the connections. So when you learn a tune, having to recall the tune from memory is what builds the memory, and the best time to do it is *right before you forget it*! Which kind of sounds like a joke, but it’s not. I think this is one of the contributing factors to why some people seem to recall tunes more easily when they were learned by ear rather than by dots. If you’re using the dots, you’re not exercising your recall of the melody. So learn a tune, and then set it aside. Maybe just for 5 minutes the first time while you play something else, and then come back to it. Then play it a bit, and set it aside again for a few hours. Then see if you can recall it. Eventually, the pathways will be strong enough that you can let it go for years, and still recall it when you want.

What I used to do was create some sort of subconscious mnemonic device which also attached the name of the tune to the melody in my head, but that has mostly gone by the wayside with all the tunes that I have learned without knowing the name… But the contour of the melody for each tune is pretty well stuck in my brain. So while I may not be able to start every tune just from the name, I can pick up on it a few notes in if someone else is playing it. And sometimes tunes that I don’t know the name of will just occur to me as well.

There’s also a really interesting blog called the Immutable Laws of Brainjo, written by a neurologist who also plays music (clawhammer old time banjo). His blog posts are all worth the read, though, if you can ignore that he’s trying to sell you clawhammer lessons ;-)

Here’s his post on this very topic: https://clawhammerbanjo.net/the-immutable-laws-of-brainjo-the-art-and-science-of-effective-practice-episode-15/

Re: How do you remember all your tunes?

Like a few people here, I can actually remember most of them. Of course, I may be very rusty on some of those I haven’t played for ages but they usually come back to me fairly quickly.

The real issue is remembering that I actually ever knew the tunes at all. So, sometimes, my mind might well go blank and I just can’t think of a set to play. Admittedly, this doesn’t happen too often these days, as I usually seem to manage to retain a few “emergency sets” in the back of my head.
However, other times I can play for hours at a time and not repeat a tune and all manner of “historical” sets can emerge.
There’s also an issue with tune titles, on occasion. I will remember names but will not always be able to tie them up with the correct tune and vice versa. Sometimes I’ll hear a familiar tune and struggle a little with it until the title comes back to me. Then, for some strange reason, I can play it much better.

So, with me (and probably many other players), it’s not a case that we don’t remember the tunes themselves but that we don’t always remember having known them in the first place.

Re: How do you remember all your tunes?

I don’t remember all of the tunes (though I remember most in my head). That’s why I transcribed them to have them accessible for future reference: http://www.norbeck.nu/abc/
There are about 2500 Irish tunes in that list, which means I have learnt (on average) more than 80 tunes per year since I started. However, this tune learning pace has been up and down. When my kids were small I only added about 200 tunes during 11 years. Now I’m back to about 100 new tunes per year.
I don’t see any big difference between remembering tunes I learnt 25 years ago and those I learnt 1 year ago. Some of them are current in my head, some are not.
I do remember very many tunes, but I often need someone to remind me of them. So at the Stockholm sessions when someone starts a tune I know it about 99% of the time. If it’s a tune I haven’t played for a long time the first times around might be a bit shaky (or VERY shaky).
So HOW do I remember?
1. I transcribe all tunes I learn, which means I go through them thoroughly and the transcribing job probably adds more neural connections.
2. I try to find out information about the tunes. The more connections I have, the better I remember. Not only e.g. who recorded them, but also which of my friends play them, where I first heard them etc, if someone played a particularly good version etc. And if there’s anything interesting about the title (I rarely forget tunes with funny titles e.g. “The Cat That Kittled in Jamie’s Wig”).
3. The more tunes you learn, the more you remember certain patterns and can connect them across tunes, e.g. “the first bars of the second part of the tune are similar to the second part of The Humours of Ballysomewhere”.
4. Trying to connect tunes into sets. If I remember one, I will remember the others.
5. Now and then going through tunes by theme, e.g. some day I might try to remember as many E minor jigs as possible.
6. I go around humming tunes all the time. On the bus, at work, when cleaning at home, etc.
7. (Most important) Throw out your television and play tunes instead!

Re: How do you remember all your tunes?

I’m a ‘list guy’. I like doing it and I like knowing I can use it as a reference. I also know that I need it because my memory is not as good as it once was. It’s a way for me to go back periodically and go over tunes that are fading. I try to go through the entire list every winter. The list in in Excel and it about 1400 tunes at this point. A lot of them are ‘tunes to learn’ still. Each tune includes, if available: name, key (common), dance (style), composer, ABC helper (first two bars of each section) Information (source or a short bit of anything), ratings (How well I played it last, How well I liked it). Along with this list I’ve collected recorded samples of each tune that I keep in one folder which I can upload into Itunes in one playlist. When I’m working on tunes from the list nowadays, I load them into “Transcribe!” so I can put whatever sample I have into the common key and adjust the pitch. It can also play the sample at any speed. I also have Anytune on my IPad which is the same basic thing with a better interface and more features. I like “Transcribe!” only because I work on a PC and Anytune never came out with a Windows version - It got to be a pain loading files onto my old IPad as my list got longer.

I play with a couple of guys (one just passed away recently) that can remember thousands of tunes. They remember a lot of titles also. I’ve gotten to know the one guy very very well. We’ve played a lot as a duet. He has an incredible memory. He can learn simple tunes in a couple passes. I can too sometimes but the difference is he’ll still remember them weeks later without practice. I think his memory is attached to playing the recording in his memory. Often times he can play tunes he never played after having it in his car radio rotation for a bit. I have a difficult with this part of my memory. I also have come to believe that if he hasn’t spent the time with the tune on his fiddle it can sound really muddled. He’ll play it at speed (from his memory) without regard to the sloppiness of his own playing. It’s like an old stretched cassette tape - it’s really interesting. He’s great for the session as a resource. If I’m having a hard time getting a tune going, he can usually help.

I used to believe my memory was really bad hanging out with these guys but now I realize I am probably a little above average. My biggest issue are the tunes that I don’t play often enough. I don’t like to play many tunes at the session that other people don’t know so these don’t get played much and start to fade. I agree that knowing more tunes helps with the overall memory but now that I know quite a lot I struggle with mixing up similar tunes - I’ll go to the wrong B part for example. I just keep working on it and try to get better. It’s a lot of fun.

I definitely would be interested in Fergal’s response - I wonder if he’d give a person a straight answer.

Re: How do you remember all your tunes?

I do a lot of solo busking and I have a personal policy of never repeating a tune (iterations within a rendition notwithstanding, of course) over the course of a day - and not repeating exactly the same repertoire on successive busking days. Like Kenny, I live in a relatively Irish-music-impoverished area, so I don’t have many people to play the tunes with on a regular basis and, in addition to providing me with a handy bit of cash, the busking helps keep my repertoire alive. (As a single, self-unemployed person, my time is largely my own and this is just one of the ways I pay the rent. The debate as to whether I am a professional street entertainer or merely a beggar amusing himself with an instrument would be best dealt with in a separate thread.)

Simply putting myself in a situation where I have to remember tunes out of necessity certainly helps, but I still often find myself temporarily stumped for a tune. Whilst I don’t have any particular ‘methodology’ to recalling tunes, I do find that most (if not all) tunes I play have some sort of association for me - maybe a title but also perhaps the band or musician I first heard play it, the place or situation I first heard it in, a particular session where I have played it, a person that was present at a session where it was played, a busking spot I have played it in*… For tunes that are named after a place I have visited, visualising (or recalling the ‘humours’ of) the place can bring the tune to mind - sometimes even visualising the place on a map, if I know where it is. So ‘scrolling’ through memories of places, people, albums etc. can sometimes yield results. As to *how* I remember them, I donlt know - I leave that to the neuroscientists.

*This can be quite annoying since, when busking, I will only be able to recall those tune that I have played in that exact location before, meaning that nearby traders get to hear the same old repertoire again and again ;-).

Re: How do you remember all your tunes?

Another method, I use, that I’ve not seen mentioned is a “hook phrase.” That’s what I call the most distinctive phrase in the tune.

I know that my fingers will (mostly) remember a tune so I don’t worry - as long as I can play a wee part of the tune.

This might be the first few notes, the end or even in the middle.

For example I couldn’t ever remember Kate Martin’s Waltz easily - I always went in to Margrets Waltz for some reason. The wee distinctive jumps in the phrase before the end phrase of the second part is my hook now. When ever I remember a tune name I (unconsciously) go over the hook phrase in my head many times.

Re: How do you remember all your tunes?

I’m a bit of a novice working on learning more tunes right now. One of our local session leaders has a couple of hints for learning tunes that I’m currently trying to put in to practice, so hopefully over the next year I’ll be able to dedicate the time necessary to make it work and benefit.

it is essentially writing down all the tunes I know and want to learn in a google spreadsheet, then using that to create flash cards with the name of the tune and the dots or ABC on the back of the card. (I’m using a digital flash card system named Anki). You go through your deck of flash cards and it shows a tune name, you try to remember how it goes and play it. If you remember, you check it as a success. If you don’t, you mark it as a fail. Once you’re done, you go back through the fails and focus on learning those tunes. The flash card system is randomized, but has some levers you can use to adjust how often it shows tunes - it’ll show tunes you fail on more frequently, and ones you’re very successful with less frequently.

You can combine this with either a list of tunes your local players play very often, or you could find a list of commonly played tunes on the internet and work with those, whichever suits you best.

It’s a lot of work to set up, and will require some dutiful practice, but it’s how I’m going to try and tackle going from what I know (30ish tunes) into the hundreds in a year or two.

Edit: And of course, listen to these tunes as much as possible :) but that’s the fun part.
