Re: Behind the times…

I also want to be very clear about something, just want to make sure we’re comparing apples to apples here.

If by “slow” you mean the tune refresh rate when typing ABC, since ABCjs does its rendering synchronously, I deliberately actually wait until you stop typing before redrawing the ABC rather than having to wait between each keystroke for ABCjs to render the tune.

I have a 275 ms debounce timer in my text input routine that won’t redraw the ABC until you stop typing for that long. If you’re typing long sets of comments or text, it’s much nicer than forcing a full tune redraw on every keystroke. Studies recommend that debounce timing for this sort of “split-attention” data input, where you shift your focus between text input and output generation to optimize the rendering work while still having the perception of responsiveness.

Anyone have a sense for what’s going on as far as future enhancements and more importantly, adoption of those enhancements by tool developers like Paul Rosen and abcjs?”

Well now there’s a complicated can of worms. All of what follows is my personal opinion BTW, I have no authority.

The 2011 standard is, for many ordinary users of abc to notate, share, and archive traditional tunes, good enough to get by on.

Guido Gonzato and others created AbcPlus, aka abc 2, which does a lot of what you’re talking about, see

https://abcplus.sourceforge.net/#ABCGuide

But whether we will ever see that, or any other substantial change, universally adopted as the univeral new abc standard to replace the 2011 spec… I doubt it personally. There’s just too much abc out in the wild (including on the session.org itself of course) that more or less conforms to the 2011 standard.

So long as any extensions obey the cardinal rule (that it doesn’t break existing notation by reassigning an existing command to a new definition), and software ingesting abc continues to ignore anything it doesn’t understand and carry on and draw/play/MIDI/etc the tune regardless, a thousand flowers can bloom as forks and implementations. And that, imnsho, is one of abc’s great strengths.

Re: Behind the times…

“But how do I turn my dots (which I understand) into ‘ABC’ (which I don’t but some musical acquaintances do)?!”

You could, as Michael Eskin suggests, pay someone to do it for you. But it’s really not that hard – it is just a very simply encoded form of staff notation. Unlike learning a real language (Scots, Occitan, Uyghur etc.), where concepts do not always correspond one-to-one with those in your native language, every symbol in abc maps precisely on to an analogous symbol in staff notation – and every abc symbol is chosen either for its visiual resemblance to its staff analogue (e.g. colon for a repeat mark) or for some logical association with its analogue (e.g. numerals to denote note length). Admittedly, it starts to get a bit cumbersome if you are dealing with polyphonic music (which, if it is for your own compositions, is likely), but once you know the building blocks, it’s just donkey-work.
