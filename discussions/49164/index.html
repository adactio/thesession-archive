Being quite new to sessions and actually playing in one as a member, I venture to talk anyways.
Have been around and visited some sessions, and they are all very different. Some close and reluctant to include new folks, others open - “chime in if you now the tunes, or bring a new song” etc.

“murders of women…” - well. There’s an old song about a murder, the victim being a man and a rebel, called “Ned Of The Hill”. We play that one regularly, a lady sings and plays the guitar, I play the flute. Lovely song from the beginning of the 18th century.

“…and other unedifying themes” - as always, depends: what, where and when, rebel again: one night I sang an Irish Rebel Song, I love them. One member took me apart. “Bad idea”, she said, “lots of protestants here for holidays.” She’s right, you don’t want to arouse strife after the Friday Agreement. These songs did have their time and reasons, although at some other place I like to go they are pretty much alive.

Last, what Kenny says. No such thing as “unwelcome member(s) at sessions” - the title is contradictory. Do you mean “guest(s)” rather than “member(s)”? Members are welcome, otherwise they wouldn’t be such: the session would spit them out if they were/became unwelcome, or wouldn’t let them in at the first place.

Yes, instrumentalists can cause trouble too - myself for instance. But nobody ever frowned when I began a tune I thought I knew really well, and failed (memory, fingers, speed, but mostly breathing issues).

Relax, enjoy (or not) and think your part - or, if it is too much, discuss there.
Not meaning that such things aren’t worth being discussed her, not at all.
All the best, keep going.

Re: Unwelcome member(s) at sessions

JWiseman, I think there are very many different kinds of session and some of the differences include whether the session has any kind of ‘ownership’ or not. If a group of people have started the session and actively curate it, then they can direct how they want it to be. If others come in to join the session and want it to be different, then it won’t bend to their wishes. It doesn’t have to. But it is quite possible for a session to kind of just ‘grow’ without anyone starting it, or with nobody taking responsibility for it, and then the session will really evolve depending on what the people turning up want. And that is also OK. I wouldn’t call it democratic, more evolutionary, but those sessions are OK too. And it is also OK to not go to them if you don’t like them.

One jaded person’s opinion maybe, but some years ago I took a young friend, visiting from England, to a well-established mixed session in central Edinburgh. He explained to assembled company that he was visiting from England, but that his mother was Australian, and throughout the course of the evening sang two songs, one English, the other Australian. I already knew him to be a very good singer, mature beyond his years. His songs received a good round of applause, deservedly so. But afterwards, he told me that some woman had sidled up to him and told him “It’s Scottish music and songs here”. I wish I knew who that was: she’d have felt the sharp end of my tongue! Fortunately young friend was not too fazed by this, and continues to sing whatever he feels like in fine fettle!
So who was the unwelcome member at that session? He who dared to sing non- Scottish songs in a very international city, or that inconsiderate besom who upbraided him?
