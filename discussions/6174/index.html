“This is a local sesssion for local people……”

Excuse me but I’m just about to have a rant!

A couple of months ago, I submitted details of a local session on this site and was asked to remove same by one of the musicians who had been responsible for getting the session off the ground. “There are too many people coming already” she said “and we want to keep it for local people.” I tried to explain that most people in Edinburgh already had their own sessions and the information here would probably be of more benefit to a casual visitor. It’s nice to know what’s going on when you come to a town. Anyway, she was having none of it. “Let them go up the town to Sandy Bells or wherever!”
So, wishing to keep the peace, I duly obliged and managed to get Jeremy to remove the details from the site.

Now you might think that all this is fair enough but I went along on Tuesday night and the founder members of this session along with most of the main stays had not arrived (not for the first time). Apparently, they had to rehearse for something or other and this was the only night one of their members could “get off”.
When I arrived with a fiddler, there was only one whistle/flautist present along with two bodhran players. We(the three of us) managed to play a few tunes and it was ok but not ideal as we don’t normally all play together. So, the evening was much flatter than normal.

Now, this is not a “paid session” so nobody, not even the founders or regulars, is obliged to attend. However, it seems a bit bizarre to me that they don’t wish to have some commitment to the proceedings. I, for one, now feel less inclined to go along and support this session if they don’t want to do it themselves.

Now, none of this might ever have bothered me if I hadn’t been told “We want to keep this as a session for local people and there’s enough here already!”

The reason why I started the session was to see if a non paid self sustaining local session can survive on it’s own merits. This seems to be the case.

Re: “This is a local sesssion for local people……”

The musicians responsible for “getting the session off the ground” are probably also the people responsible--just by showing up most weeks and having a large enough repertoire of tunes--for keeping it going. A good session is not sustained by democracy or mobocracy--it lives because one or two good players show up and play tunes on a regular basis.

In my book, what the founders want the session to be--and whether or not it is advertised--is up to them, period. If they solicit input from other players, fine, but if they choose to ignore said input, that’s their perogative.

I don’t see anything wrong with their attitude in this case. Just as they are not obligated to show up every week, they’re also free to let the session collapse if no one else is strong enough to pick it up in their absence. In other words, it’s not their problem (or fault) if other people show up expecting cranking tunes and it doesn’t happen that night. It’s not their responsibility to always provide a strong foundation (or a sheltering noise level) for weaker players or visitors to tag along.

No, Kerri, what I was trying to get across when I said I wish we could all agree was to reach a concensus.
