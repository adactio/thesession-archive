Re: Do musicians play better drunk or sober?

If Jack’s question is combined with the main question the answer is that it doesn’t matter.
Some years ago when we were on holiday in the Austrian Tyrol my wife and I went down to the village square for a concert by the village band. At the duly appointed time the band smartly marched into the square, resplendent in their Ruritanian uniforms, and took their places on the platform. After each number young women in Tyrolean costume passed among the musicians with steins of the local brew. Similar young women did the same among the audience in the square. After a few numbers musical coordination and intonation were getting noticeably flaky. At the end of the concert a somewhat disorganised rabble of musicians staggered out of the square. None of this mattered in the least, of course, because the audience had likewise achieved an equivalent state of inebriation :-)
The late great international classical violin virtuoso Henryk Szeryng regularly used to have a couple of glasses of sherry before he went on stage to play a Paganini concerto or whatever.
And didn’t someone once say that if you’re going to drink while you’re performing you should drink when you’re practicing?

↳ Posted by Gzeg on April 29th, 2005.

Re: Do musicians play better drunk or sober?

The long-term side effects of pot are also of interest. The active constituents of cannabis (the cannabinols) stay in the brain-tissue for a long time, for weeks or even months. Nicotine, on the other hand, is swiftly excreted from the body, so there is no build-up in the brain. This is basically why pot has more dangerous mental side effects than nicotine.
The occasional social pot smokers in Britain in the ’60s seem to have mostly got away with it unscathed because the stuff then in circulation was relatively mild. Today, however, cannabis in all its forms is much stronger and there is the very real risk that what is smoked may be contaminated with even more dangerous stuff.
People who work professionally in psychiatric hospitals, as does a member of my family, know that a significant proportion of younger patients with serious psychiatric disorders have taken illegal drugs including cannabis.
Trevor
