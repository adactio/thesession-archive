Modifying a piano accordion for ITM

I thought some of you may be interested in my accordion experiments. I’m new to Irish trad, but come from a musical background, playing the piano and composing.

Since first experiencing an Irish session at the end of last year, I’ve immersed myself in the music, and taken steps to learn an instrument which would one day enable me to make my own contribution.

It’s the sound of the button box that I really like, but decided to go with a piano accordion, as I’d already been playing piano all my life, and I have an affinity and instinct with the piano keyboard layout.

From trying a piano accordion, I realised why a lot of Irish players don’t use the bass buttons, or do so very sparingly – the chords on the common stradella system really aren’t suited to the music. There’s major, minor, augmented, and diminished chords, and they’re all low, loud and thick sounding.

So, I’ve bought a wooden 30 key Brandoni, and had some of the notes removed:

Major chord – removed the 3rd, to leave just 1st and 5th
Minor chord – removed the 5th, to leave just 1st and minor 3rd
Augmented chord – removed the 5th to leave just 1st and major 3rd
Diminished chord – removed the 1st, to leave minor 3rd and 6th

Here’s a chart:
http://www.flickr.com/photos/97295440@N00/4638928523

The buttons can be combined to create some really interesting and subtle harmonies, as well as the standard chords.

Has anyone else thought of trying something like this? Or could you see yourself adopting another system?

Re: Modifying a piano accordion for ITM

Yes, taking out third on a melodeon is quite common, and as you say, you can simply sellotape them shut. I used that technique on a 2 voice melodica I have to get a single reed sound, and more volume.

Its rather more complicated in a piano accordion, as the reeds are shared by different buttons/chords, so blocking a reed would have an adverse affect on other buttons/chord combinations. So it had to be modified in the mechanics between the button and the reeds.

I agree with the single reed in the right hand - this is my preferred sound, though it seems most players (button box and pa) prefer 2 reeds.

I play a box a bit crossover between melodeon and chromatic single action : 3 row-18 basses, with a register to take the thirds out of the basses. Your system is intereting, but then, I have a friend who plays an accordion with what we call “baritone basses”, that is basses without pre-established chords : you do your own chords on the left hand…
But I think the question of basses and backup is just a question of taste. For me, the most important in a box, whatever the box (button. piano chromatic button….) is the bellows. the mastery of the bellows is what will make it sound Irish or caribean… PA being heavier than button boxes, it’s more muscular to give the right bellow action… and the push-pull action of the button box makes one use the bellows more…. but you can do it on PA. Listen to Phil Cunnigham, or Karen Tweed…

I have a castagnari mory button box and it has two buttons that can take the thirds out of the bass chords. However - I love mixing the third-less chords (guitarists call them “power chords”) with each other and making poly-chords - major 7ths minor 9ths etc become so much easier to play - there’s a fair amount of maths involved, but its worth it in the right context.

Re: Modifying a piano accordion for ITM

Farmerboy, my mistake - they were originally dominant 7ths (1st, 3rd and 7th), and not augmented.

Though augmented chords are easy for me now - In the key of C, I can use a C bass, combined with the “1st and major 3rd” button“ in the E row. Or, for a higher sound, I can use 2 ”1st and 3rd" buttons - the one in the C row and one in the E row.

I also love mixing thirdless chords - the 2 next to each other for a suspended 4th, or one apart for a suspended 4th +9!

With my PA, I find that I’m always trying to thin the sound out on the bass side, to get a good balance with the right side (especially as I like playing with the single reed), so a “weedy” sound is more what I’m looking for.

Of course, if I want the original chords back, I play 2 buttons - a thirdless chord with either a “1st and major 3rd”, or a “1st and minor 3rd” in the same row…
