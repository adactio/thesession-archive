A Load of Claptrap?

What do sessioners think of punters who persist in clapping “in time” with the music?

I say “in time” reservedly, as a significant proportion of them always seem to be about a quarter of a beat behind (or ahead of) the music.

A good (or I should say bad) example of this can be found in this Barney MacKenna clip, recently posted by Páistí na nGael:

http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yiOWd7ArmWQ

(The “clapping” starts at the end of the slow air, and continues to the end of the clip).

I won’t say personally what I think of punters who do this, as my comments would be unprintable …. :-(

Re: A Load of Claptrap?

“I just feel that there is a difference between someone who is simply enjoying the music, and who probably doesn’t know a whole lot about it, and has the urge to clap along vs someone who is being obnoxious/belligerent/insinuating.”

There is a difference between the two extremes, of course, but I think there is a whole continuum in the middle - and where people fall on this continuum is usually related to age, how big a group they are with and how much alcohol they have consumed. A quiet, toe-tapping, listener could easily be transformed into a slavering, arhythmic, clapping monster when part of a crowd of beer-swilling youths. Alcohol reduces inhibitions, so a gentle drumming of fingers on the bar or rhythmic nodding of the head can easily turn into a more exuberant expression of appreciation. Alcohol also affects co-ordination, so the clapping is less likely to be in time, the more drink taken. And, of course, when one starts clapping, the rest follow, so that, very quickly, the sound of palm on palm overpowers the music itself. Alcohol also affects short term memory, so they quickly forget why they started clapping in the first place; it stops being a sign of appreciation of the music and becomes an expression of basic instincts.
