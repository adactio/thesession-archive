Re: Shall we start a trend?

“playing faster is NOT about snapping your fingers down faster… it’s all about lifting your fingers up faster”
I learned a long time ago that lifting your fingers quickly is as important as, if not more important than, putting them down quickly. It isn’t only psychological either: there are sound scientific reasons for it, except maybe keyboards (and VAF, obviously).
The lifting of a finger from a vibrating string, if done at a speed faster than the vibration, results in a satisfying ‘pop’ down to an open string, rather than muffled damping effect of lifting it slowly. The same for wind instruments -- a finger lifted slowly from the hole will allow air to seep through the gap and cause all manner of unwanted turbulence before stabilising into the new frequency, whereas a clean and rapid lifting of the finger reinforces the new frequency by initiating the vibration.
It’s all micro-second stuff we are talking about, if not nano-second; but the results are clearly audible and well worth bearing in mind.

If you want to know what I noticed during the clip (and I’m sure you’re aware, and it’s to be expected for the length of time you’ve been playing) :

Watch your coordination when you cross strings - you’ve still got the last note sounding on the A-string when you cross over to the D-string (eg at 00‘20) - in other words your ’cross’ needs to be a bit sharper and quicker so that each note on either string sounds clear and crisp, and independent.

For the future - still keeping the same speed, think about accenting the 1st note of each bar, by a little more 1st finger pressure on the bow. It just needs that little bit of ‘bite’ at the start of the note. Doesn’t always have to be the 1st note, but you can experiment to see what difference the extra pressure makes.

Hi ff, there are quite a few going around.

This one is quite good (it’s very, very basic), but toward the end it does address the little issue you have, where you haven’t used enough ‘tilt’ to get to the next note on the adjacent string, therefore your previous note on the original string is still sounding. Obviously the bowing speed in ‘the tunes’ is a fair bit higher than what you see here, and the elbow / arm / wrist movement is proportionately less, but the principle is the exactly the same.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T9xLyX7Qb2c

After you’ve got your head round that, I (personally) prefer the little exercises where your left hand does as little as possible, keeping things simple, so you can concentrate on clean crossings :

http://worldfiddlemusic.com/guest/string-crossing-01.jpg

Once you’re comfortable with these, there are a few more ‘tips’ that can physically ease (and enhance the sound of) the crossing. One of these is to decrease the bow pressure just before the crossing (at the tail end of the first note), then increase it again at the start of the next note. That preserves the ‘bite’ that I mentioned previously, when often that ‘bite’ happens on notes on the same string.

Your bow hand is not quite in the right place - not at the right angle. It needs to be turned in towards the bow a bit more so that your fingers and wrist can do some of the work for you. Re: Shall we start a trend?

I too like the Prof’s bow hold, and you couldn’t couldn’t really fault it. However, I’m a firm believer in any bow bold which works (for everything). So, what’s needed for ‘everything’?

Obviously the ‘grip’ (I hate that word) needs to be firm enough to be able to lift the bow off the strings completely if you need to, and exert enough pressure via the fingers, wrist and forearm to draw out a clean, clear tone.

The hand needs to change position slightly on a full bow, so usually the 1st finger is straightened gradually as the bow reaches the end of its travel.

The 1st finger needs to be able to control downward pressure.

The 4th finger needs to be on the stick for counter-balance (although not all of the time).

You will need to be able to tilt the bow through approx. 90 degrees (with frog perpendicular to the string, hair flat on the string, 45 degree both fore and aft).

You will need to be able to draw a full bow across the strings to within about 1" of each end.

That’s about it really. I did say a bow hold which works for ‘everything’, and not everyone does ‘everything’, so that’s just a simple guide :)
