Re: Internalizing the pulse ~ slip jig

Well, sure, that’s the pulse for that slip jig. But realize that there is no one single slip jig rhythm, just as there’s no single rhythm that fits all reels or all double jigs.

E.g., the pulse and feel for, say, Whinny Hills of Leitrim and Fig for a Kiss are quite different, though both are slip jigs.

Re: Internalizing the pulse ~ slip jig

Michael, the slip in a slip jig doesn’t come from making silly putty out of their phrasing. It’s a slip of the feet when dancing, a “step.”

All you have to do is listen for the clear call-and-response of any slip jig to hear the phrasing well stuck in 9/8. Most slip jig phrases follow the chord changes. (To get our priorities right, the chord changes follow the bar lines, the phrases.)

Think of Fig for a Kiss--the first bar is in Em, the second in D, third goes back to Em, 4th is the end phrase. Every significant pause, chord change, etc. happens at the bar lines.

Dancing isn’t necessarily a straitjacket--in this case, the type of dance is the reason the type of tunes exist. Sure you can squash and stretch them as a matter of personal interpretation, but if you want them to sound like slip jigs, you’d be wise to pay attention to the dancers.
