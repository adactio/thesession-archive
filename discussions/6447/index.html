Assassination attempt on Bliss fails

I arranged to go to Wreckin’ Rea’s session last Saturday. On arrival I discovered that the pub was down an entry, with three entrances. Two of these were “mysteriously” blocked off, so once you were at the pub, there was no way out.

Being paranoid and having a Doctorate in Godfather Films, I immediately suspected a hit. Nothing actually happened, but I learnt later that the Edinburgh flight to Belfast had been cancelled.

As for the session, all top class musicians, including a bronchial Wreckin’ Rea. Once again the bodhran “problem” raised its ugly head.

Punters kept coming up to me with such comments as “workers can identify with the bodhran” (true) and “without a bodhran you may as well be listening to structured classical music”.

Now I realise why punters identify with a bodhran, and I realise what ITM has to offer.

My question is this. Do purists dislike the bodhran because they feel that it interferes in some way with the music, or are they just pissed off with all the attention it gets?

I hasten to add that the musicians on Saturday did not have a problem, and were blissfully unaware of punters harassing me.

One time back in 1990 I walked in to a pub in Dublin that had a great session going on. One of the players heard my accent, saw my instrument case and asked if I played. I told him I did, but I told him it was concert pitch. (They were all in Eb) He said he’d like to hear me play a tune anyway -- so I reluctantly did. If you think my playing is bad now, it totally sucked in 1990, but to my surprise I got a great reaction from people in the pub and about six pints lined up in front of me. This could be very deceiving though because later I found out that the cheering section was a bunch of disgruntled musicians who were protesting the Eb criteria of the session. It wasn’t my playing they liked -- it was the key I was in.

Hmm I think when someone comes up and says “without a bodhran you might as well be listening to structured classical music”, it’s either not saying very much of the session music or not saying very much of the punter’s level of listening.

Personally I feel that bodhrans and strummy guitars are ultimately pretty redundant when the music is of a good standard. The rhythm and lift will be automatically generated by good melody players more subtly and more more interestingly than any strummy guitarist or bodhranai can do. Usually these instruments only iron out and affect the overall sound of the music making it less than what it is. Woe on him who adds bodhran or strummy guitars to Willie Clancy’s or Dennis Murphy’s music.

I hypothesize that some punters and beginner listeners not being very used to the music, simply appreciate something to make it more palatable to modern ears. Something to mark out beats, something to make less ambigious the harmonic structure stuff like that.
