↳ Posted by Michael C. Gill on October 13th, 2008.

Re: TIM "L placard?

I think it is indeed normal. Listening to the sound you make and hearing it for what it really is, is a skill that takes quite some time to acquire and, for most people, needs the input of a teacher (preferably on a one-to-one basis) to show the pupil what to listen for. Even at the basic stage of learning to tune the instrument by ear.

A second skill is working out what needs to done to overcome the imperfections revealed by the listening process. For most people this is certainly where they need a teacher from the beginning to spot what is going wrong and to show what the solution is, and to isolate bad habits.

Some people (a few) can work out the problem and its solution from watching and listening to good players; but only up to a point. There comes a stage when a sticking point is reached and it’s very difficult to work out what the answer is, even from observing the best players - the essential detail is too easily missed, and you don’t know what it is you’re looking for. This is where the services of a good experienced teacher come in.

↳ Posted by Michael C. Gill on October 13th, 2008.

Re: TIM "L placard?

How? Sit at home and relax and think of a tune you reckon you might be able play on automatic pilot. It might be something as straight forward as happy birthday or something, or the kesh jig would be a better choice, it’s a good tune. Play your choice and play it. over and over. Get to a stage where the technique is no longer in your consciousness. Keep playing. And then just listen. Pay no attention to your fingers or any other muscles you use. Don’t try and tap your foor foot or anything, try to remove all distractions from your consciousness and just keep playing.

Then listen. And listen hard.

If you find this too difficult, Try just one note. Just play a note until you get to a stage where the technique is no longer in your consciousness. Keep playing. And then just listen.

Listen hard. Listen at the sound of it.
