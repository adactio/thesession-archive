Non-Christmas tunes for a Christmas Gig

I have an opportunity to play one hour at a local historic mansion that is all decorated for Christmas for a community event. Though it would be fun and a breakout opportunity for me, I don’t think I should do it. All I ever play is Celtic tunes on the fiddle or guitar or banjo. Of all the Christmas tunes that come to mind, none of them have very interesting stand-alone melodies. I ain’t gonna sing, that’s for sure! The melody must be strong enough to be played on a solo unaccompanied violin. I just might be sitting next to a Christmas tree. I can’t play Carol of the Bells forever. My list of favorite Celtic tunes easily covers an hour, or ten hours. The general public wouldn’t recognize anything.

Re: Non-Christmas tunes for a Christmas Gig

What exactly do they want you to do?

If they are happy for you to sit there and play whatever Cape Breton or Bluegrass or Old Time tunes (yeah, I read your profile), then do the gig. If they are expecting you to play Christmas tunes and you don’t know any or can’t be arsed playing them (because quite frankly, most of them suck), then don’t do the gig.

Tell the gig organisers what you are willing and able to play, then see if that’s what they actually want. Maybe they’re keen for some nice strathspeys and reels and don’t want normal Christmas drek.

ETA: I was once asked if I could pipe in the haggis for a very formal Burns night thing. The guy in charge had heard that I was a piper, but type of pipes was not specified. I asked how important it was that I walk in with the haggis. He was like, ‘well, yes, that is part of the ceremony.’ I said, ‘do you have a wheelchair? An office chair with wheels will do.’ He looked confused. Then they found a Highland piper.

Re: Non-Christmas tunes for a Christmas Gig

Are you opposed to playing Christmas carols? I personally like those melodies a lot, but that’s a nostalgia thing. If you only have to play an hours worth of music, you could simply put a Christmas carol at the beginning of your tune sets. This way, you could play all your favorite jigs and reels to your hearts content, while still maintaining the Christmas vibe AND giving the audience familiar music that they’ll recognize. Not to mention, Irish tunes being so fun and upbeat, they go really well with the jubilant spirit of Christmas carols; And the simpler melodies of the Christmas carols create a lot of lift juxtaposed to the more complex Irish tunes. For some variation, there are carols that you could play as slow airs, like the Scotch song “Auld Lang Syne”, or the English song “Greensleeves”. This is how I do my Christmas gigs. There is a lot of good music you could mix for an hour between the carols and the tunes, even if you’re playing a solo melody instrument.

Re: Non-Christmas tunes for a Christmas Gig

Are you getting paid? If so take the money and play the Christmas carols.

If not, but you fancy doing it, work up four or five Christmas carol or adjacent tunes you can condescend to play, and sprinkle them through the set - and let’s face it you can probably repeat them within the hour, because I’d guess it’s the sort of event where people are passing rather than sitting and listening for the whole hour.

If you’re not being paid, and there’s no other upside to doing it (like doing someone a favour, or helping a friend out, or just using your particular talent to be part of something festive and community-based, or getting an opportunity to play in public) then no, don’t do it.

In The Bleak Midwinter makes a good slow air. In Dulce Jubilo is a good bouncy jig. Christmas Day In Da Morning is another good slow tune. There, I’ve given you 3 out of 5 already.

Re: Non-Christmas tunes for a Christmas Gig

1. It doesn’t have to be all or nothing - your own tunes interspersed with recognisable Xmas songs will work. If you start and end with Chrismas stuff no on will notice what you do inbetween.
2. Remember you’ll be playing for the punters not yourself: in my experience the punters at this type of gig always prefer familiar songs they can hum along to, no matter how cheesy and naff you think they are. They don’t have any interest in diddly music, however brilliantly it is played.
3. Christmas songs aren’t just carols - there have been many good, bouncy pop songs over the years. Here’s a few we’ve used:

Merry Christmas Everyone (Slade)
Fairy Tale of New York (Pogues)
I Wish it Could be Christmas Every Day (Wizzard)
Happy Christams War is Over (Lennon)
I saw Momma Kissing Santa
Jingle Bells Rock
Rudolph the Red Nose, Little Drummer Boy, Dusty the Snowman, White Christmas, I saw Three Ships, etc etc.

Re: Non-Christmas tunes for a Christmas Gig

What would give me pause for thought is not so much the expected repertoire as that you will be playing a sole unaccompanied violin. In my experience an unaccompanied fiddle sounds pretty thin and doesn’t give an audience much to hang on to if they don’t know the style of music. If you can possibly manage it, I would try to persuade a guitarist or keyboard player to accompany you, and church in a few carol tunes now and then.

Re: Non-Christmas tunes for a Christmas Gig

It can be fun to play “Hark the Herald Angels Sing” and “God Rest Ye Merry Gentlemen” (for instance) as jigs, adding/subtracting notes as necessary to make them work in 6/8.

“Taladh Chrìosda” has the advantage of being actually a Christmas song, if not widely recognizable. Might make a nice slow air to kick off a set of strathspeys and reels.

Our local downtown has Natalie MacMaster’s “Get Me Through December” in their holiday playlist, thus establishing “Niel Gow’s Lament for His Second Wife” as officially a Christmas tune.

On the bluegrassier side of things, Dan Crary’s “Holiday Guitar” sticks in my mind as an album that made holiday chestnuts not only bidable, but actually enjoyable…then again, he had the advantage of accompaniment.

We have quite a few mansions (Auto Dynasties) in my neck of the woods - they are decorated to the hilt for tours, Formal Tea & events. I’d imagine a spirited tune here & there, but the venues I’ve seen lend themselves to slower tunes. I am partial to tunes on Maggie Sansone’s Christmas Albums: ‘Cold Frosty Morn at West River’ (https://thesession.org/recordings/5202), ‘Merrily Greet The Time’ (https://thesession.org/recordings/3580), ‘A Scottish Christmas’ (Bonnie Rideout & Maggie Sansone), & ‘Ancient Noels’ (Maggie & Ensemble Galilei).

How much time do you have to learn tunes before the gig?

There’s a ton of great tunes here for the Winter Season (in general) - definitely fit for playing along with Christmas Carols/tunes! I’d prefer tunes that at least fit the season… Winter, Snow, Cold, Christmas, Wassail, Solstice, Frost, Yule, and Pudding!
I’ve loved singing Christmas Carols since I was a kid (I like a huge variety) - my favorites for the past few years on the fiddle have also come from other genres, early music, and quite a few from ‘Colonial’ Christmas Albums.

Sounds like you have a wonderful opportunity & others have offered great suggestions!

Thank you all for the advice!

I’ve been thinking about the gig and trying out tunes and have the following direction:

This is Pure Christmas event with Santa Claus, decorated mansion, figgy pudding, it’s a free community event in a small town. The main living room has a series of one hour concerts in front of a small audience. People don’t stay all evening. They come inside and are greeted by the costumed historical people, stroll through the decorated rooms above, get their photo taken with Santa in the top floor ballroom, listen to a few minutes of Christmas music, then leave. I don’t think anyone will recognize anything I want to play - Banish Misfortune, Factory Smoke, Crossing to Ireland, Drunk at Night Dry In The Morning. I’ve concluded that it would be awkward if not I’m not playing Christmas tunes if I’m featured in the living room.

I’m going to propose that I play my own list of Celtic Favorites from the corner of the ballroom on the top floor where Santa will be. The sound will filter down through the large open basement-to-attic stairwell. I think they use a cheesy CD player playing a Christmas loop in the corner for “ambiance”. I want to replace that! If I don’t play in the living room, they’ll have two hours without music. I propose I fill those two hours from upstairs. I expect that ballroom to have wonderful acoustics with all the wood, curtains, etc. A solo violin should fill it up! It has a little cubby poking out of the side wall that was intended for musicians back in the day. The staircase to get to it is partially hidden and narrow.

Comments on tune suggestions: I like the idea to start and end the gig with a recognized tune and fill the middle with my own preferences. I have two weeks to prepare! I’m up at all hours of the night playing fiddle anyway so the time isn’t a problem. Anything played by Natalie MacMaster goes to the top of the list!
