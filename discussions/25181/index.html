What to do about a bad guitarist?

Went to a new session for the first time. A dozen or so musicians, fiddles, banjos, a flute, an accordion. And a terrible guitarist.

He seemed to have four standby chords, one of which was a minor, and he played these in random order whenever he didn’t know the tune, which was most of the time, with a special emphasis on the inappropriate minor. It was horrible, and seriously damaged most of the music played during the evening.

There were two fiddlers at the far end of the room, with the guitar between them and the rest of us, and I could see that they couldn’t hear what was happening at my end, because of this guitarist. They were grimacing, cupping their hands round their ears, but in the end they just had to give up.

I’d be very interested in practical suggestions on how to deal with something like this.

Speaking from my own experience as a backer, this is absolutely true. The very first session I went to was a very welcoming affair, so much so that the people running it soon had me convinced that I was doing great. Granted, I later learned that these folks weren’t the greatest players in the world, but who cares! They were (and still are) some of the nicest folks one could ever hope to meet at their first session. After a while, I started to notice that there were other musicians in the area who didn’t frequent this session so often, who were clearly much better musicians. I started to hang around their session (not playing so much as listening; the music was so excellent, it was a bit intimidating!) & one evening, the Bouzouki player invited myself & a friend to his home for an evening of music. Later on that night, while playing what I thought were the correct chords to a particular tune, the fiddle player WENT BALLISTIC & started yelling the correct ones to me. Yeah, it was a bit heavy, but you know what? It FORCED me to re-examine my playing, and realize that, despite the sunshine being blown up my backside at the first session, I wasn’t even CLOSE to having “arrived”! You know what else? The next time that fiddle player saw me at a session, he took the time to let me know how dramatically my backing had improved. His yelling at me was quite possibly the best thing that EVER happened to my development as a backer. I’m still learning (that won’t ever stop), but can pretty well hold my own with just about anybody (I’ve even been ASKED to back some pretty famous traditional musicians in a concert situation; I won’t drop their names, but you would definitely know who they are). All because of somebody “getting involved”…pretty cool, if you ask me!
