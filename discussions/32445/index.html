Ireland vs. Scotland

I turn to Gerald of Wales, “Topography of Ireland”, Distinction III, Section XI: “Of the incomparable skill of the Irish in playing upon musical instruments”

“Scotland at the present day, in the opinion of many persons, is not only equal to Ireland, her teacher, in musical skill, but excels her; so that they now look to that country as the fountain, head of this science.”

http://www.yorku.ca/inpar/topography_ireland.pdf, p.71 of this translation

That was written (and subsequently revised) in 1188. So what of our present day, some 800 years later? Does Gerald’s statement still ring true? Does Scotland excel Ireland at music?

Re: Ireland vs. Scotland

‘I always remember our pipe major who once said to me something like: “ Ah those d**ned Irishmen. They can’t even play their graces together,” thus illustrating that he didn’t understand a thing about ITM despite his knowledge of Scottish music.’

Scottish traditional music has always seemed to me to be less unified than Irish traditional music - in the present day, at least: pipers, fiddlers and accordionists have, to a greater or lesser degree, their own instrument-specific repertoires. There is overlap between them, of course, but the (Great Highland) pipes have to have their own settings of common tunes, owing to the limited range of the chanter. So it is not entirely surprising that someone immersed in the world of pipe band music (which he must have been to rise to the level of Pipe Major) might have a blinkered view of music. (I assume he was referring to non-pipe band music. I am sure Irish pipe bands - those that compete, at least - play their graces together).

Of course, I don’t mean this as a slur on Scottish music, pipe bands or the pipe major in question. It’s just a sideways observation.

Re: Ireland vs. Scotland

When we go back to the earliest “history” we have of known Highland pipers (perhaps as much legend as “history” in the modern sense) we see that promising young pipers were sent to Ireland for instruction.

Evidently the ancient Indo-European art music now known as Piobaireachd was played at the highest level in Ireland at that time; sadly that music was extinguished in Ireland, the last link perhaps was the theme-and-variation music of the old Munster uilleann pipers, now mostly lost.

This music was played on harp also but evidently by Bunting’s time was all but extinct.

Now a scholar of these things can set me straight if I have drawn the wrong conclusions, but that’s what I gather from the evidence I’ve seen.
