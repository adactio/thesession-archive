Public liability insurance for ceili band (UK)

I’ve just been in communication with a client for an upcoming gig - a wedding ceili. He has just asked whether we (the band) have public liability cover. As a loose collective of musicians who do occasional (4 or 5 a year?) gigs together, it had never occurred to us that this might be necessary - nobody has ever asked the question before. I am aware that some ceili bands, who gig regularly, do have it - and I am aware, of course, that there is a certain risk involved in mixing tipsy and/or high-sprited dancers with electrical cables, speakers on stands etc. But I have always considered it good enough to just minimise the risk by routing cables sensibly, using duct tape, keeping all equipment clear of the dance floor etc. - and keeping the volume to a safe level.

What do people think? Have we been fools for playing ceilis without insurance over the years? Should I go and get insurance immediately? Does anyone have experience of such things? (If so, can anyone recommend a company or specific package? How much is it likely to cost?)

…Or is it something only worth bothering with if you’re gigging several times a month? Obviously, the more frequent the gigs, the greater the overall risk. Also, for this particular gig, there will be a PA set up ready for us, so it is our responsibility anyway? Am I missing any other important factors?

Your thoughts would be very much appreciated.

I am not a lawyer, but I do know something about contracts. A wise proprietor would have you sign a “hold harmless” contract for any damage that occurs to you or your equipment whilst on his or her premises. The reason for this is that, just like with a home, the owner is responsible for any mishaps whether or not there was negligence, unless concerned parties stipulate otherwise. Re: Public liability insurance for ceili band (UK)

It’s worth noting that you can get PL insurance considerably cheaper than MU membership (and doing it that way you won’t be supporting an organization that does stupid things like opposing the private copying amendment to copyright law.) My PL (as an instrument repairer rather than musician) is bundled with professional indemnity and equipment insurance, and the whole lot costs about half the MU subscription. A quick google shows that a band can get PLI alone for about £33.

Further to SteveT’s comments, there is no legal requirement to have PLI, but it would be pretty foolish not to - if someone dies and somehow it is construed as being your fault you could be faced with a damages claim running to millions. The only insurance that is legally compulsory in the UK is Employer’s liability. Unless a band is registered as a limited company it is deemed to be a partnership, and in that situation the band members are partners not employees, so employer’s liability isn’t needed.

For amateur musicians who don’t get paid to play, for peace of mind you should still have PL insurance. But in this case if you have personal public liability insurance it will still cover you while you play. Personal public liability insurance is one of those ‘extras’ that get bundled in with other policies like household or credit card insurance, so you may well already have it without realizing.

(c) Several parts of it involve dancing backwards, so folks are not always looking where they are going.

That dance should be banned on health-and-safety grounds! :-(

Re: Public liability insurance for ceili band (UK)

If your negligence (and this has a specific legal meaning) causes harm to someone who you could reasonably expect to be affected by it then you could be liable. It doesn’t really matter whether or not you are being paid, although a professional may be held to a higher standard of care. Your household insurance might possibly cover you for casual ‘hobby’ playing such as a session, but probably won’t for a formal performance, paid or not.

Most venues as a matter of policy (and perhaps the terms of their own insurance) require bands to show they have PLI, to avoid being sued themselves if something goes wrong. They will also ask for PAT tests on your electrical equipment. If you don’t comply they may not let you perform - that could put you in breach of contact with your client.

If you’re going to perform in public then you have to behave professionally, even if you don’t regard yourself as a ‘professional’. That means having PLI. Not having PLI won’t prevent you from being sued, it just means you’d have to pay any damages yourself. Are you willing to lose your house, your car or your instruments?

While you’re about it, you may find that your household policy will not cover your instruments and other gear if you’re being paid to perform. You should also consider specialist instrument insurance.

In the U.S., you can sue for anything you like - literally. However, you do so at some substantial cost to yourself if you lose. We have a small-claims system here wherein each party represents themselves and the case is decided by a judge. The limit is $7,500 in California. That keeps the petty stuff out of civil court. In the example posted above, the venue would be liable, but could afterwards come after the band if the proprietor chose to try and prove negligence. However, not having a signed contract with the band that stipulates something along that line would count heavily against the proprietor. In the U.S., having it in writing is everything.
