↳ Posted by 🎻Johnny Jay, Melody Player 🎻 on December 6th, 2011.

Bulmer and Sharpley, etc…

The Orange and Blue collections are great. The tunes are played slowly and evenly. That said, the Brendan Breathnach collections are superb. The Bulmer and Sharpley books are also very good if you can find them - simple, homogenized settings. But if you have access to this site and http://trillian.mit.edu/~jc/cgi/abc/tunefind (which is generally better since you have access to many different settings of the same tune), you really don’t need generalized books. It’s better to buy collections dedicated to one composer: Ed Reavy, Sean Ryan, Paddy O’Brien, Charlie Lennon, et. al.
It should go without saying that books should not be your first learning tool. But the dots are very helpful when learning an elusive tune that you’ve heard and played but haven’t perfected, or for being reminded of a tune that you haven’t played in a while. Just as there are very few accomplished musicians who have learned their basic repertoire from books, there are equally few who cannot read music to some extent.

Re. ‘Whistle and Sing’

Éamonn Jordan, its compiler, was just telling me the other day that the first volume has now sold more than 100,000 copies. That’s a remarkable achievement for a book which started out as a self-published aid for teachers and saw Éamonn travelling the length and breadth of Ireland with his car’s boot full of copies in order to meet the demand. The book was later acquired by Ossian and, in turn, is now handled by the Chester Novello organisation. Mally’s has both volumes in stock at somewhat cheaper prices than Amazon - http://www.mally.com/.

A couple of years ago he started planning a third volume, but other issues have conspired to put this on the back burner.
