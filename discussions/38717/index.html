Re: William Henry Grattan Flood

Well, we can begin with the preface.

The first sentence is broadly accurate, then and today.

The second sentence is not. As far as I know, there are no physical surviving instruments or fragments definitively belonging to a bagpipe prior to the mediaeval period. The historical record is uncertain, and many cited examples require heroic interpretation. Art provides the solidest evidence, fragmentary and confused though it often is, and the earliest unambiguous examples are the Cantigas de Santa Maria, of the 13th century.

Flood’s work is worthless, not just because it is largely bullshit, but because it is impossible to source the claims in it. I’m happy to agree there may well be facts in it that we are no longer able to prove are true - but so what? The job of historians is to interpret sources, not to second guess second hand material that is known to be stuffed with myth and fantasy. And Flood gives few or vague sources for many of his most specific statements, although he can go into some detail when he wants to (when he decides to lay into Scots tunes, for example).

The kindest thing you can say about it is that it is not up to modern standards of historical research, and therefore has little or no value.

If you really want to know what the present frontier of historical piping research has to say, you could dig up Keith Sanger, who hangs around the Dunsire forums from time to time.

And I repeat, that if someone makes a claim, the burden of proof is on them to back it up. What we’re talking about are really two different kinds of wrong, factually and academically. You can be factually right by simply making an assertion, but in order for anyone else to believe it, you have to have some backing evidence. Flood may be right on issues here and there, but so many of his assertions are either unfounded, unsourceable, or otherwise unverifiable. Maybe he was privy to knowledge we do not have through documents or primary sources that have since disappeared. That doesn’t matter in an academic setting, because we don’t have anything to go by. I can say that FDR had ham and eggs for breakfast on the morning of February 27th, 1938. I may be right. But unless I have the White House chef or Mrs. Roosevelt’s diary to back me up, I can’t prove it.

If you’re looking for particular examples, Flood writes in A History of Irish Music that “There is scarcely any room for doubt that the pre-Christian inhabitants of Ireland had the use of letters, the ogham scale, and the ogham music tablature.”

It’s true that they had ogham letters, but the musical portion of that claim is very much in doubt; Seán O’Boyle and Máire Egan have taken contrasting sides of this particular debate. The fact that he states it so unequivocally is the problem here, as he does not provide much supporting evidence other than that three of the “mystic strokes” look like musical signs. Again, he may be right (I may be crazy). But if he doesn’t back it up, we have to say that it’s inconclusive.
