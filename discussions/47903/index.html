Re: Tradition in the 2020s?

I’m not sure if this is exactly what you’re asking, but the short answer is I’m loving it more than ever!

I love the music itself, first and foremost, the pure delight and transcendence when it’s at its most beautiful.

I love having a neverending topic to study, learning the history and culture, different players and styles, always finding something to new to listen to that lights me up just when I’ve started to get in a rut, new things to watch (still haven’t exhausted the Brendan Begley content alone!)

I love having this niche hobby that most people find so interesting and curious, that connects me with other people with the same niche interest locally and around the world.

I love being able to see my personal growth getting through the blocks that held me back from playing for so many years, now finally honoring the musician in me with both fun and discipline--practicing and listening more and more consistently than I ever have.

I love that the deeper I go, the more there is.

I love the craic of sessions and that social setting/circle that’s so refreshingly different from where the other roles in my life take me. I love the new friends I’m making.

I love having a way to easily get to know local people when I travel, and do something that’s not just touristy looking at things or buying things. I love the hospitality and generosity of Irish music culture where you can just show up at a pub on a given night and sit in with one of the top musicians in the field.

I love that playing feels better and better the more I practice and that I am getting more and more moments of the sound I want.

As a parent and member of a “helping” profession, I love having a part of my life that’s just for me.

My only complaint is that I don’t have time to play and get to sessions as much as I would like. I’m lucky to have two sessions I’m part of that are really different but both friendly and fun.

Re: Tradition in the 2020s?

I am feeling pretty optimistic about the tradition and my place within it these days!

I have found the post-pandemic (or, at least, post-lockdown) sessions to be very rewarding. The pandemic brought my core group of session-mates closer together through our shared embrace of (and frustration with) internet technology for playing music together. We primarily used JamKazam, which I have talked about here quite a bit. Playing music with even “acceptable” levels of latency is a difficult thing, especially for playing Irish trad, since we’re all trying to play the same note at the same time. When we started playing on the “low latency” platforms, like JK, every “session” was more of a tech discussion and troubleshooting exercise than it was playing music. But with several of us being “techies”, we were able to get a core group of about 10 people from the region up and running. It took us several months to learn how to play with latency, and eventually, it became more about the music than the tech.

We were fortunate that we were able to put a cohesive group of regular players together, and get everyone over the hump of dealing with the technologies. I watched several friends try to get something similar going with musicians near them, and the tech frustrations pretty much killed it for them! One thing that helped is that we have pooled our tips from one of our sessions for 10 years. (We originally started doing that because the pub was considering canceling the session because they were being hounded by ASCAP / BMI / SESAC. Fortunately, right about then was when our session night became their busiest night, and they just broke down and paid the fees… So we had quite a bit of money put away…) So we were able to buy everyone audio processors, mics, headphones, etc.

In the end, playing on the internet was both a blessing and a curse. I would have gone bonkers if I wasn’t able to play music with other people, but it was rare that an internet session ever lived up to an in-person one. There were a few nights, though, where both the music and the craic were completely fulfilling, and even on the off nights, it was better to be hanging out (virtually) with my friends than watching even *more* television!

And in retrospect, the positives far outweighed the hassles. First off, you get really used to having individual volume control over everyone in the session. And it allowed me to play regularly with a few folks that live far enough away that I don’t get to see them in person too often. But it was the hidden positive aspects that became the greatest asset of that time. When you’re playing on JK, you’re playing into a mic, and hearing that directly in your headphones, similarly to being in a recording studio. That made a lot of the players uncomfortable at the beginning. But there’s no place to hide, and being able to hear in detail every sound that you make with your instrument ultimately made us all better players. On top of that, when you’re playing with latency, you basically have to feel like you’re leading every set. If you’re playing slightly in front of all the other players (from your own perspective), that’s when it works best. So even if you didn’t start a set, you have to feel like you’re leading it. And that also made us all stronger players. And finally, we realized early on that playing twice a week with the same 6-8 players, it was bound to get stale quickly! So for two+ years, we were constantly learning new tunes and dredging up tunes we hadn’t played for a long time!

So those hidden positives have really paid off since we have started playing in person again! Our core group are all stronger, better players than before the pandemic, and they’re all tune hounds now. Pretty much every session, someone will introduce a new tune, and people will ask about it, it will get sent around, and within a few weeks, pretty much everyone is playing it. I have increased my repertoire more in the last 3 years than the prior 7 years. It felt like drinking from a firehose for a while, but now we’re all used to the tune learning pace!

So our sessions are going strong! I recently spent 9 days in Ennis, and I wouldn’t say I enjoyed ANY of the daily sessions there as much as I enjoy my main session here. We play a wider variety of tunes, and a lot more tunes in general. (It helps that we know each others’ repertoires, and there’s a certain level of comfort with the familiar, but it wasn’t like they were playing a lot of tunes in Ennis that I don’t play…)

The biggest concern I have about the session scene here is that we’re aging. Several of our regular players are retired, and a few of us hope to get there in the next couple of years. We have lost a few players in the last couple of years too. So I can imagine that things will be very different 5 years from now if we don’t see an influx of younger players. The good news is that we’re seeing at least some signs of that. There is a group of 6-7 *great* young players in my area. They often stay within their own little clique, but we occasionally get them out into our sessions, and it at least gives me hope for the future of the scene here!

For me, personally, I am having more fun playing music now than at any time in the past, and I hope to see that trend continue!

And Jerone, I am very sorry to hear of your health struggles! Music is good medicine for the soul, but for the physical stuff it’s best to listen to the medial professionals! I wish you all the best in your journey through this!

Reverend’s comment speaks to a concern some of us have. When our main session lost our venue (pub closed), groups of us have been going from one venue to another, but not many “good fits” as they’re either too far for some, or decide to stop hosting session in favor of brunch, etc. So, it’s back to house sessions, which I guess is trad. Some of us are hosting beginners‘/slow sessions at our homes, in hopes of getting more young people, and at the same time teaching them the tunes/sets we know and love. This seems to be working, but after a time, some of the younger ones (senior high/college undergrads) move away for various reasons, to be seen again only occasionally. There are, however, some who have moved to our area from elsewhere, and who are looking for ITM sessions, so I guess we keep on keepin’ on, and post updates here and on social media such as FB.
