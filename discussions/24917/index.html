She has a way of taking the tunes and skipping them like a smooth rock being expertly tossed over the glass-like surface of the water. What I would give to play like that.

↳ Posted by premier on June 25th, 2010.

Re: Concertina favourites.

to reply to the OP,s original query, i, too, find that many though not all of my favorite players tend-to-be-female-and-live-in-county-clare. an aesthetic soul brother for me is mr. eoin o’neill of clare and clarefm, whose taste not just in concertina players but in all kinds of stuff including tony macmahon and country and western music, is right up my alley.

eoin seems to love the same concertina styles i am trying to pursue in my own playing, a clean, fluid aesthetic, and his documentation of female concertina players of county clare has opened up my world in a big way---i’ve gotten to hear lorraine o’brien and yvonne griffin’s approach to reels thanks to the clips he shares when hosting or subbing on clarefm. i wish he could do an anthology cd of his great recordings.

my favorites play both “on the rows” and “across-the-rows,” a distinction i find far less significant or interesting than the distinction between, melody-line-prominent versus melody-line-obscured-by-loud-bass-chords-and-other-FX. so, “on-the-rows” players i love include charles coen, mary macnamara, and jacqueline mccarthy, chris droney, and others (and many folks who are departed). “across-the-rows” players i love include dymphna o‘sullivan, kate mcnamara, yvonne griffin, lorraine o’brien, claire keville. don’t know which martin o‘brien of east clare is, but i like him a lot as well. i am a big fan of gearoid o’hallmhorain as well as jackie daly, and of peadar o’riada’s elegant, spare style too.

having said all that, i also listen a lot to noel hill, not because, but in spite of, his bass chords and loud rolls. i listen to concertina for the same reason as fiddle or anything else---for swing and emotional expression. i could care less what kind of technical wing-wang the player du jour has dreamed up to do with the instrument.

and noel hill’s swing and his spectrum of emotional expression, including playful humor as well as sadness and heartbreak, are great. to me, he has a pure “old clare,” style which is often overlooked because of the attention given his technical fillips. it is this, not his f/x, which to me make him great.
