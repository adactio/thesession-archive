Practice shouting at guitarists

Now, I’ll get into trouble for this. But not if we keep it ourselves, right? ;-)

Really speaking, I need the chord hat:-

http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bexIdm0awwo

But that’s old hat now. Besides, I haven’t got the turkey basters or the right accent.

What I really need is to be able to shout out the chords as I play a tune. But all I do when I try to do this is to spit, drool or make an almost unintelligible, albeit loud, grunting noise.

So, my question is: how can I improve my brain-splitting skills to the point where I can talk, or shout out chords, whilst still playing along? FFS, I can fall asleep whilst playing (as I did in a gig last week) but I can’t talk whilst playing. It’s frustrating.

Harmonica players can use finger signals - one up, G; two up, D; three up, A; thumb and forefinger curled into a zero for C; down for the flat keys. Wave from side to side for relative minors. Dunno who invented that but Iain Grant in Sandy Bells was using it twenty years ago.

If you play something that keeps your fimgers occupied as well as your mouth, you’re stuck. I’ve thought about fitting my most commonly used recorder with coloured LEDs operated by a left thumb switch.

Re: Practice shouting at guitarists

If you can play an imaginary guitar (piano, bouzouki or woteva) along in your head whilst playing the tune on your melody instrument AND think of the names of the imaginary chords as you imaginarily play them AND talk intelligibly whilst playing and imagining and thinking, then it should be a piece of cake.

“Can you imagine an imaginary menagerie manager imagining managing an imaginary menagerie?” (Courtesy of a friend’s 12-year-old daughter).

There are, no doubt, other ways of arriving at the chord names whilst playing the tune but, whatever way you do it, it must involve a fairly complex mental process, which can only detract from the quality of the playing of the tune. It is not the same thing as singing whilst playing (not that I am able to do that) since, when you learn a song to sing and accompany yourself, you learn the melody, words and accompaniment as interconnected activities; when I learn a tune, I don’t learn a set of chords with it (I might learn it with the chords in my head - perhaps the chords used on the source recording, or just the chords that sound right to my ear - but I would never have cause to think of the names of the chords unless someone asked me them.)
