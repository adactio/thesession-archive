Book of New Tunes

Hi Folks,

I’ve just had some copies printed of a collection of my own compositions from the last few years. It’s called “The Castle on the Hill,” after the name of one of the tunes. It’s beautifully illustrated, and the tunes are interesting and original, whilst being recognisably rooted in traditional music.

I’d like to know if anyone has any suggestions for selling and promoting the book.

Would it make sense to try to get a publisher interested? I’m thinking of sending a copy to “Mally” for starters. I’m also interested in getting it used in some kind of educational context - maybe as the basis for some kind of instrumental/folk music tutor.

There will eventually be recordings available of many of the tunes, but that is still a work in progress.

Any suggestions much appreciated.

To give a taste of the tunes, here’s an abc of one of them:

Re: Book of New Tunes

Sell them on Ebay, ask your local book shops to see if they’ll carry it.

Get yourself on createspace.com (amazon) and lulu.com

You can sell directly (and for free) on lulu.com and through Createspace you can sell on Amazon (also free).

You can also order copies for yourself at a reduced rate from both places.

Look into getting a wholesale printer printing your book out if you have an outlet to sell them. You can find a bunch of them online. Often times you can get copies for .50-$1.00 but you have to order several hundred usually.

Also consider getting an ISBN number. Depends on what country you live in but it’s generally a fairly easy process to do. It makes it easier to sell your book through different online publishers because you own your isbn number instead of having lulu, createspace or some other print-on-demand publisher provide you with an isbn that’s linked to them.

“You can sell directly (and for free) on lulu.com and through Createspace you can sell on Amazon (also free). ”

More trouble than it’s worth - and you’ll end up spending. You are also restricted to the book format that goes with this process. It makes more sense if you are based in the US, but if your target market is in Europe, buyers will have to pay shipping from the US, and any copies you want will carry a large shipping bill. This avenue is more for vanity publishing.

“Also consider getting an ISBN number.”

Vital if you want to sell on Amazon yourself. It is a relatively simple process, but costly. A book like this isn’t going to sell in large quantities. You’ll need to weigh up the advantages and disadvantages.

Oh, come on, Robin!

Wounded hussar has pointed out a reasonable way to share your music.

Usually tunes find their way into traditional repertoire, when someone hears someone playing a tune (on a record, a performance, on the streets, whatever) and adapts it. And then others do so, too. Or they submit it at the mustard board :)

Very few outstanding musicians compose a larger number of tunes, which become part of tradition (in the sense of being played at sessions and the like). Such as Paddy Fahy, who has not published or even recorded a single tune, AFAIK.

But if you put all your tunes, being “a considerable part of my life’s work and purpose”, into a book - who should buy it, and why? Because you say that your tunes are good and traditional? It simply doesn’t work like that, I’m afraid.

I mean, really! From time to time you stumble upon a new tune, which is just great, and then you play it. But if you want your tunes “to be received by contemporary musicians as welcome additions to an evolving tradition”, then you should publish it as public domain (or even better, play them, record them, let them be heard), so that as many people as possible have access to them. Almost every traditional is public domain, BTW.

Having read the above responses, and feeling somewhat dismayed and disappointed at the tone of many of them, I thought that I might join the discussion.

Firstly, ‘vanity publishing’ is such an unfortunate name, as it seems to imply that believing in your own vision is a bad thing. If many of the groundbreakers and innovators of history had waited for popular support, then we would probably wouldn’t have the computers that we are using to have this conversation. Secondly, I think Robin has highlighted an important issue, that when now established traditional tunes were first played by their composers, it would have been to their own communities, who valued, respected and supported their musicians, but it seems to me that we have lost that, along with faith in our own critical faculties, so that the only music that gets to reach the ears of the taste-makers, is that that has financial support behind it. I think that taking your tunes to schools, Robin73, is a great idea, as children don’t have the prejudices and cynicism of many adults- ‘It’s like a nursery rhyme with a broken leg’ - many nursery rhymes come from a folk tradition, and are now so familiar, that we have become jaded. Robin,

Take it from a fellow Tune Maker, your best approach is to put it out online first. Offer it as freely available PDF download with the option of a donation. Use social networking to promote it.

There’s little or no money to be made from the publication of sheet music these days. If you’re hoping to make money from this, forget it.

If you make enough from donations and people start playing your tunes only then might it be worthwhile printing up some copies of the book and looking for a publisher or distributor.

Just consider this, Liz Carroll’s tunes have been part of the tradition since the late 70’s, they are recorded and played everywhere there’s Irish music. Yet she only got around to publishing a book of tunes last year.

Tommy Peoples is currently working on a book, Paddy Fahey’s NEVER done a book. Charlie Lennon, Ed Reavy, Paddy O’Brien, Sean Ryan were all really well established names before any books were published of their tunes.

It takes a LONG time for new tunes to be accepted into the general tradition and the usual way is through recordings.
You’d probably be better off investing your money into a recording of the tunes and try to convince some well known players to feature on the album.

If you’re hoping to sell a decent amount of copies of your book you should really wait until people outside of your locality are playing some of your tunes.

Sorry if this seems negative to you but it’s just realistic. It doesn’t matter how good your tunes are, unless they are played outside of your locality no one other than your friends and family and some very curious collectors will buy the book.

Don’t let that dishearten you though, use it as an incentive to get your tunes into the hands of good players.

Perhaps pick 10 of your strongest tunes, record them, print them out and get them in the hands of players you admire.

Best of luck with it.

I don’t know if it’s a wind up or not. Courtesy says give him the benefit of the doubt. But if it is real, you’ve a pretty weird way of looking at things, Robin.

I research & publish material (not music) and for the few good publications I have that sell and bring in a few bob, I’ve recycled boxes and boxes of other ones that languished - unappreciated, unsold and cluttering up my storeroom.

So you look first to see what the market is for your material and you’ll gather from here, it’s very little in your case. So do your sums and save yourself a bit of hassle. That is unless, you think these tunes are your contribution to human society and you’ll see them published at any cost, just to have them there. This is called ‘vanity publishing’, unfortunate term maybe but accurate.

I’m actually engaged in a venture myself presently which probably borders on the questionable in financial terms. But it’s my little contribution to a shared heritage. I won’t be relying on it to pay many bills though..

Finally, my humble experience as a small time publisher is that print brings in money - once people will buy it. Digital publishing for the small man is not easy to make money on, in that the very essence of a digital file usually means it can be copied and freely passed around. It can appear as free downloads on other websites once it gets into the wild etc. etc.

So if you want to just get your tunes out there, make up a pdf file and make it freely available. You could ask for a wee donation, an honesty box, but don’t depend on many people using it.

Re: Book of New Tunes

What is your “life’s work” - is it the book, or is it the tunes themselves? I sense that you’ve become so preoccupied with the idea of publishing them in book form that you’ve lost sight of the alternatives. These could include ways of generating payments, although you’re not going to get rich or even make a living from composing trad-style tunes.

As plenty of people have pointed out, tune books are not big sellers and one containing unknown tunes by an unknown composer is even less likely to sell. What you should be doing is getting your tunes played as widely as possible. Publish them online. Send them to some well-known musicians - if the tunes are as good as you claim you might find them being performed or even recorded. Join PRS and you’ll get paid royalties when they’re performed.

When your tunes have got into circulation and you’ve earned yourself the reputation you believe you deserve, then a printed tunebook might find a market.
