Re: Why forget the names?

I think you actually answered your question fairly well yourself, didn’t you? :)

Another factor could also be that there are many different names of each tune, and in addition there are also some completely different tunes with the same name (for instance there is both a jig and a reel called “Swallow’s tail”) This does not simplify things!

However, personally I know the name(s) of all the tunes in my repertoire (which isn’t too big, though..) At least, so far no problems! As well as you, I’m a bit scared I will start to forget the names in the future! However, I personally think it’s important to have a name associated with what you play, because then it will also be a lot easier to remember the melody of the tune! Other people might not really give a damn about what the tune is called, thus not putting any effort in remembering the name!

One thing that I’ve noticed, when it comes to remembering the name of a tune or identifying a tune being played at a session, is that it’s MUCH easier to remember the names of the tunes that you’ve learnt thoroughly at home, in comparison to the ones you exclusively have been listening to at a session!

Bottom line - learn as many tunes as you can, preferably at home, starting at a really slow speed and increasing the tempo when it feels comfortable until you’ve reached the speed that you personally think sounds good, because when you have spent this extra time with a single tune, it’ll be a lot easier to associate it with the correct name(s) as well!

/Mattias

Re: Why forget the names?

Lingpupa - that’s not an opinion, it’s an assertion. It’s one I think is crap, because most of the good players I know who value the tunes have as much trouble as anyone else remembering the names of them.

Why do people forget the names? I think it’s because the tune is its own name, after a while. You’ve heard it a hundred times - variations on “Do you know the one that starts like this?”
Whereas if you know the name, you’ll hear “I think I know it, how does it go?”

As for the significance of the names, yes, some of the names do seem to have significance for the composer. If you look around, you’ll find a recording of Ed Reavy’s son playing all of his tunes, and each is prefaced with a little discourse about the tune’s name, and what it refers to or what Reavy was thinking of when he gave the tune that name. It’s interesting listening.

I don’t know that that can change the fact that most people who have been playing for any length of time know a lot of tunes and a lot of names, but not necessarily which one goes with which of the other.

Re: Why forget the names?

I’ve yet to meet anyone who ‘deliberately doesn’t pay attention to names’. I think that is a very cynical view of your fellow musicians.

Tunes are like people. Think about people you know. You have a circle of close friends and family who you know well - you know their names, their character and some of their background. But then there are hundreds of people you vaguely know - the cashiers in your local shops, the regulars at the pub. You recognise their faces, but don’t necessarily know their names.

It’s the same with tunes. As you progress you’ll build up a core of tunes that you particularly like are close to - you’ll know their names, where they come from, possibly who wrote them and the story behind them. But at the same time there will be hundreds of tunes you’ve heard repeatedly at sessions - you know their faces, you’ve heard them often enough to be able to play along, but you’ve never been introduced, so you don’t know their names. Eventually you’ll start using some of those tunes to padd out your ‘special tunes’ to make sets. Someone is then going to ask you the name of that tune, and you’ll have to tell them it’s The Blind Buck. It’s not that you’re trying to look smart, it’s just that you don’t know the name of the tune.

Namnes are important for important tunes, and in performance situations, but as Micheal said at the top of the thread, in sessions they really don’t matter. In a session everyone is your friend, regardless of whether you know their name or not.

Re: Why forget the names?

Oh dear, back to the wonderful world of making up behavioural theories based on culture and country of origin. You need at least one a week on this site.

I’ve never found any correlation between someone’s native country and ability to remember, or not, the names of tunes. Memory varies widely between individuals that you’re bloody hard pressed to generalize why A knows all the names and B knows hardly any.

When I was an undergrad doing a psychology degree, we did an experiment where we tested whether watching violence on TV inhibited memory. We spliced the beach landing sequence of Saving Private Ryan with fairly mundane adverts we’d recorded from the tele for one group, and spliced a walking-through-the-French countryside scene from the same movie for the other group. After watching our video, the participants were asked to recall the commercials. The experiment didn’t “work” in the sense that there were no significant differences between the “violent” group and the “non-violent” group. The interesting part of it was that the standard deviation within the two groups was huge. In English, it basically means that people’s answers from both groups were spread across such a wide range that no statistically significant differences between the groups were able to be detected.

Re: Why forget the names?

Tune names are kind of important when you’re playing in a Cheilidh Band - my Banjo Player (dear friend!) launched into the fist set of the gig and played the second set of tunes - ok a quick recovery sorted that out. But that messed up the order of the dances agreed with the caller- so we had to re-jig the first half - to add more joy each set he called for the next four sets were wrong - calling one name and playing another tune set entirely. If didn’t know him better I’d think it was a wind up! but I think he geniuenly couldn’t place the names to the tunes and I’m as bad without the tunes and the dots in front of me. Must be a muso thing .. too many tunes,,, too many grey hairs … too much beer .. nobody seemed to notice, especially those dancing -.. so really it doesn’t seem to matter at the end of the day .. just folllow the lead and let the music flow and just hope something in the memory banks steps in to save you!
