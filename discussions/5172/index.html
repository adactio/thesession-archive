Homogeneous tunes and playing styles

I came upon two musicians(I’ll not name them but both are very good players of Irish and Scottish music respectively) having a conversation the other night. Knowing them both, they didn’t mind me listening so I wasn’t being rude. :-)

Anyway the jist of it was that many of the new compositions and fashionable tunes are now a kind of hybrid, Euro-Celt style and could easily fall into the Scottish, Irish, Cape Breton or wherever camp. They could be from anywhere or everywhere. The same goes for the playing styles of many of the younger players.
Unfortunately, much as I enjoy many of the newer tunes and younger players, I feel that they have a point. Also, I find that many of the newer tunes are very rhythmic in nature and, although they can sound very effective in the hands of good musicians, they don’t always seem to have much of a melody or stand out on their merits.
Does any one else agree with all of this or is it just three(including myself) grumpy old gits talking?

Re: Homogeneous tunes and playing styles

I think there is a place for Comhaltas Ceoltoiri Eireann and their attitudes to the tradition (although these are no doubt grossly misrepresent\ed and stereotyped by the tune-playing public). What these people should realise (and doubtless many of them do) is that change is both *inevitable* and *necessary* for the tradition to survive. Immigration, technology and the media have all affected and continue to affect the nature of this music, the manner and context in which it is played.

As has always happened, new tunes will be composed and th best ones will be perpetuated by a process of ‘natural selection’ - the runts will fall by the wayside and the strong ones will survive. Perhaps even some of the runts will be transformed, through the ‘folk process’ , into gems. It is possible to hear tunes evolving right before our ears - probably much faster than it ever happened in the past, as there are so many musicians and sessions, and such easy access to music of different styles

Remember, we’re committing an unconscious fallacy by comparing the best of the past--ie, the tunes that have lived--with ALL of the present.

Theodore Sturgeon once memorably opined that “90% of everything is crap,” an expression which has become known as Sturgeon’s Law.

It’s my opinion that if we time-travelled back to the last great era of popular music--the era of itinerant musicians and dancing masters, in which set dancing was THE popular entertainment in most of ireland, and the tunes we now know as classics were being composed or honed--we’d find an awful lot of dodgy tunes floating about, as well.

Hell, a brief trip through O’Neill will bear this out. There are a lot of dreary tunes in that book that no one plays.

The doubt time and the folk process will do the same to the majority of the less-worthy tunes of today.

Influences from other traditions is nothing new to ITM. How do you think we ended up with polkas and mazurkas (definitely NOT irish) ? Re: Homogeneous tunes and playing styles

Where to begin, continue or end? First, I agree with Simon that much of the modern music will fade like any fad. Elements of it will be incorporated in the tradition and some of the tunes will survive, or will be adapted to fit future and past styles.

Second, what will happen to the young hot-shots of the day when they get older? Will they continue playing technically flashy cud? Or will they start developing a deeper style and sound to differentiate themselves from the masses coming up behind them? Where will they turn to develop this style? (Many turn to other traditions and try to find other music to play in the same manner.)

With all of the archival recordings available, won’t a future generation just bring it all back around again? Won’t there be people that will continue to be interested in the historical nature of the music?

Third, what will happen to the link between Irish traditional music and dance? Many of these modern tunes are “undanceable” to my ears. They just don’t have that good old fashion groove and rhythm. And I am not talking about modern irish dance performance here.
