Re: Why so many want Midi Tunes …

Brief comments:

1) MIDI files without any swing are excruciatingly bad examples of the music, just as playing precisely what is notated will create excruciatingly bad examples of the music.

2) MID files with swing are a bit easier on the ears, but are still mechanical.

3) Those who try to learn tunes by matching MIDI files will end up being bad players because the MIDI file will not “humanize” the music.

4) Those who use MIDI files for a quick check, comparison, or something similar can get real value from them.

5) One can use a MIDI file to transfer music from one program to another, though XML files usually do a better job.

In sum: Properly used a MIDI file can be handy. Used as the main learning source they can be a real liability.

Re: Why so many want Midi Tunes …

The thing that no-one has mentioned about the abc, the dots and the midi, is that all three are exactly the same. The information contained within them is identical.

However, while the abc and the dots are abstracted representations of sound that one “looks” at, the midi thing is an abstracted representation of sound that one “listens” to. It can only confuse

When one reads music (or abc, same thing) one’s clever little brain amalgamates the pitiful information off the page with one’s own correct or incorrect preconceptions of how it should sound. And, of course, if one’s preconceptions are correct, i.e. you have a detailed knowledge of what ever genre the music is from, then there’s a good chance that you can transform that bit of pitiful information into a reasonable piece of art. However, if your preconceptions are limited in any way, or pertaining to the wrong genre, you’ll just bloody murder it.

The only thing the midi files are good for is as an aural illustration of just how pitiful the amount of information there is in the dots/abc
