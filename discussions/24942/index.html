Re: LP Record Sleeves - Works of art or rubbish ?

The relationships between, on the one side, functionality - graphic design, typography, photography, illustration, etc. - and art on the other are complex and often symbiotic.

However, design is more like engineering than art. It’s about finding solutions. And asthetics only comes in to it if one of the funcions of your design is to appeal to people’s tastes - and here, it’s the designer’s job to understand those tastes.

Good designers have no taste at all. If one allows one’s taste to steer the work, then one will fail. However, that’s not to say that great design cannot also be great art. Concorde is the best example I can think of off the top of my head.

CD sleeves? I have seen it, but it’s very rare. Mostly, the really good CD sleeves you see are not art at all. They are merely good design that has taken into account your personal taste

Re: LP Record Sleeves - Works of art or rubbish ?

The example given by Wounded Hussar is an example of poor design posing as art. The designer has obviously never heard of letter-spacing. If you don’t know what that is, compare the spaces - not the distances - between T/h, L/a, e/a, with the spaces between h/e, i/n, a/r. The result is an unpleasant jostling of letters, which, incidentally, have been very carefully and painstakingly designed by someone who knew what he was doing. The bird’s left wing is badly positioned in the exact centre, isolating the text, which is jammed up into left side of the otherwise empty space. The sweep of the right wing draws the eye down into the right corner of the image, where it lingers somewhat reluctantly in the region just above the bird’s unwitting head before wandering off in search of something more satisfying - preferably with a bit more colour in it.

Yeah, I did mean that it’s impossible to “fix the kerning” with Americana because it’s such an imbalanced font.

Kerning? Art or craft? I’d say neither, it’s more like creative engineering. There is, theoretically, an ideal kern for any piece of typography. i.e.. the one that gives the most balance and legibility. The way I like to do it is to flip the text temporarily back to front and then tweak the kerning. This works because you see the letter forms more as abstract shapes rather than subliminally trying to read it all the time. Try it.
