Re: Does anyone else get bored of playing some tunes?

Caoimhín Ó Raghallaigh once suggested to me to take a tune like that, and play it non-stop until you have done everything you can think of with it, put every ornament you can imagine in, find every variation that you can conjure up, and then keep playing it… and keep playing it… Say, for a couple hours straight.

In my experience, this music is more about the expression of musical ideas than it is about a sequences of notes. If you’re bored with the tune, figure out how to do something interesting with it. For me, when I get to that level of intimacy with a tune, the world opens up, and all of a sudden I can play a tune in a more interesting way.

Re: Does anyone else get bored of playing some tunes?

Some good responses here.
It was a bit late in the night when I posted and I maybe wasn’t too clear.
I don’t advocate learning rakes of new tunes just for the sake of it nor just giving up on particular tunes if they are proving too difficult or they seem to be boring. As some of the others here have mentioned, some of the tunes which I thought were boring at an early stage I just hadn’t heard properly.

However, I do feel it’s good to leave some tunes for a while and either play something different or even have a rest from the music altogether for a short while. In my experience, over focusing or trying to hard can make things even worse and sometimes you can’t see the wood for the trees. When I do revisit certain tunes, they can often appear to be much easier to learn and I can actually play them better than I did when I was constantly practising them.

It’s also the case that many tunes do take a lot longer to get right. Months and even years, in some cases. I’ve always got several on the back burner at any one time. Sometimes they are more complex and this obviously takes one longer but I find that those that don’t get a regular airing never seem to be quite as polished as the others.
I’m sure most of us know some great tunes which we never seem to get the opportunity to play in a session which is a shame.

Re: Does anyone else get bored of playing some tunes?

I don’t think you should ever be static, just playing the same tunes over and over, you should always have something new that you are learning.

When it comes to practicing I think it is useful to split your practice time in two - do a session of technique practice, followed by a session of tune learning/practice. In the technique period you can use old familiar tunes or (putting on hard hat and flak jacket) scales and exercises, anything that contains the technique you are trying to learn/improve. In your ‘tune time’ you don’t need to master a tune, just learn it to the point where you remember it before moving on to another. You’ll find that in the future you can come back to those tunes that you learned but didn’t master, and you’ll be able to play them well because your playing has imporved. There is no reason to grind away at a tune until you are sick of it.

Re: Does anyone else get bored of playing some tunes?

When you get tired of the tune itself, start exploring the possibilities it opens. A twist here and a twist there and you find yourself enjoying it again. New ornaments or articulations, and extra grace note at the start, drop an octave and double a part here and there--that kind of thing. I don’t know who said it here, but these words have stuck in my head for a long time, “There are no crap tunes, only crap players.” A rather negative expression, but there is truth in there--if you can’t get something new out of a tune, look to yourself, rather than the tune.
And if we only played new tunes, and not the well worn ones that everyone around the circle knows, sessions wouldn’t be much fun, would they?

Re: Does anyone else get bored of playing some tunes?

Over the years here on The Session we’ve had various members of the in-crowd dissing certain tunes because they get played too much, etc., or because they are the tunes the riff-raff always learn first, etc. (sorry about the disingenuous straw man here…! ), but I’ve always said that if you get bored with a tune that’s been around for hundreds of years, as opposed to your own personal span of mere tens of years, then you should be looking to your own attitude first before condemning the tune. Many is the tune that my mates and I have played to destruction week after week, then - all of a sudden, out of the blue - you hear another “version” of it, at a gig, on a CD, or at someone else’s session, and the thing takes on a completely new life in your head. A few years ago I moaned on here about how I disliked Drowsy Maggie. Then Michael Gill emailed me another version of it that made it spring to life for me in an entirely different way. Now just because something’s been around a long time doesn’t necessarily make it good (I mean, think Bruce Forsyth…), but you have to at least consider that there may be something in it. I can do all manner of stuff with the Kesh Jig these days that I’ve never heard done on a record. This music isn’t just about “tunes”. It’s about having fun with “the tunes” and applying your own inventiveness to them whilst “keeping within the bounds” (which are pretty loosely-set). It’s less a case of “I’m bored with this”, more a case of “over to you”.

Re: Does anyone else get bored of playing some tunes?

I think it’s fair to say that boredom is part of the process of learning a tune; at least that’s what I have found.

So, for example, I’ve been learning the Wonder Hornpipe lately (https://thesession.org/tunes/337), which I really like, but getting it right involves lots of repetition of the tricky parts sometimes using a metronome to check the timing. And also finding interesting variations means playing the same tune over and over again. When I get bored in those kinds of situations I see it as a side-effect of the learning process and, indeed, as a good sign that I am making progress, with the promise that the boredom will eventually lift: a sort of deferred gratification, I suppose.

On the other hand, there is that boredom that comes when, for whatever reason, one has to play a tune that doesn’t interest one. This happens to me occasionally when a tune is part of a repertoire that I play with others.

When I have the choice, though, I wouldn’t grimly and stoically impose such boredom upon myself. Where possible, for myself I only choose tunes I like to play with the expectation that I’ll go through the boredom phase with them eventually, and that the boredom will inevitably lift.

Re: Does anyone else get bored of playing some tunes?

the dirty little secret is that a number of the “names” in this music are often bored with not just “some” tunes, but playing irish folk music, period. some of these folks find it an artistic prison for them. i’m not saying I would see it that way, I am saying this is so. some of these folks are identifiable by the fact that they are spending a lot of energy on “art” music of some sort. some of such folks have even more or less publicly let it out in interviews that traditional tunes played traditionally are tiresome and confining for them, only to later kind of step back from that to a place of doing “art” projects along with some traditional folk projects, the timing of which might suggest to the cynical observer that they are playing folk music because it’s dawned on them that’s the only way anyone is ever going to listen to them or come out and see them, because they are not even a blip on the horizon to any potential audience but the audience that loves their folk playing.

regarding traditional players who repeatedly play the same tunes or core of tunes---this is “true” traditional playing. the “know and play EVVVVVVerything” mentality is a vulgar latter-day phenom, possibly American in origin. traditional folk musicians often know a pretty deep catalogue from their own region and favor a core within that catalogue, often about 20 or 30 tunes that you will hear over and over, if you are fortunate. I personally would rather hear tony macmahon play “The Daisy Field” and “The Cottage Groves” another time than endure a recherchee riffle through a catalogue from some overtrained kiddiwink, inevitably and regrettably laced with their own quote-unquote “compositions.”

If you find certain tunes boring, no, you do not need to hang your head and go to the stool in the corner until you “get it.” Don’t play them. Go on with your musical journey. You don’t get to try to force your tastes on a session, but you get to play what you like and leave the rest for others. You might come to appreciate them later, you might not. It doesn’t matter. This site is called thesession, and a lot of people who get into this music are all about sessions, sessions, sessions. But sessions are not what music is at its deepest level, and any serious, master irish teacher or musician will tell you that if you know them long enough. At the deepest level playing this music is about expression through the music, and that is individual.

In the megalopolis where I live and the region surrounding that megalopolis, it is probly fair to say there are thousands of very good-to-great electric guitar players, counting whiz kids in garage bands all the way to star rockers and pro studio session guitarists, everything in between. I’m not counting classical or jazz here. I’ m talking popular music forms. Nobody expects them to play certain music. Nobody expects them to play a certain hyper-frenetic speed in order to be respected or qualify as great, though certainly some “shredders” do. Nobody expects them to sit in a public place and play with any idiot who walks in the door with an instrument no matter who incompetent they are. Nobody expects them to play music they’re not interested in unless they have chosen to do so for money in contexts where the audience makes requests, such as weddings or paid studio work. There are great, great things about the whole phenom of “irish sessions,” but there are also things about that phenom that are less great. Take what you like and leave the rest. Just don’t expect others to change for you.

Re: Does anyone else get bored of playing some tunes?

There are fashions in traditional tunes. Some get played too often in a particular part of the world and go out of fashion. Even if you aren’t bored with them yourself you get a bad feedback feeling when you play them in a session. Then maybe six years later someone plays that same tune and everyone asks why people haven’t been playing it and it rolls back into fashion. For instance, there are Kerry polkas being played again in some sessions I go to. Everyone had got to hate them, but now there are people to whom they are new and jolly.

There are also some tunes with tricks in them which get irritating if they are played too often.

Re: Does anyone else get bored of playing some tunes?

The topic has strayed onto tunes being ruined as opposed to being bored of them. In our circles there are tunes that tend to be picked up by beginners and only started by beginners (or people that never evolve beyond beginner). This is guilt by association. Often these tunes would be considered great tunes if they showed up as new tunes. I have been on a personal campaign to reclaim these tunes. What I do is wait until there’s nothing but good players in the circle and then introduce the tunes into medleys with current favorites. If no one cringes or writes the tune off because of the tarnish… it can wake up the tune again… give it a new shine. Then we can go back to wincing when the *beginner starts it up… again… and again.

*Disclaimer: we are supportive of the people starting out and play the tunes with them… we have all been there… we still wince.
