Re: Bill Monroe’s ‘Scotland’

While some may say that Bill Monroe’s music should not be discussed on this forum, I feel I should clarify a few points. It is a common misconception that Bluegrass is a generic form of American folk music. When Bluegrass first appeared in the late 1940s, the term was a specific reference to Bill’s band’s style and repertoire, which drew on several earlier sources. He often spoke of the ancient tones, but he was quick to dismiss many folk musicians as not being suitable to Bluegrass. It was tent-show entertainment. As his style developed, the influence of the Blues was introduced mainly through his fiddlers like Chubby Wise. In as much, Bluegrass is not really any older than Rock and Roll and has much the same amount of African American quality. When Bill included older Appalachian tunes, he tried to keep them true to “the ancient tones,” with questionable success, but that is where the general idea of “authenticity” muddled up songs like Footprints in the Snow, with tunes of Anglo-Celtic origin.

Re: Bill Monroe’s ‘Scotland’

Contrary to some folks notions, old-time, and even bluegrass, and Klezmer and other forms of music, do get the occasional airing in sessions in Ireland and beyond, and are generally enjoyed by most. Hell, sometimes, in the days, the theme from Star Wars would get time in the odd session in Clare… And I’ve been known to break into “If I Only Had a Brain”, if not recently… :-D However, on the other side, I have also been known for the occasional pained look when certain others have slipped into that twilight zone of melody… That’s likely about the how and the when and the general mood of the particular group of musicians gathered. I remember nights when a hell of a lot of Galician tunes were played. We had the pleasure of the company of a very good Galician piper. And his partner, Wow!, her tambourine playing was particularly amazing, including accompanying our usual sets of mostly Irish tunes… Sigh! ;-)
