ABC Transcription Tools - January 2025 Updates

I’ve (hopefully) finally figured out a way to automagically inject G/D Anglo Concertina fingering tablature into ABC tunes with my ABC Transcription Tools.

Previously, it was only possible to inject C/G fingering tablature.

It takes advantage of the ability of abcjs to independently transpose the ABC itself and the notation displayed from that ABC, then injecting C/G tablature at the transposed pitch.

The general idea is the fingerings for a tune on G/D are essentially the same as C/G if you had transposed the tune either down a fifth or up a fourth to the key of C. So for a G tune, the G/D fingerings are the same as if you you play the tune in C on a C/G.

The trick is getting the notation to show the proper pitch when played on G/D independent of the ABC itself, and this is absolutely possible in my tool.

There are two solutions, the first I call a “low pitch” solution and it goes like this:

Select your tablature style (Jeffries or Wheatstone, along-the-row or cross-row style, show Gary Coover style or button numbers with arrows) from the “Tablature Injection Settings” on the “More Tools” window.

Click the “Transpose Down” button 7 times to bring the ABC itself down a fifth.

Add the following line to your ABC to visually transpose the displayed notation up to the original pitch:

%abcjs_render_params {“visualTranspose”:7}

From the “More Tools” window, click “Inject Anglo Concertina Tab”

If you play the tune, it will be at the wrong pitch.

To fix this, you can add this line to your ABC which forces abcjs to play the pitch as displayed (but an octave down) in the notation:

%%MIDI transpose -12

Here is the “high pitch” solution, which creates tab for the tune played an octave higher than the “low pitch” solution:

Select your tablature style (Jeffries or Wheatstone, along-the-row or cross-row style, show Gary Coover style or button numbers with arrows) from the “Tablature Injection Settings” on the “More Tools” window.

Click the “Transpose Up” button 5 times to bring the ABC itself up a fourth.

Add the following line to your ABC to visually transpose the displayed notation down to the original pitch:

%abcjs_render_params {“visualTranspose”:-5}

From the “More Tools” window, click “Inject Anglo Concertina Tab”

If you play the tune, it will be at the wrong pitch.

To fix this, you can add this line to your ABC which forces abcjs to play the pitch as displayed in the notation:

%%MIDI transpose 0

Since you can transpose all the tunes in a tunebook at once, generate the Anglo Concertina tablature for all the tunes, as well as add header to all the tunes via the “Inject ABC Header Text” feature it’s trivial to do this for all the tunes in an entire tunebook at one time.

Most likely this process can be generalized for any tuning of Anglo, i.e. Bb/F, A/E, etc.

Re: ABC Transcription Tools - January 2025 Updates

Both the on-row and cross-row Anglo Concertina fingering solutions I make available are least-cost mapping algorithms that use a certain set of weightings of preferred fingers for certain notes based on specific playing styles combined with rules like avoiding having the same finger jump rows between notes. The cross-row solution in particular comes up with solutions that very closely match how I would play tunes myself.

One of the most common questions I get, usually with regards to making very long tunes less tall so they fit on one page in an exported PDF file, is how to scale the notation.

Unfortunately, the ABC

%%scale

annotation doesn’t work in my abcjs-based responsive web-based tool.

Instead you can get essentially the same results as %%scale using:

%%staffwidth
%%leftmargin
%%rightmargin

to scale the notation.

The video also demonstrates the “Notation Spacing Explorer” which you can use to preview the effects of changing these values on the notation display.

Re: ABC Transcription Tools - January 2025 Updates

Power User demo for those of you experienced with creating PDF tunebooks with play links and want additional play link functionality if user plays the tunes and then wants to export their own PDF of the single tune and have that PDF include a play link.

When you export a PDF tunebook with play links, the user can click the title of any tune to play the tune.

From the Player, they can then export a copy of the single tune as a PDF, but by default, that tune PDF, unlike the original PDF they got it from, won’t have a play link on the title of the tune.

This video shows a simple way to make sure that any individual tune PDFs exported from the Player also include their own play links by adding

%add_all_playback_links 0 0 0 fatboy
%add_all_playback_volumes 50 50

(or similar depending on your choice of soundfont and instruments/volumes) play link annotations to each of the individual tune’s ABC as well as the header at the top of the whole tunebook’s ABC.

https://youtu.be/ZAO_5Q00Guk

Re: ABC Transcription Tools - January 2025 Updates

I’ve added some code to the tool to carry-forward play links in exported PDF tunebooks or websites. This essentially automates the process I demonstrated in my last post.

There is nothing you need to do, this behavior now happens automatically when exporting a PDF tunebook with play links or a website where the tunes open in the Player.

Here’s the new scenario:

1) You open a PDF tunebook that has play links on the tunes or a website that has specified that the tunes open in the Player.

2) You click the title of a tune (or select one from the tune list dropdown on an exported website) to open it in the Player.

3) You now want a PDF of the tune by itself, so you click the “Export Audio, Image, or PDF” button in the Player, and select “Export Tune as PDF File” and save the file.

4) If you now open that PDF file and click the tune title, it will also open in the Player.

Previously, it would not unless you had added some specific annotation to each tune.

This feature is in version:

2232_010525_0930

or later. Update to the latest to pickup the new feature.

Re: ABC Transcription Tools - January 2025 Updates

Demonstration of using the new “Inject MIDI gchord Templates” feature on the “Hamburger” menu to speed creation of custom backup patterns using inline %%MIDI gchord annotations to meet very specific bass and chord playback requirements.

The demo I show in the video is adding cutom backup patterns to the reel “The Sally Gardens” for a B/C button accordion PDF tunebook I’m putting together to teach new players exactly how to use the bass and chord buttons on the left side of the instrument.

For my book, the backup when playing the tunes has to match exactly what I’m specifying in the notation.

Without the custom %%MIDI gchord patterns, it would have used the default abcjs 4/4 boom-chick backup pattern.

“Inject MIDI gchord Templates” speeds the process by injecting a meter-appropriate inline %%MIDI gchord pattern into every measure of the tune.

You then can walk through the tune and set the bass, chord, or rest values for each eighth note timeslot in the pattern using f, c, or z characters.

This feature is only available on Desktop browsers.

Feature is in version 2246_010925_1700 or later.

Check the version number on the lower left corner of the “Settings” dialog.

Click the version number to update to the latest if required.

Expanded the functionality of “Inject MIDI gchord templates” to allow optional injection of inline per-measure %%MIDI gchordstress and %%MIDI gchordduration along with the meter-adaptive default gchord control strings.

gchordstress and gchordduration are custom %%MIDI annotations in my tools that allow you complete control over the playback duration and volume of each element in the %%MIDI gchord custom backup control string.

Continuing my enhancement of a niche feature that probably nearly nobody else cares about except me. :-)

You can now specify the gchord control pattern you want to inject.

Initially, the tool will suggest a rest-only initial pattern that matches the meter that you can then edit to meet your needs.

Whatever value you inject will be retained until you use the feature with a tune in a different meter, and will then again suggest a rest-only initial pattern.

You can also now specify full gchordstress and gchordduration vectors instead of single values.
