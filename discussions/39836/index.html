A Fool’s Confession (Read at Your Own Risk)

**Be forewarned. The following post shows evidence of extreme idiocy, also induces sleep.**

Recently I have been trying to “wean” myself off the dots and develop a stronger sense of tone, by learning by ear. I have learned a few tunes so far, easy ones, such as “Danny Boy” and the (or a) Ballydesmond Polka. On my to-be-learned list I have “The Eagle’s Whistle” and Kellie’s Air (https://thesession.org/discussions/39702), among others. Learning by ear is fun and I am making noticeable progress therein, which is a pleasant surprise. Fiddling has swiftly become an addiction. I do it even when I don’t feel like doing it and often when I should be doing other more urgent things.

I reckon it’s because fiddling gives me a sense of satisfaction. A sense that I actually can do something.

Yesterday my dear ma invited an old friend over, and if some malevolent power had designed the visit expressly so that my every incapability should be displayed before a group who were complete strangers to me—well, it could not have done better. I didn’t know how to introduce myself, I didn’t know how to answer the old lady, I didn’t know how to pass the plates or the glasses around, I didn’t even know how to arrange the dessert…

All the meanwhile the children kept giving me expectant faces, as though I was supposed to know what to do; and they could speak glibly in three languages, while I have been toiling away for years and still haven’t half-mastered my second; and they were all smiles and I was all awkwardness… It was an afternoon of shame, and only one of many such for me.

My real trouble goes beyond one or two dozen disastrous visits… I just mean that the visits put my trouble on display.

The trouble is—I don’t know how to do anything.

Even writing, which of all my half-skills I do best, isn’t true writing. A true writer has wisdom and astuteness and witty turns of phrase, all of which I do not. I am merely a belletrist—one who can make little nothings sound pretty, or construct grandiose façades for inane and empty notions. A belletrist is nothing but a pretender and a sycophant…

You older folks will all laugh at me and tell me that it’s all right, you’re not expected to have your whole life in order when you’re only sixteen; life is a journey, you’ve got yours all ahead of you, etc… But if you knew this unending loneliness that traps me in a vicious cycle of self-consciousness and self-analysis, if you knew how bleakly my every sun rises, if you knew what it was to live in this sunny and spacious place that everyone calls a “fine house” but only I know as a prison cell… If you knew, I think, then you would understand my desperation to find some purpose in life—some token that I do not suffer all these days in vain.

Dear Hanan,
As a person with a similar love for this music, and a would-be writer to boot, with more than their share of depression issues, i’ll add my tuppence-worth on the dubious basis that being a bit over twice your age has given me more time to become accustomed to similar issues.

It can be a very forbidding world at times. I found that a period of relative poverty where I was busking to supplement my dire financial situation to be incredibly frustrating, confronted by a lot of apathy and even mockery from yobs. Turn on the TV or radio and see real music ignored in favour of talentless corporate inanity. It doesn’t invalidate who you are - only if you let it. Get some nice big headphones and listen to e.g. The Green Branch by Oisin Mac Diarmada instead of having other people’s crap inflicted upon you. No matter how deep the pain can run at times, there’s something abiding about ‘the music’ that raises you above the dross of life.

About creating something truly meaningful. It’s hard. Avoid self-censorship and ego and just play, or just write, and trust the process. Eventually, you’ll produce something authentic. For myself, I always knew I had a writer in me trying to get out and inflict himself on the world. Doing a lot of other stuff, there was never time, except to write the odd snippet of random thought, or perhaps a poem if something captured my mind long enough that felt like it needed articulation. And it always felt reductive, unnecessary - like I wasn’t saying something that a greater mind hadn’t already articulated much better than I ever could. The real stuff, the pure drop…. it takes ages for it to distil. At your age - and now - i often felt callow, as you do. I think feeling that is a good sign if you don’t rip yourself apart by wallowing in that feeling, as it enshrines both a yearning to produce something of beauty, and the very necessary ruthlessness to not accept something substandard in the process. In the end, i did write a novel, (which came out of nowhere - prior, i hadn’t written anything over 5000 words except university essays), and it wasn’t half bad. The next one will be better.

I don’t believe you are a belletrist, a sycophant. I’ve met my share. They tend to be characterised by a surfeit of ego and a very shallow and narcissistic outlook. I think that you are possibly very depressed, and the thing with depression is, it has its own voice, and it can sound like yours, (or even God’s). Inarguable. I think that it’s your depression talking when you say these things. Be careful what you say about yourself - especially aloud, or in writing - not to mention in the safe space between your ears. Words have power to shape you. I wish you the very best as you grow into your gifts. You’re great, and you’re going to be greater.

Also - a beginner targets being able to play a tune at all, but a performer has to go very long way beyond this and be able to play it every time, any place, perfectly.
To achieve that you have to stay well within your comfort zone and only attempt to perform what you know you can do well, and defer trying to perform anything more difficult.
