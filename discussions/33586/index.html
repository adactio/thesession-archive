↳ Posted by Jim Dorans, JustAFiddler on March 25th, 2014.

Re: A very diverse, unique and interesting set of socio-cultural phenomena and enduring aesthetic movements.

Interesting discussion.

I got the impression that Harry Bradley is mainly leveling his criticism at journalists who use a kind of progressivist language to describe the ‘Gloaming’ and projects like it because it carries with it the presuppositions that Irish trad music (a) needs to be “going somewhere” (b) and that “stasis,” the appearance of not changing, innovating, is a bad thing. Caoimhin himself has clearly said things like this as well, and I disagree. Harry, quite rightly, says this is wrong aesthetic to apply to Irish music and entirely misses what it is and that indeed, it just “is.”

It’s this capitalist, industry-driven paradigm that demands “new” music, “innovative” music, “fresh” music, because you need more consumers, constant change. I mean, why does freakin’ Facebook keep changing the look of its page every couple years, as if “stasis” is bad (and I hate the newest incarnation, by the way)? This whole idea of evolution and so-called progress being absolutely necessary. Caoimhin obviously enjoys his weird, experimentalist music and I don’t think anyone minds that. Who cares? But what Harry does mind -- and me too -- is the way the media often frames work like Caoimhin’s with a dichotomous artifice of a static, boring tradition v. forward-thinking, progressive innovation. If that’s how you see Irish music, you ain’t getting what it’s really about.

↳ Posted by DrSilverSpear on March 25th, 2014.

Re: A very diverse, unique and interesting set of socio-cultural phenomena and enduring aesthetic movements.

Terms like “narrow minded” only convey anything if one knows the perspective of the person using them.

If A does not like something B likes B may describe A as narrow minded. If B doesn’t like it either they probably won’t use that term. If C likes several things that D has never heard of D may describe C’s tastes as “eclectic”, but probably wouldn’t if they were shared tastes.

Writers on musical tastes often forget this and get grief from people with different perspectives. It probably goes with the job.
