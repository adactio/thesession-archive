Cost of import

I’ve just bought something … about which more at a later date. The thing I’ve bought is absolutely fantastic, and I don’t want to associate it with anything negative …

I bought it from the States, and I live in the UK. Here, translated into dollars, is what I ended up paying:

$
‘Thing’ 200
Postage and insurance 30
Cost of money transfer via Western Union 42
Credit card charges (because my credit card
deemed the transfer via Western Union to be
a ‘cash advance’) 12
UK customs duties 40
Parcelforce charges (for them paying the
customs duties on my behalf, about which
I had no choice) 16
____
340
====

I’ve probably left some charges out …

This is the first time I’ve done this. If it weren’t for the fact that the ‘thing’ is totally brillliant, and I’ll probably have to get another ‘thing’ from the same place ( :-) ), I’d make a very different assessment of value now than before I got the ‘thing’.

In addition, btw, Parcelforce had my ‘thing’ for a whole week (it arrived on Tuesday 26th Feb) and took it on a tour round the UK, via 4 depots, some of them twice, before I finally caught up with it yesterday. Sheesh!

Is there a discussion here? Not really, just a rant. But I thought it might be useful for UK people wanting to buy and also, possibly, for US people wanting to sell (there must be some more efficient way).

Now, where’s that ‘thing’? ;-)

I’ve ordered stuff from Stewmac as well and it comes through pretty quick.
One thing that annoys me though is that the amount charged for postage by Stewmac doesn’t seem to get through to DHL. So when it comes to paying the customs duty DHL simply weigh the parcel and pay customs duty on what they would have charged for a package of that weight.
So, for example, let’s say your quoted $48.50 by Stewmac for shipping. You should pay 17.5% VAT on that at customs. But DHL don’t have the quoted amount at hand so pay customs of, say , £55, calculated from the weight of the package. They then ask you to reimburse them this amount. There’s quite a difference between 17.5% of $48.50 and 17.5% of $55, especially when it happens all the time (FedEx do the same).

Re: Cost of import

in my business, the general advice is to get the receiving end (ooh err) to organise the shipping.
The problem with letting the vendor or the sending party do it is that once they’ve consigned it, they have no responsibility. And they are often not well-versed in the peculiarities of local taxes and duties in the destination country. So you will rarely get a WYSIWYG quote, whatever they say.

One way around this is to speak to a local UK courier or freight forwarding firm (such as DHL) and ask them for a comprehensive quote price if they were to arrange pick-up in the USA. It invariably works out more efficient and often cheaper too. That’s what we do in the engineering business. ANd you have a local contact tracking it all the way
