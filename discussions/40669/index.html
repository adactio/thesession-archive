Is is possible to play whistle adeptly without having to take tremendous gasps when playing?

Added by Arthur Gondolor on April 16th, 2017

Is is possible to play whistle adeptly without having to take tremendous gasps when playing?

I find it ruins the immersion of the musical and its mystique when I watch players and the take those huge hoofing gulps of air during the tune.

I think playing should be graceful and seem effortless but those gasps to me seem remove the veil of illusion somewhat. Like watching a magician and noticing his sleight of hand.

Is circular breathing a technique that could ‘hide’ this ugly behavior?

↳ Posted by Arthur Gondolor on April 16th, 2017.

Re: Is is possible to play whistle adeptly without having to take tremendous gasps when playing?

You should not need to take ‘huge gasps’ to play the whistle. You have to breathe, of course, but if you are having to *gasp*, then you probably need to hone you breath control.

Circular breathing almost certainly can be used for playing the whistle (although I think it is probably easier for instruments that require more breath pressure). But I don’t think that breaking up the tune in order to breathe has to be ‘ugly’; done artfully, it is very muh a part of traditional whistle style.

↳ Posted by trish santer on April 17th, 2017.

Re: Is is possible to play whistle adeptly without having to take tremendous gasps when playing?

I agree with Trish, phrasing makes a huge difference. In most western music the phrase is 4 or 8 measures.

Like you, I do not like to listen to players that stick noisey breaths in just anywhere.

Some whistles are more economical of breath than others. I have a Burke and I can play 8 to 16 measures, depending on the tune.

Develope your breath control. Find a vocal coach that can teach you how to quickly fill your lungs all the way to the bottom. Learn to release that air slowly, keeping enough back to effectively complete the phrase. It all comes with practice. Just ask a soprano who sings Handelian roulades.

Be aware of your breathing. You can do this!

Actually you can take a breath during a phrase. As long as the breath (inhalation) is silent it doesn’t need to interrupt the flow of a phrase. Anything audible, or a hot mic, even too long stopping the sound, or accenting

You can do this by leaving a note out.

Re: Is is possible to play whistle adeptly without having to take tremendous gasps when playing?

When I first started learning flute I was listening to some of the more flowing players like Paddy Carty and Cathal McConnell, who (it seemed to me) were almost trying to hide (or at least de-emphasise) the fact that they had to take breaths. It seemed to me that most flute-players were trying to blend with fiddles and boxes, or, like Matt Molloy, mimic the pipes. (I’m not saying any of this is true, only that it was my belief at the time.)

So it was a revelation to hear fluteplayers from the 1920s who took the necessity of breathing and turned it into a powerful stylistic device, giving pulse and drive to the music. It’s typical of “traditional” instruments: the players take the unique quirks of each instrument and turn it to an advantage. (Rather that orchestral tradition which seeks tonal and stylistic homogeneity.)

That’s the way I strove to play on the flute, and that’s the style I most enjoy hearing.

Re: … why do they breath through the mouth and not the nose?

Arthur, for normal breathing you don’t need to fill the lungs. So inhaling through the nose is natural. To play wind instruments players will inhale through their mouth to fill the lungs and thus have the necessary air to continue blowing as long as needs be.

Re: Is is possible to play whistle adeptly without having to take tremendous gasps when playing?

You can take a lot more air in a lot faster through your mouth. The wider you open your mouth the less noise is made by the inhalation. Unfortunately the remorselessness and heavy ornamentation of a lot of modern Irish music makes finding a place to breath difficult and the longer you leave it the more air you need to take in so the more likely it is to make a noise. I usually play remorseless music on fiddle or mandolin and use my recorder for music with gaps in it or where I can leave notes out (as described somewhere above) without altering the nature of the tune.

As well as gasping if you leave it too long before you breath in you also have a greater percentage of carbon dioxide in the gas mixture you are breathing out and this actually alters the pitch of the note. Friends who play instruments with bellows explain patiently to me that my sub-standard breathing apparatus has been superseded by technology.

↳ Posted by Richard D Cook on April 22nd, 2017.

Re: Is is possible to play whistle adeptly without having to take tremendous gasps when playing?

Hopefully, by now, the original poster has determined that the answer is most definitely in the positive.

Circular breathing is not an option for the whistle or flute. These instruments are unusual among the woodwinds in that they are “flow-driven”, rather than “pressure driven”, like the reeds. Only pressure driven instruments are suited to circular breathing.

The trick with the flute or whistle is to take lots of sips of air, and not to wait until you are running out to grab a breath. The average listener should be quite unaware of your breathing.

Places to grab a sip include long notes,which can be played as:
- A, or
- A roll, or
- A..sip..A

It’s so easy. Go back and watch Mary Bergin sipping.
