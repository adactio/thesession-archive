Re: Why so many come up for re-sale…

Many people think that Olwell’s flute are indeed head and shoulders above the rest, as you (reluctantly) say. But there are a few makers in Olwell’s class and I think that, mutatis mutandi, Doyle is one of them; and it is in fact easier to play a Doyle flute than most Murray flutes. So fair play to Ketil.
Olwell flutes are not especially “costly.” I said as much in another thread. A few hundred dollars more than most, but a few hundred less than others. Had you done some research you’d know that.
The long wait is for the keyed Olwell. The wait for a keyless is only about a year. “Long wait” is relative. Have we not gone through this before?
Olwells come up for resale, I suspect, because many people buy an Olwell thinking that because it is “the best” it will be relatively easy to play traditional music on it. These people probably had a crappy flute to begin with and assumed that it was the flute that was holding them back. In some ways an Olwell is easier than others to play. But the flute isn’t an easy instrument to master. You can see the Olwells on the market as evidence that people don’t like them, as a market survey, so to speak. But then you’d be obtuse, wouldn’t you, in denying credence to the plethora of accomplished players who love Olwell flutes … which is the flip side of the market survey.

Olwell six-key new FOB about $3,600. Add about $1,000 for C/C# keys. $4,600 (€3,600) Six year wait. Keyless flutes sell new for about $1,500. One year wait. Occasionally Olwell puts a “special” flute on Ebay and it sells for a very high price.
