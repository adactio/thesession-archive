Learn from your student

Emotional Intelligence and ITM
If you are educating the next generation of musicians, I encourage you all to read into various aspects of emotional intelligence. When a child seeks the music out, independently of their parents, you know right there that you are dealing with a different animal all together. They are blessed in emotional intelligence and what is termed “fluid intelligence” opposed to “crystallized intelligence.” The child that is forced into the music will become good no doubt and can later on develop fluidity but I think ITM or whatever you call it has a history of not understanding emotionally intelligent children. After a grand exposure to the music through dance, festivals, locals, family parties…. The child becomes stirred emotionally and due to his or her high rate of self awareness, they react to the music and begin seeking it out. This is where you fit into the picture.

This child comes to the music from a very different place. When they play, they seem to get into a trance, a flow, or “the zone” as Jordan calls it. They are not trying to make their granny happy, they are making sense of a power that comes from within their self. Critique in general does not make any sense to them. They are answering to a higher order, in which, their brain tells them how the tune goes, not you. They may have no sense of your ideas of complicated techniques to make the music sound more flowery. They see a lot of that as your ego getting in the way of the dragon stirring within them. They might seek out those techniques later, in their own sense of time, but for you to force it (even with your good intentions) can actually hinder their style and or chase them away.

Competitions to them are misguided perceptions. We live in a world where “crystallized intelligence” is rewarded and competitions are a perfect example. The person who wrote the tune lived under a bridge poor and misperceived, 100 years later someone wins a competition with his tune and capitalizes on it with cds and the like. To raise the level of consciousness in ITM, we need to understand that most or all of our tunes come from “fluid” minds, people who can make sense of nothingness and put a sound to it. They are not playing “versions,” they are playing something that belongs to them and for all you know, you might have been misguided.

“Con-game” would imply a deliberate attempt to deceive. However, there are quite constructive criticisms of the concept. Here is one - the main argument against it being the lack of empirical evidence to support it (though it doesn’t present much in the way of empirical evidence to dismiss it) - the paper doesn’t completely write it off, nevertheless:

http://uc.academia.edu/GeraldMatthews/Papers/72499/Seven_Myths_About_Emotional_Intelligence

To compare it to Ron Hubbard’s bunkum is going a tad too far - you wouldn’t find much in the way of ‘dianetics’ being advocated in the BMJ, for example:

http://careers.bmj.com/careers/advice/view-article.html?id=20001646

Note the point that “emotional intelligence” is said to be an extension of an older concept of “social intelligence”.

And Goleman is co-founder of CASEL, which is taken seriously enough:

http://casel.org/publications/the-educators-guide-to-emotional-intelligence-and-academic-achievement-social-emotional-learning-in-the-classroom/

Moreover, there has been further research by CASEL since the Matthews et al paper cited above:
