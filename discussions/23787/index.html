And I thought I knew What I was Talking About

Session, from the same word as ‘sit’. So is ‘sediment’, or dregs.
Dot - a small pile of something useless.
Organisation, from a word closely related to ‘work’.
Personal, from ‘per sona’ a mask with a hole in it to let the sound through.
Computer, originally to make clean, later to become clear, hence to think. (As distinct from ‘calculator’, little stones used for counting.)
Screen, a pile of wood.
Lute, from ‘al Ud’, wood or stick. As opposed to ‘Flute’, which means blow, as in ‘flatulence’. (Honest, I’m not making these up)
Thread, from throw, originally to whirl or twist, as in throw a pot.
Post. Originally to put, thence a stake in the ground, thence a military station, whence a public letter carrier.
Transpose, same as post: put across. But not ‘Compose’ which originally meant to lay eggs, then to rest, or pause. (Puzzle is ‘opposal’ two things put together.)
Music, from muse, obviously. But ‘musing’ comes from the same word as muzzle, taken from a dog with its nose in the air trying to pick up a scent.
Clef is key, we know. But the bone your fiddle rests on is your ‘little key’
Traditional, from the same word as ‘date’ (a given point in time), means given across, or delivered.
Folk, allied to ‘flock’, from the word for crowd.
Feck, same as ‘effect’. As in ‘I used to be feckless, but now I’m fecked’. (OK I made that last bit up.)
Lurk, originally to ‘listen’, later ‘lie in wait’.
Chanter is from chant, to whine like a beggar.
As opposed to canter, a short form of ‘Canterbury Gallop’, supposedly the speed of Chaucer’s pilgrims.
Whistle comes from the same word as ‘wheeze’, and ‘hiss’.
Fiddle, banjo, piano, I think have been mentioned before; but if you have any arcane (from arc, box, hence known only to a select few) bits of trivia (three streets) relating to your instrument, maybe you might share them.

The polka craze of the 1800s was sufficiently intense to inspire manufacturers to append “polka” to the name of a wide variety of completely unrelated products in an attempt to capitalize on polka-mania. There were several items of clothing and even food labeled “polka” at the time, much as the prefix “cyber” was slapped on everything from TV news shows to dog food in the mid-1990s. Thus the fabric pattern of “polka dots” was born. Unless one is referring to sheet music for the Green Cottage set, in which case expect a bilious missive of remonstrance from one Mr. Gill. Some authorities believe that “polka” may actually be a corruption of the Czech word “pulka” (”half”), referring to the short half steps involved in the dance.

dal lat. COMPUTARE = calcolare, contare, valutare
comp. di CUM (con=with) e PUTARE (calcolare, fare i conti=to calculate e anche nettare, potare=to clean, to cut)
a) Calcolare, fare il conto di qualche cosa (to reckon, to calculate, to work out, to calculate)
b) Comprendere in un calcolo, tener conto di qualche cosa (to include, to take into account)
