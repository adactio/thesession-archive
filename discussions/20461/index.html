Tunes like the classic big jigs, the Humours of Ballyloughlin, the Gold Ring etc are called pipe tunes, and may well have been more associated with the pipes than the fiddle and flute in the past. But not any more. They are simply great tunes, great pipe tunes, great fiddle tunes and great flute tunes.

And this is where the grouping if pipes flute and fiddle is special. Most of the articulations are identical, and the ones that aren’t are damn close.

But they make great banjo tunes and box tunes too. They even make great guitar tunes.

Re: Pipe tunes on the fiddle…

Davydd - A student of mine took up Highland pipes and joined a pipe band. The band distributed pages with such info as ornament names and notations, and pipe tunes written in traditional (but primitive) music notation with ornaments.

Search out a pipe band that will give you such info. You will notice that 1: the Highland pipes has 9 notes (plus some advanced notes such as C natural), 2: the tunes are written mostly in A or D while the pipes sound in Bb and Eb half a step higher, 3: no key signature or accidentals are written, 4: Timing of the ornaments is crucial, however notation of ornaments is not exact and cannot be understood or played without aural and oral instruction.

Together, these mean that the pipes will play different versions of tunes than fiddles, because fiddles have more notes available. Pipe tunes may be in different keys than heard on or written for fiddle. Ornaments for pipes lay under the fingers different than typical ornaments for fiddles.

Highland pipes were formerly tuned in A, then changed to Bb. But the notation didn’t change, because at the time everyone was used to it. You may find a piper who has an A chanter and can play in A, D, and other fiddle-friendly keys. My student immediately got one (the first in this area, perhaps the first in a wide area) so he could play with us on the street. He has become an excellent traditional piper with various types of pipes, as well as a modern piper playing all sorts of musics.

The most important thing about musicmaking is Time. This is most evident in pipe bands, where playing exactly together is paramount and a small mistake makes a huge difference.
Learn the timing of ornaments from a competent piper. These are usually not difficult to play, but are either correctly played or not.

Best wishes - vlnplyr

You’re right there Ionannas, it’s not entirely the case, there are some ornaments that work fine on other instruments. A couple of the shorter doublings/crans work well on the fiddle.
