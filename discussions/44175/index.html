Re: Fire breathing reel sets

Fire breathing reels. Mmmm. I’m assuming you’re after something that delivers the “Paganini of trad.”
There are basically 2 approaches to this “fire breathing” virtuoso style. The first is to play something straightforward really well at speed, with some nice super fast extra ornamentation to “spice it up”. The advantage of this is your guitarist/banjo players know the chords and can provide a nice supporting accompaniment.
The other option is to play Skinner reels, such as Bride’s reel and Baker’s reel at a cracking speed and then cleverly intersperse the reels with a quick difficult slip jig, like Drops of Brandy, then straight back to a final reel to finish. A seamless, perfectly executed change of time signature and rhythmic feel, creates great excitement in your audience and really holds their attention.
However, I still think it’s better to play something more straightforward, really well. Good luck with the fire breathing “dragon” reels.!

Re: Fire breathing reel sets

Taking the effort to write out a response that only explains why you, personally, do not like the thing OP is looking for, doesn’t seem very nice to me. People enjoy music in different ways; some people play for the joy of playing together with others in a relaxed, non-forced atmosphere. Others play for the “sport” of seeing how far they can push themselves without tripping. Most people do a bit of both, at different times. Don’t judge.

My two cents then: recordings with the “fire-breathing” energy you describe get that quality for maybe 20% from the composition, the other 80% from the execution. If you play it right, you can make pretty much any standard session tune sound like that. Are there a few naturally syncopated notes in the tune? Play them that way. If not, create one or two. Really master the ornamentation, and if there’s other melody players, try and play the same ornamentation at the same time without sounding “forced” - it makes the entire tune much smoother. Make sure your backer is familiar with the tune. One or two long notes that are emphasised as such by the backing can make things a lot more interesting. Don’t be afraid to mix up the harmonies either - while this is not always a good idea at a session (where it can confuse people, or interfere with the harmonies played by others), for performances it’s one of the most important ways to create suspense in a tune.
