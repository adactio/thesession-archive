Re: ADG three row for Irish jigs and reels

You can play good Irish music on any old instrument. But it helps to be musical and to be the kind of person who doesn’t mind figuring things out for himself. You could make lovely Irish music on an ADG. But you’d be pretty much ploughing your own furrow.

Every system of button box involves tradeoffs - pros and cons.

Downsides of ADG or any 3-row:
a) bigger and heavier than an equivalent 2-row.
b) much harder, if not totally impractical, to play in keys without sharps
c) even tunes with sharps in the key signature, if they contain lots of accidentals, will be difficult (if you have accidentals on an ADG they’ll be in awkward places)

Upsides:
a) you’ll have a lot of duplicate notes across the rows, so if you put the time into learning to play ADG as 3-rows are meant to be played, you’ll have lots of phrasing choices, allowing great fluidity if that’s what you like. The downside of this upside is that learning to master all the possibilities will take time and can be confusing.

Up to you. I think I’d enjoy messing about with an ADG, if one fell into my lap. But if I were in the position of buying a box to play Irish music on, I’d choose a semitone job (C#/D or B/C). The best thing you can do is try to get one or more systems on loan - maybe from a shop - and experiment.

You might just find that the push-pull system drives you mad. The annals of online forums are littered with evidence of people who thought they’d give the button box a try and only lasted a few weeks or months.

Here’s a page of keyboard layouts: http://info.melodeon.net/index.php/page,keyboard_3_row.html I looked at these and also layouts of bigger European 3 rows for sale at the Button Box website and decided it would solve the problems I’d been having; maybe that would be of interest. http://www.buttonbox.com/instruments-in-stock.html#3row

The Button Box still has a Saltarelle Cheviot ADG for sale. One thing about this box is that it has low C and G as per the fiddle. The Hohners have low A, B, and C#, but no low C or G, so if you want those you’ll need to have some notes retuned. There’s also no Bb in the 2nd octave, which crops up in various Gmin Irish/Scottish tunes.

The cross rowing has tremendous potential; it doesn’t seem much more baffling than working out things on the C#/D, where a bit like the 2nd part of Gillian’s Apples really takes a bit of careful bellowing. On the ADG you can press or draw for the whole thing and take a nap. ;) You have about 4 different ways of playing this phrase, I think; including playing it with a bellows change on every note, too.

I’m having a Castagnari Rik made - ADG, LM reeds. Bit over $4k USD. These 3 row boxes built by Italian/French makers for the continental European market include those low notes standard, contrary to what the page of layouts from melodeon.net suggests. There are lots of Hohners and then medium range brands like Dino Baffetti. My ADG was a bit over $700, it’s from the 1960s. Incidentally make sure the buyer actually knows what pitch he’s selling - what was listed as ADG on eBay turned out to actually be in Bb/Eb/Ab! But I like it enough I didn’t complain, lucky for him. It’s good for playing along with Cooley, De Dannannnnn, Mac Mahon, etc.

A local shop had a GCF Hohner I could noodle on too; perhaps your local dealer has one too; or you might have a norteno player in your area who’d let you do some test driving. All of these 3 row “quint” boxes are the same, just the overall pitch changes, like how you have tin whistles in Eb-D-C-Bb-etc.; and of course the difference in layout between manafacturers I described above; but even there all of these are basically 3 melodeons in one handy frame, with the differences being at the top and bottom of the scale.

Re: ADG three row for Irish jigs and reels

Kevin is one of those casualties of the button box I alluded to. If you take a semitone box there are a couple of things you have to realize.

1) You have very little choice regarding phrasing - at least as far as bellows changes are concerned. If you’re a fiddle player, forget about phrasing passages the way you’d like to on fiddle with the bow. It ain’t a fiddle, it ain’t a flute and it certainly isn’t a piano accordion. It’s what it is. If you don’t like the way it sounds in the hands of good players, choose something else. Resistance is futile - go with the flow. Rewire your brain.

2) Bellows changes are your friend. The soul of the push-pull box. If you don’t like them, stick with the piano accordion. If they are your enemy, it’s because you haven’t learned to use the bellows properly. Play a whole phrase on the draw and take a nap? Your listeners will be yawning too. ;-)

Re: ADG three row for Irish jigs and reels

Some other things you can do with an ADG or the like:

Choice of grace notes. On the C#/D if you’re gracing with the next button up on the D row for a high A you get an oh-so-abrupt high D for a cut. This is something I might do while playing the pipes for a laugh/to wake someone up with/various types of p*ss-taking; on the box it’s “the sound.” Oh, you could reach over to the C# row for a C#, much better. Or as we liked to say back in the 1980s: Not! ;)

ADG lets you choose your grace note, including a B grace for that high A. Or a G in the 3rd octave. In your Ear! I see that B/C doesn’t have this bleeding eardrums aspect to it, with B gracing A on the pull. Huh, no high D at all on the B/C.

Double stops: You can hold down, say, a high E on the D row and play a unison high E on the G, to cite just one example. Stare at the ADG keyboard layout for more ideas if you like.

“Melodeon magic”: On the D row the notes B-C#-D have an awesome punch; on the ADG you have the same on the G row for E-F#-G, albeit it’s higher pitched so you don’t quite hear the air move like on the D row. I’m finding myself playing bits of tunes in G on both rows a lot, to maximize this punchy quality.

Bass button freedom: Being able to play long passages either on the push or pull you can obviously play most anything you want on the basses, as opposed to having to arrange them to suit what the treble side happens to be doing. One fellow on this board cited this as a drawback, as it would allow the player to play all through the tune…

Some more caveats: ADG and other quints are 12 bass standard, that obviously adds cost - the Italian companies’ 3 rows listed at Button Box are about $2K USD more than the comparable 2 row Irish jobs. As regards bulk I haven’t weighed my Hohner but doesn’t seem any more heavy than my C#/D Weltmeister.

Also, these Hohners are tuned “tremolo Americano,” which means pretty wet. Mine is MMM, I taped off two banks of reeds for learning, so you can take care of that problem if so desired.

A final note on the D/G - A/D/G question. I feel there is a disproportionate amount of noise being made in favour of these systems - by people who (AFAIK) aren’t accomplished players of Irish music on them (well not yet anyway).

As I said, yes you can play lovely Irish music on a D/G or an ADG. There are certain advantages to them, but I feel these are outweighed by the limitations. If you happen to have one, learn on it, you can do great things with it.

If you don’t have a box yet, choose one of the mainstream Irish systems. B/C and C#/D are the stable end result of decades of experimentation with various systems. 50,000 Irish box players are unlikely to have got it wrong.

“Decades of experimentation,” I dunno about that. In the playing, sure, but Stephen Chambers’s investigations revealed a lot about where the boxes originally arrived from, i.e., Scotland, via the Wyper Brothers. Their fan Michael Grogan bought a B/C, then Paddy O‘Brien, then Burke. So semitone boxes have been the norm for 80 years now. Mostly I’d chalk that up to musicians’ natural conservatism; that, and that chicken/egg phenomenon of instructors, who teach the system they know, leading to a self-perpetuating orthodoxy. Wouldn’t a 2 1/2 row box with DG and accidentals have done the job just as well? Perhaps the semitone boxes were cheaper thus more marketable in Ireland.

Incidentally a solution to the problem with the bad location of the Fnat/Eb key on the ADG occurred to me - a long key above the G row, between it and the bellows, with the touch in the middle of the box’s body, that would press the button for you. Maybe use brass U section material for a body, affix it to the body of the accordion with hide glue, which is soluble in water - it’s what violins use, in case you need to repair something afterwards. Install a rod inside that, there are various ways of springing such keys, flutes sometimes sport such mile-long keys. A pipemaking friend of mine could whip something together elegant for me, for the Hohner I might just throw something together on my own. Asking Casta for an extra button would no doubt cost a mint more.
