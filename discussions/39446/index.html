Sharing instruments at a session

I mostly play flute and whistle.

I really don’t like it when people ask to play my instruments and then they look all insulted when I say no. I know they’re beautiful handmade instruments but geez, I put my mouth on those things. Please don’t even ask, ever. It really makes things awkward.

I’ve been trying to come up with a good standard response that says “no” but isn’t as blunt as “no”. So far “no” seems to be the best one. I suppose I could always lie and say “no, I have foot and mouth disease” or “no, I heard you have foot and mouth disease”.

Thoughts?

Re: Sharing instruments at a session

I have a sly answer that I kind of stumbled upon. I say, “Sure, but please don’t change the position of the head joint.” Nearly all flute players will want to twist the head joint one way or the other and, if you play with the embouchure hole adjusted up or outward as I do, this tends to dissuade borrowers without your having to say no. If you don’t want to chance it and “no” seems too brusque, lead in with something like, “I make it a policy not to do that. Sorry.” Nothing terse or rude about that and no further explanation is needed. If one is asked for, I submit you are off the hook to try to be more than courteous, as opposed to accommodating.

Truth be told, though, I have no issue with hygiene (no less so than a kiss, what?), so if I like, know, respect, or wish to encourage/help the person asking, they can try my flute.

I still have nightmares about one of the late-night sessions at Celtic Connections, where beautiful guitars were being treated like “pass the parcel” by a crowd of raucous blokes who came in without their own instruments, and just expected (didn’t ask) to borrow from anyone who had one. They were guitar thrashers: a drunken Norwegian got mine, and somewhat predictably, broke a string after a few minutes of thrashing. I told them to leave it alone, I’d put it away and re-string it at home, but no, they had it down on the floor to the get the broken string out, and re-string it, hammering and banging away - even more horror.
I also had my melodeon with me: left it tucked away in a corner (hidden, I thought) while I went to the loo: came back to find someone who patently could NOT play it having a go, and extracting some totally tuneless series of notes out of it.
Meantime, the session host looked on, shrugged her shoulders, rolled her eyes and did nothing. Never took my good guitar there again, and hung on to my melodeon throughout.
