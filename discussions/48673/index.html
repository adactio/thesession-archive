Recommendations for transition between Boehm and simple system flute

After yearning for one for ages, I finally got a 6-keyed simple system flute. I have played trad music on a Boehm for many years and I have been very excited about this moment. However, the experience so far has been… underwhelming. Or rather (to recognize that the problem is not the instrument but the player), I’m finding the transition much harder than anticipated. I knew it was not going to be easy but it feels like I’m learning a totally new instrument and it is very frustrating.

To be sure, I’m very much used to playing on holes. I played the pipes for a very long time so, if anything, the fingering and the way to do ornamentation feel more natural. That is, except for the keys: I can see the logic behind them but the fingerings with keys feel very awkward. Also, for a flute that is subtantially shorter than my Boehm, the location of the holes creates a good amount of discomfort for me — in both hands! I will say that I have now even more respect for the masters of the instrument.

But I’m not here to complain. I know that the issue is that I underestimated the difficulty of learning a new type of flute so I am actually interested in advice from those who play both Boehm and silver flute. In particular, I have two questions:

One — I bought a second-hand flute so maybe the problem is that the default measurements from this maker are not ideal for me. For instance, I’m noticing that the G# key “feels” too close to my ring finger while the long F natural is too far. Same with the B flat key — the way I position my left hand, it requires a substantial movement for my thumb to go from, say, A to B flat. But perhaps that’s where the keys need to be for a player with hands my size and I just need to work harder to reach them. So my question is, what should I be looking at to decide whether the problem is the length of the keys in my particular instrument?

Two, regardless of whether the problem is my specific flute, are there specific exercises that you recommend to get used to the simple system? Yes, “playing tunes” is always good advice but I know it’s going to be more efficient if I spend a little bit of time during each practice working on technique. I think I’m thinking here about note changes that it’s worth spending some additional time on — specially considering that sometimes you have a couple of different options to play, say, C natural.

Eventually I’ll have more questions about improving embochure and tone but let’s start with the basics!

Heh heh, I faced this journey too, back in 1974. I started it by putting the Boehm flute away forever, vowing to muscle it out. It proved a long journey, because it was guided by ignorance. I struggled to adapt my Boehm experience to the 19th century flute I had picked up in London. I would have done much better to abandon the Boehm experience and research how the 19th century players held and blew their flutes. I have done that since, and successfully made the transition and reaped the rewards. Can I invite you to look at this article and the others linked from it to see if it will help you too.

https://www.mcgee-flutes.com/Getting_the_hard_dark_tone.htm

Given you are finding the keys difficult to manage, you might even want to start with some of the linked articles that talk about how to hold these flutes. You will be surprised how different the 19th century approach was, but very gratified when you get it! Freedom to operate any of the keys, greater flexibility of all the fingers, and a tone that people comment on.

Heh heh, I’m suddenly reminded of a visit I made to the flute-physics lab at the University of New South Wales. I arrived as they all sat down for morning tea. They were all experienced Boehm flute players, but no experience of our flutes. I showed them my flute, then lashed into a tune. “Shit it’s loud”, gasped one of the Professors, spilling his tea as he rocked back into his chair.

Re: Recommendations for transition between Boehm and simple system flute

I wonder if the problems with the placement of the keys (and key-blocks) might be specific to that flute.

My first flute, in the 1970s, was a keyless “Irish flute”, but soon I graduated to an antique c1830 Rudall & Rose flute.

Being that this was in no way an “Irish flute” but built for professional orchestral use, intended for all the keys to be in constant use, every key was perfectly ergonomic. How could it be otherwise? Obviously professional flutists who used the key work fulltime wouldn’t have anything even 1mm out of the most facile position possible.

But that flute was built for the world of 1830. Two centuries on, it’s a different reality. We’ve had, what, a half-century of makers creating dedicated “Irish flutes” built specifically for players some of whom never use the keys, some only every now and then.

For some makers keyless flutes are standard, only added by customer request from time to time.

Yes most of the makers who put keys on their “Irish flutes” are basing their work (more or less) on antique flutes, but I’ve picked up keyed “Irish flutes” with oddly placed keys.

Also sometimes the keywork on “Irish flutes” is very stiff, not the light-touch keywork on mid-19th century orchestral flutes.

Re: Recommendations for transition between Boehm and simple system flute

You’ve received excellent advice from the previous posters. Mine will be lass advice than an account of what I’ve done myself. My background includes playing the silver flute for many years although not for the last 10 or so and also playing the baroque flute for many years. So I’m very used to using the the Eb key. I’ve played keyless wooden flutes now for many years and have recently (about a year ago) acquired a Sam Murray flute with 5-keys (no long F nat). The keys are very stiff as Richard Cook has noted. I find descending notes very difficult, when multiple fingers have to be used to go just a half-step or a whole-tone. Here is what I have done to try to make the transition (although I still play my keyless flutes regularly):
(1) take the time to go through each note to see what is the best fingering, both for tone and intonation. For the first two octaves, this means seeing if depressing the Eb key improves or ruins the tone and intonation or makes no difference. Also learning about the different c naturals (key, -xx ---- -, and -x-xxx (x) for the first one; etc. And carefully studying the various fingering charts that are available for the 3rd octave notes and noting which fingering comes closest to being good.
(2) practice chromatic scales in two octaves
(3) practice scales in C, F, Bb, Eb, Ab, G, D, A, E including the 3rd octave some of the time.
(4) play simple etudes from the 19th century, e.g. those of Gariboldi
Most of this will not be needed for trad music. It is just to help you be comfortable with the keys.
Finally start playing those tunes where you’ve been frustrated with your keyless flute. For example, the F naturals in the second part of Chief O’Neill’s hornpipe. Some F naturals are easy to half-hole on a keyless flute but others are not easy at all. I always found G# difficult on the Boehm flute, and it is no easier on the simple system flute, so work on tunes that use it. I didn’t find the Bb difficult, but you may. So, again, work on common tunes that use it, e.g. Paddy Fahey’s well-known jig. Work on the 2nd octave Cs in tunes that use them.
This all takes time, and to honest, is not very rewarding, at least for me. I don’t do any of this every day -- may be only every 4th day or so. But I am able to hit those few chromatic notes that I need in sessions now, so the regime has worked. Chet

Re: Recommendations for transition between Boehm and simple system flute

“I always found G# difficult on the Boehm flute”, note that G# key on modern flutes is not how mr.boehm designed/intended it to be. it is inverted, causing an unwanted irregularity in the fingering. the F/F# irregularity is unavoidable (shift between key of D and key of C), the Ef irregularity (together with the C foot) was copied/carried over verbatim from the old-system (“irish”) flutes. other than that, a boehm flute plays exactly like a tin whistle, “close one finger at a time to get the next note”, but chromatically and in more keys. German/Bavarian design at it’s best.

“world of 1830”, back then, people were generally of a shorter stature (smaller), i am not sure how this translated to arm and finger length, but it could be that flutes made for an average 1823 person would not be quite right for an average 2023 person. and I would not be surprised if back then some flute were “made to measure” of specific individual customer.

“exercises”, yup, double clutching, manual starter, etc vs uttering “tesla, take me to nearest mcdonalds”.

(fascinating stuff, mandolins and other stringed instruments are so boring in comparison, here is your pick, here is your frets and strings, go at it, good luck!)

Re: Recommendations for transition between Boehm and simple system flute

Those are great questions, Asoenga. Good suggestions from several people.

“are there specific exercises that you recommend to get used to the simple system?”

One recommendation is playing scales and arpeggios (D E F# G, A G F3 E, D d F# A D, E F# G A, etc…)

Perhaps less boring than scales: Look for rock-star tunes that repeatedly present your problem fingerings. I found the B part of Wise Maid to be tangly for my fingers, so I concentrated on it. Similarly,
- Working up Imelda Rolands at speed,
- Pigeon on the Gate for pedaling/jumps and rolls/crans.
- Palmers Gate in F# minor to practice G#.
- Brown Coffin in G-min a la Martin Hayes to practice Eb, F Nat,

“I’m noticing that the G# key “feels” too close to my ring finger while the long F natural is too far. Re: Recommendations for transition between Boehm and simple system flute

“world of 1830, back then, people were generally of a shorter stature (smaller), I am not sure how this translated to arm and finger length, but it could be that flutes made for an average 1823 person would not be quite right for an average 2023 person."

I’ll have to disagree on two counts.

One, in 1830 nearly all professional orchestra musicians were male. Two centuries on, female flutists are as common as male. On average females have smaller hands that males. Thus one would think, everything else being equal, that modern orchestral flutes would be designed for smaller hands, not larger.

Two, I was talking to an expert on ancient Rome who told me that the widely-held belief that Romans two thousand years ago were shorter than modern people is incorrect. A study was done taking measurements from a large number of ancient Roman skeletons which showed that they averaged the same height as the people of Rome today.

Since 19th century flute evolution was driven mostly by German, French, and English flutists it would be interesting to see if these regions have had significant height increases over the last 200 years.

Re: Recommendations for transition between Boehm and simple system flute

There are advantages and disadvantages to both systems.

Clearly, players like Joanie Madden demonstrate that a Boehm flute can be used for traditional Irish music with appropriate adaptation of technique.

I used to be a Boehm classical player, but switched to simple system for traditional Irish music and gave up playing my Boehm flute entirely.

For me the biggest challenge when I first started playing wooden flute probably 30 years ago was sorting out a grip that would work with my smallish hands.

I finally settled on a modified pipers grip, which fixed a lot of problems on my left hand, the ring finger reach is too far and actually painful for me if I use a standard grip. I’ve never felt like it was a bad choice.

So, when first transitioning from Boehm to simple system flute, don’t feel like you have to hold the instrument any specific way just because someone else tells you you “must” hold it a certain way or you see players in YouTube videos using a specific grip.

Figure out what works best for you and allows you the most flexibility without causing any pain or risk of tendonitis or other issues.

Re: Recommendations for transition between Boehm and simple system flute

I’m no expert. I suspect it’s an individual journey for each and every one of us. I am a modern Boehm flute player with a closed g# key for 42 years. I played whistles and recorder 46 years. I got my first keyless flute approximately 25 years ago and my first 6 key flute only 5 months ago. I love my 6 key but it is more of a challenge then I first imagined… I’m still getting to grips with it now. But enjoying every minute. For what it’s worth this has been my approach with a couple of tips i’ve found.

1: If you have a two piece body to your flute rather than a single piece. Experiment with not aligning the finger holes up in a straight line. I personally found that moving them ever so slightly offset gave me extra space between the G# key and the long F natural key.

2:Not all flutes are the same, I luckily got to try a number of different makers flutes before settling for what ‘felt’ right for me. I even ended up purchasing two flutes both really good makers but found that on one of them the third finger hole on the left hand (G) was considerably more of a stretch on one than the other, giving me hand cramps unless I completely modified my hand positions. Look up pipers grip if you are finding it a stretch on your left hand.

3:Practice scales slowly. All of the scales,the like you would on Boehm flute, eventually you create new muscle memory for the simple flute. I agree with all the other advise before mine in so far as keys around D and G are easiest… probably why Mozart flute concertos were in D and G :-)… then try keys around A finguring, the G# addition I found compariable once i’d made the slight change in putting the flute together. I’m still finding playing in scales and modes around F challenging… this is where I’m ‘fighting’ the habitual fingering from Boehm flute… it’s getting there but is slow and I have to remind myself to be kind and enjoy the process :-)

4:Where there are multiple fingerings for the same note… practice your scales using all of them. When playing tunes it usually becomes apparent which fingering is easiest, to practice them all in scale form just gives you options

All the best in your journey

Coming back to this post.

I tried a few different simple system flutes in the last 6 months and I was almost ready to give up. They all felt uncomfortable no matter what I did to adapt my posture. Consistently, the keys were the worst part -- they always required some awkward contorting and I couldn’t see myself playing anything fluently with them. And, what do you know, the problem turned out to be very simple: I have small hands. Never even thought of that! I have always played wind instruments without any issue before so I guess it never crossed my mind. I worked with a luthier (Tony Milliard) who very quickly diagnosed the issue, suggested some modifications and built something that is simply perfect for me. Terribly happy with the result. Lesson learned: always work with professionals!
