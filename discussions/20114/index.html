Re: Trying to buy an Irish flute…

Celtic marine is absolutely right, there’s no way you’ll get a good Irish flute with all those keys for $300. You’ll barely get a good Irish flute with no keys for that little. The Casey Burns folk flute is a good way to go for a non-expensive good quality Irish flute. You’ll be able to play in D, G, E minor, B minor, and you can even pull off A and C if you’re good at half-covering the holes. You should consider learning the whistle first, as most “I want to learn the Irish flute” aspirers are told; it’s a sound piece of advice because whistles are much cheaper and you can learn the fingerings/techniques and decide if it’s for you before you run out and spend a bunch of money on a flute. Of course you can jump right into flute if you really want to, it’s just a suggestion!
Good luck!

Re: Trying to buy an Irish flute…

If you have a keyless flute in D (the bog standard Irish flute), you’ll be able to play nearly anything. With cross fingering and half holing, you can play in any key…just some are harder than others. However, 95% of all Irish music is in keys that are easy to play on a keyless flute.

You do not want any flute other than a key of D flute to start out on. I’d suggest the Forbes, a delrin Copley, or a Sweettheart keyless Irish flute.

Unless you like tin whistle, although the fingering is the same, it’s drastically different to play a transverse style vs end blown flute. If you want to play flute, and that’s the sound you really like, just get a flute.

Eric

Re: Trying to buy an Irish flute…

Mark Minkler- a good maker.

The standard keyless Irish flute in D plays tunes in D major, G major, E minor, B minor, plus other modes used in Irish music like E dorian, A dorian, D mixolydian, and A mixolydian.

For those other keys you’ll probably end up doing what most Irish flute players do, and that is have a bag of whistles to cover all the different keys:
C whistle for C major and F major
B flat whistle for B flat major and E flat major
A whistle for A major and D major (gets you lower notes in the key of D than a D whistle or flute which is sometimes handy)
E whistle for E major and A major

Note that all Irish whistles and flutes play two major scales, one based off the fundamental (“six finger note”) and one based off the fourth (the “three finger note”). This is because the top hole plays both the sharp and the flat upper 7th depending on fingering.

As people have said, good choices are Casey Burns and M&E.

The Sweetheart (Ralph Sweet) basic plain Irish D flute in maple is the most bang for the buck, but it will probably not perform to the level of the Casey Burns basic flute.

So if you go with one of the inexpensive flutes mentioned, and inexpensive Generation whistles in C and B flat, you can cover most of the keys you need for around $500.
