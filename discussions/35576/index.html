Re: Pitch Corrected Molloy, Brady, Peoples

You can sometimes do a little forensic snooping if there is any hum on the recordings, which there sometimes is. Record a quiet section to Audacity or some other DAE, select the section and then use the Plot Spectrum option (in the Analyze drop down) to interrogate the spectrum. Set the spectrum “size” to the maximum to enhance the resolution. If the hum partials come out at the relevant power line frequency or a multiple, the recording is probably recorded at correct speed.

I’ve used that to correct archival recordings where the recording machine speed was felt not to be accurate. That was before the availability of DAEs. I used a sharply tuned filter to isolate and amplify the hum, and an oscilloscope set up in lissajous mode to compare it with current mains frequency.

The recording in the video above has quite audible hum which is very obvious in the spectrum. It should not have originated in the studio, but this is television, so it might well have!

Re: Pitch Corrected Molloy, Brady, Peoples

Just for interest, bearing in mind all the points of view in this discussion, I used Audacity to “slow the turntable” on this recording by approx 415/440, ie a bit slower and pitch down by about a semitone.

To my mind a big improvement, much more natural sounding. Whilst there’s absolutely no question that these masters could have played at the speed on the CD or faster if they had so chosen, the slow-down has taken out the slightly frantic edginess of the original and for me, has made the recording more enjoyable to listen to.

I’m sure some people would disagree, but try it and see what you think.

Re: Pitch Corrected Molloy, Brady, Peoples

Maybe this will be controversial and maybe not but with what this thread has become I am inclined to make my opinion known and see what bounces back.
I believe that the recording is clearly sped up because of the way it feels. That group of musicians can pretty much play any speed they want and when they do play as fast as the tracks on this particular recording (from other sources) I believe it has a completely different rhythmic feel - it feels more natural. The tracks on this recording have a rhymically mechanical feel IMO. When musicians speed up their tempo it’s not a direct 1:1 increase. There is interior stretching or retraction within the rhythm. I think the main component of this is that the swing of the rhythm tends to straighten out. Let me point out that this is not a criticism - I love the recording. But this is the thought that makes me want to test my theory and slow the recording down (based upon my speculations) and see if it feels like I believe they would have made the music feel at the time. It’s possible that I’m just bent but I’m enjoying the whole thought experiment.
