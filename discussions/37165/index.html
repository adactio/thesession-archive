Respected Music.

There has been some pretty amazing music created in the world. Timeless classics that were masterfully composed, and have been expressed in the most beautiful of ways. But sometimes, these works of art are so difficult, they require a lot of experience and skill to tackle. Not only that, but because of the level of emotion and passion put into them, we need more than just technical skill, but also an understanding of what the composer was saying.

Out of respect, not many people attempt to perform these pieces, because they know without a shadow of doubt that they wouldn’t be able to do them justice. Others however, are bold and brazzen enough to go after these pieces and if not skilled in the ways of articulation and expression, leave the pieces butchered, defiled, desecrated.

Slow airs and laments are especially treated with care and caution when choosing to perform them, as they are naturally sacred and full of emotion. Heartfelt, painful, and most times very serious. But I wonder, are there any tunes that have that same sentiment and passion put into them? Tunes that you personally won’t even attempt to learn because you don’t feel you could ever do them justice?

I feel like “The Morning Thrush” has this quality. It’s definitely the most “lovely” reel I’ve ever heard. Any others?

Re: Respected Music.

If you stir up any emotions while you’re playing any kind of music, then go for it and enjoy these feelings. Who cares what the composer was saying? He created a piece of music and sent it out into the world to be cherished and enjoyed by everyone in their own way. If my butchering The Morning Thrush makes me happy, there is noone and nothing that can or should stop me from enjoying this happiness. Should I torture others with my rendition of this beautiful tune, on the other hand, is another question - one, though, which should be asked with any tune we play in public. We have, after all, the capacity to “defile” any tune with poor playing.

And then, when we started out playing, we butchered pretty much every single tune. Should we not have kept butchering them in order to improve over time just to spare their composer’s feelings? Or should we single out “ugly” tunes for our initial butchering until we’re worthy enough to play beautiful tunes?

If you keep working at The Morning Thrush, you will do the tune justice probably rather sooner than later, because in your heart you already cherish this tune so much. Just pour your heart into it. And this feeling and all the emotions of the greatness of this and other tunes are - at least for me - the biggest motivation for working on them and playing them. Not to play them necessarily with others or in public - although I also want to share the beauty of these tunes and my feelings about them with others -, but for my own well-being, so to speak.

And when you’ve mastered the tune to some extent and play it for others - isn’t the enjoyment you bring to others enough reverence, wouldn’t the spreading of joy - even with maybe a few shortcomings - make the composer happier than someone saying, “I’ll never be able to play it like Seamus Ennis. So I won’t even try”?

Re: Respected Music.

“Question for all: If you think you are not doing a tune justice, does that mean you are not playing it as well as you’d like to or does it mean you should never play the tune in earshot of anyone but your dog or cat?”

To me it means both. If I’m not playing it as well as I’d like then I’m certainly not going to play it in public. But “not doing the tune justice” can mean even more than that (and I think this is what Jerone was alluding to in the OP). Sometimes you can find a tune and practice it up until you play it really well, but you still don’t produce the beauty that originally attracted you to the tune - either it just doesn’t suit your personal style, or it might not suit your instrument - there are a lot of pipe airs that don’t transfer to fiddle at all well. In that situation sometimes you can turn it into something else that is equally beautiful but in a different way, and sometimes you might decide you can’t do it justice, and just drop it in favour of something else.
