When Music Makes Our Lives Beautiful…

…It was late in the evening. The sun beneath the horizon and stars beginning to wink. I’m at my main busking spot, a pedestrian bridge in Nashville that connects the football stadium to downtown, preparing to close out a long day. As is routine at this time, I start my Night set, a set of songs and pieces that I reserve until the late evening. You know, “Night Music”. Though they cross several different genres, they all fit neatly together because they all have that soft, gentle, easy, “late” character to them. One of the songs in this set is a traditional Scottish Song, “Tha Mo Ghaol Air Aird A’ Chuain”(My Love Is On The High Seas).

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=huJ46vcBxFg

Even though it’s “obscure”, it’s a daily “must-play” because it’s one of the most beautiful melodies I’ve ever heard. In all of its simplicity, it sits beautifully on the piano. Even though I’m stylistically extravagant and embellishing, this song helps me dial it back a bit and keep things simple, out of respect for the melody. Though, I do get carried away sometimes as the story is so emotionally evocative. And it’s an audience favorite.

So as I’m playing this, somewhere into the 3rd verse, a gentleman walks over to me and asks what I’m playing. And me, blushing having forgotten its English name says, “It’s a Scottish song actually… I can’t pronounce its name. Let me get the English name for you…”. Low and behold the guy goes, “Really!? I’m Scottish! I thought it sounded familiar!”. Albeit, he knew the song. So now we’re both raving about how absolutely stunning Julie Fowlis’s voice is and making fun of my Gaelic, and he wants to share his favorite song by her, with me. Wouldn’t you know it, it’s one of my own favorites. “Bothan Àirigh am Bràigh Raithneach”(A Sheilling on the Breas of Rannoch… or something like that).

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8zOU-JnePnM

I play my arrangement of it for him and his two friends that are with him, and now both of us are beaming. So now we’re all talking and his friends asked me how long I’ve been playing and such. I tell them about how I play Irish Traditional and play Irish and Scottish songs on piano. His friend goes, “No Sh!* I’m from Ireland!”. So I play him a set of blazing reels I’ve been working on these last couple of years. I think I left him thoroughly impressed because he said: “Man, you should go do that in Ireland…”. We all took pictures together and connected on our social media sites. They had a third friend, a doll of a young woman. She asked me if I knew any French music and that’s where it all went silent. I know no French. At all. She happily wrote down some songs for me to look up though.

It was a great way to close out the night. I was so elated from the experience, to bring someone close to home who is an ocean away, and give them the pride of knowing that yes, we foreigners do love your music! But what changes this experience from ridiculous to baffling, is that this JUST happened a week or so ago, with a Chinese Woman and a Chinese Traditional Song. There is a lovely, Chinese song called “Liu Yang River”.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V03n8IRNnbc

It’s a simple melody, so I play it as if it were a tune. 3 times through. A Chinese woman recognized it and began dancing around my piano and singing it. It was adorable, but really really cool because those songs are even more obscure than the Irish and Scottish ones. After she had her fill, she actually insisted she play a Chinese song for me. That was a fun experience too.

Isn’t it wonderful how we all can simply love and enjoy music, together, no matter who or where from? If busking has taught me anything, it is that music truly is a universal language.

Yes, indeed. Great story.

This reminds me of a related, albeit more boring, story.

I work in a warehouse. Exciting, I know. There was another guy who started working here about 3 years ago. Portland, Oregon is not exactly the universal center of diversity, but other cultures are not uncommon here either. This guy is from Morocco. He is extremely quiet and very respectful. I made it a point to talk to him so he felt at least a bit welcome. One day, it occurred to me that he might be a musician. He seemed like the type. So I subtly asked him, “Are you a musician?” Come to find out, he plays 4 instruments.

During the conversation, he told me about the banjo he had to leave behind when he moved to the States. He really missed it, but had no way to replace it at the time. I asked him what kind of banjo, and he told me about it. 4 strings, etc. So I ended up gifting him my cheap banjo. It is exactly like what they use in Morocco for the native music. So he’s making good use of it, and I made a friend.

What are the odds?
2 million people in this area ->Moroccan immigrant -> Irish musician of German/English ancesty -> nothing in common except an instrument that happens to be shared by the two [unrelated] musical traditions? And we meet in a warehouse? Not just meet, but are both employed together? Crazy odds, yet music makes it all possible.
