Re: How did people learn tunes before the invention of sound recording?

There was an interview some while back on Clare FM with Jack Coen where he spoke of cycling to spend evenings with other players in the area. If he learnt a new tune then often he would have forgotten it by the time he’d got home. So he would keep his flute by his bed and sometimes in the middle of the night the tune would come back to him and he would play it as quietly as he could to try and fix it in his memory.

There was also a comment to the effect that if people today had to learn tunes the way that his generation did then we wouldn’t have more than a dozen tunes between us!

Re: How did people learn tunes before the invention of sound recording?

Differentiating between the good tunes and the bad ones is a lot like applying local community standards when determining whether or not something is pornography. It boils down to “I’ll know it when I see it”, or in the case of the tunes, “I’ll know it when I hear it”.

People will invariably know what they ‘like’. Whether they are attracted to a certain twist or turn of melody, or contour peculiar to a given tune, they will ‘know’ it when they hear it. Different people have different likes and dislikes, and therefore will be attracted to different (kinds of) tunes. One man’s favorite is another man’s crap tune.

So how do you account for different people’s taste/preferences? Are there absolutes?
I know--I’m getting into a whole ’nother can of worms here.

Lastly, a well played/performed tune will be heard differently by many and perceived as a ‘good’ tune, than a poorly/less well-played tune.

Is beauty of a tune perceived in the ear of the listener, or dependent on the skill of the performer to be perceived as beautiful?

Re: How did people learn tunes before the invention of sound recording?

Speaking personally, if not for sound recording, I would probably have somewhat fewer tunes than I do. My first (traditional) tunes were learned from recordings - partly because it was easier, partly because I didn’t know where the sessions were. I suppose you could say that I learned to learn by ear through learning from recordings. But I would say that a large part of my repertoire (perhaps most) was learned by ‘osmosis’ - or by a more conscious process of learning a bit more of a tune each time I hear it, until I have enough of it to play it all the way through. Certainly, it has taken time to get to this stage; it would probably have taken me considerably longer, had I not had recorded music as a ‘springboard’.
