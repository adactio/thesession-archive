I read bits of fRoots standing up in W.H.Smiths, but practically never buy it. It is a glossy, fairly expensive thing with articles about people and groups from all over the world - copious information about stuff I’m very unlikely to see or buy, or maybe like very much if I heard it.

Its predecessor was Folk Roots, which laid out its stall by the quite dishonourable tactic of jeering at The English Folk Dance And Song Society at every opportunity, as some pathetic out-of-touch anachronism that didn’t understand the tradition properly and didn’t deserve to be its guardian. Well, I don’t know how many traditions Folk Roots rescued from oblivion, but the EFDSS made quite a good job of rescuing English ones and making them available to others, as far as I know.

Folk Roots went on to claim the street-credible high ground by hyping every wacky band that came along. On the plus side, though, their team did (and do) know enough about music to present good articles on people and scenes I find interesting.

I have a problem with such ‘attitudes’ too, whatever the negative and inflexible is directed toward, as mentioned previously, as exercised toward one aspect of ‘English’ tradition, the E.F.D.S.S.. I think you can sometimes draw a very clear analogy with bullying. I’ve been there at times with regards to another set of letters, the R.S.C.D.S.. And what makes it bullying? ~ since others are ganging up on these dinosaurs, you jump in too. But, both collected letters have done their share of bullying themselves. I just think that maybe, despite my own guilt here, the old addage of not doing unto others might be the better option. These acts often seem little more than childish vandalism rather than informed critique, and are at times blatantly ‘cute’ rather than useful. Informed critique is just that, it is informed and it informs, it has a constructive nature, it doesn’t just rant about fault but presents or seeks alternatives, it opens discussion rather than punctuating it with a final period… The best leave you asking questions rather than just nodding in agreement, or feeling the adrenalin rush of the kill… That’s what I prefer in a review, not just entertainment value, though I do value humour, wit and sarcasm. I want something that is not merely inciteful but insight rich, that tells me something of substance. I’m not seeking agreement. I value more when my views are challenged and when I gain some new understanding and appreciation.

One large problem with reviews, I think, is that because it is ‘within this small family’ there’s a feeling that the reviewer must support it at all costs, including true and informed critique and their own credibility…

Re: Trad/Folk Music Magazines/Journals….Discuss

Wow, the knives are out (and so they should be)!

Firstly, I write reviews and occasional articles for both fRoots and Songlines. I’ve also written a couple for IMM in the past, but only because the editor asked me to do so. The latter were not the standard ‘this is the best album since sliced soda bread’ in the normal IMM style, but it took so long to get paid that I never considered writing for the magazine again.

IMM is completely geared towards supporting future editions of IMM and its owner’s pocket (that’s a man called Robert Heuston, by the way - not the pocket, but the owner). If you send a review copy of your album to IMM, then you’ll almost certainly be contacted by the mag’s advertising staff with a view to paying for an ad. in either the same edition as the review appears or a subsequent one. Since bad reviews don’t exactly entice advertisers the mag’s policy is to be absolutely nice to absolutely everybody about absolutely everything. Just occasionally the mag’s editor, Seán Laffey (or Séamus Chuckley as he’s known in some parts) is allowed to get away with the odd negative review, but that’s presumably because the subject has turned down that lucrative advertising possibility.

Presumably to save costs, IMM doesn’t employ a proof reader with any knowledge of ITM which regularly produces some remarkable howlers and monthly deliveries of garbage.

Next, fRoots does cover ITM, but it’s a struggle to place any kind of article. I’ve occasionally managed to do so, but am always fighting against the latest Belgian Klezmer band or whatever new trend the editor sees fit to prioritise. As he’s got older (that’s Ian A. Anderson, by the way, and not the one with the same name from Jethro Tull), the mag’s covers have veered alarmingly towards featuring women musicians in short skirts.

fRoots‘ reviewing policy is clearly stated on its site - http://www.frootsmag.com/. Read that and you’ll discover that the mag’s only interest in negative reviews is if either a) it’s a poor album by a known artiste so we’ll print it, or b) it’s a pile of crap from some unknown, so we’ll stick it in the ’And the Rest’ section. I’ve never been able to put my finger on it exactly, but I suspect there’s an advertising connection.

By the way, fRoots doesn’t pay a penny for reviews.

Thirdly, Songlines does publish negative reviews, and has included several of my own. However, placing articles on ITM is again a problem because the editor has other priorities.

However, it operates a star-grading system which is closely linked to advertising revenue and the tracks compiled on its cover-mount CD. I’ve managed in the past to get musicians such as the fiddler Gerry O’Connor and his son Dónal’s collaboration with John McSherry wider recognition by deliberately giving the albums 5-star reviews and recommending them as tracks for the cover-mount - and neither review was undeserved.

The magazine does pay £20 for a review which at least keeps the wolf from the door while it nips down to Abrekebabra.

Number four - I can’t comment on The Living Tradition since I haven’t seen a copy for several years.

Lastly, as Steve Shaw has pointed out, there are some highly critical reviews on my Irish Music Review site - http://www.irishmusicreview.com.

The site hasn’t been updated for some time, but that will be rectified over the next couple of weeks.

Back on topic, well, sort of, a wish list ~ As I think I already said, I’m not seeking agreement as much as challenge, and that includes with reviews. I’d love to see someone have the balls to present conflicting reviews, not necessarily pro and con, but that would be cool, but more than one impression of a given recording or book.
