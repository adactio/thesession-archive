↳ Posted by Dick Miles on March 28th, 2014.

Re: Playing Irish tunes on the recorder.

The recorder has one advantage of the whistle in that it is chromatic. The whistle could hardly be said to be chromatic, except by the extensive use of cross-fingering and half-holing by a very skilled player. However, this isn’t really an issue, as most ITM tunes can be played on a D-whistle without the extensive use of these techniques.

The whistle has a major advantage over the recorder in that its fingering system lends itself much more easily to ornamentation, and that’s a very important consideration in ITM.

True, the timbre of the recorder differs somewhat from that of the whistle and some folks don’t like the timbre of the recorder. And as ornamentation (cuts, rolls etc.,) is more difficult on a recorder many recorder players articulate solely by tongueing, which doesn’t suit the music so well.

That’s why the whistle is the generally the preferred instrument for ITM.

Having said that, some tunes (e.g. O’Carolan) do suit the recorder.

Re: Playing Irish tunes on the recorder.

@ Jim -- there could be more to it than personal opinion in a session environment. True, when playing solo it is a matter of taste, but as soon as other instruments are involved the combination of sounds has to be taken into consideration.
As a simple experiment, play a tune in a midi file while accompanying it on the fiddle, and change the instrument type a few times. What sounds OK (or horrible) by itself, might well sound different (less or more horrible) when you are playing along with it. Some frequencies get masked, others stand out where they were hidden before.
To my ear, the recorder has too much of a bland consistency that robs it of pulse and character, with one note sounding much like another in all but pitch, and no attack or fade unless the player makes a great effort, as does Hatao in the video, to emphasise the beat, where it has all the subtlety of a rim-shot.

Again I stress that there is nothing wrong with Hatao’s playing, or with the recorder as an instrument. I just think he would, in my opinion, make better music using the instruments that were chosen by our antecedents -- presumably with good reason -- to play the music they gave us.

Re: Playing Irish tunes on the recorder.

Well said by several people inc Mix O’L and Richard DC. The recorder will always struggle in ITM because it is closely related to one of the fundamental instruments, the whistle, yet is different enough to make a difference. The recorder speaks with a foreign accent, it can’t sound like a native!

The recorder is a wonderful instrument, it was arguably the technically most capable of the Baroque woodwinds and reached an amazing level of chromaticism for such a simple instrument. It lost out as music moved on into the Classical and Romantic eras because it wasn’t loud enough, and it didn’t have the range of expression of other woodwinds.

As already mentioned, chromaticism came at a price, lots of cross-fingering which makes it hard to do proper Irish ornamentation. Yes, there are people who can play a recorder at the necessary speed, but they are relatively few professional standard players.
