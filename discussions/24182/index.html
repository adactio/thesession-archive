Session playing for bodhran players

Over the months there have been lots of comments about bodhran players and their contribution to sessions. All the old jokes (?) have been trotted out and the comments have been generally negative. The fault, if there is any, often lies with we bodhran players. We have to remember that even if we are good and feel we have a contribution to make, we have no God given right to join in whenever we please. I always wait to be invited to join in and I only do so if I know the melody player and am comfortable with the tune.
There are a couple of other reasons why we bodhran players are sometimes shunned. We are accompanyists so we can’t by definition lead, so only join in if you feel you can make some positive contribution, this comes with experience and confidence. Also, a top of the range bodhran only costs about £500 at most so that can give us the false impression that our ability matches our instrument. The main thing, though, is that within a relatively short time we can become good enough to play at a session, or so we think. We need to grow into the role of the session accompanyist, which is more than just being good on the instrument. We grow into the role with experience, but not at the expense of others. Wait to be invited, melody players don’t expect to play every tune at a session, so neither should we. It’s up to us to prove that we’re needed. Sorry for going on but I feel strongly on this issue.

Returning to the original post, I would suggest that one thing that might lead to a poor image for the bodhran player is the pretensions that have sprung up around the instrument. I’m not even talking about the idea of paying 500 pounds, euros, or dollars for a wooden hoop with a skin stretched on it - if you’ve got money to burn, go for it. I’m talking about the little ritual of “tuning” the thing, followed by the laying out of a dozen specialized beaters, including the requisite paintbrush and the odd pair of spoons, just in case, and like that, and the bit where you switch tippers in the middle of a tune (like anyone notices). It’s a drum, people. If you didn’t put on airs about it, people might take you more seriously. Come in, sit down, and play on some of the tunes. While you’re at it, have a listen and maybe learn a few as party pieces. If you can batter your way through Donnybrook Fair and Tripping Up the Stairs on a whistle, all of a sudden your cred will go through the roof.

And before you get cranky with me, bear in mind that I’ve played the bodhran for about fifteen or twenty years, and I think I know something about it at this point. I enjoy playing it and hearing it played well, but I get as annoyed as anyone when some palm pilot with a ridiculous drum that cost more than my guitar or my box walks in and gives the tune a footrub, the more so when they’re taking up half the table with gear they don’t need.
