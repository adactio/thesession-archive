A funny little roadtrip story

A couple weeks ago, I was on a bit of a road trip from Oregon into Northern California and stopped at a rest stop to use the facilities. I pulled in to park and saw an older gentleman sitting at a bench playing a fiddle. I grabbed my trusty whistle and jumped out of the truck to go have a listen and possibly share some kind of a tune.
As I approached, he was scratchin out some kind of mish mash the way a novice does. I should have known better and turned around and headed for the toilet. But my curiosity got the better of me. So, I went on over.
He saw me standing there with my whistle, got a real determined look on his face and struggled to show off his best mish mash. It was quite amusing.
When he finished, he sort of proudly swaggered and looked me square in the eye. Then, with an air of knowledge and authority, he informed me that he had just played some (can’t quite remember the name he put to it) Reel.
Inquisitively I asked, “Oh? That was a reel?” He say’s “Oh yes it’s a reel from Cape Breton, and it’s in 6/8 time.”
I just about pissed myself laughing. “Dude! You don’t know what you’re talking about.” He looked at me sternly and said “Oh yes I do know what I’m talking about. Reels are in 6/8 time and jigs are in 4/4. Here I’ll go get my book and show you.”
I said “wait just a second, before you get the book, I’ll bet you $100 that you’ve got it wrong, Jigs are 6/8 and reels are 4/4.” He says “ oh no you’re wrong. Reels are 6/8 and I don’t want to steal your money from you.”
Too bad he wasn’t more of a sporting fellow. I coulda used the gas money.
Thinking to myself, sheesh is this guy ever a moron. What the fek am I wasting my time on him for? But this was so amusing that now I had to follow him to his truck just to see how he explains himself when he gets his book.
He pulls it out, opens it up, points at St. Annes Reel and says see here, it’s in 6/8.
I look at it and it has the standard c time signature designation meaning 4/4. He intists that this means 6/8. “Holy freakin fek fer fek sake!” says I “ Whaddaya know! I gotta go take a dump now dude you have a nice day.”
Now, it’s two weeks later and I still can’t stop chuckling and chortling about professor mish mash and the rest stop music theory lesson. . :-D

“the story actually brings up a whole range of other issues, for example, as to why the old guy would want to take up such a challenging hobby anyway at his age. ”

When I was a freshman in college, I took a course called “Intro to Hispanic Literature” - a survey of some twentieth-century authors, taught in Spanish. One of the participants, along with a half dozen freshmen and sophomores, was a 70-some year old emeritus professor of psychology - human sexuality was his area - who was still active in his field, and enjoyed taking courses in the things that interested him.

Think about that: an intro course, somewhere north of seventy. To me, that’s beautiful. It’s an optimism of the right sort, it says “even though I may be dead in the next year, or the next ten, or the next twenty, I still have time to learn something new.”
It’s also a fundamental act of humility: here’s a guy who has a lifetime of achievement in the study of the psychology of human sexuality, and he’s saying that there’s vast areas of study that he has not pursued, and that in a life apparently devoted to learning new things.

This is the guy that made me realize that people do two things: they learn, and they die. If you’re not doing one, you’re doing the other. Taking up the fiddle at sixty, or seventy, or eighty makes all kinds of sense to me - would have made more sense to start sooner, but that’s done. It doesn’t matter when you start: you always start at the beginning, and there’s always going to be more to learn. Might as well do it now, no?
