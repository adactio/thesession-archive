The other point I was going to make - and this will become easier as you gain familiarity with the instrument and build up strength in the fingers of your left hand - is to hold the string down as long as possible before you move onto the next note. This will give you a more flowing sound. It could be that your mandolin has some slight set-up issues, making the strings harder to press down; it may just be that you are pressing the strings harder than you need to. Either of these will cause your fingers to get tired quicker, making you release the string sooner. A would one-on-one lesson with a real flesh-and-blood teacher would highlight exactly what is going on, but you could experiment by varying your finger pressure and seeing just how much you need to produce a clean note (much like experimenting with breath pressure on the whistle).

Re: Update on Mandolin Playing

People have already chimed in with most of my advice. But I will reiterate. Slow down, until you can keep the rhythm steady. Your picking, and especially your triplets are messing up your rhythm. What you should be trying to do is stay steady, and that’s where a metronome can come in handy.

As far as your hand rhythm goes, picking Down (D) on the down beats (1 & 3) and Up (U) on the up beats (2 & 4) is a great way to go for reels. The places it gets tricky is where you play a quarter note, and still need to move your hand back without picking. One thing that I often work on with beginner students that are working on picking rhythms is “picking through every eighth note” (if you have a D quarter note, play it as two D eighth notes) You wouldn’t want to really play a tune like that, but it can help with getting the hand rhythm ingrained to the point where you don’t ever have to think of it again.

And, of course, adding triplets in will generally force you to pick two down strokes in a row on reels if you want to keep your hand rhythm going. Like DUDU (dud)DU DUDU, where the (dud) is the triplet. So it can be difficult to keep that from messing up your timing, but practicing that slowly can help you get that ingrained as well.

Thanks for keeping us informed of your progress. As I said before, for a month into it, you are doing quite well!

Re: Update on Mandolin Playing

“I heard somewhere that the picking pattern you want with jigs is Up Down Down. Is that true?”

I think you might have misremembered - that sounds a very unusual picking pattern. As Will Evans says, there is some dispute over picking jigs. Many (like Reverend and myself) use DUD DUD as the basic pattern; others (like Will) prefer DUD UDU; others yet use DDU DDU.

When I started mandolin, I used ‘wild’ picking - that is to say, I had never given much thought to pick direction, although, in retrospect, I was using mostly alternate picking (DUD UDU) in jigs. I continued along this path for some years, but the picking would quite often trip me up. So, perhaps 7 years in, I decided to address the problem by working intensively on my right hand technique. I chose to adopt DUD DUD for jigs and found it to beneficial to my playing. As Will says, however, each tune is an individual and may ask for deviation from the repetitive picking pattern at times; you can also change the pattern on the odd phrase for some subtle rhythmic variation.

If I were teaching, I would teach DUD DUD. This is not to say that other patterns cannot be used equally effectively, but I feel that this pattern ‘forces’ you into a jig rhythm from the start, which alternate picking does not. Once you have developed a solid picking technique, I would absolutely advocate trying different picking patterns. One thing you will find is that different patterns favour different triplet placements - and it is good to be aware of this to add variety to your playing.

Re: Update on Mandolin Playing

Ha! Didn’t know that I had gotten the reputation as the big DUD DUD advocate on the forum.

I do advocate using DUD DUD as a picking pattern for jigs for the simple reason that it automatically helps you play with a nice jig rhythm, which is especially useful for those of us that didn’t grow up around the music, and haven’t really internalized the lilt of the jig rhythm.

Basically speaking, in the 3 notes of a bar in a jig are not equal in length, accent, or importance. The first note is long, the second note is short, and the third note is somewhere in between. You can vary the amount of swing or lilt in the jig, from fairly straight to heavily swung like a 6/8 march. But what sounds right to me for jigs is in the middle. And the DUD DUD picking pattern tends to give you that automatically. First off, down strokes are stronger, both because of the physicality of our wrists, but also from a gravity assist. The two notes that might be emphasized in a jig are the 1 and 3. A lot of the lift of a jig can come from an accent on the 3, which is leading you to the 1 down beat (some people think of the lift as making the dancers want to jump higher and land hard on the down beat). So both of the emphasized notes are being struck with a down stroke. The middle note is a shorter note and is almost never emphasized, so the up stroke plays right into that. And finally, the third note can be emphasized, but it’s a shorter note. And with the picking pattern, you basically have to shorten it, because you need to move your hand back up for another downstroke.

So the DUD DUD picking pattern was something that really started making my jigs sound better. But trust me, it also ‘ruined’ my playing for a few months, while I was getting the pattern to become second nature. It was a lot of work, but it was worth it in the end, because now I never have to think about it. And no matter what variation or ornament I might play, my right hand is going to stay consistent.

However, having said all of that, the DUD DUD picking pattern for jigs really gives you a unique challenge with ornaments - specifically triplets. The reason for this is that if you want to put a triplet on a jig AND keep the hand rhythm going, you either end up doing 3 down strokes in a row, OR you need to learn to pick triplets starting on an up stroke. There are two places that you would most commonly put a triplet in a jig. It would either take the place of notes 1 & 2 in the bar, or it would take the place of 2 & 3. Either way, you’re stuck with the issue. “Front side” triplets - my terminology - would be DUD (dud)D DUD. So the third note of the triplet is down, and the next two notes (3 & 1) are Down as well. A “back side” triplet would be DUD D(dud) DUD. So in that scenario, you have the 3 & 1 Down, and then another down to start the triplet. Neither of those scenarios is ideal… So you could play them DUD (udu)D DUD or DUD D(udu) DUD. Once you’ve come to terms with starting a triplet on an up stroke, those become viable options, but I find the “back side” one to be easier.

There is another argument to be had about whether it sounds Irish to play “back side” triplets in jigs. I’ve had multiple teachers from Ireland tell me they would never play a back side triplet - although, I’ve watched them do it right after telling me they wouldn’t a couple of times. But I also know a few great players that play nothing but “back side” triplets in a jig… For myself, I like having both of them in my tool kit. And for what it’s worth, depending on the situation in the tune, I will more often play the triplets starting on down, and just not think about the fact that I am doing 3 down strokes in a row. (Part of that is because that’s how I played them for a long time, and part of it is that I tend to “miss” my up-triplets more frequently). For me, a triplet is a “gesture” that feels like one movement, not 3. But it’s also the reason that I do a lot of left hand ornaments in jigs as well…

And for what it’s worth, I do encourage my students to learn to play jigs DUD DUD, but I don’t ever require it. If your jigs sound like jigs, I don’t care which direction your pick is going at any time…

So, that’s probably more information than you were looking for with one innocent little question about picking jigs ;-)
