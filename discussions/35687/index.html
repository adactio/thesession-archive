There’s a big gulf between the guitar and the viola in ITM, and they would function differently (rhythm/melody) in a session context. If you’re invested in the viola, but still yearn for something more guitar-like, you might consider a Mandola (tenor mandola in Europe), which is tuned like a viola. It has a voice that can be used for either melody or rhythm. It’s a little less common than an octave mando or mandolin, but is a lovely instrument.

I play mandola tuned DAEB (i.e., a tone up from CGDA), which I find works really well for me. You get the nice deeper mandola voice with an easier tuning since it’s like the top three strings on a mandolin/fiddle plus a B string. The only problem is you lose the low G string, but that’s not used all that much in this kind of music, and you can find workarounds as whistle players and pipers do.

Mandola is to viola as mandolin is to violin tuning-wise, so that might work for you. Disclaimer: I don’t know anything about viola or fiddle strings though, so I don’t know if a viola is up to being tuned a tone higher, or if something might break.

You have to make up your own mind. We can offer all sorts of advice, but that is from our point of view. It is biased by our own preferences.
I had a similar crisis, many years ago. I ended up collecting many different instruments that sit here collecting dust. What helped me finally decide was by simply taking some time off. I stopped all playing. For me it was 10 years, but for someone else, it may only take a day, a week or a month. I did some soul searching.
I believe that if you truly want to play an instrument, that desire will allow you to play almost any kind of music you want with it. I play whistle. In my whistle repertoire is Japanese folk, classic rock, classical, medieval and renaissance and , of course, ITM. Some of it is quite challenging and requires quite a bit of filangial gymnastics, but I do it.
That doesn’t mean it is the only thing I play. I am interested in playing most instruments. I am currently teaching myself mando and fiddle. I don’t take organized lessons. If I can’t figure out a sound, I go to youtube and see if anyone has posted a lesson that will answer my question. Failing that I will ask other players or just puzzle it out.
YOU need to decide. What you want out of music, what instrument will accomplish that and also capture your dedication. It take time and though and, maybe, some experimentation.
Watch lesson videos on youtube. I though I would like to learn harp, but after watching some lesson videos, I realized that it was not the instrument for me. I still love to listen to it, but I know I can’t play it.
Take your time, clear your head, step outside the problem. Approach it with a fresh perspective. You will be a better musician, with whatever instrument you choose, once you have clear goals and can focus on them.
Just my 2c.

Well there’s really nothing wrong with using a viola for Irish trad. There is a lot you can do with it. Probably sounds great for those low tunes, but there aren’t as many of those as there are high tunes, which you’d have to play partially out of first position (not impossible) or an octave down like you’re doing. Mandolin could be good, it’s not exactly different fingering, more of a different tuning, but all the proportions are the same. Different fingering to play the same tune in the same key, but some similarities. You would have to learn to work those out on the fly. I play differently but similarly tuned string instruments, the 4 and 5 string banjo. I tune my 5 string aDAde so it has three strings in common with the 4 strings GDAe. Plus add alternate tunings on the 5 string and someone once suggested I also learn GDAD tuning on bouzouki but I’m just like “no way.” You have to draw the line somewhere, the similarities and differences of all these tunings is a lot to keep track of when you’re on stage and the pressure is on. Sessions are another story. Personally I think the piano is a great idea, the initial learning curve might be steep, but it’s not really *that* hard. There is a lot you can do with piano in any genre.

Also have you ever heard somebody play tunes on a standard strung guitar? It’s really quite nice, and I can really appreciate a well picked tune on a nice sounding guitar. More instruments is always more work, but you do get better at multitasking over time, and you might not add much repertoire on a certain instrument for longer periods of time than other instruments, but the tunes do build up over the years. I’ve been a very slow learner on the concertina for 7 years or so and maybe know 20 tunes at the most, picking up just a few every year.

Viola in ITM - https://thesession.org/discussions/33394

The same Gerry O’Connor clip is there too, and indeed the fingering is different. The way I play Dr Gilbert’s, it already has a range from low A up to high B. Playing it in the same key in first position with viola fingerings makes it nearly unplayable - either too high or too low, resulting in a lot of octave jumping.

Octave jumping can’t really be avoided unless you:
* only play tunes that can be played on the G, D and A string (perhaps an octave down)
* shift to higher position (or use a capo, for CGDA banjo players)
* retune

If you are sticking with viola - and even if you switch to fiddle cause you’ll have the same issues as a beginner - what exactly are the issues atm?

Are you holding your bow correctly and without tension? This takes ages to learn. Have a look at all the online stuff about violin bowing; the bow hold is the same for viola, though you need to put a little more weight into it because the strings are thicker. By weight I mean let gravity and the weight of the bow and your hand be on your side, not dig it into the string (which makes a scratchy hard sound).

Is your ear already good enough to find the exact note without frets? If not cut a plastic label into strips and get a fiddler to place them correctly or use an electronic tuner (if you have access to a smart phone, there are lots of free aps. I use pitchlab which you can set for different instruments) to do this yourself.

The strings on violins violas and cellos are five notes apart:
Violin is g d a e (low to high strings)
Viola and cello are c g d e.
I think the tuning in fifths is pretty good for learning chords, so I wouldn’t fiddle about with new tunings.

I do however know an awesome scottish fiddler who plays a viola tuned g d a e. This means he can play things written for fiddle without going down the octave. If you’re doing lots of sessions this might be worth looking into. I’m not sure what sort of strings he uses. Could be a violin e…
