Re: Too Old at 52!!

Your are never too old but do expect some obstacles. I’ve a theory that most people can learn any skill withing their physical limits regardless of age if you have sufficient interest, motivation and time. However the predominant view is that young people learn quicker and better etc.
Fact of the matter is though, if you take up fiddle at say 10 yrs of age, you’d spend the next eight or so years of your teenage years learning the instrument, learning the music. You play with your peers a fair bit - learn together and other musicians will have a tolerance for your weaknesses.
As a 52 year adult with similar amount of time, you can expect to be 60! + to reach same zone of comfort. When you play with your peers who have been playing for years, they kinda expect you to be better automatically - i.e. same leeway isn’t always given.
I’ve seen this in action in summer schools during ‘grading’ - the adults and young people of similar skill level will tend to end up in different groups.

Hi emdub, I’m 41 and just started learning fiddle, you’re not too old, but get yourself a teacher! Seriously, I don’t know how anyone learns this instrument without a teacher. I would never have the patience for it.

Anyway, even a classical teacher will be fine to help you learn the basics, how to hold the instrument properly, how to finger the notes, how to hold the bow, draw the bow, cross strings, and all the other bazillion things you do with the bow. Posture is so important and a teacher can watch you and instantly tell you where you can make corrections, and even then it’s not easy!

As for finding a teacher of Irish music, I’m lucky to have found an Irish teacher, but if I had to find one, I might start with an Irish dancing group near you, because they might have musicians who play for them, and you could talk to them.

And then there are the workshops and festivals in the summer---you might have to travel for those, but it will be well worth it. I have my vacations planned for the next few years around them!

Re: Too Old at 52!!

age: i have a theory that the stereotypical conventional wisdom about it being difficult to master instruments as an adult has not been fairly tested. due to factors such as lack of time, work/family commitments, discomfort with being a beginner/journeyman, etc, etc, the majority of adults taking up instruments don’t perform the essentials of mastery: putting in a ton of practice, practicing correctly (which usually means getting a teacher or some lessons), and most of all, sticking with the above for a long, long time. i think we may see some amazing success stories if people continue to live longer and continue to have disposable leisure time & money…..

teacher: if you have any desire to play well and sound good, violin/fiddle is one instrument that demands at least some time with a good teacher. from my experience the teacher’s effectiveness at seeing to it that you gain excellent fundamental violin technique---intonation, tone. bowing, posture, etc, is the very most important thing, far more important at first than whether they are a specialist in the area of music you’re interested in. not all irish fiddle teachers can do this. i believe it is the most important thing to screen for!

Re: Too Old at 52!!

Old Scraper, O‘Neill’s “1001” is a prime example of the inadvisability of relying on the dots as Holy Writ. That particular publication, as has already been observed, is replete with errors, both transcriptional and typographical. To reliably sort out what the underlying music really is you’ve got to have a background in listening to and playing The Music, for perhaps a year or more. The classical tight-arses we’ve both come across are unlikely to have had this background, so they’ll play the O’Neill dots literally, and the ensuing mess will only confirm their mistaken opinions.
Modern music publications can have the occasional typos, so it does take the experienced player to spot them. I have a theory that some of these typos may be deliberate so as to catch copyright infringers. I’ve been reliably informed that town and city street maps have a deliberate error on every page for this very reason - usually a fairly harmless item like a cul-de-sac with a non-existent little kink in it - and it will be something that takes considerable local knowledge to spot.

Re: Too Old at 52!!

I think there is something a lot of people dont realise, New Zealand as a culture is very relaxed, many music teachers here also play classical, band backing, jazz, bluegrass, ITM and god knows how many other styles to make ends meet. There is no such thing as teacher kickbacks, tipping, or commission sales on instruments. No teacher can specialise in one style of music to make ends meet. Any teacher who is inflexible will very quickly be out of business, word gets around - and fast. The music industry in NZ is very, very different to that in the UK, Ireland and the US. NZ music teachers are used to people wanting lessons at any age. Many of those are people who are self taught and want to move on, or those making a life change. It is not all that unusual. Auckland is a very large city (in comparison to the rest of the country), and there are hundreds if not thousands of music teachers there.

Lazyhound, as for copyright markers…..its usually a tiny grammer mistake on one word on the map. There is a case going on here where the map maker used the map for a local newsletter and the publisher who stole it off him is suing him for copyright. Go figure. The publisher is not very welcome in many circles these days and he is finding information gathering a tad difficult.
