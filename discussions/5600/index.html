beginning fiddler seeking advice

Hi. I’m an adult in Chicago who just started to learn to play Irish fiddle in the last few months, and I’m looking for some advice. I have no previous fiddle or violin experience. I have an old fiddle which sounds all right, at any rate an old Irish (musical) priest from Southie in Boston that I met last week tried it out and says it’s okay. I’m absolutely in love with it and with Irish fiddle music and though i don’t seem to have much musical talent I figure if I keep practicing a little bit every day eventually my playing won’t be a torture for people to listen to and I can go to sessions and will have the joy of fiddling for the rest of my life. I guess I’ll just say what I’ve done so far and ask questions as I go.

1. Obviously I bought quite a few Irish fiddle CDs and have been listening to them a lot. I am interested in finding strictly traditional CDs that are not jazzy or new agey because I would like to learn the old-fashioned tradition and stay within it, certainly at least until I know it well. If I could become a decent strictly traditional player I would be ecstatically happy and I don’t care if it takes me years to get there. What are the best fiddle CDs that are very traditional in your opinions? I have some Denis Murphy/Julia Clifford/Padraic O’Keefe, and some Michael Gorman, a few very old recordings of Michael Coleman, James Morrison and Paddy Killoran, a Sean Keane disc and some Kevin Burke and Tommy Peoples and a Matt Cranitch disc. I am thinking about getting a Johnny Doherty collection and maybe Tommy Potts, but I am curious to hear what the fiddlers out there like best in strictly traditional playing. So far I seem to like fiddlers that are more austere sounding and less sweet and treacly. But I really don’t know how to explain the quality that I am looking for.

2. I got an intructional DVD by Kevin Burke that teaches a few polkas, jigs, and slides and have started to go through it playing the tunes slowly at first with a metronome and then working up to speed and trying to play in sync with Kevin, but without ornaments, just trying to get the rhythm right. Is it a good idea to just focus on bowing/rhythm first and stay far away from ornaments until I can play a good number of tunes well enough without them? How many tunes? Ten? Twenty? Fifty? And should I just try to learn one kind of tune at first, like jigs, until I start to really get the rhythm right, and later move on to hornpipes and reels? It seems to me so far that bowing is the hardest thing to learn. It seems like I am still fighting the fiddle to get music out of it instead of letting it do the work and that my bowing is too heavy-handed. Does anyone have any advice about bowing? ie what are the best things to do when starting out and trying to concentrate on bowing?

3. I am thinking about getting Matt Cranitch’s Irish Fiddle book because I understand it has bowing notation in it for beginners and that it’s a pretty good book. Are there other books or instructional material that fiddlers out there would recommend for a starting fiddler who is especially interested in bowing?

4. I am wondering whether it makes sense to start fiddle lessons now or whether it would make more sense to learn basic fingering and bowing for a lot of tunes and get them down pretty well and then go to a teacher maybe a year from now to try to learn ornamentation. I thought maybe if I developed basic skills before going to a teacher I would get more out of lessons. Will I do more harm than good if I wait? Also, does anyone know a good teacher in the Chicago area who might be willing to take on a student who is willing to practice every day and work hard and really wants to learn and loves the music but maybe isn’t really gifted with much natural musical talent?

5. What are the rules on sessions? Is it all right to play at sessions if you can do the basic tune and rhythm but can’t do ornaments yet, or does that just annoy people? Should one wait to play at sessions until one is actually a decent player and until then just listen? I am a bit shy about going to sessions because I feel like an outsider and an ignoramus and don’t want to annoy people or detract from their enjoyment of the evening. I am certainly not ready yet because though I know many tunes in my head I can’t play them flawlessly yet as to timing and rhythm. How good should I be before I try to sit in? It seems like nothing could be more wonderful than playing at a session. It’s almost like a group of people sitting around a warm fire, but the fire is the music. --And like I am out in the cold! I guess I’ll have to just rub bow and fiddle together to try to get warm.

6. I have one other question that I’m wondering about that’s not related to beginning fiddle playing. I have an early quasi-field recording of Tommy Makem and Liam Clancy and a number of other people on it called The Lark in the Morning on the Tradition label which is mostly songs but has a few reels and hornpipes on it as well but no names are given for the tunes, they are just called “Hornpipe” or ‘Reel". Denis Murphy must have been hanging around and plays a couple of hornpipe tracks on it and they are very beautifully played. One track I have identified as Byrne’s/The Rights of Man, but the other hornpipe track, which is more quickly played, and which I also really like, I haven’t been able to figure out the usual name(s) for. Does anyone out there have this recording and know the name of the tune so I can get the basic musical notation for it? Also, Padraic O’Keefe plays a nice simple little jig on it but the name for that isn’t given either. I am much more interested in advice for beginning fiddlers but I thought someone out there might have this recording and could tell me the names.

Thanks!

--a beginning fiddler in Chicago

Re: beginning fiddler seeking advice

“i don’t seem to have much musical talent I figure if I keep practicing a little bit every day eventually my playing won’t be a torture for people to listen to. . . ”

With that attitude you have already completed 90% of the journey.

“Obviously I bought quite a few Irish fiddle CDs and have been listening to them a lot. . . ”

There’s another 8%.

“I got an intructional DVD by Kevin Burke that teaches a few polkas, jigs. . .”

Another percent and a half. So far yer doin’ ok kid, without our collective wisdom.

“Is it a good idea to just focus on bowing/rhythm first and stay far away from ornaments until I can play a good number of tunes well enough without them?”

I’d say start learning ornamentation as soon as you can get through a couple of tunes at slow speed without mistakes, but keep it *simple,* a roll here, a slide there, only a few per tune, just to start getting used to it. Work up from there as the skills improve and the Irish spirit begins to inhabit you.

“Does anyone have any advice about bowing?”

Practice, practice, practice. These things come with time. Like learning to drive a standard tranny. It’s a feel thing. No one can tell you how to feel. That fact that you’re aware of your heavy handedness, again, 90% of the journey. You’re paying attention. You’re *aware.*

I thought you said you had no musical talent? Being aware *is* what musical talent is. The rest is just skill building through repetition.

“And should I just try to learn one kind of tune at first, like jigs, until I start to really get the rhythm right, and later move on to hornpipes and reels?”

While there might be something to be said for this approach, I’d say just make yourself happy. You’ll play more if you’re playing whatever it is that moves you at the moment.

“I am wondering whether it makes sense to start fiddle lessons now. . . ”

Yes, even if it’s only for a few weeks. If you’re going to take lessons the absolute beginning is the most critical phase. It could save you hours, weeks and months of unlearning bad habits. Once you’re on your feet properly you already show the sort of attitude that will let you pick up the rest as you go along.

I’ll let the real experts take over now, many of whom will disagree with me, and each *other.*

KFG

Re: beginning fiddler seeking advice

definitely get lessons right now. you’ll regret it later if you don’t.

as for books, you can’t go wrong with matt cranitch. i’d suggest getting pete cooper’s book, too. i think the official title (in the US) is Mel Bay’s Complete Irish Fiddler. make sure to get the CD with the book -- sometimes they’re sold separately.

cds? the ones you name are a really good start. you might also want to get the first bothy band album (1975) -- tommy peoples’ fiddling is amazing, and most of the tunes are session staples. i also really like liz carroll (who hails from chicago) -- maybe Lost in the Loop or Lake Effect would be good. don’t try doing any of what Carroll does at home (yet), but you’ll surely be inspired by her playing.

the other thing i’d recommend is to get out and hear as much Irish fiddling live as you can.

finally, good luck! i’ve only been playing fiddle for three years, so i know what it’s like to be just getting started.

sarah (in portland)

The deeper you go, the deeper it gets. The further down the road, the longer the journey becomes.

Re: beginning fiddler seeking advice

“The rhthme of the tune is harded to relearn if you learn it wrong that the notes (for me atleast) .”

The trick here is to learn the notes without rhythm at all, which is done by playing without tempo. Ironically this is one of things a metronome is good for.

Then work out the rhythm without notes which is done by using a pure rhythm instrument, like a ballpoint pen (if you have a tonal ballpoint pen I suspect you paid too much for it).

After that the whole thing will just kinda flow together of its own accord.
