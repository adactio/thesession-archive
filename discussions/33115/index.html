↳ Posted by Free Reed on January 11th, 2014.

Re: What makes you play trad Irish music?

I grew up with Scottish Traditional Music, which is in my blood. Then, when I was in my teens, we had LPs of songs & tunes from Ireland, Scotland, Wales & England. I had a ‘folkie’ phase & would rush home from school to play the LPs in turn. (What a weirdo! :) ) As you do, I graded them in my mind:
1st place: Irish - for the haunting melodies & cadences - so ravishing, so wild
2nd place: Scottish - for its virility and verve that make me feel so alive - so wanton, so dark sometimes too
3rd place: Welsh - still Celtic, still beautiful - lyrical & mystical
4th place: English - honest, rhythmical, comely - telling it like it is.

I would now swap the first & second places. I still think the Irish tunes are more beautiful, but as you get older, your roots matter.
I would also now swap third & fourth places. I love the old English tunes, which are so minor-key & eerie. And I love the Northern hornpipe style, so sonsie & homely.

But these two islands have a wonderful repertoire - like the scenery in both, so varied, so beautiful.

Mollie

Re: What makes you play trad Irish music?

For those of us with limited skills:
Very little need for backing/harmonizing.
Tuneful tunes.
Short tunes.
Mostly in D or G.
Low entry cost - whistles must be the world’s cheapest instrument.
Lots of opportunity for informal playing with others - no matter how bad you are. (tip: just move your fingers on the whistle at first - don’t blow!)
Don’t need to worry that everyone says all Irish music sounds the same - just daddle-dee-daddle, you soon find out it is not true, and there is huge scope.
Great web support - what other type of music can offer something like TunePal?

Re: What makes you play trad Irish music?

I mean that I have Scottish ancestry & feel ‘connected’ to Scottish music in a way that I don’t feel to any other.

Just a metaphor. But my life experience bears it out. I once had to train a troupe of eight-year-old lads to dance the ducks’ ditty for the school play, Toad of Toad Hall. They were laughably uncoordinated. One day one of the worst of them asked me if I liked Irish dancing. I said I did, and he said his Irish father had taken him to see Riverdance. He then proceeded to give me a brilliant demo of Irish jigging - it seemed to come naturally to him, even though he was so clumsy at the choreography that I required.

My husband, also ethnically Irish (but grew up in Scotland), is learning the piano with the usual adult struggles - but when it comes to playing Carolan, with no music for left hand or chords, he can just naturally ripple along in the most lyrical fashion. Nature - nurture - or motivation? I don’t know, but it’s fascinating.

Mollie
