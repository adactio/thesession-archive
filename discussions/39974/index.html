Re: Did I do this Irish Song justice?

O’Neill simply recognized that not all airs are songs, but all songs are songs. (Also all songs have tunes or airs, which are the melody).

Slow air is a term that’s meant to apply specifically to the air, tune, or melody (these being relatively interchangeable, certainly in this context) of a sean-nós song, or a piece in a similar style. Often people (particularly people who are not very steeped in the tradition, but others as well) will use slow air to mean any slow piece that’s not sung. It looks like you’re using that last definition, but not really making a distinction between unsung melodies (played instrumentally or lilted) of sean-nós songs and other types of airs; for example an O’Carolan piece, either slow or fast, could be called an air. Since a song is usually broadly defined as anything sung, and more specifically in Irish traditional music includes sean-nós songs, ballad songs, faster songs, comedic songs, (and more) all of which are sung, and therefore does not have to have a steady rhythm, verses, and so on, it follows that slow airs, using the term per the first specific meaning, do not differ structurally from songs that are sean-nós songs, but may differ from songs that are ballad songs.

More broadly, slow pieces from the Irish traditional music repertoire may include melodies (for example O’Carolan pieces) with quite disparate structures, which would of course be different structurally from songs that are structured… differently (ex with verse and chorus, or per sean-nós, or ballads that do not have choruses, etc).

Hence “slow airs seem very different structurally than songs” is pretty much a meaningless statement. A better wording would be “*some* slow airs seem very different structurally than *some* songs” or “My personal definition of a song is only a verse/chorus structure, and hence precludes anything I decide is not a song, and my personal definition of slow air is any slower paced instrumental piece, so under those very idiosyncratic definitions slow airs seem very different structurally than songs” (except even the latter should distinguish between all slow pieces, which would include slow pieces with very different structures).

I think the biggest problem is that you have a very personal definition of song, but think it’s a general definition. It’s not!
