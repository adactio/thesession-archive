I know it is a ‘touchy topic’ but what would have been so hard about just making a slow airs section which is not time sig dependant?

As I have been reading around the forum to gather some more slow airs I saw the reason explained many times as to why there is no slow airs section (due to them not conforming to a regular time sig) and the constant complaints people have at them having to be shoehorned into some random class and the site owners’ subsequent frustration at having to explain the reasoning over and over again.

I don’t see what is so wrong with just having slow airs section which has a little note by it saying that unlike the other sections it is not time signature dependent.

It seems the site owner has caused way more trouble for himself by refusing to deviate from the structure of every tune must have a time signature credo and all the fallout it caused rather than just making an exception for one tune type.

↳ Posted by trish santer on May 27th, 2020.

Re: I know it is a ‘touchy topic’ but what would have been so hard about just making a slow airs section which is not time sig dependant?

A few further observations…

I think there is probably a distinction between a “traditional” (for want of a better word) slow air i.e. one which has had its origins in the singing tradition e.g. Irish, Scottish Gaelic, or other and a more recently composed instrumental tune. By recently, I’d include the likes of Niel Gow and so on.
While both examples can and are still capable of being open to varying interpretations by the player, I suggest that the latter can more easily be notated in a regular format.

From what I’ve seen of written slow airs, it’s not so much the time signature which is the issue but, certainly, individual notes and even their pitch are often given slightly different emphasis by individual players.

I mentioned on a recent thread that “Traditional Slow Airs of Ireland… Tomás Ó Canainn” was an interesting book. There are lots of good airs therein and the time signatures do remain constant throughout. While there are lots of “ties”, the notation is relatively straightforward and could be fairly easily transposed to “ABC” for inclusion on this site. I’m not suggesting that we copy tunes from books, of course. Only that posting basic versions of airs would be quite possible. Of course, time signatures vary between different slow airs but the same argument would apply to marches.
Trish’s suggestion re suggested speed is good and, in fact, each tune in the aforementioned book does this e.g. “crotchet etc symbol=50” etc.

↳ Posted by 🎻Johnny Jay, Melody Player 🎻 on May 28th, 2020.

Re: I know it is a ‘touchy topic’ but what would have been so hard about just making a slow airs section which is not time sig dependant?

People have been writing airs with no time signatures, no bar lines, for nearly two thousand years.

It’s not a new concept, in fact music was all written like that since the early Middle Ages; time signatures and bar lines didn’t appear until the 17th century. Even today some church music is written without bar lines or time signatures.

So as I said in an “air” category it would be left up to the poster to decide whether to go with no time signature and bars, or to employ whatever time signature they felt best. It’s never going to be perfect, after all, the perfect is the enemy of the good.

↳ Posted by Richard D Cook on May 28th, 2020.

Re: I know it is a ‘touchy topic’ but what would have been so hard about just making a slow airs section which is not time sig dependant?

I think the problem with that, Richard, is that ABC without an M: field is not valid ABC. (I could be wrong on that, but most, if not all, ABC players and converters seem to balk if they don’t have that field). And this site’s tune archive is based on ABC, and automatically converts the ABC to sheet music. So it’s all fine and dandy to say ‘let the poster decide to go with no time signature’, but in reality, that won’t work for the technology used here.

Again, I could see Jeremy adding a new, broader tune type that allows the user to choose a particular time signature. But in my mind, while it would be helpful for some tunes in non-standard time signatures, it would also open up the archives here to some very messy ABC, and will have the effect of reducing the quality of the resource. So I can understand why Jeremy doesn’t want to spend the time and effort to do that, and he is right that anyone else is free to go start a site dedicated to slow airs if they want one bad enough…
