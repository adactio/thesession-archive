Re: How does my faedog sound?

The biggest mistake I hear many new whistle players make is over blowing, particularly in the upper register. The trick with any whistle is finding that sweet spot with your wind that creates the clearest sound with the least chiff. Surprisingly, the upper register needs less wind than the lower to sound balanced.

If you like the tone of the Faedog, you might consider one of Jerry Freeman’s inexpensive Mellow Dogs. They are clones of the Faedog which have less chiff and are more accurate in the upper register.

The whistle is probably the simplest instrument to learn how to play, but it takes a lot of work to master. Keep practicing!

Re: How does my faedog sound?

Life of Apple Pie,
Here’s some advice from a newbie whistle blower, so take it for what you think it is worth.
First a comment from me, then my advice: it sounds to me as if you may be over blowing your notes as several have suggested. On my Feadog Pro it is so very easy to do.

My advice: It has been suggested above to find the “sweet spot” on your whistle. This is how I learned to do it on mine and hope it helps. Begin on the 1st octave D and blow very gently and increase the amount of air slowly until you “jump” the octave. Continue increasing the air slowly then back off slowly until it drops to the lower octave. Now do this again and listen to the tone as you increase. Go a bit further this time in the upper octave. Listen to it in both octaves. There is “a window” in each octave note where it will sound the best. Continue to go back and forth, pushing the range until you can find the “spot” where that amount of air produces the best sound. From that point blowing any harder will only degrade the sound. Now do this for each of the notes. Find your whistle’s spot and practice hitting that spot. Some whistles the window is wider or bigger than others and that goes for the notes on the same whistle. The window for your low octave D will be somewhat different than the window for another note.

It may be advisable to take the blue tac out for now and do this with just your un-tweaked whistle. If you then want to put the blue tac in, then do it again, once you have modified your whistle with the blue tac.

I hope this helps you and it is not meant with anything but hopes to help you improve. I fully understand your comment about “some” of the commenters on the C&F forum.

Re: How does my faedog sound?

One thing I’ve learned in nearly 50 years of playing any instrument (and nearly that many performing in public for pay), is to never ask what others think without being willing and able to take the compliments along with the criticism equally. It’s human nature to get defensive over what we perceive to be our work and progress, but this isn’t a competition. It’s a journey, and it’s imperative that we remember that as we walk it. Many people, of all skill levels, have walked it before us. Whether we’re playing for our own personal enjoyment, or to sit in with groups that enjoy the same tradition, or to make money at it, you have to learn to treat criticism and praise as just another hill to climb in the journey.
