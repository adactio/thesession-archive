Jealousy in music

I was at a “session” on Long Island, in New York, and there was another accordion player there when I got there. He played one of those little accordions with the buttons on both hands. It only had 8 bass buttons and he hardly used them, so I joined in and played bass for him with my left hand and harmonized with my right hand. I didn’t know the song, but I did and do harmonize tastefully. This Irish guy gets up in the middle of the song and starts cursing, puts his accordion in a knapsack and walks over to the bar with his friends trying to calm him down. I’ve seen this group play before and have never seen trouble there. Do you think that my playing the bass pointed out that his bass was limited? I didn’t mean it that way. I hope I didn’t come off as superior because I had a bigger accordion(120 bass). He didn’t want to talk, and at this point I won’t talk to him either, whether he was drunk or not.

Re: Jealousy in music

There are occasions when it is important to be precise about terms used in a discussion, and recent posts in this thread illustrate this.
The “strict” definitions of “classical” and the other eras in the history of Western music are,
pre-1400 Mediaeval
1400-1600 Renaissance/Early
1600-1750 Baroque
1750-1820 Classical
1820-1910 Romantic
1910-today Modern
These dates are approximate, and there’s overlap between the eras. Several of the eras can be subdivided, both chronologically and as branches within an era, and musicologists make a living doing just that.
I’m inclined to think that ITM has affinities with the music of the Baroque, not least because there are tunes dating back to the 17th and early 18th centuries.
