Sessions fact check? What are the pros & cons of being part of a regular, local, once a month session…

At this point I’m looking forward to a day when I have time to return to my weekly session. Life & work has been way too interesting lately. As a result I decided to devote my attention to being at our most durable (and much abused) staple session. We have had a standard session/gig the 4th Thursday of the month @ a pizza joint w/a $50 tab. The reason I commited to this particular session is not because the pros outweigh the cons. The reason I am strongly commited is simply because I know exactly what the pros and cons are. This alone gives me peace of mind equally on the good nights and those which are not always so pleasant.

Last night was a prime example of the best of times and the worst of times. First thing I learnt about sessions when I began to discover this phenomenon we affectionately call the Irish session is it’s one constant theme is that the experience varies; it’s never the same twice. It’s also rarely perfect. People tell stories of how ideal it is, or has been. Even the stories change.

You want a question, don’t you? My question is in the header.

I’ll kick it off with my pros & cons from last night.

Pros
The joint was full of children who had no fear. They came right up to see what we were playing. The children danced, they laughed, they were not loud; though not quiet. The children were pleasant. Children are not always pleasant. But these children nourished me with their joy & curiosity.
Cons
Minor incidents. Mostly from musical mishaps. We weren’t perfect and we agreed to improve. By “we” I mean session members. Everyone else in the joint liked our gig/session because the children were having a good night out.
My personal con is I want to know how to encourage my fellow players when they play tunes (which they are not very familiar with a tune) to spend time listening to the tune outside of the session. I don’t mind if players aren’t perfect but it helps me to know others are putting in an earnest effort when their playing is off in an early stage but it gets better over time.

Re: Sessions fact check? What are the pros & cons of being part of a regular, local, once a month session…

In the monthly sessions I have encountered around my area, not a session or traditional music hotspot there is a problem which, rightly or wrongly, I identify as this:
Monthly sessions allow people who are only interested in playing as an idea and not a practice turn up time and time again, hiding their own lack of development and practice, from themselves, much more easily than they could if the affair was more regular. A month of dedicated, not work like, practice ought to be plenty for a motivated hobbyist muso to make a decent enough fist of a 16 bar tune I’d have thought. Time and time again though the same people tend to turn up and fluff the same lines. With monthly events attracting hobbyists the problem is something akin to school homework syndrome, it all starts with good intentions and gets left to the last minute and nothing is learnt.
Sure not everyone attending will be like this by any stretch, it is merely my observation that monthly sessions make it easier for this to keep happening. A weekly session can become tight knit quite quickly, there is less room for the never learners to hide.
Another issue with gatherings with extended periods of rotation, in places without the common heritage in the musical DNA of the area, is that lacking direct contact with other musicians, people draw on the vast array of tunes and sources that abound these days. This can often lead people down their own wormholes, so to speak, and not create a commonwealth of tunes that can be played together. Publishing a session tunelist or book can overcome this but also feeds directly back to my first point and is classic “homework syndrome” territory.

Re: Sessions fact check? What are the pros & cons of being part of a regular, local, once a month session…

Weekly, monthly or daily it is not really my point. It is just an observation that some people are more committed to the idea of playing an instrument than to the practice of doing so. In monthly sessions this can be got away with a lot more easily than in weekly ones. In “book club” sessions the same goes, there always being a reason why someone has chosen to sit in front of a page of dots again rather than to actually put the time into learning a tune. It’s all about choices we make.

I dip in and out of the local sessions myself. My work makes it practically impossible to commit to regular schedules and the irregular monthly affairs are almost always guaranteed to clash, a lot more than they would if things were more regular. I can’t do much about that. As a 9 note borderpiper/smallpiper I play a fair amount of what is standard session music for these instruments but locally the tunes are not very well known if they are known at all. I often end up feeling a bit of a novelty act although generally I am made welcome.

The common repertoire around here is very, very mainstream with the majority of the tunes being in D or massacred into G for box and simplicity. There is little common musical DNA except these “standards”. Without the opportunity to play more regularly these tune lists do not get expanded very much and much is sidelined. When this type of set up gets coupled with the attendance of a proportion of never learner types musically the results can be somewhat mixed.

As something of a sideline musician I am used to and happy to sit and listen, play in the gaps and start and join the handful of tunes that I share with the group. I enjoy session music whether or not I am playing. My gripe, when I do, is that sometimes these groups who limit themselves to what has been handed out in a book as standard must know tunes become blinkered to opportunities to learn other standard “must know” tunes because they haven’t been included in their version of the truth. People learning standard sets (or pretending to learn standard sets!) as a way of playing with other musicians often have an idealised version of “session” in mind.

I’ll say again this may or may not be the case with the sessions that you or anyone else is familiar with. Sessions can be great as impromptu one offs, or on any other kind of cycle imaginable. They can also be conceited , unmusical rubbish. In my opinion regular sessions give the chance for musical DNA to be created and shared. This is the stuff of a decent bash.

Re: Sessions fact check? What are the pros & cons of being part of a regular, local, once a month session…

Weekly or monthly doesn’t matter. What did the people who started the session intend for it to be? Is it a beginner session, a tune learning session, an advanced session, (where people are all expected to know the tunes and how to play, etc.?) Many times musicians are paid by the pub to show up on time and play for a certain period. There are expectations from the pub in these cases. Sometimes patrons are borrowing the space to hold what amounts to being learning and/or beginner sessions conducted more like club meetings. The expectations from the pub in those cases is completely different.

In our case everyone is welcome at our session but they come understanding it is neither a beginner or learner session. (Not to imply we never learn anything, just that the tunes aren’t played slowly and repetitiously enough to accommodate it.) There have been occasions where the concept is lost on someone and they will try to effect or manipulate our session to accommodate their needs -- which becomes disruptive. In these cases we have had to remind them that it isn’t that kind of session and would they please refrain from doing that.

The dodgy part is communicating these things to players that visit your session. It’s really the visitor’s responsibility to sus out these things before joining in, but many times the ability to recognize these things is a byproduct of experience. If people stumble in with errant preconceptions and expectations feelings can get hurt and people can be offended. Sometimes there’s no explaining and it can become dramatic. This is why few people even attempt to do anything. It can bring a session down if everyone isn’t careful.

So to answer your question, go to the session that best suits your interests and hope for the best.

Re: Sessions fact check? What are the pros & cons of being part of a regular, local, once a month session…

It’s fairly important that the “regulars” or “mainstays” do their best to attend every week if they want to keep the session going in the manner they like or even going at all. That’s a reason why the management of many even very session friendly pubs will pay the “lead players” a retainer of some sort just to ensure that they will turn up on a weekly basis. Even when it’s a very informal arrangement and there are not necessarily expectations.

However, it shouldn’t be expected that everyone else should turn up every week without fail. Commitment and priorities are fair enough but “Life” means that this also applies elsewhere as well.

A respected and competent musician should always be welcome whenever he or she can manage to attend a session and will always have a worthwhile contribution to make. Even your less experienced “drop ins” are unlikely to cause much harm providing there is room for them and they are generally prepared to “go with the flow” and conduct themselves properly according to the etiquette of that particular session.

What is most important, in my opinion, is that those who can manage to(and do) attend a session on a regular basis DO make an effort to improve and learn the repertoire. They have less of an excuse than the more irregular attendees but you then sometimes get the “It doesn’t matter. We’re back here next week, anyway!” attitude.
