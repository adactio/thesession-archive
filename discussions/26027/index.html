What’s the best way to set the tempo for a tune in a session?

There seem to be two major conventions:

1) the person who starts the tune sets the tempo and everyone tries to stay at that rate

2) once a tune is started, the tempo may increase to a kind of ‘consensus’ rate set by the fastest players

The question assumes that an overall goal in playing most tunes is to keep the tempo at a steady rate.

Should this rate be set by the fastest players, even if it causes slower players to drop out, or should it be set at the speed at which it is started even if it’s started by a slower player?

There’s always the tension. Sometimes this is complicated by the fact that many (dare I say most?) players are not always aware of it when they speed up - and then they wonder why the tunes always get going so fast. There are also players who tend to push the beat - whatever tempo you’re at, they’re at the leading edge of that. Other players will follow them, and again you’re speeding up without anyone meaning to.

That in mind, I prefer to defer to the person who started the tune, in principle, and I think it is pretty rude to intentionally speed up the tune, but I recognize that it’s not always intentional.

Re: What’s the best way to set the tempo for a tune in a session?

You have to be confident in your ability to maintain the tempo at which you want to play, even if others are speeding up. This can be difficult if you defer to other musicians at the session, and consider yourself lower down the food-chain than they are. If you look up to other players as somehow better than you, you’re likely to allow them to take over your set. Building up confidence in your own ability can counteract this tendency.

You can also make a point of stomping your foot a little louder than usual, at the pace you’ve set. If this doesn’t make people aware that they’re rushing, call ‘hup’, then go into the next tune at the pace you started the first tune. It breaks the continuity, and it’s a little obnoxious, but so is speeding up somebody else’s set. Generally, things speed up as they go downhill…

Re: What’s the best way to set the tempo for a tune in a session?

Yep. A set can only have one leader. If you are that leader, don’t listen to what others are doing, just play your tunes at your own pace. If you do notice someone wandering off tempo, don’t be tempted to follow them, just play louder to re-assert your leadership. If you’re not the leader, listen only to the leader, and play only with him/her. Don’t be tempted to follow others who might drift off.

Re: What’s the best way to set the tempo for a tune in a session?

I participate sometimes at a session with a “circle of death” format with a mix of all skill levels. There are several older Irish and Irish American gents who come out for it, too, and they are my main reason for going; they have good tunes and good style. The rawest of raw beginners also come out as well, and some of them insist on playing from sheet music and don’t listen to anyone else. There are also a few intermediates who attend sporadically and speed everything up, often rushing tunes started by others.

It can be great fun hanging out with just the old fellas and learning tunes off of them, but some of the other participants are mostly there to perform their little party pieces in front of a captive audience - they’re not there to learn, they’re there for validation.

But it’s not my place to tell them to take the sheet music and toss it in the garbage., although I want to telll them that. It’s not my place to tell them to stay with me, as more often than not someone takes offense. I make no bones about steadying up a tune started by one of the noobs, especially if he (it’s almost always a guy, caught up in that stupid, macho, “speed is good” mentality) seems intent to crash and burn at least once before the end of the tune. With the intermediates, I follow the tempo they set and hold them to it; if they rush ahead, it’s their own fault, I’m not following them over the cliff! I get irritated when others try to speed up a tune I started, too., and I hold my ground. But I will follow any tempo set by someone who is steady after a bar or two and expect the same in return. I think that’s pretty normal for musicians of a reasonably good standard.

Re: What’s the best way to set the tempo for a tune in a session?

Start playing your tune (in a gap in the music, obviously, not in the middle of someone else’s set…)

Get locked into the tempo you intend to go on at, pretty damn soon, and stay in it

Shrink into your dulled inner being. Give nearly all your concentration to the tempo, and the rest to playing a sequence of notes that hopefully corresponds to the tune. (Best not to play tunes in such a circumstance whose fingering you haven’t thoroughly mastered.)

DON’T look round. Be an unmanned drone stolidly puttering away through space, far from the world of men.

Any luck, the session will come to heel and join you, at your tempo.

You may, on the other hand, come to a stop and find they are all slumped around like figures in some history painting, lifeless with boredom, drink or psychic trauma, having in no wise joined in.

This may be because they found your playing gruelling and your tune repulsive.

But then again, it may not.

The session in which I “anchor” every week sometimes has a fair number of relatively inexperienced players, that come from the tune learning session that I help run. Re: What’s the best way to set the tempo for a tune in a session?

Some players launch into a tune concentrating on the notes instead of the pulse. Better to take a second before you start and lilt the tune in your head with the feel and pace you want to give it. Then start it with that feel and at that pace.

If you can’t do this yet, keep trying (maybe in the safety of your living room). And meanwhile, play along with better players, focusing on internalizing their pulse and sense of pace. There’s nothing wrong with playing along at a session without ever starting a tune or a set of your own.

cboody, here’s a thought. Our regular pub session sets up in a corner, and as the session ‘anchor’ I sit in the crook of that corner. Most of the time, everyone else in the session can hear me, thanks to what acoustic engineers call ‘corner loading’--the sound bounces off both walls and out into the rest of the circle.

So when someone braves launching a tune and the gang starts to speed it up, I can usually (not always) rein it back in to the original pace. And I’m not above barking “Don’t speed up!” Doesn’t always work, but at the very least this has made people more aware of the potential problem.

Re: What’s the best way to set the tempo for a tune in a session?

I’ve always found it a lot harder to slow down than to speed up. I don’t know exactly why that would be, but it seems that way. If I were to guess, I’d think maybe it’s because people are afraid of dragging the tempo, so they try to match the leading edge.
I guess the trick is to get a sort of critical mass - multiple players in sync seem to have a sort of gravitational attraction - and to try to do it bit by bit. The other thing, if you can pull it off, is get a tune to cut of abruptly and establish the new tempo. That’s hard, though - you have to get the attention, bring the current tune to a solid end and start a new one on the beat, but with the new tempo. So you have to have both tunes in your head, and also project your personality enough to get the attention of the masses, some of whom are also thinking of some similar designs.
