Re: what are the key elements which make Irish music attractive and realtively popular?

I don’t think it is anything to do with musical qualities - there are a lot of other traditions from all over Europe which share the same musical properties but aren’t as popular outside their native area. I think it has more to do with culture and heritage - a lot of Scots and Irish emigrated in the past, and the music gives them and their descendants a connection with their roots. And there is a knock on effect that when they start playing it their new communities are exposed to it, whereas they have probably never heard music from Brittany or Portugal, so don’t know whether they would like it or not.

Re: what are the key elements which make Irish music attractive and realtively popular?

Yooval, I’m sorry, but I can’t see anything ‘special’ about Irish music that makes it any more likely to get taken up by non-natives than any other tradition. Yes, there are Japanese musicians playing ITM. There are also Japanese musicians playing Scandinavian music and klezmer. Presumably you’ve only noticed the ones playing ITM because ITM is what you are interested in.

What happens, throughout the world and across genres, is that a band from one particular tradition goes on a world tour, and in their wake there is a craze for their genre of music. There was an ITM craze following De Danann/Planxty touring era, and another following Riverdance, but in between we’ve had Cajun, bluegass/Appellation, Scandinavian, Sheltand, klezmer and ukulele orchestras. In each case a few individuals get so hooked they carry on playing that music after the craze dies down. But it happens for all genres, there is absolutely nothing special about Irish music. Go and look at some Appellation old timey, or Cape Breton fiddling. Although the style is different it still has all the same musical qualities as Irish music - and half the time they’re playing the same bloody tunes.

At the moment, in this little corner of Scotland we are seeing far more bluegrass bands than either Scottish or Irish, so perhaps you should be asking what makes bluegrass so unique that people all over the world want to play it. Or country & western, or rock‘n’roll….

Re: what are the key elements which make Irish music attractive and realtively popular?

I think the answer is that both the Irish diaspora and inherent qualities of the music itself have contributed to the popularity of Irish trad. The Irish people that spread around the world are doubtless a major part of the reason it’s found in places so far away form Ireland. It’s also in a nice pocket, structurally:

• It’s relatively easy to play (though difficult to master!) so it lends itself to the hobbyist (I believe this is a very good thing and do not sneer at ‘hobbyists’ -- I don’t think it’s a demeaning term. I am an ardent hobbyist.)
• There is an extensive repertoire to be explored
• It lends itself to individual artistic expression with it’s system of variations and ornamentation, but not too demanding --it’s a restrained, minimal approach. You don’t have to be Thelonius Monk to play it.
• It sounds great solo (so you can play alone and still experience the music completely, unlike styles that require separate rhythm and melody parts) yet it also sounds great in ensembles from duets to small orchestras

Re: what are the key elements which make Irish music attractive and realtively popular?

Yooval, I’m not sure where everyone else is from that has answered, and even though I agree with much of what they’re saying, I think I may be able to shed some light as a possibly pure-blooded foreigner(no Irish ancestry at all). #BlackAmericanFromTheHood

To answer the original question, what makes Irish music unique, I agree that nothing really makes it “unique” in a musical element sense(because the elements are just that: Elements, and they’re in all music). The ONLY thing I can think of, is that it is one of the only styles of music in the world that can be interesting and entertaining without words, bass lines and heavy percussion instrumentation. But, this doesn’t necessarily make Irish music more popular, just different. Look at the most popular and widespread musics in the world, they all have all three of those elements.

Now to answer the alternative question, what do I like about Irish music, being a black american from the hood? Well, you may want to take a seat.

I like melodies. I like dance music. I like instrumental music. That’s where tunes come in. I like ballads, I like the gaelic language, I like gaelic melodies. That’s where the songs come in. Irish music features an abundance of these things.

Think about the tune types and their individual rhythmic styles…

Marches
Polkas
Germans
Strathspeys
Highlands
Hornpipes
Reels
Mazurkas
Waltzes
Slip-Jigs
Slides
Double-Jigs

That’s what? Maybe 12 rhythms amongst about 6 different sub-times, amongst two main times… 3 and 4.

Then there’s those undiscriminating melodies, written not only in major and minor, but also dorian and mixolydian, utilizing 4 out of the standard 7 modes.

The there’s all those acoustic traditional instruments!

Uilleann Pipes
Harp
Fiddle
Flute
Tin Whistle
Concertina
Button Accordion
Piano Accordion
Mandolin
Guitar
Piano
Bouzouki
Banjo
Bodhran
Just to name a few!

That’s every musical family, excluding the brass instruments. That’s a lot of instruments! Imagine the combinations, variations and possibilities! So many ways to keep the music fresh and alive with so many different instruments to listen to.

So I like Irish music for it’s dancing, impressive melodies, it’s barely discriminating instrumentation, and it’s impressive collection of rhythms that promise to get you on your feet! But I can tell you right now, it’s mostly just the melodies for me ;-) Several of those instruments and tune types I could live without, and probably wouldn’t even miss if I never heard them again.

All in all, I respect Irish music as a strong music that has been around longer than even some cultures and nations of the world.

Re: what are the key elements which make Irish music attractive and realtively popular?

I don’t know what the most likely ‘correct answer’ is, and doubtless a lot of it is to do with social joy in the session or dance hall as others have said.

But I personally think that Irish music does have its own special character, which is different from American or European or even other Celtic traditional musics. For me, Irish Traditional musical habits convey emotion par excellence. A happy Irish jig or reel lifts the spirits and makes one feel wild and wanton - I think it’s to do with the lilting rhythm and the use of ornaments, the musical equivalent of ‘way-hey!!!!’.

A slower Irish air or jig - what Kevin Burke refers to as ‘miserable’ jigs - has lyrical minor-key cadences which are immediately identifiable as Irish (don’t know enough musical theory to say why, but they are). These tunes are stamped like seaside rock with the words ‘heart-breaking beauty’. :)

For centuries Ireland was seriously done over by the English, sending emigrants off to all corners of the world with little more than the shirts on their backs, memories of famine, and a heart-wrenching attachment to their origins and their music.
Compare this to Scotland, who suffered too but retained its pride at home and found a place in the English hierarchy - you can feel it in all those crashing triumphant A-major reels - or particularly compare with English trad music which I find jolly (jolly nice, sometimes) but a bit complacent and even banal - they never really needed their traditional music to fight for their identity…. and then of course there’s French trad music (where I live) which is groovy and sexy but somehow not altogether trustworthy… but perhaps I’m getting carried away…

Re: what are the key elements which make Irish music attractive and realtively popular?

A few generations ago, ITM was generally a relatively more subdued affair. Sean Not singing, solo pieces on fiddle, flute or piano may have been the height of it. What really kick started modern ITM was Sean O’Riada introducing the idea of a trad group and bringing more of an orchestral sensibility to the genre.

This innovation spawned the many trad groups that have then developed the art form, and both old and new styles of solo/singing and ensemble praying along with Irish dancing reached a global audience with the Riverdance phenomenon. Riverdance also linked with other artistic traditions.

So I think one of the key elements is Sean O’Riada.

Re: what are the key elements which make Irish music attractive and realtively popular?

As an American of Polish heritage, I’ve got to say, I think there is _something_ unique about ITM. You can say that Cats, Elvis, Thelonius Monk or whatever have fans, or that Andean or Scandinavian music is also well loved. But it seems to me that those are, relatively speaking, either flashes in the pan or much smaller audiences. We’re playing tunes that have lasted for hundreds of years, still sounding fresh. But in 50 years, I expect Elvis will be only as popular as Al Jolson is now.

So what makes ITM connect? Aside from items already mentioned - the vast number of tunes, the relative ease of playing them, the sociability, etc. - I think there’s much benefit from use of the ancient modes, plus the occasional pentatonic tune. Mixolydian and Dorian aren’t commonly found anywhere else, AFAIK, but I think they have a fundamental attraction for lots of people. That may be just because they remind us of comfortable, ancient church hymns. But OTOH, maybe the ancient church hymns were also tapping into something fundamental in human consciousness, something that stays present despite centuries of changes.

Many of the earliest tunes (and many good more modern ones) in folk/trad use pentatonic scales, so are particularly easy to play.

It is so much easier to learn how to play ‘Kaliope House’ than Beethoven’s 5th Piano Concerto. Both are equally good, but rather different levels of skill required.

Part of the attraction in ITM is that it is so easy to get into playing compared to other types of music, particularly classical. Learn one jig or reel and you really feel you have achieved sometime, and can bring a smile to other faces when doing your ‘party piece’. And gain another level of pleasure playing at a session (if you can keep up).

The ITM genre is great for us participants.

Re: what are the key elements which make Irish music attractive and realtively popular?

One thing which I like about accompanying Irish music at the local Sessions is that the chord progressions are non-standard. Unlike ninety percent of popular music which has only three chords (I sarcastically call them “three chord wonders”), Irish tunes have four or five or more chords and the chord changes don’t show up when and where you expect them to either. Also, the chord changes are unorthodox compared to most popular music. They don’t follow the standard guidelines of music theory which shows up in most popular music.
