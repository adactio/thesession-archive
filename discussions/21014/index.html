Re: Is the tunes list missing Carolans?

Carol, welcome to sessions, and to this orange online session.

Another reason O‘Carolan’s tunes aren’t spotlit here is that they’re not your typical session tune. O’Carolan was influenced by the formal music of his day, and the difference between that and the more “crossroads dance” sound of most jigs and reels is easy to hear.

I agree that O’Carolan wrote many beautiful melodies (Eleanor Plunkett is one of my favorites), but they don’t readily fit into the flow of a typical Irish session. In fact, I’ve sat in on some sessions where playing his tunes is actively discouraged. Whether or not you agree with that attitude, it helps to know beforehand that it exists.

Re: Is the tunes list missing Carolans?

The tunes on the website are intended as dance tunes, and each category has its own time signature. O’Carolan tunes, like marches, and airs, come in a variety of time signatures. This issue has been addressed by Jeremy from time to time, and his decision has always been to stick with the original intent and categorization scheme.
You will find a lot of different tunes tucked away in the database, however, with 6/8 marches in the jig section, 2/4 marches in the polka section, and a lot of slower 3/4 tunes in the waltz section. While there is not a special section for O’Carolyn tunes, quite a few of them are here.

Re: Is the tunes list missing Carolans?

Belfast_ceol writes “Through his priviliged social connection he was only introduced to the Harp by Lady Mc Dermott in his teenage years” and “Even with modern learning aids it is more difficult for most Blind People to learn an instrument.” Actually, the tradition was to teach blind children to play music because it was just about the only way they could learn an instrument. They were even given a special diet with extra eggs and cheese to make their nails grow stronger for plucking metal harp-strings. If it is harder for the blind to learn music, nonetheless many of the great harpers of that period were blind. The training was offered as an act of benevolence: “Privileged social connections” had nothing to do with it, O’Carolan’s family had none.

The reason for O’Carolan’s less-than-virtuosic playing abilities (which were noted by his contemporaries, as Belfast_ceol correctly states) is that he only started learning late in life: he wasn’t blinded until he had a bout of smallpox in his late teens.
