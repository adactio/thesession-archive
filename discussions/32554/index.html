Re: No airs?

Here is Jeremy’s response:

"Here’s the long answer: maybe…

if the time signature of the slow air fits to one of the tune types on offer (waltz, polka, jig, etc.), then you can submit the tune under that heading and add in the comments that it should be played as a slow air.

However, I can’t add “slow air” as a category because it says nothing about the nature of the tune (apart from the fact that it’s slow, of course). If a tune is a jig, then I know it’s in 6/8 time and the standard note length is 1/8. A polka is always 2/4, a slip jig 9/8. But “slow air” says nothing about the the time signature or note length.

I have to categorise tunes somehow, and I have chosen to do it by tune type. If I allowed “slow air” as a tune type, then I might as well allow “fast one” or “ really good tune” as possibilities.

So, as I said, if the air fits into the time signature of an existing tune type (and most should), then by all means submit it. Otherwise, I’m afraid you’re out of luck.

Anyway, as it says on the front of the site, this is a site for the traditional dance music of Ireland. Slow airs and Scottish tunes will be tolerated but not encouraged :)

Viva la jigs’n’reels!"

Re: upload proper sheets

The point of ABC is that it takes up very little disk space to store a tune. This website has over 13,000 tunes and each tune has multiple tune settings.

If you take Cooley’s reel https://thesession.org/tunes/1 and save the ABC to a text file for just the first setting it takes up 239 bytes. If you take the sheet music and save it as a .PNG (compressed image) it takes up 32,700 bytes. Over 100x the size. Multiply that by 13,000 different tunes and you have a huge difference in size.

This website works by storing all the tunes as ABC in a database and when a user wants sheet music or a midi file for a tune or tune setting it generates on the spot that sheet music or midi based off the ABC. The tune database is setup as described previously and also like the others have mentioned there could be another way to do this that might include airs or whatever but it would involve completely redoing what is here right now and as previously quoted by Jeremy he doesn’t want to do that.

Jim Dorans, Jeremy laid out how his system functions on the IRTRAD link I posted above. It’s a good read for anyone who wishes to understand how thesession.org abc tune database is organized.
Here are a few of the pertinent points;

"The database of The Session consists of a number of inter-dependent
tables. The table for tunes consists of the following fields:"

tune_id
name
mode_id
type_id
date
member_id

"The tune_id is a unique identifier for the tune. Using the name as the
unique identifier wouldn’t work because many tunes share the same name."
…
"Now, here’s the important bit…

The type_id links to yet another table which holds the information about
*types* of tunes.

The fields are:

type_id
typename
meter
notelength

Here’s an example for the tune type “slip jig”:

type_id: 3
typename: slip jig
meter: 9/8
notelength 1/8

So, you see, every tune type listed at the session says something about
the time signature for that kind of tune: all polkas are 2/4, all
waltzes are 3/4, all double jigs are 6/8, etc.

Are you with me?

Now let’s try and add the tune type “slow air” to this table."

No, because of the interdependency of the tables. Re: No airs?

Transcriptions of airs (ie. non-rhythmic tunes) can of course be done, as there are many in various tunebooks - but I suppose one has to have the understanding that these have to be approached with a GREAT deal of licence. I guess the main difference is that dance tunes by their nature have a regular pulse (though the meter can temporarily change, as in the ‘crooked’ Quebecois tunes). But the pulse remains.

So perhaps the complication with notating airs is that they have no real pulse, but are driven by the nuances of the melodic shape alone (?). This makes them generally tricky to notate, often with lots of time signature changes; that’s not to say it can’t be done, but as the form is so free, it will always be a transcription of some particular version rather than a basic model to go by. So I guess the problem is a technical one - you can’t have an air category because it would require notational technicalities that are too problematic for ABC (or am I wrong here? I don’t recall seeing any tunes on here with time signature changes, but maybe there are?)

For my part I don’t believe it’s necessarily ‘wrong’ to notate airs, but as a player they’re probably most difficult to do justice to if you learn from the dots alone - each is more or less unique in structure, whereas at least with dance tunes you understand the form and meter each time you learn a new one (that’s not to say that dance tunes of any one type should all be played the same way though!). Sure, to get any of this music right I think one needs to work primarily with the ears, but I would say an air can be too easily trivialised if not first understood through aural means, and maybe that’s why they in particular are thought unfavourable in notated form.

As such it’d probably be best to notate airs with no time signature, but simply with a fairly abstract use of different note values to suggest where the important points are, and perhaps the occasional barline - but again, presumable this is something that ABC can’t manage? And as people have said, any of this would mess with the database categorisation too.

Sorry if I’ve repeated anyone, or made a duff point due to my inexperience with ABC - I have managed to read most of what’s been said here before me!
