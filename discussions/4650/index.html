Novels that feature the music

I’ve just finished reading Dermot Bolger’s “Father’s Music”, a very well-told story and one of whose central characters is an old Donegal fiddler Prionsias MacSuibhne. Bolger really understands the music not just as a punter, but from a musician’s perspective (and yet he claims not to have any tunes himself). Well worth tracking down if your taste runs to highly literate contemporary Irish novels …

Over the course of the past year I’ve also re-read John B Keane’s “The Bodhran Makers” in which the music features heavily. (Highly recommended) And, like a lot of people, I was dazzled by Joseph O’Connor’s “Star Of The Sea” which - among numerous subplots and cul de sacs - hypothesised an alternative genesis for “Arthur McBride”.

Anyone else come across any novels (let’s forget about other sorts of literature for the moment) in which the music features prominently?

Re: Novels that feature the music

RL Stevenson’s Kidnapped/Catriona sequence of novels has a section where two pipers have a drinking session and play tunes at each other, each time returning the tune with more inventive or more subtle variations. It’s very well described, but they’d probably be thrown out of a modern session for such competitive behaviour!

John Buchan’s WW1-era “Greenmantle” contains the following passage, in the chapter “Companions of the Rosy Hours” (great band name) which has always convinced me that the strait-laced Buchan had sampled the “Happy Havelock” (Wacky baccy)at least once:
- Then slowly from the silence there distilled drops of music. They came like water falling a long way into a cup, each the essential quality of pure sound. We, with our elaborate harmonies, have forgotten the charm of single notes. The African natives know it, and I remember a learned man once telling me that the Greeks had the same art. Those silver bells broke out of infinite space, so exquisite and perfect that no mortal words could have been fitted to them. That was the music, I expect, that the morning stars made when they sang together.-

he is describing the fiendish foreign charms of Turkish music but I think we can see some parallels!

Re: Novels that feature the music

There’s “Ireland, a Bicycle and a Tin Whistle” by David Wilson. It isn’t a novel but it is very entertaining.

In a slightly different genre - I just finished reading “My Old True Love” by Sheila Kay Adams. Fictionalized account of her own family’s history in civil war era North Carolina. Adams is a great ballad singer and storyteller, and the whole book is tied together with the old British-turned-Appalachian ballads.

Along the same lines, “The Songcatcher” by Sharyn McCrumb (*not* the same as the movie by that name that came out a couple years ago). Basically fictionalized history but has an interesting thread about the transmission of traditional music. McCrumb has a whole series of novels named after old ballads, but this is the only one (so far) that actually uses the music as a theme.

And a friend just recommended a series of books to me, “The Songkiller Saga” by Elizabeth Ann Scarborough. I don’t know the plots but Scarborough is a Nebula award winning science fiction author, and I’m planning to check them out.

Re: Novels that feature the music

I mentioned this in a similar thread a while back, so I’ll copy and paste here:

Try Eugene McEldowney’s “The Wee Faloorie Man,” his novel of Martin McBride, a Catholic boy growing up in post-World War II Belfast, based on some true-life experiences of his wife, Maura. It does have a few of the trademarks of 20th-century Irish biographies, namely the impractical, soft-souled Da and the self-sacrificing Ma, as well as the protagonist’s tug-of-war between his adolescent sexual urges and his faith. But there are some quite poignant parts describing changes in Martin’s relationship with his parents, not only with age but his discovery of a family secret. What’s more, there are numerous references to the Irish music revival of the 50s and 60s.
