Scottish Tune Recommendations for the Whistle?

Hello!

I’m a beginner whistle player living in Hawaii who has been focusing on ITM and I’ve just started to feel relatively comfortable with jigs and reels, but I’ve found that I have a real love for the sound and feel of the Scottish tunes I’ve heard.

Don’t get me wrong - I absolutely love the Irish music I’ve been exposed to, but I’ve been saving for a set of smallpipes (and I love the bagpipes in general) and there is just something about the ‘feel’ of Scottish music that really calls to me…

So, I was wondering if anyone could suggest any Scottish tunes that would be suitable for a high D whistle? I really don’t read music well, and learning strictly by ear is challenging for me, but I’ve found that I can learn a basic version of these tunes using the ABC notation and MIDI file, and that jigs, reels, and simple waltzes are among the easiest tunes for me to decipher, while strathspeys, airs, and other types tend to sound unrecognizable at my current skill level (but I’m open to learn and working hard)!

Thank you for anyone taking the time to read this and respond - I live in Hawaii and have yet to meet a single person interested in any type of Celtic music on the island that I’m on. Also, I’m not a particularly gifted musician, so the initial learning curve was challenging with the whistle, but I’ve been listening closely to plenty of great music, practicing daily, and finding great personal reward with the journey. (I wanted to offer this brief introduction so you know I’m not intending to perform and don’t have any nearby sessions or like-minded friends to connect with here - just lots of space with no neighbors and no one who knows how these tunes should sound anyway ;-)

Lastly (if this helps), I have most recently learned to play decent renditions of Miss Monaghan, Garrett Barry’s, Rolling in Ryegrass and some great jigs like the Blackthorn Stick, Lark in the Morning, the Kesh, and others using cuts and simple ornamentation…

Thank you all for reading this!

Rich

Re: Scottish Tune Recommendations for the Whistle?

There is some pretty advanced stuff mixed in with that tune list above, for a beginner, so don’t get disheartened by trying to tackle all of that. Be aware too that a lot of the abc sheet music on this site is not very accurate when it comes down to it and a lot of people have posted some very dubious, by ear, transcriptions of tunes which are played quite differently in the real world.
If you want to play smallpipes eventually and are using the whistle as a learning aid all well and good but my advice would be to get hold of a bagpipe practice chanter as soon as you can. That way you will be learning the proper fingering for the smallpipes when you finally get them.
There are a lot of Scottish music resources around when you start to look and a few half decent session videos on youtube that sometimes even include tune lists. As you are here though and given your interest in the pipes I point you in the direction of “bogman’s” profile on this site. You will find a lengthy list of many of the tunes that are posted here suitable for smallpipes and by default whistle there.

Re: Scottish Tune Recommendations for the Whistle?

There are a lot of good tunes in the lists given in previous posts, but not all of them make good choices for the whistle, especially for a beginner. A great number of the fiddle tunes use the lower octave quite a lot, which is not brilliant for the whistle. Most of the tunes in G need a C natural and many in A major need both high and low G#, which all require fingering which is not easy to do on fast tunes (one notable exception is the Spoot o’ Skeery, which is hexatonic in G and doesn’t need a C note).

My advice to a beginner would be to concentrate on Highland pipe tunes. The entire GHB repertoise can be played on the whistle, using the octave A to A’ plus the G natural below. This means that you get a good variety of tunes in A mixolydian and D major (i.e. both tonalities using C#, F# and G natural), plus some other tonalities. These will mostly use the upper octave, meaning that you can actually be heard along with other instruments.
