Re: Why do so many ceili(dh) bands have a sound equivalent to ‘paint-by-the-numbers’? :-/

A lot of ceili music is played fast, perhaps too fast, in order to facilitate dancers. When playing that fast for dancing, the main thing is the rhythm (hence, the prominence of drums). The faster you play, the more of the nuances, variations, and interesting ornamentation, that tends to get left out just so you can keep up. It is meant to dance to, not so much sit and listen to. Sort of like listening to disco music. It does get old after a while and starts to sound the same. But, in the proper context, the dancers love it.

About 3 winters ago I and my brother were at our local music pub Malarkey’s, taking a break from holiday shopping. The pub was closed, but we saw our barkeep friend inside stocking or doing something, and he let us in for a glass of whiskey. Before long I was conducting an education in Irish music for what turned into about 8-10 friends. None of these people had any experience of Irish music beyond Flogging Molly or Celtic Women. A few had hear *of* the Chieftains, but none actually heard their music. This was the audience.
After viewing some great clips on the big screens in the pub, everyone was enjoying it and expressing positive comments about all that I shared. I inadvertently clicked on a clip of some céilí band, and paused for a few moments, sort of fascinated, before quickly turning it off. This got the biggest reaction of the night, with my other musician buddy making a sour face and saying in so many words that he didn’t like it. Interestingly, everyone that commented on the clip (about 3-4 people) referred to it as the Irish equivalent to the German polka music we’re forced to listen to from time to time around here at festivals, church gatherings, or every Saturday morning on the public radio station.
Another time my classical musician friend actually referred to them as simply “polka bands” always with sour face, and said adamantly he “did not like that kind of music.” Anyone I’ve ever spoken to about them here sees this comparison. The “boom-chuk” and the “oompah” aren’t too distantly related perhaps.

Re: Why do so many ceili(dh) bands have a sound equivalent to ‘paint-by-the-numbers’? :-/

If you mean competition ceilidh bands all sounding alike, it’s the nature of competition, in most cases.

People who are serious about winning competitions soon find out what the judges like, and follow suit. This tends to create sameness, conformity, because “the nail which sticks up gets hammered down”.

Look at the Irish dancer’s costumes! They all have to wear those wigs now, and all the costumes look more or less alike. Why? Because if you show up in a costume of the style of ten or twenty years ago you’re not going to win. Having the current style of costume shows that you’re “in the know” or “in the loop”.

Same in the Scottish scene, with competition Highland Dancing and competition Pipe Bands. All the Pipe Bands dress alike: at The Worlds last year every single Grade One band wore the same kit: kilt, plain hose, Ghillie Brogues, waistcoat (no jacket), shirt and tie, Glengarry. It’s as if, if a band doesn’t wear waistcoats, or wears Balmorals, they’re yelling at the judges “hey! We’re from some backwater place and we don’t know what’s what!”

Same with the girls’ Highland Dancing outfits, those garish fuchsia & white tartans etc.

Re: Why do so many ceili(dh) bands have a sound equivalent to ‘paint-by-the-numbers’? :-/

First of all, I’d say ceili and music is a specific style. As others have pointed out, this style is centered around keeping time for dancers. I am a bit confused by the paint by numbers concept… If that is a commentary on how ceili bands play tightly together, note for note if possible, then that is a criticism of the style of music itself. If it is a comment on how all ceili bands sound the same, then you are criticizing the bands.

The first possibility confuses me. Ceili music is dance music, which puts the dancers to the forefront. Why would this be a bad thing? I will take my confusion to infer that you meant the second possibility, that all ceili bands sound a like.

If you are making this claim, then, I disagree. We could ignore the historical spectrum of ceili bands and still disagree, but let me touch on this first. The modern ceili band sound is a 1940s-50s Clare sound, derivative from the bands that won competitions in that era. In short, it was easier to judge bands if they had the same style, and it was easier to win if you copied previous winners. I got this from my friend who learned how to adjudicate the ceili band competition from PJ Hayes, who surely played a significant role in solidifying this style (I’m not saying PJ himself said this, but rather my friend, who may have deduced this themselves). I was in a band once that threw this tradition out the window, and did 1920s style ceili bands from New York. The band leader sourced every tune and instrument from archival recordings. This meant that we had a symbol on the drums, a rhythm banjo 5 string), and at one point had an upright bass. The sounds was very different, with tunes so old fashioned my great grandmother would have thought them passé. However, we still played very tightly in a rhythmic style suitable for dancers. Thus, ceili bands don’t have to sound like the classic Clare style.

Moving onto the modern ceili band scene, I still disagree that they all sound the same. I went to a few ceilis during Willie Week in Miltown Malbay and every and was very different. For example, the Tulla Ceili band and the Brian Ború ceili band had very distinct sounds. I forget how the latter sounded, to be honest, because it was my first ceili in a while and I was concentrating on the sets. Nevertheless, I enjoyed the music very much and know that it was different in style than Tulla’s. The Deenagh ceili band, in turn, was very different from the other two bands mentioned. They only had 3 members, and the keyboardist definitely didn’t play in a paint by the numbers style. He had a lot of flourish and pizazz, and even stood up and did American rock style theatrics while playing the last few sets.

Although ceili music may not be everyone’s cup of tea, I think that by necessity every band develops it’s own sound to ensure that it gets booked for gigs. Now, I have seen bands play for ceilis that don’t play in the ceili style, but that is a different can of worms altogether!

I’ve no doubt that has twisted my perspective on it all, and the recent away to Ireland, which was, already admitted, time and geographically limited.

Is there a problem? Possibly not? Some folks might like things as they are, or as they might be percieved as developing toward. Others won’t want to question it at all, seeing no reason. It is easier to accept the status quo, to take things for granted. Sometimes in questioning a thing, real or possible, one takes the extreme stance with hopes to irritate a discussion into being, or chooses emotive language, such as ‘paint-by-numbers’. Hey, even painting by numbers requires a some skill to do it well, while other folks manage to work it creatively within and beyond its percieved limitations. Others make a mess of it, and some are easily satisfied whatever the outcome.

After this recent bout of heavy listening, focused on bands, primarily ceili(dh) bands, and not yet finished, I’m feeling a bit numb as well as suffering from ringing ears, and not just from the tinitis in my right ear (heavy antibiotics caused that - fighting pneumo) ~ thump, thump, thump, thump… Another curious irritation is that tendency to pump up the tempo in the final processing of some of these commercial recordings, evidenced from the weird keys, everything up a half step, and in some cases a full step. Crazy! More daftness! But none the less influential…

There’s a loose kind of notion some folks at times raise that once you’ve made something to order, purpose built, usually about buildings, but also businesses and organizations ~ that it is the first evidence of ossification, fossilization? That doesn’t always seem to be true, but there might be something slightly IKEA about modern ceili(dh) bands. This is a tradition, in one form or another, like the main focus here, that is owned and cared for by many. It is not just tied to the island of Ireland, or any one county or organization there. Mind you, with care in the assembly you can get some damned nice things from IKEA ~ functional, useful, decorative. Other proof of possible growing rigor mortis is when ones passions become almost a religion, something not to be questioned, goosed or poked at, where any doubt of its magnificence might be considered sacrilegious.

And then there are those novitiates who think you’re only allowed an opinion if you qualify on certain points, like being a high priest or priestess, where you might be even less likely to call your faith into question, and often thinking they are in that position of privilege themselves, in the know, possessors of the only truth, the only true way. Folks with such notions seem to present their opinions as if they are part of an inner circle, amongst the privileged hierarchy. I understand, some folks do invest a great deal of their time, efforts and heart into all this, and they deserve respect, have my respect ~ if not for when they choose to shut others out because they think they know it all. I’ve been there myself, letting my passions get the better of me and that resulting in a lack of consideration for another’s differences of experience and view, where I’m not really listening, not trying to understand but have thrown up a block to that. I don’t like it when that happens, but too often I’m only aware afterwards, and it always makes me feel like shight. Like with music it’s a listening art too, one that includes how we respond to another’s opinion, with or without courtesy and consideration and patience.

Nuff said, too much really, as I let the passion run away with me again, partly generated by a recently reread few threads on site here, not this one. We’re needing and planning to head out for a long walk with just bird song and maybe the added music of a babbling brook or a rolling river, the wind in leaves, light filtered and dancing through branches and leaves, varied but solid ground underfoot ~ and not a flat-pack ceili(dh) band in earshot… ;-)

Biases, many, and we’d choose a house dance or session over a big name ceili band or festival every time, no competition… ;-)

But, when it comes down to it we generally enjoy all the dances and dancing we’ve been party to, as in the end, whatever the music is doing, or not, it’s the company that has the greater appreciation and welcome, and we’ve been lucky that mostly that has been good. There have been those few cases where someone with more exuberance than sense had bounced off of things, including other dancers, the musicians, in once case dear Johnny O‘Leary, and even responsible for causing a serious injury, as happened with one of our friends. Our friend, she ended up unable to dance for about a week, and also ended up shy of it ever since. Sometimes ’courtesy & consideration’ is lost in the melee, and that can be by both dancers and musicians…

I know accompaniment can be good and interesting, we have experienced that some, best without amplification, but the usual ceili band emphasis, after all this listening can wear ones patience down, and has resulted in this curious thread. Thanks everyone for he contributions, appreciated, something for me to think about, including introspectively. More often than not I start such things to challenge my own notions, which I often ‘question’, especially after they’ve been exaggerated by experience, in this case of commercial recordings of ceili bands, some of which I’d also discovered had been pumped up, the tempos raised in the processing… :-P I still prefer it more intimate and unamplified. :-D

Part of the reason for using ‘paint-by-numbers’ as a description is the tendency we two have noticed, by some, toward reducing a melody to its bare essentials, eliminating anything not necessary to keeping a strong and clear and steady bop. After all, as one old codger put it, “all they need is a steady beat and clapping hands can satisfy that.” I was feeling that a bit too much was being sacrificed, even down to reducing the number of notes played, a kind of minimalist take on it all, so we can ALL agree on those few notes and keep it simple. But, not everybody takes it down that way, but it’s not just about size, I’ve heard that paint-by-numbers approach with just a box and a guitarist ceili band…
