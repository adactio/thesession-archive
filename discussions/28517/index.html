Thanks for that Silver Spear. I suppose what I have in mind is an open session, but with a threshold for regular participation. So if somebody turns up and asks if they can play, then I would tell them what would be expected. If somebody turned up with a descant recorder or a bass I would tell them that those instruments aren’t appropriate for the kind of music we play, I’d let them play for an evening if they still wanted to, but I would expect them to come back with a suitable instrument next time.

In my admittedly limited experience of sessions, most problems are caused by guitarists. I would tell a guitarist that we only want one set of chords going on at any time, and that they must be appropriate chords and played in an appropriate fashion.

We would only want one bodhran at a time, and again I would insist that it must be played appropriately.

I don’t have any problem at all with sincere and musically sensitive learners on instruments like the fiddle, concertina, flute. In my limited experience they play relatively quietly, more experienced musicians help them keep in tune and in time, so there isn’t a problem.

Re: I’m desperate to start a session near Whitley Bay. Second attempt.

Het Bernie, there is absolutely nothing traditional about the type of session you want to run. Sessions where only specific instruments and specific types of tune are welcome are very much an invention of the 1970s.

The real tradition is of people gathering in someones home and entertaining each other while they waited for television to be invented. If little jonny was learning the trombone at school, little Jonny would play the trombone. If the only song Aunt Mable knew was Danny Boy, thats what Aunt Mable would sing. Every evening. And there was always a plethora of bones, spoons and other beaty, rattley things that we all abhore (though I think they were at least spared the shakey egg). So if you want to be ‘traditional’ , and to have any chance of running a successful session, you need to be inclusive - put aside your preconceptions of what you want your session to be, and concentrate on making the best music you can with whatever musicians turn up.

Re: I’m desperate to start a session near Whitley Bay. Second attempt.

It’s possible to establish guidelines that stick, under certain circumstances. My partner and I started a new session a little over a year ago. It’s “intermediate” level, for people working their way up to playing at the more advanced sessions in the area.

We’re the “official” hosts of the session because we made the original arrangements with the owner of the bar, and we continue to maintain that relationship. We send out email reminders before each session, and we maintain a small web site with tune lists, links to learning resources, etc. We make an effort to attend every scheduled session, to make sure at least someone shows up if it’s a slow night. All of this is a lot of work, but it helps keep the session alive.

From the very beginning, we had only two rules we tried to enforce: One guitar player at a time, and one bodhran player at a time, please.

We started the session with a small group of like-minded friends, so this has worked out fine, so far. Because we’re in an area where OldTime and Folk music is hugely popular, we would be swamped with guitar players if we didn’t have that rule, and if we didn’t make an effort to enforce it. That one rule about guitars keeps our session an Irish session and not a Grateful Dead hootenanny.

We’re much more relaxed on other things. One session member plays recorder. I’d rather this person would play whistle or flute, but he’s good on recorder and is eager to learn and play the tunes. Enthusiasm and a desire to work on repertoire counts for a lot, in our little group. Another session regular plays chords on mandolin. I’d rather they learn the melodies, and maybe they will someday. The fiddlers are the core of this session, and they keep things rolling along.

We’ve only formalized a rule that’s applied informally at just about all the higher-level sessions in the area. The second guitar player to start opening his or her case, while another guitar player is already playing, is likely to earn a gentle shake of the head in many sessions around here. No, it’s not completey friendly and welcoming to everyone who wants to play, but there are plenty of OldTime and Folk jams in the area where any number of guitar players are welcome.

I think the exclusion of melody instruments is more problematic, but if a group of like-minded people wants it that way, then more power to ’em. I would never try to tell an established group of players how to run things.
