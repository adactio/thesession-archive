Many zero alcohol beers are much higher in sugar than their alcoholic counterparts, so if you are aging with a tendency towards type two diabetes (as I am) it’s worth looking at the labels. Guinness zero has a higher sugar content than regular Guinness (in cans, 0.5g per can vs 3g for zero, which is still only half a teaspoon), and it’s better than a lot of others. And it tastes pretty close. (I believe that Heineken zero has 10x the sugar of its regular beer.)

Re: Zero alcohol beers during sessions in Ireland

Interestingly, the New York Times had an article recently on the recent popularity of non-alcohol beers in Ireland’s pub culture.

Here’s a link, should be free of paywall: https://www.nytimes.com/2026/03/11/world/europe/ireland-pubs-alcohol-free.html?unlocked_article_code=1.ZFA.I7Yv.9z05gdfJG5CD&smid=url-share

As a brewer, I can confirm that beers of any sort are a high-calorie proposition. If you taste the pre-fermented beer (“wort”) you’ll find that it’s about as sweet as a typical soft drink - some of that sugar is converted to alcohol in fermentation, but not that much, and the calories remain. As I understand it, there are two ways to get to non-alcohol beer, either by stopping fermentation early or by filtering out the alcohol. In the former case, all of the sugar remains. In the latter case, a very small part of the fermented sugar is removed. In both cases, you’re left with basically the same sugar content as an equivalent amount of soda pop. But much tastier, if you ask me.
