Re: Irish National Anthem/A Soldier’s Song

I don’t get to many sessions nowadays because of other commitments, but the one I try to get to is an eclectic English session which always finishes with the attractive English tune “In the Garden Green”, the signal for the barman to lower the lights.

I’m old enough to remember the days in British cinemas when after the last showing of the evening the National Anthem was played, everybody stood to attention and would not have thought of moving until it finished. Understandable, I suppose because WW2 was fresh in everybody’s mind, and we were still going through its grinding aftermath (food and fuel shortages, rationing, etc).

One of the few occasions today when the British National Anthem is played is on the Last Night of the Proms. I don’t know how many of you watched it this year (some spectacular violin playing there by Janine Jansen BTW - worth watching on YouTube), but it was obvious that “Rule Britannia”, “Land of Hope and Glory”, and even “Jerusalem” were sung with significantly more gusto by the audience than they did the National Anthem. For reasons that elude me the Proms organizers chose Benjamin Britten’s setting of the NA sung by a professional choir, and I’ve never heard it performed so sloooow. The professional choir was evidently necessary because they were able to sing at that speed without having to take a breath after each word.

The tune of Britain’s NA isn’t actually an unattractive tune if speeded up a little and played instrumentally, but it is difficult now to forget its Royal Hymn association. I believe the tune is older than the lyrics and was good enough for Beethoven and Paganini to compose sets of variations on (Paganini’s version of course being next to impossible to play except by the Chosen Few).

One wonders what is going to happen to the National Anthem (not to mention the Union Flag) if the Scottish Independence vote tomorrow should be in the affirmative. Interestingly, there is the possibility (statistically possible given a vote that is too close to call) that the result could be exactly 50/50. Apparently, no-one has considered this possibility and there is no legal or constitutional provision for doing anything about it. Personally, I think this would be a rare case where the Crown would have to exert its authority and cast a deciding vote, in which case the usual protocol would apply as in the case of a Board Chairman (neutral by definition) casting a deciding vote in the event of a hung vote by the Board, whereby he should cast his vote so as to maintain the status quo.

Anyway, to undigress to the Irish National Anthem, I remember a ceili dance in Bristol many years ago in the days when it was customary for the evening to finish with the Anthem, except that on this particular occasion some young fool of an Englishman thought it was another dance and continued dancing. The Irish there were not entirely sympathetic to his misunderstanding.
