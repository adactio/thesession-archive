Am I behind the times?

I happened to follow a few links from the session section on this website and I
sensed a void in my soul and stomach as it dawned on me that I am now
undeniably behind the times. I do not have a facebook page and/or use any other social networking vehicle on which to announce my every waking activity, thought or complaint. This is (almost) the only place where I will write anything that I deem worthy of permanent recording for all time.

On this website, just being curious, I clicked on a link to a random session, to see what others are reporting elsewhere in the world about their local sessions.
The first session I landed on here had links to a facebook page and a twitter page about the session. Thesession.org clearly is insufficient for this crowd to stay abreast of what’s happening at the local session.

I clicked on the Facebook link and I read a few of the posts on the public Facebook page and that’s when I started to feel queasy. There was just too much information. There were too many pictures. There were too many invitations to share on twitter and other websites about what I was thinking. I just couldn’t go on reading about the details of this session, as juicy and non-controversial as the reporting was.

I am not condemning any session or group of musicians. This is not a complaint about you who may use these other electronic social networking vehicles for promoting the session. You should go on doing whatever you think is best. I only clicked on one session from here and this led me to the facebook pages. For all I know this is the only session that has this level of reporting outside of this website. I’m not going to do this again. I won’t follow any more session.org links to public facebook and/or twitter pages. I didn’t like the feeling it gave me. It was like spying or eavesdropping. It was none of my business.

I guess I am behind the times….

Re: Am I behind the times?

Halfway, you are not behind the times - you may even be ahead of them. It is those who have not learned to use technolgogy *discriminately* who have yet to catch up ;-)

I don’t use Facebook, and hove only just started a very limited MySpace site - and then for the single express purpose of posting some music I made. No gossip, nothing. I don’t even keep my mobile phone switched on most days (it’s just there for emergencies etc.) and our T.V. remains resolutely switched off most of the time. Too many better things (like ITM) to do!

But we are not anti-technology, we just use it when we need/choose to, and as my information-professional wife would tell you, knowing how to sift skillfully for what you actually want (let alone exercising judgment about what to post int he first place) is a skill many people still don’t have. It is a matter of using it *appropriately* - for example, I choose the appropriate ‘technolgy’ to create the kind of music I love - doesn’t matter that it is supposedly ‘old’ - it works - better than any newer technology does.

On the other hand, there is no doubt in my mind that traditional culture is the better for technology: this website being the prime example. What is more, how many people have used it to make contacts when they would otherwise have had none? (says he who has finally just tracked down a local ITM session using the internet). And what is more, I can watch/listen to broadcast media such as BBC Alba that I would never have been able to see otherwise - some great music on there!

I agree, there is a major lack of electronic ettiquette out there, but we ought to be able to do something about that in time. There is also a load of drivel (such as the seven vacuous phone calls that a car-passenger of mine felt compelled to make in a 60-minute journey the other day…) - but is it not just the modern equivalent of the ‘traditional’ natter over the garden fence?
