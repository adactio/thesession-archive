Re: What’s in you instrument case other than your instrument?

“Jeez, dudes… no weed?”

That reminds me of a story. I was in France, hitching from Calais to Lorient. I had spotted a large patch of Mugwort (Artemisia vulgaris) growing by the road and, having heard tell of its properties (promoting lucid dreaming, apparently), I was curious to give it a try. So I picked a generous bundle of it and stuffed it in the pocket of my fiddle case, with the intention of putting it under my pillow (i.e. pile of spare clothes) in my tent whilst I slept.

Later on, I was trying to pick up a lift from the beginning of an autoroute, just the right side of the ‘no pedestrians’ sign. I was aware that, at a junction close by, there was a team of police on speed patrol. I was conscious of the fact that i. they might take issue with my standing in this location and ii. I was carrying substantial quantity of a strong-smelling herb, whose leaves bear a passing resemblance to those of Marijuana. I had images of being asked to show them the contents of my instrument cases, being arrested on suspicion of possession of an illegal substance, being strip-searched, ultimately being let off when they managed to confirm that this was not Marijuana (nor had I any hidden anywhere about my person), but being left looking the fool, as they would obviously think *I* thought it was Marijuana.

So, I tried my best to hide myself just out of view. But I couldn’t resist a peek, just to see if they were still there. Naturally, I was spotted, and two police officers came striding purposefully towards me. The female officer informed me, “I’m sorry, sir, you can’t stand here – it’s a motorway,” or words to that effect, and helpfully directed me to a more suitable spot. No search, no arrest, no trouble…

Sorry if you were expecting some dramatic climax.

“One time our fiddler opened his case only to discover that he’s left his fiddle at home.”

I once *left * a session without my mandolin. It was an after-hours session in a pub in Camden, London (which is no more, but shall nevertheless remain unnamed). Camden is not a place you leave your prized possessions unattended (like anywhere in London, but a bit more so than some places). I had walked a couple of hundred paces down the street towards the bus stop when I noticed my case feeling uncharacteristically light. I laid it on the ground, opened it and, sure enough, it was empty. In a blind panic, I ran back to the pub, found the door still unlocked – and there on the table, exactly where I had left it, was my mandolin. Two of the musicians were still sitting there deep in conversation, probably unaware that I had left the pub at all… Occasional occurrences like this manage to feed an ever-present fear of having left something (usually a musical instrument or associated item) behind somewhere.

Re: What’s in you instrument case other than your instrument?

“There’s also those times when you’re away just one night for a gig or session and you try and get a change of underwear, toothbrush etc crammed into your instrument case.”

Depending of the size of your instrument relative to that of your girth, you might be able to put your clean unmentionables *on* your instrument, which would only add half a millimetre or so all round (as opposed to folding them neatly and placing them underneath, which would occupy several mm of depth). Alternatively, if it has a large soundhole, you could stuff them inside. You could probably just about stuff them into the barrel of a low D whistle – but it would be wise to extract them before arriving at the session or going on stage.

If you have a stringed instrument, a toothbrush ought to fit fairly comfortably alongside the neck. If you play bodhrán, you could probably get a full change of clothes inside – and wrap a few extra layers around it.
