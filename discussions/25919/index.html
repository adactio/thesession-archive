Added by David Levine on November 4th, 2010

Playing music or playing notes?

Fiddlers especially - and beginning fiddlers in particular - should heed what Caoimhin O Raghallaigh has to say about music, most notably the last sentence:

“Well, if the thing that interests you about traditional music is the notes, then it makes perfect sense that you’d want to play as many of them as possible, as fast as possible,” he says. “But if the thing that interests you is the state of mind created, I think it makes sense to create the kind of music I’m creating now.

“I think of music much more in terms of sound than in terms of notes.”

So…. how’s your tone? How’s your music?

Re: Playing music or playing notes?

The notes are only there to convey the tune. If you could play a tune without the notes (see some of Sara’s videos from O’Raghallaigh’s concert with Brendan Begley…) then you’d be fine. Playing the notes without the tune is far too common, and not so fine.

As for ornaments on the harmonica, they’re like trying to play a roll on the mandolin. You can get each of the notes in that the book says a roll should have, if you work at it, but it’s not a roll because a roll is not a set of notes. Instead, you have to focus on shaping the tune using the instrument you’re playing, rather than trying to pretend that a harmonica is a fiddle.

Actually that is not strictly correct. I’ve heard a chromatic harmonica played on many, probably dozens of, occasions in comhaltas competitions.

But they compete in the miscellaneous category along with other instruments that don’t quite fit. The harmonica, or mouth-organ or whatever it is comhaltas calls the specific category is not for chromatic harps. This means that people are being judged on their playing of the same basic instrument as each other (except of copurse in misc :-)).

In a similar manner recorders don’t compete with whistles, nor do three row button accordeons compete in the 2-row competition (or separate melodeon competition), 5-string banjos don’t compete in the (tenor) banjo competition. Bouzoukis, Mandolas etc don’t compete in mandolin.

You could perhaps querie the last example since “low whistles” compete (correctly in my opinion, but I’m open minded as to agruement) in the whistle category. Re: Playing music or playing notes?

>The consensus seemed to be that it really doesn’t matter what
>instrument you play, the style and sound should really always be
>the same, so therefore take inspiration from *any* instrument.
>But that doesn’t square with the above.

Oh, but it does indeed. You can’t play a fiddle roll on the mandolin, even if you can execute the notes, because a fiddle roll is not about the sequence of notes. A roll on the fiddle (or the flute, or the pipes) is a means of breaking up a continuous flow of sound, not a set of little notes that you put in as “ornaments” to the tune, to sort of “doll it up”. You should never perceive the “graces” in a roll as individual notes, the roll is one piece of added percussive sound. This is what you can’t do on the mandolin, because there is no continuous stream of sound to break up.

What you can and should and must do as a mandolin player is to hear what it is that the rolls and crans and other “ornaments” are in service of, and try to get that. And that you can do with the tools you have available to you on the mandolin.
