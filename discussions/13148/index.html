Re: 3 months in and looking for constructive criticism

Good to keep a tempo where you have and can maintain a steady beat, as KML says, don’t rush it, but not just expectations, tempo too, find where you can be with full control and little problems, and then step down another notch.

You sound and seem to be constricting your breathing. When you inhale it can also at times sound a bit raspy, suggesting you may be closing down your throat. You would benefit from some basic diaphragm exercises and lessons, as for singing. You need to open it up and learn what that exactly means. If you can find a singer or coach in the area just ask for pointers. One clue ~ be fat. Breathing has to be the diaphragm, or belly if you like, not in the chest. As well, all passages to and from the lungs have to be open. Some flute players tuck in their chin to their neck too much, and this can restrict flow. Breathing is, as you can imagine, a very important part of wind playing, and eventually, once you get more control, it is also a wonderful tool for adding interest and driving and varying a melody. The goal eventually is to choose to breath because you like what it does where you put it, rather than just having to breath. In its simplest form this it is used to accent and articulate phrases…

Don’t think that, as everything else considered ‘natural’, there isn’t something more to learn about breathing, even in its most basic life sustaining form. The Buddhist focus on breath is not just a whim, aside from our basic need of oxygen to sustain life. If you learn to breath more openly it will benefit more than just your flute playing… Opening up that opens up other things in the process ~ including the flow and life in your music making…

You’ve done well for 3 months, and as others have said, are brave for risking your early efforts here, but what better way to seek some additional guidance, well done, and best of luck in your development…

Re: 3 months in and looking for constructive criticism

Sounding just fine, Rhod--you’re obviously carrying over everything you already know about this music from the fiddle, eh? And since eyou have all that--timing, a sense of phrasing, pulse, intonation, etc.--the key to flute will be embouchure. So here are some tips from a fellow fiddler turned fluter….

To firm up your embouchure while keeping those lips relaxed, spend as much time as you can on long tones, always listening to refine and focus the tone. Do this every day, and every single time you play. I often start on an “easy” note, like the first octave G or B, and then slowly work up or down from there. But I also have a low tolerance for scale-like exercises, so I’m more inclined to play a tune chock full of long tones, like the Aran Boat.

Along with that, really pay attention to where you tend to blow harder--those are precisely the spots you need to focus your embouchure (and NOT blow harder). The best trick I’ve found so far in these situations is to catch myself blowing harder or trying to push more air, and then force myself to do the exact opposite--reduce the amount of air, and then use my lips to bring the tone and volume back to where it was without increasing the volume of air.

That’s what I notice about the 2nd half of your recording--as you start to get dizzy and out of breath (I know the feeling :-) ), you try to push even more air in to make up the difference. Of course, that only makes things worse. Instead, try using less air with a more focussed embouchure.

You’re well on your way--and it’s a ton of fun, eh? Brian’s lesson on the Knotted Cord is also a good one and helped me no end.

I like ‘c’’s point about keeping your throat open. An easy way to do that is to look slightly up as you play. Someone once told me to look at where the wall joins the ceiling from across the room. This lifts your chin just a bit and keeps you from staring at the floor with your windpipe bent over on itself.

Re: 3 months in and looking for constructive criticism

Great stuff is right, lots of excellent guidance. I was away and came back with what I realized I hadn’t said, but the ol’ Cheshire Cat got there first, chin up! And the bit about playing long notes, that is part of the slowing down and taking things in their stride. If you start out right you won’t have a load of bad habits to undo later. Get the tone, and as the cat said, don’t overblow…

While ‘breathing’ is natural, or so we think, for many of us it becomes an expression in some ways of our frustrations and pent up emotions ~ being ‘uptight’ would be a good way of putting it. It is easy to build in tensions, including in your breathing, even without knowing it. “In and Out” is good, not “Up and Down”. The most common fault in breathing is to breath with the rib cage rising up and down, holding in the belly. That’s what I meant about “be fat”. That’s where the movement should be, not in your rib cage.

Another sign of ‘struggle’ is when odd sounds become a part of the experience. I knew a mandolin / banjo player that used to grunt when he played. He was actually quite good, but his grunts were out of time with his music and very off putting, more so than others I know who make faces or stick their tongues out when they play. It is hard not to laugh. It is also damn hard to undo something later on, like grunts or rasps. These things in wind playing can be part of the want to control the air, which should never be done by constricting the throat or getting ‘uptight’. That’s what your lips are for…

‘C’ I get a bit of a rasp on my inhales no matter how relaxed my throat is. It’s less noticeable now only because I don’t gulp air in as big a chunk as I did when first starting out on flute. Like slainte, I too wondered about working on easier, more flute-friendly tunes.

Mountain Road is a simple reel, good for long tones on the F and second octave D, and a fair tune to start practicing cuts and rolls on.

Lots of jigs are good beginner flute tunes. One of my favorites on flute is The Orphan. Also try Christy Barry’s No. 1, The Kerfunteun (in D), and Joy of My Life.

All of these are in the archives here, and are amply recorded.

I also did a lot of long tone practice on things like MacPherson’s Lament, Down By the Sally Gardens, Skye Boat Song, and a John McCusker waltz called Sailing Through the Narrows (also in the archives here). That way, I was learning to focus my embouchure while also coming to grips with breathing and phrasing on flute. And enjoying some brilliant melodies.
