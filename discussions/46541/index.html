I just watched this clip of Sean Ryan playing Paddy Kelly’s reel in the tune section.
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q0pXmS1Ljmk
While his fiddling in that clip is unquestionably excellent, the video quite depresses me. I mean, look at all of those serious and gloomy faces! The music is great but I would have hated to have to sit though it like that. I’ve seen a few video’s of old Irish trad players like this. It seems intuitively wrong to me that such music should be listened to with such intense seriousness and no joy on anybody’s faces. Is this, or was this a normal thing?

Like a plasterer throwing ‘pebble dash’ on the outside of an Irish cottage during the post WW2 era, a couple of pointers.

First and most important of all, it is Kildare and the fiddler is not a local. None appear to be familiar with him. Second, devoid of fiddling and fiddlers, most busy with just surviving rarely was it a thing among them. Then there is the fact that in Ireland most families did not get into Traditional music at all, Country & Western being the big thing even as R&R became popular next door in the UK. The audience was probably recruited from some small village for the movie, with little concern about involvement.

To help get this picture, recall at Mullingar, Westmeath Co., the music so rare that in the late ‘40’s locals organized ’Comhaltas Ceoltóirí Éireann’ in order to rescue it. Kildare Co. borders Westmeath Co.
