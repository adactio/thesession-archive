I think there are a number of tunes which get played in sessions because they were recorded by such-and-such, presumably brilliantly. But these recording, as seductive as they may be, might lock their versions in amber to the point most sessions cannot reproduce their quality. So if an entire session is eager enough to play them I’m fine with that. What I don’t like, on almost any tune, is when a session is fragmented (due to what players think about the tune). If I do not like a tune I either don’t play it; or I put aside my prejudice and make my best effort to join in.

We play any number of tunes which were recorded (or composed) by such and such. We probably don’t come anywhere near the original. But if most of the session wants to play them it’s a pleasant surprise. Usually it’s only disappointing when we hold the bar too high for ourselves.

↳ Posted by 🎻Johnny Jay, Melody Player 🎻 on May 3rd, 2017.

Re: Most disliked tunes.

I’m a bit of an Irish trad purist, and not to like recent foreign imports from other traditions and modern compositions that don’t really sound ‘Irish trad’. I know, I know, at one point Ed Reavy or Paddy Fahey might not have sounded ‘trad’ and I love their tunes, and I know a load of the Irish trad tune arsenal may have originated in Scotland, but you know what I mean.

Within the established ‘Irish trad’ repertoire I feel that the playing of the musician is generally more important than the tune itself. A good musician can make the most basic or overplayed tune sound fresh. I think Paddy Keenan is a good example - he’s recorded a heap of those old warhorse tunes and they all sound great under his fingers. Actually now that I think about it, quite a lot of pipers in particular seem to continue to record and play those warhorses (think Banish Misfortune, Garrett Barry’s Jig, Rakish Paddy, The Old Bush and so forth).

I’m very fond of some of the most hackneyed of old tunes, and if you stayed in my house for more than a day or two you’ll most likely hear me play The Kesh, The Blarney Pilgrim, Jim Ward’s, Craig’s Pipes etc.

All that being said, I’m not sure the Concertina Reel has any redeeming features. I can just about tolerate it when played by Tony MacMahon or The Chieftains, but I wouldn’t choose to listen to it.

Trish makes a good point about certain tunes being unappealing for instrument specific reasons.

Either it doesn’t sound quite right on a certain instrument or there may be some tricky bits that you just can’t quite get on your chosen instrument. So, a lot of the time “I hate that tune” really means “ I hate that fact that I can’t play it properly….yet”. Of course, we can play them eventually by which time we are often heartily sick of them.
That’s another reason why we often have a disdain for so called beginner tunes or old chestnuts. It’s because we probably spent a disproportionate time trying to learn these at a time when our skills weren’t quite honed. So, it is maybe “Us” rather that the tunes themselves?

Re: Most disliked tunes.

I am not a fan of hornpipes or polkas - though there’s always the exception which I like.

Funnily enough, I don’t like Tam Lin either - the way it’s played at Sessions - but I have heard it with a different rhythm played as part of a Scottish dance set, and then I like it. :)

In Scottish dancing there are some wonderful reels, jigs and strathspeys - and there are the ones I call ‘chuggers’ because they’re honest, sonsy, thistle-waving tunes played by chugging accordions. Perhaps my two greatest bugbears are J. B. Milne (my friend’s favourite!) and The Trip to Bavaria.

A lot of the ‘in-vogue’ tunes at sessions I am not very fond of either - Catharsis, Jump at the Sun, Maggie’s Pancakes - and there are some others that I consider plodding, such as Lady Anne Montgomery. I tend to like the older tunes - Neil Gow & William Marshall for Scottish, and the lyrical tunes from O’Neil for Irish.

But I don’t often get my wish to play the good yins at sessions, so I do it (I mean play my favourite fiddle tunes, Gobby) in the privacy of my own home, and it’s heaven. :)
