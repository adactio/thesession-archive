“A must for those across the music spectrum who love a dance. If anyone ever thought to ask, ”What would the world be like if James Brown played the accordion?“ This album is the answer! http://www.footstompin.com/products/cds/attention_all_personnel

“Complimenting traditional Scottish folk music with a mix of dance and funk grooves, Croft No. Five’s debut album ”Attention All Personnel“ is a must for those across the music spectrum who love a dance. A personal favourite, these tunes are a brilliant combination of differing genres with complete conviction and originality. Those who find this to their taste will be begging for more - Fantastic!”

What worries me about such categories, name calling, is that if someone decides they don’t like a certain way things, that they can be slotted into a convenient category, called a so-and-so. There’s also a bit of “if you don’t agree with me your wrong, you must be a hardcore so-and-so”.

While I can appreciate hard fought for skill, and talent, and while I might find something cliche, naive, hackneyed and silly, and I may not like it, skill and talent aside, why should I be bracketed into a category that is unclear, derogatory in use, or simply in now way could ever represent my world view and attitudes. Weejie, with me it is NEVER, or usually so :-/, about an individual or a group. And where terms and name calling are concerned, it ain’t really about whoever wrote the original review. It is about the whole divisive nature of it all, the terminology, the silliness of it being ‘scary, to anyone, though maybe that’s what is imagined, to shake things up, to challenge the status quo, whatever that is. And it’s this cop out aside that if someone doesn’t like the silliness they MUST be a ’hardcore traditionalist‘ or ’traddie’?

Personally, I don’t like what they’ve done, but I’m neither a ‘hardcore taditionalist’, or don’t expect I am, though some have thrown that at me as if it successfully devalue my opinion, and I’m hardly scared. I’m never bored, but I can always choose to go elsewhere for a listen, or to have a good laugh, snarl or wince. However, while I don’t like it, there’s no way I could deny someone else’s pleasures there.

I quite enjoy some experiments and fusions, and sometimes they are done well and with a humour I can enjoy. Sometimes, as here, sorry, I don’t enjoy it, don’t find it well balanced, and find it a bit too much in ones face, that, in my estimation of it, they are trying too hard to be cute… What Silver said, to draw attention for attentions sake. Make a lot of noise, that will do it. Some folks like noise. I’m not a big fan. I prefer, as a first choice, things acoustic, more laid back, more fun, less egomaniacal. :-D

But, my opinions aside, thiis in the main about the use of terminology to pigeon hole someone’s opinion as less valued…
