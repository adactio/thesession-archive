I see a lot of banjo players leave their clip-on tuners on the headstock while they play. What’s up with that?

I use an electric tuner on my fiddle before a session and during it if I’m off. I’m a little embarrassed, wish I could just do it by ear but I’d rather be in tune than cool. There’s one concertina that’s always a few cents north and if it’s there I try to take that into account. The clip-on tuners are best for me in a noisy pub. Tell the truth, when I’ve had a few pints I’d rather rely on the tuner than my ears.

I just loaned my mandolin to a nephew whom I’m trying to wean away from the rock and blues his dad plays. I mentioned that the strings were old and had lost the sparkle, and he said not to worry, he’d take it to the music shop and have new ones put on. I told him he needed to learn to do it himself, that he’d learn a lot about the mechanics of tuning and pitch after he’d broken a few strings.

Almost every string player goes out of tune now and then. But it’s hard to believe that anyone could be around stringed instruments for any length of time or be serious about playing and not know how to change and tune up the strings.

Re: ?????

Electronic tuners are frowned upon by many musicians and several regulars here. There’s been several discussions re this in the past.

In sessions, it’s the usual practice to try and tune to each other or (preferably) the instrument which is most difficult to retune eg an accordion or similar or, perhaps, the leader who may or may not have the best ear in the world. So, the pitch you get on the elctronic tuner may not always be identical.

Also, there are many of these tuners which are inaccurate…. but they are much more accurate than some dissenters would have you believe….. it’s more likely that they are not used properly!

Another problem is intonation and how the instrument is “set up”. The open strings may be in perfect pitch but the “fretted” or “fingered” notes are slightly out. I’d imagine temperature, age of strings etc would all be a factor here too.

I like to use a tuner as it’s a quick way of getting “near” to where I should be and it’s also very handy in a busy session. However, I still tend to do a lot of “Fine tuning” by ear.

Re: ?????

“In sessions, it’s the usual practice to try and tune to each other or (preferably) the instrument which is most difficult to retune eg an accordion or similar”

As an exponent of one of these more-difficult-to-retune instruments, I wouldn’t let anyone tune to me. Harmonicas are tuned to around A442-443 generally and if everyone else tuned to that the advantage of the slight brightness of sound this gives me would be lost. In addition, many harmonicas are not tuned to equal temperament, so “give me an A” could result in double-figure cents inaccuracy. I know that harmonica players really ought to know about how their instruments are fine-tuned, but my experience is that most haven’t a clue.
