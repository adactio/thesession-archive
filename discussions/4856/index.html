Recording in Sessions

Hello All,

I’ve been reading and enjoying most of the posts on this site for the past year or so.

I’d like to know what people think about the use of minidisc/tape recorders in sessions?

Do you find it off putting? Are you generally asked by the person recording if it’s o.k. to record?

The reason I ask is a few of the older players I know hate the things and feel very uncomfortable with the whole idea of someone recording not only their playing but everything they say during the course of an evening.

From various discussions I’ve had, the cardinal sin seems to be when an individual whips out the minidisc/tape recorder plonks it on the table and starts recording without asking anyone beforehand…

Apologies if this subject has been done to death, I did a quick search of the old threads but couldn’t find anything specific.

All the best

Re: Recording in Sessions

I find it a bit off-putting myself, because then I have to wonder if my own interpretation / variations / setting / blunders of whatever tune I happen to be playing are going to become “the tune” to whoever is doing the recording. I don’t put half as much effort into learning tunes meticulously from reliable sources as I do into farting around with “bare bones” settings until I’m playing something that sounds good to me, so I’m a terrible resource for tunes. Being recorded makes me want to stop playing to avoid adding further confusion to the soup of interpretations ITM players have to slurp from to fill their bellies.

Consider this: I recorded Maudabaun Chapel for a friend a while ago, drunk, in the kitchen, about a week after I “learned” it. Later, I listened to the source again and realized I’d been playing the B part all wrong. The next time we played together, he had diligently learned the tune off his recording of me, but I was playing it differently, so he gave me some funny looks. We didn’t talk about it, though. I let him think he’d botched it up himself, for a lark.

The trouble is, if he ever says “I thought it went like THIS (etc.)” I can’t say “I dunno where you got that, I’ve always played it this way,” because he has PROOF. The sort of proof that would impress a jury and they would find me guilty as sin.

On the other hand, I know some people who play every tune EXACTLY like whatever source they got it from, and I think recording them is taking money out of the pockets of the artists they are mimicking. If I was that sort of player, I’d be inclined to say “Don’t record me, go buy a Tommy Peoples / Martin Hayes / Jean Carignan album.”
