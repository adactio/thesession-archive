Music Competitions!

As a college lecturer I know that an ability to do well in exams means just that, and does not always signify a deep understanding of the subject (trust me on this one….many students passim).
I also know that colleges’ league tables are meaningless as the bases are never equal and the tables are, therefore, suspect statistically. I am, however, happy to quote, on open evenings, my college’s excellent exam results and subsequent very high league table position.
Can music competitions give a similar skewed result or are they more worthwhile?
I derive pleasure from the fact that my band contained, at one point, two All-Ireland champions and two All-UK champions, even though I abhor the whole idea of music competition.
Do I need treatment for my hypocrisy gene? ( I know the answer to that already so don’t cover me with opprobrium).

Are competitions a good thing and do they move the tradition on or do they just test technique on what is, hopefully, a good day?

If yes, why?
If not, why not?

(Shakey egg and rainstick competitions do not count as standards have yet to be ratified, despite good work from the Sydney and SE London chapters).

Re: Music Competitions!

Human nature being what it is, competitions in the arts have been, are, and always will be, with us. They are a fact of life. If they weren’t around, they’d have to be invented, or the human race would be zombies.
At the great Panhellenic festivals in ancient Greece, for example, instrumental soloists competed along with the athletes for prizes and glory, and the best of them would have been something approximating to professionals. These competitions (“agones”) tended to develop the music and the musicians artistically and technically, and things aren’t any different two and a half thousand years later.
Win a major competition and you’ll be remembered; play your cards right and you’ll always be a busy musician, perhaps not necessarily rich in monetary terms, but with a fulfilling life.
The down-side is, if you haven’t got much of a competitive spark then you’re not likely to get far in competitions, or even enter them, even though you may be musically the equal of a winner. So where does this leave you? Possibly with a job/career outside music, and music is your abiding interest outside work. Such amateurs are the mainstay of the musical life of society, whether it is in sessions, bands, orchestras or choirs. The professionals wouldn’t exist without the amateurs.
Btw. the term “amateur” is only too often used to imply that someone’s technique isn’t up to scratch. It shouldn’t be used as a description of technical ability. There is, and always has been, a big grey area in music where the only distinction between amateurs and pros is that one doesn’t get paid (other than “expenses”!) and the other does. I know a number of “amateur” orchestral players who could take their seats in a professional orchestra with no questions asked. Similarly in non-classical music.
Anyway, that’s enough of my rabbiting. I think I’ve got a cold coming on and it’s time for a hot whisky and an early(-ish) night.
Trevor

Re: Music Competitions!

Some people, probably most people, love to organise things in such a way as to introduce order, and the more labelling and heirarchy that can be involved, the better. I’m not sniffing at this, or pronouncing the words ‘labelling’ and ‘heirarchy’ with a sneer - just observing that they are important concepts to the human mind. And there are plenty of people who find it hard to enjoy anything unless it incorporates lots of structure and organisation and ordering and heirarchy. All sports depend utterly on those things, for example.

The harmful thing about competition is the effect it might have on young people, in particular, and their attitude to the music and their pleasure in it. And the fact that it might derail anyone, young or old, from the music itself and into extraneous aspects which are not essentially musical.

*But* it does give people a crux on which a lot of other things can hang. So a Fleadh, for example, can be a great cultural event due to all the extra-curricular activities; the competitions are the excuse that enables everyone to turn up in the same place at the same time.

People who organise festivals and so forth have to organise *events* - either competitions, or workshops, or concerts, or whatever. Because it just never happens that someone says “hey, lets all go to the same town at the same time and have lots of fantastic sessions in all the pubs, for an entire week/weekend!”. And why not, since lots of us go to these things purely for the sessions? Because people expect a structure of some kind, that’s why…
