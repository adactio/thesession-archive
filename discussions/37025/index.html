Concertina Progress.

Hey folks! So it’s exactly 3 months(a quarter year) since I began learning concertina, and this thing is getting really really fun. The learning curve is proving to be really high, and as I take more lessons, things keep getting smoother and making a lot more sense.

It still feels like a Rubix-Cube a lot of the time, and I’m having a few drawbacks that are hard to explain, but navigating it has become a game creating a flowing rhythmic contour.

The problem I’m having is the availability of different fingerings. My fingers and brain get into some serious knots when learning a tune. It’s like a constant musical tongue-twister. I figure out one fingering, then another fingering works better. Then the second fingering doesn’t exactly work well with the next phrase, or I’m not getting the “legatoness”, bounce, or drive that I’m looking for, and then again figure out a different fingering. When I find a fingering that truly works for the entire tune, I’ve already learned fingerings I probably shouldn’t have, and other fingerings I would do good to use. It’s a mess! And don’t even get me started on how crazy it gets when you mix in harmonics! Gah!

This first is a set of polkas that I’ve been playing for a while and learned first on my other instruments. You’ve probably heard me play the last one on fiddle.

https://youtu.be/6B5r6DLze5U

(Anyone know the name of that second tune? It’s listed on the album here as “Charles Harris’” but “Mulqueen’s Reel” comes up when I click the link, and the only other name I have for it is “A Polka”.)

This 2nd is a set of two reels. The first is an old favorite, the 2nd, a new favorite.

https://youtu.be/0dLZXmMjbio

As always, all forms of critique and advice are welcome!

Re: Concertina Progress.

It sounds great man! Keep at it, and get a proper concertina when you get the chance. I’m hoping to get one in the next couple of years. All that stuff with the fingering just kind of goes along with the territory. I find a good rule of thumb is when the note is available on the center 4 buttons that would be played with the index finger, that it’s usually the best fingering to use. This approach lends itself to more back and forth on the bellows creating lift in the tune. That doesn’t mean you shouldn’t use the high d or e on your right hand ring finger in the G row, I like to use it at times when I want that high d on the pull or the high e on the push instead of the other way around. It seems like you are mostly sticking with the middle buttons instead of favoring one row or another anyway, so you have it I’d say. What I like about concertina is that it feels more like playing piano or more keyboard like because you are using both hands to play melody and also able to do some harmony with the left hand much like you would on piano. I can tell your right hand piano skills are coming in handy on that last reel as that bit can pretty much only be played on the right hand fingers.

The opening of the 2nd tune is a pick-up (which should begin at the end of the first tune)
d>B|:AF DF|EF DE

Whereas you play it like:
d>B AF|DF EF|DE

..and that moves the beat like Creadur says, and correspondingly, the pick-up is lost at the end (of course, you shouldn’t play those notes, but you have to play something, e.g. d2 d2||).

All this being said, you play well! Impressive!

Re: Concertina Progress.

Alright, if somebody were to play it (or another tune) like that in a session, with a strong beat on the wrong beat, and with an abrupt ending before next tune, I’d assume that it happened by mistake. If it was consistent each round, I’d assume that the musician either didn’t understand the tune or had copied a recording of some noted musician or band (who can do what they want…). (I mean, there are musicians and bands who tamper with the rhythm in a way that would NEVER work in a session… shorter bars, longer bars (even if it all adds up in the end), deleted beats, jig-into-reel rhythm and what not.)

Anyone who would know this very polka (maybe they learned it from Kevin Burke’s playing) would get a strange feeling, as if someone had taken the ending and moved it to the beginning.

As you say, it all adds up, but if you want to play with other musicians, that doesn’t help unless they’re on their feet and ready to adapt to this surprise element.
