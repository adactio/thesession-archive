Re: 2 X 7 X 3 is 42

This does highlight why mathematicians and linguists are at odds with each other. It’s OK if you use the figures “42”, but if you say “forty-two” you are linguistically incorrect. The “ty” in “forty” actually stems from “tig”, meaning ten - so “forty” means “four tens”. Interestingly, the Old English language had the equivalent of “eleventy” and “twelvety” before other influences changed things. There was a duodecimal system. Apparently, the Old Norse “hundrað” was 120, and “þusend” was 1200. It seems that the arrival of Christianity changed things to base 10. It is said that Icelandic fishermen and farmers still use the duodecimal system - one hundred and a half is 180.

The Danish counting system is based to some extent on “scores”. For example, the word for twenty is “tyve”, and the word for 60 is “tres” - which is an abbreviation of “tresindstyve” (thre times twenty). It gets even more interesting when you see the words for 50, 70 and 90. For example, 50 is halvtreds (short for halvtredsindstyve - and it sounds nowt like it looks to an English speaking person). This is the equivalent of “half three times twenty” but means two and a half times twenty - or three times twenty minus half of twenty.
