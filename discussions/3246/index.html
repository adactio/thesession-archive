Re: A wooden recorder

The only problem with recorders in ITM is that it is yet another “easy” instrument that many people have at home. So they think they might just take it out and play along as long as they can get some notes out of it. For that reason, I have “discouraged” some people from playing the recorder and encouraged them to learn how to properly play the tin whistle instead.

If the use of recorders (by great players) in ITM should spread, that might incite more people to mess around… The same is probably true for the use of other “unorthodox” instruments such as the ukulele, mouth organ, etc., that many people have lying around at home (I have suffered through several of these attempts).

Re: A wooden recorder

I’m amazed that there’s so much controversy over recorders in sessions. But before I go any farther, I’ll mention that yes, my main instrument is recorder, but I always play whistle at sessions. I generally think it fits better, but sometimes I like the sweeter tone of a recorder.
First, just because one doesn’t like the sound of an instrument doesn’t mean that it isn’t a worthy instrument. Perhaps, Joyce, you’ve never heard one played well? But even if you have, you’re welcome to your opinion. (I don’t have to agree with it though! ;-))
Second, I agree with Tish that the tone doesn’t always fit. It is ‘purer’ than a whistle, and even a flute. But a good player can do wonders, and it sounds better on some tunes than others. It is much softer than most whistles though, so a problem would be that you just might not hear it. (Which, it seems, some people wouldn’t object to.) Tish, what would you think about playing the music on a Renaissance recorder (a440, of course)?
But how do people feel about whistles that have a very recorder-like tone? Many people feel that Ralph Sweet’s whistles sound like recorders, and I agree. My feeling is that if I want to hear a recorder on a tune, I’ll play a recorder and not a whistle.
As for recorders being an ‘easy’ instrument. Yes, that’s true. But whistles are too, and pose the same problem of someone just picking it up and thinking they can play. However, the solution to that isn’t to discourage playing it, but to encourage learning to play well.
Apologies if I’ve offended, since this seems to be a very delicate issue around here.

Re: A wooden recorder

I’m not wishing to stir things up any further here but there appears to be two distinct groups on this site who, unfortunately, are never going to completely agree.

Group One. Those “pure Irish” players who wish to play in Irish sessions, play pure Irish music with pure Irish instruments to the exclusion of everything else. Well, maybe not quite as narrow as that. If that’s what they want, then that’s ok and anyone wishing to join their session should respect their point of view and act accordingly. i.e not bring in unorthodox instruments, play exotic or “foreign” tunes etc. If they can get a perfect session that they like, then good luck to them. The “real world ” isn’t always as perfect.

Group Two. Those who have an interest in Irish music but not to the exclusion of everything else. This may be by choice, or a previous background in other music/tradition, they might be beginners/improvers or for a nuber of reasons. It is possible that they will choose to attend different types of sessions (or where they live, the choice may be made for them) as they don’t have as great an Irish repertoire, they may wish to continue to play their own “Scottish or whatever” tunes as well--I mean, why waste tunes, when you’ve learned them. Some people just like to play a wider variety of music than “pure Irish”. I realise that with these looser arrangements, you’ll often get a lowering of quality, oddball instruments and people but if people want to attend such sessions, that’s their choice.

I see nothing wrong with having both types of session. You pays your money and takes your choice or you could alternate between both. The main thing is to behave appropriately in whichever one you choose to attend.

It’s interesting that you should mention, Mike McGoldrick, PP. He’s a great Irish musician who also plays in Scottish band, Capercaillie. That should tell us all something.
