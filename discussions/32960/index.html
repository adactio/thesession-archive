What romanticized world are you living in? Producing “Art?” Every record company since the beginning of melted wax has been in business to make a profit. Otherwise they cease to exist. What has changed in the past 50 years is that there are now only a few record labels left that control everything. This is where the next “big thing” will come from. Art for art’s sake is noble, but it doesn’t pay the rent. Conversely, just because it becomes very popular doesn’t mean its crap either.

I’m not going to read all of the above conversation, but I’ll go ahead and throw in my 2c worth.

I’m about 16, and play at least 5 different instruments very fluently, and can play many others when put into my hands. Around here there are multiple orchestras, bands of all kinds, quartets, chamber ensembles, instrument camps, a session, 2 rather large annual Irish fests. We’re home of one of the largest and longest running youth orchestra organizations out there (LYO), we even have a full out public school based solely around the arts (I don’t know if there are many others out there or not), and more groups. that I’m not going to bother naming.

I will agree that the media is pushing a lot of pure sch!te music, however this is much because of the audience who likes it, and the groups who made it “cool” (thus “forcing” kids to like it to get into the cliques). Most all of them include rock, rap, electronic, maybe country, but also Irish (and occasionally classical and/or jazz). Even if they don’t listen to Irish music regularly, most of them respect it, and like to hear an occasional tune. When I go to parties, I might bring along my fiddle, break into a set, and then turn right back around and jam with the guitar players, etc. in a popular song like Radioactive (by Imagine Dragons). Some of them have never seen a live group of Irish musicians, so seeing a session, or even just one player (like me) is a pure novelty.

Many youngsters are musical, some are not. Some just don’t have it in them at all, no-matter how hard they try. I’d say about 45%-ish of guys my age around here play guitar, piano and/or electric bass, and maybe 25%-ish of the girls play piano. They may not be really good, but at least they can beat out a song or two.

I guess the easy answer to OP’s original question is yes and no. :)

I will say, though, that, contrary to popular thesession.org belief, modern music *does* require a certain amount of talent, at least in writing it or playing/singing it. Listening to it, not so much, but I hold my point. To make dubstep, a good amount of mathematics and huge computer competence is required. In rock music, it takes a good deal of skill to pick those insane guitar solos, but also to make words that are relatively meaningful, sometimes rhyme, *and* make it not sound like other songs out there. There is musicality in rap as well, but I won’t go into that.

What I’m saying is that much of the musicality that was passed down through generations has taken a drastically different form. Much the same could be said about 50s vs 70s music. For example, take the difference between, say, Elvis Presley and Pink Floyd.
