Re: Celtic Women Dublin PBS Event

I agree with Jusa Nutter Eejit -- like it or not, there are quite a lot of PBS viewers who do like this stuff, in that it’s a somewhat different take on the popular songs of yesteryear. No doubt some of these viewers are not particularly interested in traditional music, but like -- in small doses, anyway -- what might be considered the trappings of Irish/“Celtic” culture. It’s just different enough, yet also familiar enough, to attract their attention.
We can carp on it as we will, but until and unless PBS decides that rerunning “Celtic Women” stuff ad infinitum will _not_ help, and might even hurt, their fundraising efforts, then you can expect it to show up.
Merits of the production aside, and being purely clinical and analytical about it, you can point to “Celtic Woman” as an apt example of the “branding,” if you will, of “Celtic.” What do you need to make something “Celtic”?
*An ethereal, vaguely other-world quality (hence the fog machines)
*Suggestive, but not especially detailed, of tradition, faith, ceremony, ritual and custom, vis a vis the wearing of gowns, ancient-looking icons and symbols, echo-y massed singing, etc.
*Rooted in agrarian-communal, rural society, hence the men in “peasant” shirts which, somehow, are remarkably clean.
*Generally affirming of “positive” human qualities and emotions, e.g., laughter, social gatherings, spirited recreation, without dwelling (not much anyway) on less pleasant or unsightly aspects of human behavior.
*Women whose hair is no shorter than shoulder length.

I don’t have much against Celtic Woman really, I mean, if they can put on a show and people like it, more power to them. I can’t stand to listen to it myself and I really think that they are lip-synching and fiddle-miming for the most part--sure looks like it to me--but they are real, talented musicians and if people are paying them for their act I can’t fault them for doing it.

Likewise, I can’t really fault PBS for airing this stuff. I haven’t seen this particular show but I happened accross a Celtic Woman show a while back on PBS and at first was very put off by it. If NPR can give us Thistle and Shamrock, surely PBS could do a little better.

But they are giving people what they want, and frankly, this is the kind of “Irish” music that most Americans want.

What I really hate is telling people that I play Irish fiddle and getting comments like “Oh, like Riverdance/Celtic Woman/Enya? I love celtic music!” Ugh.

But you really can’t expect the average American to have much knowledge about or appreciation of traditional Irish music. Those of us that are into actual trad are in a tiny minority. I can’t stand “celtic” musicians who don’t understand trad, but when it comes to people looking for entertainment I can’t fault them for listening to what they like.

It does bother me a bit that most Americans confuse the theatrical fluff for real Irish music though…it pollutes the waters obscures the real Irish trad music which has been in America for a long time. But on the other hand, I’m sure that some Americans only find their way to the true trad stuff because they heard the fluff and liked it, and took the time to learn more about it.

It’s both a blessing and a curse I guess. But it’s not like the tradition is under threat from the bogus stuff, so no need to worry about it too much.
