Life choices and music

I’m interested in hearing from other people who have the desire to play music either professionally or semi-professionally, but made conscious choices to either postpone or not pursue this path.

In my own case, I fell into a situation about 8 or 9 years back that left me broke, deep in debt, jobless, and raising my son on my own. I was living in western mass at the time, which is a great area for arts and music, but I needed a real job. I was managing to get some housepainting work and some freelance work with calligraphy, but playing music for money was very sporadic. Out of the blue I got offered a full-time position as a lettering artist for a greeting card company. This required relocating to Cleveland, not a place I wanted to live. I’ve been in Cleveland for 7 years now and in a few more years my son will be out of High School. I’m now thinking about how I can make that transition. I’d like to live in a small rural college town area, and do a combination of lettering (and typeface design), and music. I’ve visited Bloomington, Indiana a few times. I’d like to travel a bit to other sessions this summer with a college town environment.

Any thoughts or stories you’d like to share?

Re: Life choices and music

The opportunity has never presented itself to me, for very good reasons of being crap, but it seems every band I’ve played in over the last 30 years has had a least one person trying to make a living solely from music. Sometimes I’ve been able to observe them chucking their “day job” and making the transition.

IN two cases, running a music shop and giving lessons as well as playing. In one case, being a small-time drug dealer as well! In every case, being in more than one band at a time, or combining solo gigs with band gigs.

A general observation would be that, unless they are young and single, musical life has become more stressful for them rather than less stressful, as the pleasure of playing and just accepting gigs you want and appreciating the few extra ££ or $$ in your pocket, starts to come second to the necessity of making a living.

However, a lot of that stress comes from being in a band where members have conflicting aims. It would be different for a solo artist.

I’m sure every one of us who goes to sessions will have got to know one or two “famous” trad musicians. It’s pretty plain to see that even some of those who tour internationally and sell modest amounts of CDs and merchandise, are still struggling financially.

Good luck whatever you do.

I’m 47 now but back in my 20s I decided to NOT do music full time. My best friend went on to do it starved and had no health insurance. I can hardly keep up with 1 session a week (the pints don’t make it easier either) . My musical growth has been only limited by time, which is frustrating. But I have a nice house, family, and a very secure future. As the kids get off to college I intend to do more but I can already feel the slow down from age coming on. So it’s a race against time.
One other thing is that short of quiting the straight job, I’ve made music a priority in my life, sometimes to my wifes dismay. It’s tricky to hold onto it when forces are pulling against you.
This also borders on the larger discussion of “trad music professionals”. Kind of a weird notion when you think of it.

Re: Life choices and music

Well, you’re about to all think I’m crazy, but I actually just transfered faculties from engineering to music. And no, I wasn’t failing engineering, I was set to go into the specialization of my choice, and I even landed a summer job working in a laboratory with a local engineering firm. Most people are aware that engineers, as a broad generalization, make a lot of money, work good hours (8am-5pm or similar, weekdays only, and when one goes home, they don’t have to do any planning before the next day, etc.), great benefits, and in Alberta, Canada, have excellent job security. After much debate with friends, teachers, mentors, parents, and to the most significant extent, myself, I had decided that engineering was the route for me. Heck, I might even like the work - I’ve always loved math (awful as that sounds). But I’ve been a musician since as far back as I can remember - I started piano lessons at 3, and I probably sang along with the record player in the short years preceding that. This past year I’ve really limited myself musically because I knew that I must choose one thing to focus on if I am to reach my potential, and I had decided that my focus would be engineering. I quit playing oboe, trombone, trumpet, and piano. I quit all jazz and concert band ensembles. I took weekly viola lessons, and played viola in my local youth orchestra, and conducted my church choir. I took very few gigs until my exams were over. I had dramatically reduced the amount of time I spent studying music, and yet I was still spending more time than most people spend on a mere “hobby”. I found that this was not enough. So much of the life I had known was - missing. I tried to envision myself as an engineer. I saw myself doing the calculations, sure, but when I imagined the lifestyle, I saw something else. I saw myself using my time and my money to study music, and I realized that to wait until I was “financially secure” to study music in my spare time would leave me in a compromised situation. If a 50-year old and a 25-year old of equal playing ability audition for the same orchestra, the 25-year old is going to get the position. I understood that if I was to study music, it’s now or never. I can be an engineer when I’m 50 if I have to be, but right now I have to follow my dream, my passion, which is to be a musician, come hell or high water. So here I am, 18 years old, transfering to music, and more excited for life than I have ever felt before. That’s surely got to count for something.

Re: Life choices and music

Well, all I can say is middle age is great. After the kids are out of the nest and you have less responsibility, you can play to your Hearts Content… which is actually the name we took for our band, three middle agers. Playing the music when and where you want, gives a contented heart for sure!

I lived near NYC back in the 70s and early 80s and was playing professionally, a lot, making great money playing about five gigs a week and that was on three nights… oh youth…! Then moved about 1 1/2 hours away, raised my little family and am now back to playing, not so many gigs, I couldn’t withstand that in my 50s! Way less, but am happier for it. I have two other jobs I love and have worked from home for nearly 30 years, which pay the bills (mostly, LOL!), and when a gig comes by it does and I am certainly grateful.

It definitely was a choice to give it up, not that I ever stopped playing, but played mostly alone, fingerstyle guitar. I lived too far to get to any sessions, there weren’t any within decent driving range for me. Sometimes people would stop by and we’d play but that was it, and it was like my lifeline was shut down musically, but looking back it was worth it.

It’s really the best time in my life now. I loved having the kid at home of course, and got bent out of shape when she grew up and left, but she lives nearby with her family now, and I see them all the time.

Youth really is wasted on the young. I am having a wonderful time now and hope it goes on for a long time to come. Everything has a right time, and getting back to playing, now it is just wonderful. If I had to do it again, I would do the same. We can’t have everything we want all the time, part of being a grown up!

Once you choose the muse, all your choices become life-choices.

After 15 years of full time professional playing, I was handed the choice of “the music or the marriage”. I chose the marriage - funny thing - the marriage didn’t last but I stil play music.

After 16 years of working straight jobs that seemed like an extended holiday with cash raining out of the sky. I began feeling like there was no reason for me to be on the planet - I was a thing of glass, a collection of skills and flaws amongst the awards and trophies in some showcase in a darkenned empty room. I handed myself a choice - “ The music or nothing”. Then all the colour came back.

You gotta be prepared for the blacks and browns as well as the primary reds, blues, yellows and greens - it aint all pastels and cash, but it’s a life.

Maybe I’m too old now to get good enough for stage work again, but the preparatory stuff and being on the path is like re-birth.

I know the back-side of teh proscenium is all gaffa-tape and scum, but it’s real.

For some, music is not a choice - it IS life.
