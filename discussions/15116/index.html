That long downhill run in Harvest Home isn’t as tricky as it sounds. Just “huff” it - don’t force the breathing and keep the embouchure relaxed. Another hazard is running out of air in the first couple of bars which are all “blow!” I generally play it on an Echo tremolo, something I resort to only occasionally. A “related” tune, The Cliff, played fast and straight a la Pauline Cato, gives good exercise for nipping around on the harp. I have no easy answers for Cooley’s though. :-(

Actually, if you google the term ’traditional nabjo", the first thing that come up is a link site that shows that Folk of the Wood offers lessons for mandolin, nabjo, and violin.

But alas, a search of YouTube returns nothing for nabjo, so it can’t be THAT well known ;-)

But back to the topic… I find that the “double time” runs in hornpipes are very difficult on banjo too, at least when you get up to a certain speed. I tend to like hornpipes slower and swung pretty hard, but if they get up to a certain speed, I usually don’t do the full runs.

On banjo, my first instinct is to do those runs picked like a jig (DUD DUD DUD D for me), but that doesn’t work very well. First off, those runs are often faster than you would play a jig. And secondly, the rhythm of the run should be smoother than a jig, with more equal note equivalence. So I try to pick them DUD UDU DUD U… But for me, getting my hand to switch speeds smoothly like that is difficult. At a high enough speed, they almost end up being back to back triplets, which are difficult too!

Pete

Grego, is there a “right” way to play any of this music? (I mean, I know there’s a hundred WRONG ways to play it…)

The hornpipe debate is a long standing one. But I wouldn’t take dance competitions as being the holy writ of how things should be played. They manipulate the music around to make it fit the dance, not the other way around. In step dance competitions, the hornpipes are often played extra slow for the advanced dancers, and somewhat faster for the beginning dancers.

But look at a ceili band playing a hornpipe for ceili dancing… They’ll often play hornpipes fast (http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OacB9CywjmE&NR) So which form of dance is the “right” way? :-/
