lol, re the windswept gridiron streets!
The issue with this phenomenon (in the OP) always seems to me that constructing an Irish music (or any other genre of activity for that matter) outside of the native origin of that activity, is just that - a reconstruction. Along with the music there is a whole cultural milieu at work in the native country that probably isn’t operating when you just reconstruct the music in the non-native location. It’s a holistic thing, not just the music. I was at a very memorable session in Kilfenora, where none of the OP concerns were evident in any way, just the opposite in fact. The same I found at a lot of sessions in Ireland. People grow up with the music in those places, it is just part of the normal culture there, nothing out of the ordinary to them, there is a very close community feeling around it as well, a lot of the families and therefore players in those places are related, the differences go on and on. It can be a tight community in and around the music there. I love it; and the music can be absolutely brilliant with that degree of understanding between the players. Something you just want to be around and listen to - not play in. Maybe it’s a monocultural rather than a multicultural phenomenon. It has it’s strengths.

Nothing worse than that dichotomy that Steve Hawson apparently experienced.
