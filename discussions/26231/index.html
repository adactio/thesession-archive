I lost my violin but didn’t claim on my insurance. Find out why……..!!!!!

This happened to me about two years ago. We were playing at a gig on Friday evening; my friend who is the caller in the band and general quartermaster for the PA and trailer, called round at my house to pick up a few bits of equipment and my violin as he was loading up. ( He lives just round the corner from me). Shortly afterwards I turned up at his house and we set off. When we got to the venue and unloaded, it quickly became apparent that my violin was missing. My friend then realised that he must have left the violin on top of the trailer prior to packing - as we were quite a long way from home there was no point in going back to look for it. Fortunately I was able to borrow a violin and managed ok for the evening.
I didn’t have specific music insurance but I thought it might be covered on the house insurance, so the next day I phoned up the insurance company. To my surprise they said I was covered and that I should complete the claim form and get some costings.
So duly the form came and I contacted the origional shop where I bought the violin; they said they would provide replacement cost. Eventually, about three weeks later, it was all done; replacement costs for case, violin and bow were around £800. All I needed was a stamp for the envelope which I didn’t have. That evening I was going to the local session so the letter remained unposted.
Sometime during the evening, mid session, a man walked into the pub and asked if anyone had lost a violin. I was flabbergasted because he was holding up my violin case. He said that he had been driving home ( three weeks previous ) and had seen a case lying across the white line in the centre of the road (it was a busy road). He stopped the car and retrieved the case. He knew that there was a weekly session in pub and came down on the off chance that it might belong to someone there. The violin was totally unscathed.
The next day I was confronted with a dilema - I had my violin back and there was the letter on the mantlepiece waiting to go. Now I’m a pretty honest chap and would never set out to deliberately defraud anyone, or insurance company, but there it was,signed sealed and all I had to do was deliver. I decide, after much mulling that I would make the claim. At dinnertime as I sat round the table I told my family what I was going to do - I was taken aback by the blast of moral outrage - dad was a rip of merchant, even a thief!! Eventually I relented - the letter never got sent
What would you have done?
