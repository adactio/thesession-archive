The drum kit and trad………..

Dont worry, Im not gonna bring my kit to your session!!!
However I get many drummers who want to play with me.So for you wannabee’s here’ some advice:
LIke jazz, rock, reggae, trad is an entire style. Just as your rock drumming wont work with jazz ,the same applies to trad.You need to listen carefully.Be sensitive . Work with dynamics..
If you cant play with a metronome, you cant play with me.
Leave your crash cymbals alone! there is only so much bandwidth, and that part is mine!
So any one got any other helpfull advice? Helpfull being the operative word!

Re: The drum kit and trad………..

Not too long ago, a fellow with a snare drum and pipe band pom-pom drumsticks showed up at our local session. He was all set up and ready to go when the musicians who anchor the session came. He proceeded to play along to EACH AND EVERY tune, totally oblivious to the stares, sighs and muttering of not only the other sessioneers but of those of us who go weekly for our dose of great tunes. This is a lovely session normally, with two very welcoming anchor musicians who like to give everyone a fair chance (including singers and dancers) but this was very inappropriate as the “drummer” never took a break, tried to play along to airs, and was in total disregard of the dynamics of this session. The best we could figure out was that he was a pipe band drummer in need of lots of practice and possibly someone sent him to the session and told him to learn to play along to jigs, reels, hornpipes, etc. Finally one of the anchors spoke to him as a young man was playing a whistle solo (drummer trying to play along) and after a bit he packed up his kit and went his merry way.

On another note, though, about Ceili bands---one of the best ceili band drummers is Aidan Vaughan, who is also a sean nos dancer and set master. He has taught ceili drumming in our area and is a great resource for drummers who think they know how to play for dancers, but really don’t have the finesse and light rhythmic drumming style needed for a ceili band. And no, you don’t need a full kit, all Aidan uses is a snare, a bass, a side cymbal and a bass pedal.

Re: The drum kit and trad………..

I teach drums, all styles, and I agree with a lot of what’s been said on here. I too am very hard to please when I have a drummer playing along with my accordion playing. I always maintain that to be a good traditional drummer you should know the tunes you are drumming to. You should know exactly when to put the accent on the beat etc. I’m afraid that over the years there has been a crop of bad drummers with bands. Most of those that I’ve seen in action with bands haven’t a clue, and are just keeping the time. Consequently that fact leads to a Thread like this one. As for Bodhran players, I’ve had some good ones play along side me, but there is only so much you can do with a goatskin. Many of them can’t seem to adopt to anything but Reels, Jigs, or Hornpipes. Re: The drum kit and trad………..

For sure there is plenty of scope for rhythmic spontaneity in trad; Within the structure of the tunes. But i feel it is essential that the structure is understood. For example A and B parts, I would require a drummer to differentiate between these parts.The tunes with ABCDE parts also!

If the drummer doesn’t know that the tune has a bunch of triplets in a certain place then how can he accent that?
What is the role, if any, of a kit in trad? To my mind it is simply to play the tune, add dynamics, add variation etc.
I am of the school of thought that believes a thorough understanding of the music, idiom, and tunes are required for a player to do justice to trad.

I agree that a player with knowledge of the idiom, and who is sensitive and listens, can do justice to tunes they do not know.

But i strongly disagree that an expert in, say jazz, or flamenco or classical will automatically be able to play trad! It is not the case.This music is only superficially simple. Evan the greatest jazz violinist in the world will need to learn the tunes first!
Once the tune is learnt then comes the interpretation, this is where the art lies.
With our latest drummer i work with him on each tune of each set. So that he knows what is happening. So i can rely on him being there when i need him. So i can relax and just play the fiddle without being distracted by a 1/8thnote roll when the tune is in triplets!
