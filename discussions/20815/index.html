We’re helpful - but we’re not psychic!

The nice thing about the discussion forum section of this site is that it can be used to ask questions in order to solve problems. And there are plenty of users within the membership who are not only knowledgeable, but also very happy to help. A great “free service”!

But it beggars belief that many of the people who are asking about problems are either too stupid, (or simply can’t be bothered) to provide the basic information that is needed to answer the question that they are raising. So I’m taking the liberty of suggesting a few guidelines that could be followed when asking such questions:

Geographical Information
-----------------------------------

- Are you asking about sessions etc. in your area?
- Are you looking for a new member for your band?
- Are you wanting to join a local band?

State where you live. Not just your village, town or city but COUNTRY as well - this website is used internationally. For example, there is a Halifax in England, but there is also a Halifax in Canada - and another one in South Africa as well.

Technical Information
------------------------------

Many questions are raised concerning IT problems. If you were PAYING for an IT support service, you would be expected to provide technical details about your system before any help was provided. So this is even more important when you are asking for FREE help.

I suggest the following as a MINIMUM for any IT-related problem:

- System Hardware specification (CPU and RAM details).
- Operating system (Windows, Mac, Linux etc)
- Operating system version number and service release number.
- The application name, and application version number.
- Have you (a) always had the problem, or (b) is it something that used to work, but no longer does?
- If (b), When did you first notice the problem? Did you install or download anything just before experiencing the problem for the first time?
- Are you normally logged on to your system with administrator privileges?

General Caveats
-----------------------

If someone asks for some additional information that is needed to help answer your question, please try to respond in within the thread (and by email) as soon as possible.

Don’t forget to post a note of thanks to all those who responded. Even if no-one is able to specifically answer your question, they took time out to help you.

Something of a rant this, admittedly. But a justified one, I feel.

For my part, I’m still happy to help with any problems raised that are commensurate with my knowledge. But from now on, if the questioner fails to supply the necessary information, I’ll just post a link to this thread …

@P-K - Thanks for the suggestion, but we wouldn’t tend to use a tune like that. We play for dancers at barn dances and ceilidhs - I feel that P. O’R is more of a session tune.

The tunes that we look to play are the kind that have a strong differentiation between the “A”, “B” and “C” parts - when the part changes, it subtly reminds the dancers that they have to do something different. In this respect, “Sweets of May” and “Dingle Regatta” are ideal. Just get fed up with playing them all the time!
