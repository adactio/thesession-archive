I find these funny : visual (video or live) - facial expressions, chewing on nothing, dropping gob, exaggerated over-enthusiatic movements and the like.

Audio - Whether by accident or design, bum notes, out-of-tuneness, mis-coordination, wrong chords, extra phrases put in, up-for-a-song singers driving backers nuts with their creative sense of timing and phrasing.

To DK : (you said you’d welcome more discussion, so …)

Yes you are right, this is a forum about Irish traditional music, and of course we do discuss tunes and playing, deeply, at times. I don’t get paid to write about tunes, but I often do transcriptions of some pretty complex stuff, stuff which is way more complex than most Irish dance music. That in itself is no big deal, but it does involve intense listening and scrutiny. To the same degree as in teaching an advanced fiddle student - listening intently, and often spotting things that other people would miss (fiddlers included). Again, no big deal. I’d expect the same of my own teacher.

So - back to the tune we were discussing earlier (I’ve been playing that tune for many years, btw, so it’s nothing new to me). I watched the vid and listened through speakers. It was obvious the players were having a laugh, and my first thought was that it was good playing, and I still think that. All sounded good and fun, but when the tune moved to the high parts, something didn’t sound quite right, so I listened again through headphones. Yes, curiosity made me scrutinise. I’ve already said what I thought, about the sound, and the players’ intentions. Others disagree with me - fine.

I’ve been thinking about my choice of words earlier : “murdering a tune”. I still think that’s the case here, but it’s not meant pejoratively, nor does it necessarily detract from the talent of the players.

Fiddler Sean Maguire was often held in high regard for his technical accomplishment. Audiences watched him in jaw-dropping awe as he ripped through a blistering set. However, a significant amount of other people were of the opinion that what with his deliberate speeding-up, using quasi-classical bowing techniques and other flashy devices, he simply murdered some Irish traditional tunes. He was an excellent fiddle player (of that there can be no doubt whatsoever), and could also (and did) play tunes in a more normal traditional style, just like everyone else. However, he often chose to play a tune set just the way it suited him. Not everyone else could play like he did, though.

Of course I saw the funny side of gam’s vid, but the reason was different from other peoples’ reason for the amusement :)

imo, if you really wanted a top-class version of that tune, Jean Carnigan would have been the man for that.

An easily described piece of musical humour that sticks in my mind was as song in two part harmony by English folk-circuit duo “The Kipper Family”. The harmony part was various numbers of beats longer than the tune in many phrases, so the effect was of the ‘old’ member of the duo not being able to keep steady tempo and his ‘son’ having to wait for him. There were various amusing harmonic and rhythmic effects as one part slid ‘out of time’.
