Re: Need advice on buying a flute

I’m not sure I would be so fast to rule out polymer flutes. I have a Copley Delrin flute and I love it. Playing other wooden flutes, I don’t notice much of a difference at all in sound quality. Yes, wood will harden and respond somewhat better over time if well kept, but a good polymer flute will sound quite good; and you don’t have to worry about maintenance or dropping it. I’m not sure what your reasoning for wanting wood is, but with your budget it could be better to get a reputable polymer flute now (copley, forbes, seery, to name a few) and then save up for a good quality wooden flute later. That’s just my 2 cents though. Take it or leave it.

Re: Need advice on buying a flute

I’ll speak from my experience, as I was in your situation not too long ago. When I was starting out I bought a WD Sweet Shannon, which is a polymer flute with a slightly non-traditional look/design. I have been extremely happy with this instrument, and while I’m looking to buy a keyed wooden flute sometime soon, I will certainly be keeping this one around and playing it. I have gotten many compliments on the flute’s tone both from people hearing me play it and from other players trying it out, and at $275 it’s well below the price of similar flutes.

That being said, I’ve tried Seerys and M&E flutes and both seemed like good flutes for the money as well. Forbes, Copley, and Somers also have good reputations. They are all polymer, but in your price range pretty much everything is.

If you do go for wood, all signs point to Casey Burns as your best option. I haven’t played one of his Folk Flutes but do know a few people who have his pricier models and they love them. The only other option around your price range for wood would probably be something used, or maybe an antique of some kind.

Here’s another thread about McNeela flutes, again stating that they’re cheap mass-produced flutes that have been tweaked to play better (or, for that matter, play at all):

http://forums.chiffandfipple.com/viewtopic.php?f=2&t=100445

I’d say they don’t look awful, but you’re probably better getting a well-made delrin or Burns flute IMO.

Re: Need advice on buying a flute

Everyone who has tried my 10-year-old M&E keyless polymer has been surprised by how well it plays (and I’m always surprised with how good it *can* sound in the hands of a much more capable player than I). It’s survived many years of neglect (and at least one encounter with a curious German Shepherd when left momentarily unsupervised on a coffee table). Polymer/ebonite/delrin still can make some great flutes when worked by the right hands, and they are practically bulletproof. I’d say even if you decide to upgrade in the future, you’ll still have an inexpensive flute that you wouldn’t mind taking a bit of a beating here and there (camping, travel, other various adventures). And in the unlikely event you decide it’s not for you, you’re not out too much money and it will likely sell for close to the original price (unless of course it contains a series of distinct canine teeth marks; adds character I say!)

Desi Seery, Micheal Cronnoly, Doug Tipple, Copley and Boegli, and Garry Sommers all make good synthetic flutes right under your price range. I tend to be very suspicious of anything in that range that’s made of wood (excepting the excellent makers mentioned previously) that I haven’t had a chance to play… you could luck out and get a good player, then again you could wind up with a $250 table leg.

↳ Posted by Ailin on July 31st, 2015.

Re: Need advice on buying a flute

I have a Burns with the top hand third hole offset but the bottom inline and another flute with them the other way round.

The offset top hole is comfortable and I prefer it but I don’t find the slight extra stretch a problem when using the one with the inline holes. A problem I find with the offset bottom hole is that I then don’t have my bottom hand fingers at right angles to the flute. Doing that on a keyed flute risks catching the edge of touch of the short F key with the side of the third finger and makes C/C# keys harder for the fourth finger.

It’s not big deal to learn to hold a different flute differently though. I only realised how much I was catching that touch when I noticed the beginnings of a callous on the side of my finger.

↳ Posted by Rob Ripley on July 31st, 2015.

Re: Need advice on buying a flute

The offset holes are just to make the reach easier. The open holes on a wooden ‘simple system’ flute are further apart than the equivalent keys on a Boehm flute. People managed for generations with inline holes but when the Boehm came in the opportunity was taken to make the reach easier.

If you have average sized male hands you shouldn’t need offset holes. Casey would probably give advice by email.
