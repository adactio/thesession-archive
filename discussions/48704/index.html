How Many Pub Songs Would You Like Per Session?

Between 0-100 give an honest answer. If it were up to you how many pub songs would you like sung during your session?

Another thread got me to wondering what are the thoughts of your average sessioner on the song question.

I’m not talking here about an amazing singer that you may have in your community. I’m talking about the average guy singing pub songs.

Re: How Many Pub Songs Would You Like Per Session?

I think there are three types….and in my opinion, all have their place :-)

1. Traditional songs - “pure drop”, voice with no accompanist
2. Pub songs - Paddy Reilly, Dubliners…guitars, chords and choruses
3. Parlour songs - Gather round the piano everyone…it’s Percy French time, might include also songs with Victorian music hall roots

There is of course plenty of crossover and intermingling.

Where does the Pride of Petravore / Eileen Óg fit in? It can be a tune, but it can be a “Parlour Song” and started life as such.

But insofar as Traditional singing goes, songs are as much part of the tradition as any other instrument….so it depends on who is there, what they know and how well they can perform it, and what the atmosphere of the evening is.

Ideally, zero songs for me. It shifts the focus from the group having fun playing tunes together as a single organism into one player’s performance, where everyone else has to sit on their hands and become a temporary audience while they wait for it to end.

It’s a reversal of the normal session dynamic of group effort vs. solo spotlight. If I wanted to participate in a spotlight round-robin event as an amateur musician, I’d go to an open mic night.

I’m not rigid about it though, it’s just my preference. There is one local session where on occasion a very good singer will show up and sing just one song unaccompanied in Gaelic. A local fiddle player has observed that one of the crucial aspect of the session is the fact that about 2/3rds of the people at the pub don’t really care about the music, and that the being ignored is as important to the feel of a good session as the tunes and the craic.

In a busy pub the challenge (musicians or singers) can be background noise. Over a certain pitch and the music gets overwhelmed. If punters aren’t interested/ignore there’s a tendency to talk (shout) over the music. That’s not good for the session. And it’s doubly difficult for singers.

We are lucky with our regular venue - there are front, middle and back bar areas with the trad in the back. ↳ Posted by gimpy on October 24th, 2023.

Re: How Many Pub Songs Would You Like Per Session?

Steve T, I don’t think “limiting interactions” is the question here, I think it’s about the relationship the session players want to have with the customers in the pub (assuming it’s a pub session).

Our session has very few songs, and when there is a song, it’s almost always an unaccompanied sean nos singer.

Once you start having pub songs, particularly popular sing-alongs, the session now is “entertainment”.

When we’re just playing tunes in our session, we are essentially invisible, we’re just part of the overall customer experience.

They’re at the pub with a friend for a pint, maybe there’s a game on the TV and incidentally there’s some music coming from those folks over there in the corner. They’re not paying attention to us.

When someone starts singing, particularly a pub song, the customer’s attention is taken away from whatever they were doing, watching the game, chatting with their friend.

Now it’s all about the song. All eyes are on the singer and the musicians, and maybe they get to sing along on a few choruses of a pub song.

Once you start down that path, there’s an expectation that “that’s what we do”, that the pub a place to go on Tuesday nights for sing-alongs, and at least among our local players, we universally cringe at that thought.

We have no interest in being the “entertainment”. The pub owner likes having us there to create an authentic atmosphere, with zero expectation of us entertaining the customers.

We enjoy being wallpaper, and very much appreciate the occasional customer who listens to the tunes and responds with applause, but that’s not our primary goal.

Of course, every session is different.

If other sessions are more about audience participation and pub songs, that’s great, whatever works for your group and meets the expectations of the venue management.

I wouldn’t judge any other session for having pub tunes, it’s just not our thing.

I suggest that those of you who have lots of songs at your sessions give the same space and respect the choices of those who prefer sessions that don’t.
