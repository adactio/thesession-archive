Re: Going on a trip to Ireland

First of all, I’ll second the Cobblestone as a place to hear music of a consistently excellent standard. I have always gone there expecting to listen, not play.

Conservatoire? Not exactly. There is a Traditional Music degree course at The University of Limerick but that is, I think, intended for those that already have a solid grounding in traditional music - and want an academic qualification. There are also various beginner/intermediate traditional music courses around the country - but you would probably meed to be resident in Ireland long-term.

The best starting point for you, I think would be to go to classes at one of the many Traditional Music summer schools that go on in Ireland. I listed a few of them recently in this discussions https://thesession.org/discussions/39530#comment798297 . They are called ‘summer schools’ but, in reality, the classes are only part of the whole event - they have programs of concerts, recitals and talks and the pubs are often full to busrting with musicians, who are there for no reason other than to play with other musicians, to meet up with old friends and make new friends. Instrumental classes are graded - you might be asked to give a very brief ‘audition’ (i.e. playing the first few bars of any tune) to see which class would suit you - and, with a little forward research, you might find that there is a particular tutor who specialises in helping those with a classical background to make the transition to Traditional Music. (Bear in mind that many in the classes will be kids and teenagers that have grown up with Traditional Music.) Learning one-on-one is, of course, great if you find the opportunity. You might be able to find some leads to this by asking one or more of the summer school tutors.

Sessions: Impromptu sessions sometimes happen - and it’s great when they do - but there certainly are regular sessions in Ireland. Depending on where you go, they could happen every night (usually with different booked musicians each night) once a week, once a month… or not at all (remember that Traditional music is only played and appreciated by a small minority, even in Ireland). Some areas, like Counties Galway and Clare, are teeming with musicians, so you’ll find a session somewhere on any night. In other areas, like the SE Counties, you might have to travel some distance just to find a weekly session. (In some instances, the regulars in the session may be working musicians, so the session will only happen when they are not on tour or performing elsewhere.)

I don’t live in Ireland, but the above is based on my experiences when visiting Ireland.

I hope I’ve given some useful answers.

p.s. In response to the previous poster’s comment: Yes, Dublin is no doubt a very expensive place to live - and not where I would choose to be if I had the opportunity to spend several months in Ireland, anyway. But it is worth a visit. There’s good music to be found there and, as cities go, it’s not too bad.

Re: Going on a trip to Ireland

We have had great experiences going to summer schools in Ireland. This includes morning workshops (graded), depending on the festival there can be afternoon workshop options or lectures or concerts, then also often evening concerts. Sessions can be any time, usually starting after lunch. Some organised/programmed sessions others impromptu. My experience of the schools we have attended starting early July - Willie Clancy week in Miltown Malbay - great workshops with a large option on tutors, lots of opportunity to go to recitals and concerts, also lots of sessions but can be very crowded pubs and hard to get into a good session. South Sligo Summer School, great smaller festival with good tuition and recitals, concerts etc and plenty of sessions which are easier to take part in. Joe Mooney Is bigger than South Sligo so more range of tutors, also has some concerts and has plenty of sessions more crowded than SS but not as busy as Willie Clancy. Scoil Acla on Achill Island - great location but classes spread around the island so you would need a car. About the same size as SS - I enjoyed the fiddle classes, but less concerts/recitals and sessions happen but harder to find as they can pop up anywhere on the Island. We also went to the Fleadh School in Ennis this year. I wouldn’t go again - whilst they advertise for all ages it seemed to be more geared for kids and not many adults attending. But it would be worth going that week for the sessions early in the week before it gets busy and for the range of concerts you can go to. There are also some other smaller summer schools - one at Keadue which has a focus on harp but some other instrument classes too but only one tutor per instruments other than harp, lovely location but not many sessions. Another at Spiddal which has great sessions but we found the school not very well run. But go to Spiddal any time in the summer for great sessions. A good thing about the summer schools is meeting people both students and tutors in classes/at sessions etc I’d recommend going to any of Wiilie Clancy, South Sligo or Joe Mooney or two or all three if you have time. Generally sessions in pubs outside of summer schools in Ireland during the summer are often “anchored” by 2 or 3 paid musicians who lead the session, but you can join in. It would be best to check with them first that it’s OK to join in the first time you attend a particular session. There are also some weekend festivals that have workshops, concerts and lots of sessions eg Feakle in early August. Hope this is useful.

Re: Going on a trip to Ireland

My trips to Ireland have, at least sometimes, been centred on rugby weekends, (Ireland v Scotland) thanks to my husband’s obsession with the game! On the way back from one match, I remarked to my son that it might be hard to find any really good music on a rugby weekend. A guy in front of us overheard, and to cut a long story short, suggested the back bar at Sheridan’s on the quay. We went, and had a great night there: ok, some predictable stuff (Wild Rover et al), but also a champion box player, and other brilliant musicians in the resident ensemble that night.
Another time we were in Doolin on a Sunday night in February: really didn’t expect to find much music then, but turned out we’d just walked into the Micho Russell festival. Our B & B hosts were both musicians, and “apologised” for going down the road to play at O’Connor’s bar. Great night, and we also visited another pub, where I got to do a wee floor spot with a borrowed guitar, and local musicians joined in, all very friendly and welcoming.
One slight disappointment was in Letterkenny, where there was a session in a hotel. Seemed very cliquey to me compared with our sessions at home: all the musicians in a huddle in the centre of a very large room. Anyone else sat a long way back, around the edge of the room. I did go and chat to one of the accordionists, as I had liked one of her tunes, and got the name of it from her. I remarked that the session was only instrumental, and she said : “ Oh, our singer is not here tonight”. Well, at home, anyone new would have been asked if they wanted to sing or play, and not just have been assumed to be an onlooker!

Re: Going on a trip to Ireland

If you are a flute player, you might consider the Cruinniu Na bhFliuit (apologies for spelling) in Ballyvourney, which takes place in the Spring. I attended last year as a beginner and will return in 2017 (I live in Florida). Many of the best players from Ireland are there as faculty and great players are there as students. Classes fill the day, and session play at night at one of several local pubs with everyone from the morning’s classes goes on into the early morning. The area is gorgeous, and the locally brewed beer is a plus as well. A great chance for a flute player to immerse himself or herself in ITM in a flute-centric manner.

Re: Going on a trip to Ireland

One of the best, Conal O Grada, lives in Cork and gives private lessons. Hammy Hamilton lives down there as well, if you’re in the market for a flute he makes some of the best. In Cork City between the Sin É, Spailpín Fanach, and Corner House you can go to a session every single day, twice on some days (and there are many other pubs with at least one session a week going, like Charlie’s, the Gables, and the Oliver Plunkett).

+1 on the Cobblestone in Dublin, on Wednesdays I believe Catherine McEvoy plays there, and she’s a fantastic role model on the flute and also teaches lessons (I’ve had her as a teacher at the Willie Clancy Week before and she’s wonderful). The various weeks and festivals that happen around the country are great places to get lessons, and will also keep you immersed in the music.

As for whether there are spontaneous sessions, yes, they do occur, but you’d either have to be lucky or know someone to figure out when. Trad is a minority interest in Ireland, even though it’s obviously much more popular there than anywhere else. But the good news is, people are generally lovely, and many of the top class players regard themselves as teachers and mentors as well as performers. I am in a similar boat to you, only a few years playing the flute, but I’ve sat in on sessions with some of the best, and they’ve all been wonderfully welcoming. If you’re polite and respectful, you’ll be sure to find people to talk to and learn from at any session, even if you decide that you’d rather just listen. And many times that is the best of course of action, when the tunes are flying too fast or are wholly unfamiliar. Sit back, maybe record a few to learn later, and soak it in.

If you’re wanting a cheap home base for a while, can I recommend the Boghill Centre in Kilfenora, Clare? It’s a hostel, with an organic farm, run by ITM-friendly people. They have two weeks of ITM workshop in the summer, but I’ve also stayed there at other times of the year and used it as a home base, getting private lessons from local musicians by day, and finding sessions each night - it’s only a few minutes drive to Doolin, Kilfenora, Lisdoonvarna, and Ennistymon, and Ennis is under an hour too.
