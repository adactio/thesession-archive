Do you really prefer *Irish* sessions?

This seems a daft question to ask, particularly on this board but I started thinking about this after reading some of the recent and more controversial threads. I’m sure there are many people out there who are also influenced by traditional music from other countries so I expect to get a variety of views here.

Although I love Irish music and I’m constantly learning new tunes, practising rolls, triplets and so on, I can never see myself becoming completely focused and absorbed in Irish music exclusively. I’ll always want to play and learn more tunes from Scotland, Scandinavia and elsewhere as well. So, I somehow doubt if I’ll ever fit into some of the “full blown” Irish sessions to the extent that I’ll know every tune etc. Of course, I’ve been in and thoroughly enjoyed sessions where it’s been all Irish music but there’s always been plenty of standards. I’m not sure if I’ll ever totally immerse myself in the music enough to master all the potential repertoire of certain sessions. So, there’ll always be situations where I’ll only know a handful of tunes. The same, of course, applies to some Scottish sessions.

At the moment, I find that my ideal session consists of a mixture of Irish or Scottish tunes along with others from elsewhere. Generally, there may be a few standards but I also like to hear rarer tunes too which I can learn by and by. There will also be a mixture of “old and new” tunes. However, such a session may still be very Irish or very Scottish. It just depends.

I’m not advocating a “free for all”, of course, or suggesting that things should be made any easier in this situation. I still want the music to be good and the standard high. It’s just that I don’t feel that I will ever be able(or commit myself) to focus enough and learn all the repertoire for certain sessions. So, I choose those which suit me. There are quite a variety up here--even within the confines of Sandy Bells itself--so fortunately I’ll never be stuck.

I realise too that there are many people on this board who are as equally au fait with Scottish and Irish music including, for example, Kenny. What’s your secret?
You’ll obviously have been playing for much longer, of course, but did you feel that you had to totally immerse yourself in either Irish or Scottish (or whatever) music for a lengthy time or was it possible just to continue learning and playing a bit of everything?

Does it really matter in the end as long as we “know our place”(in the nicest possible way) and enjoy what we’re doing?

Re: Do you really prefer *Irish* sessions?

Many of my musical friends and acquaintances tend to favor what I like to call a “New England Celtic” type of house session: a mix of Irish, Scots, French Canadian, Cape Breton and contra dance tunes, maybe an occasional English country dance piece -- actually, that’s pretty much the repertoire of a contra dance band.
I rather like the mix, for the most part. It’s fun, and challenging, to adapt to these different styles -- sometimes mixed together in one medley.
Those are as much social occasions as musical events, punctuated by potluck dinners and the like, which is all part of the attraction.
But of course, there are times when I feel more like an out-and-out tune-fest, and that’s why I feel lucky to have a good choice of Irish sessions around. Not that there isn’t socializing at those, either, but there’s a certain purposefulness to them, if you will.

Re: Do you really prefer *Irish* sessions?

I think that here in Indiana (and somewhat similarly in the KY and Ohio sessions I’ve been to), there’s not a lot of distinction among the sources of tunes played. They seem to be predominantly Irish, but if they’re Scottish, Breton, French Canadian, they fit in ok and without particular notice.

It seems most common that the folks who bring these tunes up got them from players, or recordings of players, best known as Irish trad players.

American OldTime tunes come up sometimes, too, but are generally recognized and pointed out as such (as the others aren’t) because so many folks play both Irish and OldTime, that a session can get tipped over from one to the other easily. So OldTime players only allow a couple of Irish tunes in a session and Irish sessioneers only allow a few OldTime tunes. <GG>

It doesn’t matter to me. There’s quite enough pure Irish and also quite enough other music around to keep me satisfied. So far the crossing over of one to another hasn’t been problematic.
