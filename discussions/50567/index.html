Music and Fitness 2.

This last year or so when I’ve been out busking with the piano, my wrists have been chronically sore when playing. Not tension, but feeling weak as if I don’t play piano regularly. I never figured out what the cause of it was, but I think I’ve stumbled upon a remedy.

A few weeks ago, I started walking the treadmill at the gym. While walking, I carry two 25lbs dumbbells hanging in my hands. I alternate walking for one minute loaded and then about a minute unloaded, and I do this for 10+ minutes. It’s not a bad exercise, and it’s an easy way to get a sweat. I think it may be counteracting whatever dystrophy I may have experienced in my wrists.

Today at the piano, I wasn’t noticeably sore at all. It was just… gone. And remember that classical piece I practice, Chopin’s Etude Op. 10 No. 5? For the first time, after years at this point, it felt EASY. Like, I was able to play it like I would want to perform it. And yea, there’s been moments over the years where the piece really started to open up for me, but there’s always been “barriers to fulfillment”(a tricky fingering here, tension build up there, can’t catch the tempo here, etc. etc.). Now it feels like I’m finally wide open. There’s still a tad bit of tension, but it’s like it’s dissolving before my eyes now. I’m now relearning it at speed, and I expect it to be at a performable standard this upcoming season.

This is reminiscent of when I started doing dead hangs, and my comfort with the fiddle started improving by leaps and bounds. I’m curious to know what other kinds of experiences others have had in terms of their musical fitness, so I’m opening the conversation back up for a 2nd round.

I strongly recommend the use of so-called Indian Clubs to keep the shoulders, arms, wrists and back strong and loose, and to improve grip strength. There’s loads of instruction videos on YouTube. There’s something about the loose, flowing, flexible strength they build that feels very compatible with the physical needs of a musician.

Re: Music and Fitness 2.

I have found being in the peak of fitness and health has always aided and improved one’s playing of the Trad Irish music.
I keep up to Olympic-style buffness by following a simple physical regime.
Firstly, exercise the hands and arms by lifting pint after pint of Guinness (other beverages are available) to the mouth and drinking it.
Generally doing this sitting down is better as one doesn’t waste energy standing up.
I save my energy for playing by always getting the ‘bus to the session. Walking will just fritter away your strength. Even better is Über because then one doesn’t even have to walk to the ’bus stop.
Lung health is preserved by a regular stroll outside to smoke things which involves exercising one’s chest muscles and usually one’s voice, as a trip outside for a fag is seldomly a solo activity and there is always loads of bollox to be spoken. Inside one can liberally consume snuff.
Diet of course is a large part of the regime. When in the pub’, keep the pangs of hunger away by eating loads of crisps and peanuts. And, on the way home, always have a large donner and chips or a portion of Dallas fried chicken and chips with a 7-Up or strawberry Mirinda. This will supply you with the necessary fuel to keep you going.
Finally, don’t get tempted to carry more than just one’s normal first choice instrument. Don’t take the additional hurdy gurdy or octave mandolin down to the pub’ session. Hauling them will waste energy and you’re only taking them to show them off anyway! And make sure, if possible, you have a nice lightweight zip-up for you instrument. Those hard, wooden cases are far too heavy.
Good luck with this!

Re: Music and Fitness 2.

I assume that fitness - strength and flexibility - is an important factor in how well one can play music. And I mean “factor” - if one is good enough one could still play well despite being in poor physical shape, but being fit would presumably make it easier and better. The longer I can hold the fiddle and bow up the longer I can play, and the longer I can play the more practice I’ll get. I imagine if the only arm exercise I got was holding up the fiddle I would not progress very fast. Similarly the more flexible I am the easier it will be to play fast without tension.

It’s well known that being as relaxed as possible while playing is hugely important, both for being able to play well and also for not developing soreness and pain. i.e; playing with tension will cause damage, maybe in small amounts depending on the type and degree of tension, but it will cause damage and any damage if it doesn’t heal early enough can compound to being worse and longer term. Strength and flexibility are helpful in being able to stay relaxed - though relaxing is itself something that must be practiced - strength and flexibility on their own do not imply relaxation.

I think posture is also crucial long term. If I damage my back by having it hunched or curved while playing, or if I don’t keep my shoulders back and relaxed, or if I contort my neck at all, these will lead to subtle or possibly severe injury that could make it harder to play relaxed for as long. And I can practice posture - keeping a straight back and neck and keeping shoulders rolled back and relaxed - throughout all of life, not just while playing.

Importantly to me I want to be able to play as well and as long as possible into old age if we get to that point. I figure the more I exercise - in a safe way - my whole life from now till then, the longer and better I’ll be able to play. The stronger and more flexible I am the less likely I’ll get injured. Of course there could always be the random injury that will end the music no matter how fit I am, and, I think that for me a significant majority of potential injury and health problems can be mitigated if not prevented entirely through healthy habits.

So yes, I highly recommend taking good care of the body including by being fit. A gym or equipment is not necessary, there are exercises and stretches that cover most of the body without equipment. What’s worked well for me was finding a few simple exercises that strengthen a lot of the muscles and joints and can be done anywhere, and doing as many as I can (safely) in a 20min window. The number has gradually increased. It also helps to generally move the body as much as possible in the day too. But I know with my 20min window where I exhaust myself, if that number I can do in 20min goes up I’m getting more fit, even if my day was mostly driving or at the computer or something. So it requires very little time.

I learned the fiddle at school - gave up & took it up again when I was thirty. The first few weeks were absolute agony - my fingers and wrists hurt so much. Eventually I got used to it and my hands became more fluent. Then work got in the way and I gave up again.

Ten years (plus) ago, I returned to the fiddle in retirement. I was expecting a lot of pain again. But there was no problem at all and I even seemed to have improved in thirty years of not-playing.

I think the answer lies in all the typing I’ve done, as a teacher preparing notes, and as a would-be writer. I taught myself to touch-type when I was young, and it’s kept my fingers supple.

I’m sure fitness must help a lot, but flexibility and stretching and keeping moving can do a lot for the small muscles, it seems.
