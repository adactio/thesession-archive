Re: Writing ABC

It depends on how you construe the “off-beat roll” sequence. Since you are a banjo player, I can see that having a dotted crotchet (F3, or G3) makes no sense considering how you are obliged to play the tune on your instrument. But for fiddle and wind players, it may make perfect sense, so who is to say it’s incorrect?

As both a fiddle and wind player, I don’t like it either, but I have other concerns. If I had to notate it with ornamentation (i.e. indicating the roll) I would do it like this dF ~F2 i.e. without the brackets to indicate a tie -- or, if I wanted to notate it exactly as I would play it, as d(F~F2), because that is how I would articulate this figure on both fiddle and whistle/flute.

Most of the time though I think dF F2 is better, leaving the player to add graces/ornaments to suit their particular instrument.

Crossing a bar line is basically what makes it a tie. It’s only a tie if used where a long note can’t be written as a single note, ie across a bar line. Between two different notes it’s a slur. And even then there is no rule to be broken, in classical music it’s not at all uncommon to find whole multi-bar phrases slurred together.

Re: Writing ABC

I’m coming to this discussion a little late, but hopefully my contribution will make some sense. thesession is about ITM in the main, and in that context ornamentation is about cuts, taps (pats, snaps, whatever), long rolls, short rolls, etc. Any symbols are there to give clues to playing those ornaments and nothing else, whatever the meaning of a symbol such as the tilde (~) may be in official ABC. Many players, myself included, often write “c”, “r”, “s.r.”, before the ornamented note so that it is printed above the note when the notation is viewed on a computer. Out of laziness, I sometimes use tildes (although not on anything I would post here (I hope)), and
d~F3 would mean d followed by a long roll on F (that’s an F, a cut on an F and a tap on an F); dF~F2 would mean d followed by F followed by a short roll on F (a cut on an F and a tap on an F). That’s it. Whether the two ornaments sound different would depend on how they were articulated and possibly how they were timed. Or they might only feel different. I tend to agree that ornamentation shouldn’t be indicated in the tunes we post here although I have sometimes given ‘close’ transcription of a particular player’s version of a tune in which I try to give all the ornamentation used.
