Chairs, carpets, smells, rowing boats but no hot water bottles…

How important are the furnishings and accoutrements of a pub’ to a session?

Some players find a chair more appropriate than sitting on part of the built in settee-like seating places. Some players insist on a chair without arms (e.g. pipers & six string devil twanglers). Some prefer a low stool. A high stool (bar stool) is often not right for anyone although players at gigs often use them. I suppose you always look a bit of a nonce sat on a high chair at the table! Low comfortable upholstered chairs don’t really work.
I quite like a set up with built in furniture along one side (e.g. under the window) and ‘loose’ chairs around the other side. When a session is held around a couple of tables with only ‘loose’ chairs, it tends to limit the number of people taking part (a good thing some will say!). You can always sneak one more whistler on a sofa-like built in piece of furniture.
Then there is the hunt around a pub’, looking for a chair for yourself to drag over to the players.
Does anyone ever take a small folding fishing stool just in case? How do you feel about people standing up a playing at sessions?
Do you prefer sitting on wood or upholstery? Sitting on plastic tends to lead to sweaty arse situation. Never a good one. Do you prefer patterned coverings or plain? Laura Ashley, Arts & Crafts, Deco, London Transport Tube Stylee, Tartan?

Do you prefer carpet or boards? Carpet tends to muffle the foot percussion brigade (a good thing in my opinion). Bare boards would normally lead to a ‘liver’ acoustical quality.
Talking of acoustics, do some pubs have a better ‘sound’ than others? My local session pub’ is decorated with loads of old junk everywhere including a complete rowing boat suspended from the ceiling. We have often discussed how the acoustic quality of the session would change should the owner modernize the place and chuck out the junk (‘junk’ in the sense of ‘rubbish’ not a Chinese boat!). By the way: I just hope it’s really well stuck up there. Sometimes people haven’t really noticed it before (Gawd knows how!) and when you point it out it worries them slightly!

And what about lighting? Do you like moody low lights or full on fluorescent strips?

Olfactory conditions: Personally, I preferred pubs smelling of fag smoke. At least it covered up the halitosis, BO, toilet-y smells, farts, bog cleaner, pong of wig glue (SyrupStik), cheap ladies perfume (I mean cheap perfume of ladies not the perfume cheap ladies wear or, indeed, the smell of cheap women- all depends where the pub’ is I suppose!) and cooking food smells that now permeate the places.
