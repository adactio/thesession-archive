Advice Needed : Facebook Or Other Forum For Visiting/Travelling Musicians ? ? ?

Over the past few days we had a very good discussion about changing attitudes in Irish traditional music,
(https://thesession.org/discussions/40105 ) ..I took a lot from the various comments, and feel vindicated that I am not the only one who has noticed or witnessed a severe decline in attitude, manners and lack of acceptance outside the ‘select few’ in many sessions in Ireland today As said in the previous post , I have been playing trad (and folk) for nearly 40 years, much of it at semi-pro level and am quite irate at such ‘selective’ and ‘exclusive’ governance on many open sessions , by many of my fellow players …but the discussion got me thinking ;

One poster (on another thread) was asking for advice as to which instrument to bring from America across to Clare /Kerry on his honeymoon, as he would like to play in a session or two and more or less feel the buzz. It was hard not only for myself but for others to advise him, "look…you really wouldn’t be accepted as a player in many of these sessions, regardless of your ability..most of those session are closed, and the participants can be quite rude and straight forward about excluding you, regardless of your talent’ ..which truthfully, is the bottom line, and begs the question is an open session a session any longer..?

This got me thinking..how disappointing it must be for musicians visiting Ireland , and the so called Big Trad Counties, not to be allowed to sit-in or play with Irish musicians, share a bit of craic and banter, share and play a few tunes and experience the Irish pub session, in Ireland…to be honest it’s not acceptable.

SO HERE‘S MY QUESTION…should or could some type of closed group be established that would generate a community of trad musicians , that would allow members to meet , play and share the elements of the tradition, whatever country they are in or they are visiting. I am in no way advocating anything like CCE (grrrrr….definitely not…), and not on that scale. I am suggesting something that would allow travelling or visiting musicians , for example here in Ireland to go into a session without fear of confronting such ignorance and ’lock out’ from the select few , but rather play with group members and their trusted associates. Musicians would know were they were going, who they were playing with and have a better idea of what they would actually be playing as the materiel could be shared on the forum/group and used as a framework for any session and played in relaxed, stress free comfort..

This is only an idea, whether it would work or not I dont know. But take our friend above on his honeymoon, travelling to Clare, at least if there was other musicians over from the States or England at the same time, part of the group, he could have meet with them as well a players from Ireland, again part of the group and shared a session or two in the safe comfort of friends and acquaintances, knowing there would be no rejection or ignorance…. The group, be it Facebook or whatever could also be used as an excellent resource for local information, accommodation , help & advice and allow for less anonymity providing the group in closed..

I would appreciate any input…as I said, from what I see it has more positives than negatives going for it and perhaps would offer an alternative approach to the problems associated with either finding or playing at a session in various regions by visiting musicians… I have currently hung up my session boots because of the current attitudes associated with many sessions, something needs to be done to stop the poison of ignorance and self servitude at many sessions in Ireland, which is excluding to many well able players from enjoying the craic as well….

Please note..I understand not all sessions in Ireland are like the ones I have described, so I hope I have not offended some people.. but there is a large proportion of sessions that are situated right in the heart of big trad and tourist music areas around the country , and they do ‘close’ the session and lock out all but a small favored few.. ( a lot of them are staged gigs, not sessions ) ..I also find these sessions tend to be very conservative, stiff upper lipped and dry, there is little or no craic and the whole session in simply self serving for a small number of individuals, including the publican..that to me is not what a traditional Irish pub session is all about, and I would love to see something that fights back against it and opens the door again for all standards.

Re: Advice Needed : Facebook Or Other Forum For Visiting/Travelling Musicians ? ? ?

I’ve found the discussions started by Leitrim Lad to be pretty interesting, and somewhat discouraging. Maybe an unfortunate result of the popularity of Irish music.

This whole competition/contest thing strikes me as really weird. Taken to the extreme, it might become like ballroom dance competitions, which are far removed from actual social ballroom dance. See the movie “Strictly Ballroom” for a great send-up of the whole scene.

You are pointing out that these closed sessions are sometimes more like gigs than jams. What’s the trend? Amplification and a sound man to make sure the mix is right. Stage with colored lights?

To the extent that they get more gig-like, that will make it easier to distinguished them from “real” sessions.

That will also make it easier for real sessions to promote their openness.

Getting back to your original post, maybe we just need better mechanisms for identifying whether a session is open or gig-ish. Maybe not Facebook; perhaps the Sessions tab at this website could have more interaction or a “like button” or ratings, or even just reviews that pointed out what a visitor would expect.

Or maybe if you want a session to be Traditional, you need to shift expectations away from gig-like through marketing language and shared agreements with the core players, like “We play at 80% speed so that the largest number of our crowd can play”.
