↳ Posted by Jusa Nutter Eejit on August 7th, 2008.

Re: How do i start learning faster irish music

cq0, find a good teacher or mentor you can learn from face to face. Failing that, go here: http://www.rogermillington.com/siamsa/brosteve/ and follow Brother Steve’s excellent tutorial and advice.

In the beginning, focus on good timing, rhythm, and tone. A jig or reel will sound better played slowly with a steady beat than it will at speed but sloppy. Don’t try to play a tune faster until you can play it cleanly with rock solid rhythm.

Some “easy” tunes for whistle include:

Kerfunteun Jig
Joy of My Life (aka Donnybrook Fair)
The Orphan
Terry Cuz Teehan’s

The fast tunes are just the same to learn as the slow ones. I don’t really know what software people use, but those who don’t read sheet music often slow the tune down until it is at a reasonable speed. Re: How do i start learning faster irish music

I’m learning all this now. There is nothing more ornamented than GHB music, where each note has a specific pattern of ornament notes preceding it, depending on the note that comes before it. You could have four or five short notes in an ornament, and you need to learn each one cold.

Only extremely slow and steady playing, taking each note out of rhythm, will give you success. Little by little, it’s sounding more authentic, and so will your reels and jigs.

Re: How do i start learning faster irish music

what you are really doing when you build speed is you are training your nerves and muscles to execute things with less conscious direction from you. Like Greg said, “Finger memory”

That’s why the hours of practice, over and over. That’s what trains the hand to just play without you having to think about it.

they say “slow is fast” because when you practice a run slow and clean, you don’t practice in the mistakes that will tie you up at the faster tempo.

In regard to learning to hear faster, with old phonograph records, we used to listen to Charlie Parker bebop records at 45 for an hour or so. Then when you played the record at the normal 33rpm everything seemed so much slower, you could catch what was going on.

Sort of the opposite of that software that slows things down for you. Is you speed up your brain, everything slows down.

It worked for me 30 years ago, but I don’t know how you’d speed up a CD

Re: How do i start learning faster irish music

For most instruments learning slow and then speeding it up is probably okay.

For the fiddle, you might be better off learning a bar or two at a time instead of slowing down a tune dramatically because by slowing down the turn you could be dramatically affecting the bowing.

Slowing down a tune is great for figuring what the notes actually are but after that, I prefer even on the whistle to play a few bars a few dozen times and then moving on.
