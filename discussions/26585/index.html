Re: Switching to a smaller size violin

What you don’t mention is whether you are still growing, or are a 5’ adult. If you’re still growing you could persevere with the 4/4 or get a cheap 3/4 to tide you over until you fit the 4/4.

But if you are an adult, and this is your ‘violin for life’ choice, then I would avoid going down to a 3/4 if you possibly can - the size is too much of a compromise, and you won’t find one that sounds really good. But there are a lot of very nice 7/8s and ladies’ violins (which are halfway between 7/8 and 4/4) out there, and they tend to be reasonably cheap because they are harder to sell than the 4/4 equivalent.

As Greg says, the person to fit you out with the right size instrument is a well stocked dealer.

Re: Switching to a smaller size violin

@OP. Using an over-large fiddle is asking for trouble, both in the short term (as you’re already finding out) and in the long term. I’m a little surprised that your teacher doesn’t seem to have advised you on the matter. As Skreech says, there are good 7/8s around, but not 3/4s. When you try out a fiddle for size put it in the playing position and see if you can cup your left hand comfortably round the end of the scroll, with a slight bend in the elbow. If you can’t do this with a 4/4 then don’t play it, and look around for a 7/8. The danger of physical strain and damage is too real for you not to do this.

Re: Switching to a smaller size violin

Trevor, you appear to have missed the part where she said “My teacher thinks I need to switch to a smaller instrument”.

@OP:
The problem with smaller violins (as people mention) is that almost nobody wants to pay for a good one (since they’re usually built for kids, who are much more likely to drop it getting on or off the schoolbus), and so there is little incentive, outside of a completely custom construction, for any decent violin maker to build them. You can get lucky, but you’re much more likely to get raked.

That said, the conventional measurements for undersize violins are really quite small. I’ve never been a large person, but I remember going to my first full-size violin at the age of 10, when I was (if memory serves) 4’7" and around 70 lbs.

I wouldn’t presume to diagnose your own individual situation without having seen you first, but if “tension” is the problem, there are other things you can do to relieve it before you drop a pile of cash on a virtually custom instrument. You might consider taking one or two lessons with a different teacher, for instance.

It’s possible that many of your issues are actually merely psychological; that merely playing on a rented 3l4 fiddle for a month or two might fix whatever issues you have with the full-size.

If all else fails, then maybe you do need a special instrument… But I’d try to be certain that you need to go there before you really

My Wife has a full size fiddle (in fact slightly oversize) which is a mighty beast indeed, but just after she had bought it, she was too shy to take it to Scotland for a New Year trip home(my home). So we decided to borrow a violin for the duration of our trip from Stringer’s in Edinburgh to “try out” with no real intention of buying it. However, we learned the hard way why the shops are so willing to do this since we ended up buying the loan violin too…

It was a lovely 7/8 Scottish made violin for not really that much money (some acoustically insignficant repairs) and it has a better sound than most full size ones I’ve heard played in the flesh. Naturally a bit quieter than her other violin, but then most are. So, I can confirm that good sounding 7/8 violins do exist. This is the one she takes to sessions.

However, though I am not a fiddler, following my wife on the search for violins taught me the importance of setup, both for sound and for comfort. Good fiddles sounded crap when set up one way and like completely different instruments when setup well.

Re: Switching to a smaller size violin

If you’re in the U.S.,
Gliga U.S.A has 7/8 violins- they have their US distribution center in Los Angeles, and you can try them out there if you are anywhere close.
One lady went down from Ventura just to try them out, found a 7/8 she really liked and bought it.

Elida Trading Company carries Gliga in the U.K.

I’ve heard Eastman Strings makes them too.

One of the challenges is that your selection is going to be somewhat limited compared to full size, even if a shop carries some, and the places that carry them are most likely to be in big cities.
So you may have to go a ways to try one…
but you SHOULD try it out, because here’s the deal-
since a 7/8 size violins are unusual, it’s going to be harder to resell if you don’t like it-
so it’s best to make sure it’s a “keeper” by playing it first.

Other alternative:
Because they ARE hard to sell, there may be one out there that the seller is getting desperate to sell, and you may be able to pick it up for cheap…. but that’s if you like gambling on eBay and such. You could do that a couple of times and maybe end up with one you really like…. maybe!!!
